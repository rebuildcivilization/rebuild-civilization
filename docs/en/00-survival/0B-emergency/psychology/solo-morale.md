# Solo Morale & Mental Health Maintenance

## Objective
To provide concrete, daily practices to keep your mind functional and your motivation intact when you are alone, as the greatest threat to a solo survivor is psychological collapse.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
The prerequisite for these practices is being a solo survivor in extended isolation.

## Material
### The Danger of Isolation
Within the first week of total isolation, most people begin experiencing:
- Day 1-3: Anxiety, hypervigilance, difficulty sleeping, racing thoughts
- Day 4-7: Mood swings, irritability at minor frustrations, obsessive checking behaviors
- Week 2-3: Distorted time perception, talking to objects, decreased motivation for self-care
- Week 4+: Paranoia, auditory disturbances (hearing voices or sounds that aren’t there), emotional numbness, questioning whether survival is worth the effort

### Warning Signs to Monitor
- Stopping eating when food is available
- No longer caring about hygiene or shelter maintenance
- Sleeping more than 10 hours per day consistently
- Engaging in reckless behavior (unnecessary risks, neglecting safety protocols)
- Persistent thoughts that nothing matters

### Building Daily Structure
Routine is the single most important psychological tool in isolation. The non-negotiable daily framework includes:
1. Wake at a consistent time — use sunrise or a watch.
2. Personal hygiene — wash face and hands, brush teeth, change clothes if possible.
3. Physical movement — minimum 20 minutes of deliberate exercise.
4. One productive task — repair something, improve shelter, gather resources.
5. One maintenance task — clean space, organize supplies, check perimeter.
6. Evening review — review the day, plan tomorrow, journal if possible.

### Task Scheduling
- Morning (first 3 hours): High-priority survival tasks (water, food, security).
- Midday: Maintenance, organization, skill practice.
- Afternoon: Secondary projects, exploration, resource gathering.
- Evening (last 2 hours before sleep): Reflection, planning, creative or recreational activities.

### Sleep Discipline
- Set a sleep time and stick to it (7-8 hours per night).
- Create a sleep ritual (same actions in the same order every night).
- Ensure darkness and quiet.
- If unable to sleep, get up for a quiet task for 20 minutes before trying again.
- Limit naps to one, 20-30 minutes, before 2 PM.

### Psychological Survival Tools
**Journaling & Self-Dialogue:**
- Journal daily:
    - Three things that went right today.
    - One thing you’re worried about and one concrete step.
    - Tomorrow’s plan.
- Talk to yourself out loud to narrate actions and name internal states (affect labeling) to reduce emotion intensity.

**Goal Setting:**
- Daily goals (e.g., “patch the roof leak and boil water”).
- Weekly goals (e.g., “build a fish trap”).
- Long-term goals (e.g., “make contact with other survivors”).
- Goals must be specific and achievable.

**Managing Fear and Anxiety:**
- **The Worry Window:** Designate 15 minutes daily for worry.
- **The Control Inventory:** List things that can be controlled vs. things that cannot.
- **The “And Then What?” Technique:** Follow catastrophic thoughts to a conclusion to generate a plan.

**Finding Purpose:**
Sources of purpose include:
- Someone is waiting for you.
- Knowledge preservation.
- Rebuilding the foundation for recovery.
- Curiosity.
- Spite.

### The Physical-Mental Connection
**Exercise:**
- Exercise is the most effective antidepressant.
- Requires walking/running, bodyweight exercises (pushups, squats, lunges, planks), or practical exercise (chopping wood, carrying water).
- Key principle: consistency over intensity (20 minutes daily is effective).

**Nutrition and Mental State:**
- Eat regularly to prevent blood sugar crashes.
- Prioritize protein and fat to stabilize blood sugar.
- Drink water consistently to combat dehydration, which causes irritability and poor judgment.

**Sunlight:**
- Get direct sunlight within the first hour of waking to reset circadian clocks and boost serotonin.
- Spend more time outside on overcast days.

### Maintaining Identity
- **Personal Hygiene:** Wash daily, keep the space organized, maintain grooming to maintain self-image as a capable person.
- **Creative Expression:** Engage in activities like carving, arranging stones, singing, or drawing to provide mental rest and produce something.
- **Holding On:** Keep a physical object connecting to the previous life to prevent the pre-collapse identity from dissolving.

### Common Mistakes
- Sleeping too much (limit to 8 hours plus one short nap).
- Abandoning routine on “bad days” (do the minimum framework).
- Suppressing emotions (use journaling/worry window for controlled release).
- Neglecting physical health (skipping exercise or meals).
- Waiting to feel motivated (motivation follows action).

## System
The system relies on building external structure when the external world lacks it.

## Technology
- Sunrise or a watch for setting a consistent wake time.
- Potential use of writing materials for journaling.
- Potential use of earplugs for sleep discipline.

## Tools
- Writing materials (for journaling).
- A watch or sunrise for time tracking.
- Physical objects for holding on to identity.

## Process
1. **Establish Structure:** Implement the Non-Negotiable Daily Framework.
2. **Allocate Time:** Divide waking hours into Morning (high-priority), Midday (maintenance), and Afternoon (secondary).
3. **Execute Daily Tasks:** Complete survival tasks, maintenance tasks, and productive tasks.
4. **Manage Internal State:** Utilize psychological tools (Journaling, Worry Window) to process emotions and fear.
5. **Maintain Physical State:** Engage in consistent exercise, prioritized nutrition, and sunlight exposure.

Notes
- The core premise is that the greatest threat is psychological collapse, not physical needs (starvation, dehydration, predators).
- Exercise is explicitly framed as the most effective antidepressant due to the release of serotonin, dopamine, and endorphins.
- External accountability (a visible schedule) is critical when internal accountability is absent.
- The concept of 'affect labeling' (naming emotions) is a key technique for managing fear and anxiety.
- The distinction between daily goals (keeping you moving) and long-term goals (keeping you caring about moving) is crucial for sustained motivation.
