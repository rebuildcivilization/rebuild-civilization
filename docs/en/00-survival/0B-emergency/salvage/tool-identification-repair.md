# Tool Identification & Repair

## Objective
To teach the reader how to recognize, evaluate, and restore tools from wreckage, thereby turning debris into capability.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Having the necessary tools or materials to perform the repair or identification.
- For electrical tools: A 12V source for charging or a 12V-to-120V inverter for running generators.
- For electrolytic removal: A car battery and washing soda (sodium carbonate).
- For manual alternatives: A brace and bit or similar hand-operated drilling tool.

## Material
- Hardwood for handles: Ash, hickory, oak, maple, beech.
- Steel for structural elements: Rebar, steel pipe, leaf springs, hardened steel for files/rasps.
- Handle replacement material: Straight-grained hardwood, wire, cord, tape, leather, wax.
- Abrasives: River rocks (sandstone, quartzite, slate), ceramic tile bottoms, wet/dry automotive sandpaper, emery cloth, diamond-coated nail files.
- Chemical agents: White vinegar (for soaking), penetrating oil (WD-40, PB Blaster, kerosene, diesel fuel, or 50/50 ATF/acetone mix), motor oil, cooking oil, rendered animal fat, petroleum jelly, beeswax.

## System
### Power Tools System
- **Cordless Battery Types:**
    - Lithium-ion (Li-ion): Modern tools, hold charge for months (300-500 cycles).
    - Nickel-cadmium (NiCd): Older tools, self-discharge in weeks, virtually indestructible, revivable from full discharge.
    - Nickel-metal hydride (NiMH): Moderate performance, self-discharge in days to weeks.
- **Power Generation System:**
    - **Generator:** Justified when a task would take 10x+ longer by hand (cutting steel, drilling concrete).
    - **Solar Alternative:** A 100-200W portable solar panel system with a charge controller and battery bank provides indefinite charging with zero noise and zero fuel.

### Tool Utility System
- **Function-Based System:** Tools are categorized by their basic function:
    1. Cutting tools (separates material).
    2. Striking tools (delivers impact).
    3. Gripping tools (holds, turns, or pulls).
    4. Fastening tools (joins materials).
    5. Measuring tools (precision).
    6. Digging tools (excavation).
- **Manual vs. Power System:** Manual alternatives exist for every power tool, providing torque and durability.

## Technology
### Rust Technology
- **Surface Rust:** Cosmetic; appears as an orange-brown coating that scrapes off to reveal solid metal. Does not weaken the tool.
- **Pitting Rust:** Small holes eaten into the surface. Cosmetic for thick tools (wrenches, crowbars); weakens thin tools (saw blades, knife blades, springs).
- **Structural Rust:** Eaten through the cross-section, resulting in flaky, crumbly, or visible holes in load-bearing areas.

### Sharpening Technology
- **Bevel Angle Application:** The required bevel angle depends on the tool type for optimal balance between sharpness and durability:
    - Razors and scalpels: 12-15° per side (30° total).
    - Kitchen knives: 15-20° per side.
    - Bushcraft/utility knives: 20-25° per side.
    - Axes and hatchets: 25-35° per side.
    - Chisels: 25-30° single bevel.
- **Burr Test:** A correctly sharpened edge creates a slight burr when run perpendicular to the edge, which is removed via stropping.

## Tools
### Tool Categories by Function
- **Cutting tools:** Knives, saws (hand saws, hacksaws, pruning saws), scissors, shears, chisels, tin snips, bolt cutters, wire cutters, glass cutters, axes, hatchets, machetes, razor blades, utility knives, scalpels.
- **Striking tools:** Hammers (claw, ball-peen, sledge, rubber mallet), mallets, clubs, dead-blow hammers, heavy wrenches, lengths of pipe, rocks.
- **Gripping tools:** Pliers (needle-nose, channel-lock, linesman), vise-grips, pipe wrenches, wrenches (open-end, box, socket, adjustable), clamps (C-clamps, bar clamps, spring clamps).
- **Fastening tools:** Screwdrivers (Phillips, flathead, Torx, Robertson), nut drivers, rivet tools, staple guns, wire, cordage, adhesives (epoxy, super glue, contact cement), tape.
- **Measuring tools:** Tape measures, rulers, levels, squares (framing square, speed square, combination square), plumb bobs, calipers.
- **Digging tools:** Shovels (flat, pointed, trenching), picks, mattocks, post-hole diggers, garden forks, trowels.
- **Leverage tools:** Crowbars, pry bars, wrecking bars, pinch bars, nail pullers.

### Improvised Tool Sources
- Leaf springs (high-carbon steel) for forging into knives, chisels, scrapers.
- Rebar (mild steel) for hooks, pry bars, handles, reinforcement.
- Steel pipe for cheater bars, handles, structural support, weapon shafts.
- Angle iron for shelving brackets, bed frames (drill and bolt into custom jigs and frames).
- Files and rasps for chisels, scrapers, or knife blanks.
- Lawnmower blades (hardened steel) for machetes or chopping tools.
- Car coil springs (high-quality spring steel) for arrow points or trap components.

### Where Tools Hide
- Residential garages and sheds (richest general-purpose source: pegboards, rolling cabinets, workbenches).
- Vehicle trunks and truck beds (tire irons, jack handles, jumper cables, toolboxes).
- Maintenance closets (basic hand tools, cleaning supplies).
- Construction sites (power tools, hand tools, hardware, lumber, heavy equipment).
- Farm equipment sheds (wrenches, pliers, wire, fencing tools, welding equipment, grinding wheels).
- Restaurant kitchens (heavy-duty knives, sharpening steels, heavy pots, propane connections).
- Janitor closets (basic tools, mop handles as useful poles, buckets, cleaning chemicals).

## Process
### Tool Identification and Evaluation
1. **Recognition:** Scan for tools, thinking in terms of function, not brand names.
2. **Categorization:** Mentally scan for function (Cutting, Striking, Gripping, Fastening, Measuring, Digging).
3. **Condition Assessment (The Test):**
    - **Metal Integrity:** Grip and flex the tool. If it moves or crumbles where it should not, it is structurally compromised.
    - **Rust Assessment:** Differentiate surface rust (cosmetic) from pitting rust (potential weakness) and structural rust (failure point).
    - **Handle Evaluation:** Check wood handles for cracks (structural failure) and softness (rot). Check plastic/rubber grips for brittleness.
    - **Moving Parts Evaluation:** Check for seized/frozen joints.

### Repair Process
- **Joint Repair (Seized/Frozen Joints):**
    1. Attempt to soak the joint in penetrating oil (10-30 minutes).
    2. If oil fails, heat the joint with a torch (heat the outer piece to expand it).
    3. Work the joint while warm.
- **Handle Replacement:**
    1. Select straight-grained hardwood (Ash, Hickory preferred).
    2. Split or saw the blank 20% larger in all dimensions.
    3. Shape the cross-section to be oval.
    4. Seat the head by tapping, inverting, striking the bottom, and driving a wedge to lock it.
- **Rust Removal:**
    1. **Vinegar Soak:** Submerge rusted tools for 12-24 hours, scrub, rinse, and dry.
    2. **Electrolytic Removal:** Use water and washing soda; attach the positive terminal to a sacrificial steel piece (anode) and the negative terminal to the rusted tool (cathode). Bubbles indicate effectiveness.
    3. **Light Rust:** Use 120-220 grit sandpaper or #0000 steel wool, working in one direction.
- **Sharpening Process:**
    1. **Stone Sharpening:** Use hard, smooth stones (river rocks, ceramic tile, concrete sidewalk).
    2. **File Sharpening:** Use a bastard file (10-12 inch), filing into the edge, matching the existing bevel angle.
    3. **The Burr Test:** Run a thumb perpendicular across the edge; the edge should feel smooth after stropping.

### Power Tool Alternatives Process
- **Battery Management:** Determine if the task requires the power tool (5x+ longer by hand).
- **Charging Options:**
    - **Generator:** Use when task is major (cutting steel, drilling concrete) and fuel is available.
    - **Solar:** Use for indefinite charging with zero noise/fuel.
    - **Direct 12V:** Use carefully, requiring a boost converter for 18V/20V batteries, with fire risk awareness.
- **Manual Substitution:** Match the required action to the best manual alternative (e.g., Brace and bit for drilling, crosscut saw for circular saw).

## Notes
- Surface rust is cosmetic and does not weaken the tool.
- Structural rust is the most dangerous and means the tool will fail under stress.
- Handles are replaceable; the head is the critical component.
- Ratchet mechanisms in socket wrenches and ratcheting screwdrivers are generally not field-repairable if the pawl or spring is broken.
- For cordless battery conservation, avoiding full discharge is crucial for Li-ion cells.
- For handling degraded grips, restoration methods include wrapping with cord, tape, or leather.
