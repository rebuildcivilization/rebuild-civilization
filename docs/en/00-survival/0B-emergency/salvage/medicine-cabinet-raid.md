# Medicine Cabinet Raid: Salvaging Medications

## Objective
To salvage medications from a post-collapse world, prioritizing antibiotics because a bottle of antibiotics is considered more valuable than a rifle. The objective is to determine where to find medications, which ones matter most, how long they last, and how to store them for maximum lifespan.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Knowledge:** Understanding cross-referencing doses (especially for veterinary vs. human medications). 
*   **Storage:** Ability to maintain optimal storage conditions (cool, dry, dark, airtight).
*   **Tools:** Access to desiccants (silica gel) and vacuum sealing equipment for long-term preservation.
*   **Judgment:** Ability to make critical decisions regarding controlled substances and medication potency.

## Material
### Medications Found
*   **Pharmacies:** Prescription stock, OTC drugs (ibuprofen, acetaminophen, aspirin, antihistamines, cold/flu meds), controlled substances (opioids, benzodiazepines).
*   **Hospitals/Clinics:** Larger stocks, IV fluids, surgical tools, specialized medications.
*   **Home Medicine Cabinets:** OTC pain relievers, antihistamines, cold/flu medications, leftover prescription antibiotics, chronic disease medications (blood pressure, cholesterol, diabetes, thyroid, psychiatric), topical antibiotics (Neosporin, bacitracin), antacids.
*   **Veterinary Clinics/Farm Supply:** Chemically identical medications to human ones (e.g., Amoxicillin, Cephalexin, Metronidazole, Doxycycline, Penicillin G, Ivermectin).
*   **Medicinal Plants (Fallback):** Willow bark, Garlic, Honey (Manuka), Yarrow, Plantain, Activated Charcoal.

### Critical Drug Categories
*   **Antibiotics (Tier 1):** Amoxicillin, Ciprofloxacin, Doxycycline, Metronidazole.
*   **Pain & Fever:** Ibuprofen, Acetaminophen, Aspirin, Tramadol, Codeine, Hydrocodone/oxycodone, Lidocaine.
*   **Specialty Medications:** Epinephrine (EpiPens), Insulin (rapid-acting, long-acting, intermediate), Anti-seizure medications (carbamazepine, phenytoin, valproate, levetiracetam), Cardiac medications (beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, nitroglycerin, statins), Thyroid hormone (levothyroxine), Psychiatric medications (SSRIs, antipsychotics, benzodiazepines).
*   **GI & Respiratory Essentials:** Loperamide, Oral rehydration salts (ORS), Diphenhydramine, Albuterol inhalers, Prednisone.

## System
### Degradation Factors (What Actually Degrades Medications)
Four factors destroy medications:
1. **Heat:** The single biggest factor. Every 10°C increase roughly doubles the degradation rate.
2. **Moisture:** Water activates chemical reactions that break down active compounds. Humidity accelerates degradation.
3. **Light:** UV radiation breaks molecular bonds.
4. **Oxygen:** Oxidation degrades many compounds. Once a bottle is opened, the clock accelerates.

### Shelf Life Reality
*   **SLEP Study Data:** 88% of tested medications retained full potency for an average of 5.5 years beyond expiration. Some remained potent 15+ years past expiration.
*   **Stability:** Tablets and capsules in sealed, original packaging are the most stable.
*   **Liquid Formulations:** Degrade faster than solid forms.
*   **Specific Expirations:** Tetracycline can cause kidney damage (Fanconi syndrome); Nitroglycerin loses potency rapidly once opened; Opened liquid antibiotics expire in 7-14 days.

### Optimal Storage Conditions
*   **Temperature:** Below 25°C (77°F) is good; below 15°C (59°F) is excellent. Cool storage (root cellar, basement) is preferred.
*   **Humidity:** Below 60% relative humidity.
*   **Light:** Store in opaque containers; use cloth or foil if using clear containers.
*   **Oxygen:** Keep bottles sealed. Remove oxygen by filling empty space with cotton balls.

### Repackaging & Desiccants
*   **Desiccant Packets:** Place 1-2 packets per medication bottle to absorb moisture.
*   **Vacuum Sealing:** The gold standard for long-term storage; removes oxygen and creates a moisture barrier.
*   **Mylar Bags:** Used with oxygen absorbers and desiccants to seal medications inside original bottles.
*   **Labeling:** Critical for crisis situations; containers must be clearly labeled with drug name, dose, quantity, original expiration date, and repackaging date.

## Technology
*   **Automated Dispensing:** Pyxis or Omnicell cabinets in hospitals/clinics (require power to operate).
*   **Chemical/Biological Equivalence:** The concept that many veterinary medications are chemically identical to human medications.

## Tools
*   Desiccant packets (silica gel).
*   Vacuum sealer.
*   Mylar bags with oxygen absorbers.
*   Syringes and pen needles (for insulin/Epinephrine).
*   ORS preparation supplies (sugar, salt, water).

## Process
1. **Prioritize:** Focus on broad-spectrum antibiotics (Tier 1) first.
2. **Locate:** Search pharmacies, hospitals, and home medicine cabinets.
3. **Assess:** Check the shelf life and storage conditions of all found medications.
4. **Select:** Grab the most critical, stable, and essential items (Antibiotics, Insulin, ORS).
5. **Preserve:** Repackage unstable medications using vacuum sealing or Mylar bags if long-term storage is planned.

## Notes
*   Antibiotics are the leading cause of preventable death in austere environments.
*   When dosing veterinary medications, always check the milligram amount on the label and cross-reference with standard human dosing, as concentrations can vary.
*   For pain management, alternating acetaminophen and ibuprofen doses can offer enhanced effect.
*   For GI distress, Oral Rehydration Salts are highlighted as saving more lives globally than any antibiotic.
