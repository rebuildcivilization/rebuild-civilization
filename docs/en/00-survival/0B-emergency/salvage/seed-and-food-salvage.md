# Seed & Food Salvage

## Objective
This guide covers finding and assessing preserved food from ruins, and locating viable seeds that will become one's agricultural future. The goal is to ensure survival by utilizing preserved food and establishing an agricultural base.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Cool, Dry Storage:** Essential for seed longevity and material preservation.
*   **Identification Skills:** Critical for identifying wild plants to prevent poisoning.
*   **Testing Equipment:** Required for the paper towel germination test.

## Material
**Preserved Food Materials:**
*   Commercially canned food (for long-term caloric intake).
*   Dry staples (white rice, dried beans, lentils, sugar, salt).
*   Cooking oil.
*   Pet food (dog food and cat food).
*   Institutional stocks (canned goods in #10 cans).

**Seed Materials:**
*   Commercial seed packets (vegetable and herb varieties).
*   Wild seed collection (Dandelion, Lamb’s quarters, Purslane, Amaranth, Plantain, Wild onion/garlic).
*   Seeds from food (dried beans, popcorn kernels, whole spices, garlic cloves, potato eyes, onion bottoms).

## System
**Seed Viability System:**
Seeds are alive and respire slowly, eventually dying. Viability is highly dependent on storage conditions.

**Seed Longevity by Species Table:**
| Seed Type | Viability (years, proper storage) | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Onion, parsley, parsnip | 1-2 | Short-lived. Plant these first |
| Corn, pepper, spinach | 2-3 | Moderate life |
| Beans, peas, carrot, lettuce | 3-5 | Reliable mid-term storage |
| Beet, cucumber, melon, squash, tomato | 4-6 | Good storers |
| Brassicas (cabbage, broccoli, kale) | 4-5 | Fairly durable |
| Radish, turnip | 4-5 | Reliable |
| Wheat, barley, rice, oats | 5-10+ | Grain seeds last well |
| Lotus, date palm | Decades to centuries | Extreme outliers |

**Storage Condition System:**
The sum of temperature (°F) and relative humidity (%) should be under 100.
*   **Excellent:** 50°F (10°C) and 40% humidity (Resulting in 90).
*   **Poor:** 80°F (27°C) and 50% humidity (Resulting in 130).

**Seed Library System:**
Seeds must be labeled with species, variety, date collected, source, open-pollinated/hybrid status, last germination test date/result, and notes.

## Technology
*   **Canning Process:** Heating to 121°C / 250°F under pressure to kill bacteria.
*   **Desiccant Packets:** Used inside sealed glass jars to maintain optimal storage conditions.
*   **Oxygen Absorbers:** Used in Mylar bags for standard food storage prep.
*   **Air Squeezing:** Technique used in Ziplock bags to reduce moisture.

## Tools
*   **Testimonial Tools:** Paper towel or cloth for germination testing.
*   **Measurement Tools:** For measuring seed density and storage conditions (temperature/humidity).
*   **Identification Tools:** Field guides for plant identification.

## Process
### Canned Goods Assessment Process
1.  **Inspect Seals:** Check for intact seals, no bulging, no deep rust, and no leaks.
2.  **Check Dents:** Dents are cosmetic unless on the seam; seam damage breaks the seal.
3.  **Check Date:** Cans past the “best by” date are still safe regarding safety, but quality degrades.
4.  **Test for Bacterial Activity (Do NOT Eat):** Discard if bulging, leaking, rust-through, or if the contents smell “off.”
5.  **Botulism Risk Mitigation:** For low-acid canned foods, if in doubt, boil the contents for 10 minutes to destroy botulinum toxin (eat immediately after boiling).

### Seed Viability Testing Process (Paper Towel Test)
1.  **Prepare Medium:** Wet a paper towel or cloth until damp but not dripping.
2.  **Seed Placement:** Place 10 seeds evenly spaced on the towel.
3.  **Moisture Retention:** Fold the towel over the seeds or place another damp towel on top.
4.  **Containment:** Place the setup in a ziplock bag or covered container.
5.  **Incubation:** Store in a warm location (20-25°C / 70-80°F).
6.  **Monitoring:** Check daily, keeping the towel moist.
7.  **Counting:** Count germinated seeds after the expected germination time for that species (3-14 days for most vegetables).
8.  **Interpretation:**
    *   8-10 out of 10 sprout = excellent. Plant at normal spacing.
    *   5-7 out of 10 = moderate. Plant at 1.5x density to compensate.
    *   3-4 out of 10 = poor. Plant at 2-3x density.
    *   0-2 out of 10 = not worth planting unless all stock is used.

### Seed Storage & Saving Process
1.  **Harvesting (For Wet Seeds):** Collect seeds from mature fruit, ferment the gel coating (for tomatoes/cucumbers) in water for 2-3 days, rinse, and dry thoroughly.
2.  **Drying Protocol:** Spread seeds on screens, plates, or newspaper in a warm, dry, ventilated area until they snap (large seeds) or shatter when hit with a hammer (grain seeds).
3.  **Storage Protocol (Optimal):** Store in sealed glass jars with a desiccant packet.
4.  **Storage Protocol (Acceptable):** Use paper envelopes inside a sealed container with desiccant, or Mylar bags with oxygen absorbers.
5.  **Organization:** Label every container with species, variety, date collected, source, open-pollinated status, last germination test date/result, and notes. Organize by planting season, plant family, and urgency (planting short-lived seeds first).
6.  **Annual Renewal:** Replant a portion of the stock annually to keep the seed library alive and adapt varieties to local conditions.

## Notes
*   **Wild Plant Caution:** Never eat a wild plant unless positively identified due to the risk of toxic look-alikes. A field guide is essential.
*   **Hybrid Seed Warning:** Hybrid varieties (F1) produce unpredictable results when saved seeds are planted, as they revert to parent traits.
*   **Oil Quality:** Rancid cooking oil is still functional for cooking, although the flavor is unpleasant; the calories are real.
*   **Pet Food Utility:** Pet food is nutritionally complete for humans in an emergency.
