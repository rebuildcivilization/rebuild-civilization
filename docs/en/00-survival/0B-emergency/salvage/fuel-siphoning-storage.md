# Fuel Siphoning & Storage

## Objective
To safely extract and store fuel from abandoned vehicles and depots to extend the operational window by months.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Clear flexible tubing (1-2 cm inner diameter, at least 2 meters long).
- A container lower than the fuel tank (e.g., 5-gallon jerry can, bucket, or any vessel).
- For starting flow (jiggle method): Vigorous movement of the hose.
- For starting flow (submersion method): Water to fill the extra hose length.
- For modern vehicles: A working 12V battery to run the fuel pump.
- For diesel storage: Biocide (Biobor JF), 10-micron fuel filter, and water separators.

## Material
### Fuel Types
- Gasoline: A blend of hydrocarbons refined from petroleum, with additives including detergents, anti-knock compounds (octane boosters), and (in most countries) 10% ethanol.
- Diesel: Hydrocarbons requiring microbial treatment to prevent degradation.
- Propane (LPG): Stable indefinitely in a sealed, undamaged tank. Stored as liquid under pressure.
- Kerosene (paraffin / #1 fuel oil): Shelf life of 2-5 years in a sealed container.
- Denatured alcohol/ethanol: Used as a solvent or antiseptic.
- Biodiesel feedstock: Used cooking oil processed with methanol and lye catalyst.
- Rendered animal fat and plant oils: Work in oil lamps and some diesel engines.
- Charcoal: Produced by heating wood/biomass in a low-oxygen environment.

### Approved Containers
- Jerry cans (NATO-style): 20 liters, metal, sealed pour spout, ventilated (Gold standard).
- Plastic gas cans: 5-20 liters, HDPE plastic, self-venting (Adequate for short-term storage).
- Metal drums: 55-gallon (208 liter) steel drums, must have sealed bungs.
- IBC totes: 275-330 gallon (1,000-1,250 liter) plastic cubes in a metal cage.

## System
### Fuel Sources
- Passenger Vehicles & Trucks:
    - Passenger cars: 40-70 liter tanks, mostly gasoline.
    - SUVs and minivans: 60-100 liters, gasoline.
    - Pickup trucks: 80-135 liters, gasoline or diesel.
    - Semi trucks: 300-600 liters, diesel.
    - Delivery vans: 80-120 liters, gasoline or diesel.
- Fixed Tanks & Depots:
    - Gas station underground tanks: 10,000-40,000 liters each, typically 2-6 tanks per station.
    - Farm bulk tanks: Above-ground diesel tanks (1,000-5,000 liters) with gravity-fed or hand-cranked pumps.
    - Heating oil tanks: 275-gallon (1,040 liter) tanks in basements or alongside houses (Heating oil is essentially diesel).
    - Backup generators: Dedicated fuel tanks for diesel generators (500-10,000 liters).

### Fuel Degradation
- Gasoline Degradation: Color changes (clear/light amber to dark amber/brown), sour/varnish smell, visible layers/sediment, gummy/varnish residue.
- Diesel Degradation: Primary mechanism is microbial growth (bacteria/fungi) at the water-fuel interface, producing sludge that clogs filters and injectors.
- Cold Weather Issue: Diesel contains paraffin wax that gels at low temperatures; requires treatment to resist gelling (Winter diesel).

## Technology
### Stabilization
- Commercial Stabilizers: Sta-Bil, PRI-G (gasoline), PRI-D (diesel). Contain antioxidants to slow chemical chain reactions.
- Improvised Stabilization: Start with fresh fuel, fill containers completely, seal tightly, store in a cool, dark location, and use the oldest fuel first (FIFO).

### Siphoning/Transfer Mechanisms
- Hand siphon pumps: Squeeze bulb in the middle of a hose to start flow.
- Rotary hand pumps: Crank-operated pumps that thread into fuel drums or tank openings, excellent for gas station tanks.
- Battery-powered transfer pumps: 12V pumps that move fuel faster than siphoning and work regardless of relative height.

### Anti-Siphon Devices Bypass
- Workaround 1: Smaller tubing (6-8 mm or 1/4 inch) to fit through anti-siphon flaps.
- Workaround 2: Access the fuel line (cut the rubber section running from tank to engine) and drain into a container below.
- Workaround 3: Puncture the tank bottom (drilled/punched) and position the container below.
- Workaround 4: Use the fuel pump by removing the relay, connecting a jumper wire, and routing the fuel line into the container.

## Tools
- Flat screwdriver or pry tool (to open locked fuel doors).
- Wrench or pry bar (to open metal fill ports).
- Clear flexible tubing.
- Non-sparking tools (brass, aluminum, or plastic) for filling ports.
- Fire extinguisher or dirt/sand (for extinguishing gasoline fires).
- Water separators (to remove water from diesel).

## Process
### Fuel Extraction (Siphoning)
1. Open the fuel filler cap.
2. Feed the hose into the tank until it hits the bottom.
3. (Jiggle Method Preferred): Rapidly push the hose in and out of the tank by 15-20 cm while keeping the free end below the tank level until gravity takes over.
4. (Mouth Method - Last Resort): Suck on the free end until fuel approaches the mouth, then quickly lower it into the container.
5. Stopping Flow: Raise the outlet end above the fuel level or pinch the hose.

### Fuel Storage and Handling
1. Use approved containers (Jerry cans, metal drums) to minimize air space.
2. Seal containers tightly.
3. Store in a cool, dark location to minimize temperature swings.
4. Follow the FIFO principle for fuel usage.

### Fuel Degradation Prevention (Diesel Specific)
1. Keep tanks full to minimize condensation (water) space.
2. Add biocide (e.g., Biobor JF) per label instructions.
3. Filter through a 10-micron fuel filter before use.

### Fire Safety Protocol
1. Ground yourself before handling fuel to prevent static discharge.
2. Work outdoors or in well-ventilated spaces because gasoline vapor is heavier than air and pools in low areas.
3. Do not siphon in direct sunlight on hot days if possible.
4. Use dirt, sand, or a Class B fire extinguisher to smother gasoline fires; water spreads the fuel.
5. Allow the spill to evaporate completely (15-30 minutes in warm weather) before igniting anything nearby.

### From-Scratch Fuel Production (If Modern Fuel is Exhausted)
- Wood gas (syngas): Convert wood, charcoal, or biomass into a combustible gas.
- Ethanol distillation: Ferment sugar-rich crops, then distill to run modified gasoline engines.
- Biodiesel: Used cooking oil + methanol + lye catalyst.
- Rendered oils: Work in lamps and some diesel engines.
- Charcoal: The most accessible fuel; higher energy density than raw wood and burns cleaner.

## Notes
- Gasoline degreases skin; prolonged contact causes chemical burns and systemic toxicity. If swallowed during mouth-siphoning, do NOT induce vomiting (aspiration of gasoline into the lungs is worse). Drink water to dilute.
- The key insight in survival context is that salvaged modern fuel buys time to establish renewable fuel cycles (from-scratch production).
- Propane is heavier than air and pools at floor level; never store in enclosed below-grade spaces (basements).
- Gasoline vapor is the explosive element, not the liquid itself.
