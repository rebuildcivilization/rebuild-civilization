# Library & Bookstore Priorities: Which Books Save Lives

## Objective
To determine and prioritize which books must be salvaged during a collapse scenario to ensure the survival of critical knowledge and prevent generational knowledge loss.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- A functioning electrical grid
- Server infrastructure
- Telecommunications network

## Material
- Printed books (identified as the most durable information storage medium)
- Knowledge (medical procedures, engineering formulas, agricultural techniques, chemical processes)
- Specific reference materials (e.g., Medical References, Field Guides, Scientific Textbooks)

## System
- Human knowledge (stored in heads and on physical pages)
- Civilization (rebuilt through preserved knowledge)

## Technology
- Fragile Digital Knowledge:
    - Smartphones (dead within 24 hours without charging, require apps/OS/internet)
    - Laptops and tablets (hard drives/SSDs degrade over 5-10 years without power)
    - Servers and data centers (require continuous power, cooling, and maintenance; data degrades over 3-10 years without refresh)
    - The Internet (most complex and fragile machine, requiring numerous components to operate simultaneously)
- Durable Physical Medium:
    - Paper and ink (survive for 500 years)

## Tools
- Salvage lists (Priority Salvage List)
- Field guides (regional specificity required)
- Maps and Atlases (Topographic, Road, Geological, Hydrological)
- Preservation tools (canning pots, smoking/curing equipment, desiccants)
- Basic engineering tools (for blacksmithing and mechanics)

## Process
1. **Prioritization:** Identify books based on immediate survival needs (Tier 1: Medical References).
2. **Geographic Specificity:** Select local field guides based on region.
3. **Knowledge Acquisition:** Utilize books to retain technical skills across generations.
4. **Preservation:** Apply techniques (canning, smoking, storage) to protect physical books from water, insects, rodents, mold, fire, and UV light.
5. **Digital Backup:** Create durable hard copies of critical digital content.

Notes
- Physical books are the backup for everything else because they work without batteries, software updates, or screens.
- For generational knowledge preservation, a single well-preserved engineering textbook allows a person three generations from now to resume progress.
- Water damage mitigation: Stand wet books upright, fan pages open, and dry in a well-ventilated area; do not apply heat.
- Mold control: Use desiccants (silica gel, rice, charcoal) in sealed storage containers to control humidity (above 60% relative humidity).
