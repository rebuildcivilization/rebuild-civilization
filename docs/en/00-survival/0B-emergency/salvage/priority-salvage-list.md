# Priority Salvage List: What to Grab First

## Objective
When modern civilization stops functioning, the physical remains become the most concentrated source of survival resources on Earth. The objective is to prioritize salvage and scavenging efforts based on time-limited survival realities.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Survival is limited by three core constraints:

1. **Time Pressure:** The salvage window is finite, with degradation occurring over different timelines (Hours 0-72, Days 3-14, Weeks 2-8, Months 2-6, Beyond 6 months).
2. **Carry Capacity:** A healthy adult can carry approximately 20-25 kg over distance without rapid exhaustion. Capacity is dramatically increased with vehicles or carts.
3. **Safety and Risk:** Every minute spent in a damaged structure is a minute of risk. Structural integrity must be checked before entry, and hazards like gas leaks or pests must be assessed.

## Material
Material priority is structured across four tiers based on immediate survival need:

**Tier 1: Immediate Survival Gear (First 6 Hours)**
*   **Water & Containers:** Water bottles/jugs, water filters (Sawyer, LifeStraw, Berkey, Katadyn), purification tablets, pots for boiling, water heater tanks.
*   **Shelter & Warmth:** Sleeping bags (rated 0°C or below), tarps (minimum 3x3 meters), wool blankets, clothing layers (wool/synthetic), work gloves.
*   **Fire-Starting Equipment:** Butane lighters, strike-anywhere matches, ferrocerium rod, magnifying glass or Fresnel lens, petroleum jelly and cotton balls.
*   **Cutting & Prying Tools:** Fixed-blade knife (full-tang), multitools (Leatherman, Gerber, Swiss Army), crowbar, hatchet or hand axe, folding saw.

**Tier 2: Medical Supplies (First 24 Hours)**
*   **First Aid Kits & Trauma Gear:** Commercial first aid kits, tourniquets (CAT or SOFTT-W), hemostatic gauze (QuikClot, Celox), suture kits/butterfly closures, SAM splints, antiseptics (betadine, chlorhexidine, rubbing alcohol, hydrogen peroxide), dental emergency kit.
*   **Medications & Antibiotics:** Broad-spectrum antibiotics (amoxicillin, ciprofloxacin, doxycycline, metronidazole), pain relief (ibuprofen, acetaminophen, aspirin), anti-diarrheal (loperamide), antihistamines (diphenhydramine), epinephrine auto-injectors, and insulin/chronic disease medications.

**Tier 3: Tools, Seeds & Reference (First 72 Hours)**
*   **Hand Tools & Hardware:** Hammer (claw/ball-peen), pliers (needle-nose, channel-lock, vise-grip), screwdrivers (Phillips/flathead), hand saw (crosscut/hacksaw), wrenches, files and rasps, tape (duct/electrical/medical), rope/cordage (550 paracord, nylon rope), fasteners (nails, screws, bolts, wire, zip ties).
*   **Seeds & Preserved Food:** Canned goods, dry staples (rice, beans, lentils, pasta, oats, flour, sugar, salt), garden seeds (open-pollinated/heirloom), honey, cooking oil, spices, and salt.
*   **Reference Books & Maps:** Local topographic maps (USGS equivalent), regional field guides (edible plants, tracks), comprehensive first aid manuals, and 'Where There Is No Doctor'.

**Tier 4: Strategic Reserves (First Week)**
*   **Fuel & Energy Sources:** Gasoline (add stabilizer), diesel, propane tanks, batteries (sorted by chemistry, lithium last longest), solar panels, firewood, and coal.
*   **Communications Equipment:** Hand-crank or battery AM/FM radio, FRS/GMRS walkie-talkies, Ham radio equipment, CB radio, signal mirrors, whistles, and flares.
*   **Barter Goods:** Alcohol, tobacco, salt, coffee and tea, ammunition (common calibers), and hygiene products.

## System
The Salvage Triage Framework dictates the process, built on the principle: **prioritize items that are impossible or extremely difficult to make from scratch, in the order you will need them.**

**Time Pressure Realities:**
*   **Hours 0-72:** Best window; structures are relatively stable.
*   **Days 3-14:** Competition intensifies; easy-access locations are stripped first.
*   **Weeks 2-8:** Perishable food is gone; fuel begins degrading.
*   **Months 2-6:** Structural decay accelerates; rodents infest food stores.
*   **Beyond 6 months:** Only durable goods remain viable (metal, glass, ceramics, sealed containers).

**Carry Capacity vs. Value Rule:** Calculate value per kilogram. If an item cannot be explained in one sentence why it keeps you alive in the next 30 days, leave it for the return trip.

## Technology
*   **Communications:** Hand-crank or battery AM/FM radio, FRS/GMRS walkie-talkies, Ham radio equipment, CB radio.
*   **Energy/Power:** Batteries (AA, AAA, D-cell, 9V, car batteries), solar panels, propane tanks, gasoline, diesel.

## Tools
*   **Cutting/Processing:** Fixed-blade knife, multitools, crowbar, hatchet/hand axe, folding saw.
*   **Measurement/Adjustment:** Pliers (needle-nose, channel-lock, vise-grip), wrenches (adjustable wrench, socket set), files and rasps.
*   **Fastening:** Hammer, screwdrivers, fasteners (nails, screws, bolts, wire, zip ties).
*   **Repair:** Tape (duct, electrical, medical).

## Process
**Before entering any salvage site (Competition & Risk Assessment):
1. Observe from a distance for at least 10 minutes (look for movement, tracks, vehicles, smoke, noise).
2. Check structural integrity (look for leaning walls, cracked foundations, sagging roofs; do not enter if the structure shifted).
3. Note exits (never enter a structure with only one way out).
4. Smell the air (detect gas leaks, chemical spills, decomposition, and mold).
5. Work in pairs when possible (one person inside, one watching the exit).

**The “No Ruins” Fallback Process:**
1. Find a water source (flowing water).
2. Create a cutting edge (knap flint, obsidian, or chert into a blade).
3. Secure cordage (inner bark fibers, plant fibers).
4. Establish fire (bow drill, hand drill, fire plow).
5. Establish shelter (debris hut, lean-to, snow cave).

## Notes
*   **Salvage Ethics:** Occupied homes and defended positions are not salvage targets. Taking from the living is considered robbery; leaving a note about what was taken and where one went prevents conflict.
*   **Food Viability:** Seed and food salvage requires viability testing and storage planning.
*   **Fire Redundancy:** Redundancy is critical for fire-starting equipment.
*   **Material Superiority:** Items that take months or years to produce from scratch (e.g., wool blanket, antibiotics) are worth risking a trip for.
