# Expedient Water Crossings

## Objective

To cross rivers and other water obstacles while minimizing the risk of drowning, injury, and loss of equipment.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

Before attempting a crossing, evaluate:

* Water depth.
* Current speed.
* River width.
* Bottom conditions.
* Weather.
* Upstream conditions.
* Downstream hazards.
* Available flotation.
* Alternative routes.

## Material

Potential equipment includes:

* Rope or cordage.
* Staff.
* Flotation material.
* Waterproof containers.
* Pack or load that can be released quickly.

## System

### Site Selection

Do not automatically cross at the shortest point.

Look for:

* Wider, shallower sections.
* Lower current velocity.
* Stable banks.
* Fewer downstream obstacles.
* Suitable entry and exit points.

Avoid crossings above:

* Waterfalls.
* Rapids.
* Large submerged obstacles.
* Other areas where a fall could have severe consequences.

### Load Management

Protect essential equipment from water.

Do not allow a load to become an uncontrolled anchor that can pull a person underwater.

## Technology

### Staff

A staff can provide additional stability when crossing shallow moving water.

### Rope

A rope can provide assistance in some situations, but it can also create an entanglement hazard.

It must not be treated as automatically making a crossing safe.

### Improvised Flotation

Containers and other buoyant objects may assist flotation, but their reliability must be evaluated before committing to a crossing.

## Tools

* Staff.
* Rope.
* Waterproof containers.
* Flotation equipment.
* Footwear appropriate for the terrain.

## Process

1. Observe the water before entering.
2. Identify hazards downstream.
3. Identify alternative crossing points.
4. Select the safest practical route.
5. Secure essential equipment.
6. Remove or manage unnecessary load.
7. Test footing before committing weight.
8. Maintain stable contact with the bottom when possible.
9. Cross facing the current when appropriate.
10. Move deliberately rather than fighting the current with unnecessary force.
11. Exit at a stable bank.
12. Dry and inspect equipment after crossing.

## Notes

* A crossing should be avoided when a safer alternative exists.
* Water that appears shallow may still have dangerous current.
* Cold water increases the consequences of a fall.
