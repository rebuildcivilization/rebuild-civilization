# Signal Fires

## Objective

To attract attention over long distances using fire and smoke.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

Signal fires require:

* Sufficient fuel.
* A safe location.
* Ability to control the fire.
* Visibility from the expected direction of rescuers.

## Material

* Dry tinder.
* Kindling.
* Fuel wood.
* Green vegetation or other material capable of producing visible smoke.
* Rocks or other fire-control material.

## System

A signal fire must be optimized for visibility rather than warmth or cooking.

The fire should be:

* Large enough to be visible.
* Controlled.
* Located in an open area.
* Supplied with sufficient fuel.

### Smoke

Smoke can provide a stronger visual signal during daylight.

Use appropriate material to increase visible smoke without losing control of the fire.

## Technology

The signal consists of two elements:

**Fire → nighttime visibility**

**Smoke → daytime visibility**

A prepared signal site can be maintained so that a smoke-producing material can be added when rescuers approach.

## Tools

* Fire-starting equipment.
* Knife.
* Fuel-gathering tools.
* Rocks or other fire-control material.

## Process

1. Select a visible location.
2. Clear combustible material around the fire.
3. Prepare tinder and kindling.
4. Prepare a substantial reserve of fuel.
5. Build the fire.
6. Maintain a controlled flame.
7. Add smoke-producing material when visibility is needed.
8. Monitor wind direction and fire spread.
9. Extinguish the fire completely when it is no longer required.

## Notes

* Signal fires should never be allowed to become uncontrolled wildfires.
* Wind direction determines whether smoke will be visible from the expected search direction.
* The FM lists smoke and illumination signals among survival equipment.
* A signal fire is complementary to mirrors, whistles, and ground-to-air signals rather than a replacement for them.
