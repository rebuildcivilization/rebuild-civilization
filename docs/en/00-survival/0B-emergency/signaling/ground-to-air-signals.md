# Ground-to-Air Signals

## Objective

To make a survivor location and condition visible to aircraft or distant rescuers.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

Ground-to-air signaling depends on:

* Visibility.
* Contrast.
* Correct placement.
* Knowledge of likely search areas.
* Remaining in or near the expected search location.

## Material

Possible signaling materials include:

* Rocks.
* Logs.
* Clothing.
* Fabric.
* Cleared vegetation.
* Smoke.
* Fire.
* Reflective material.

## System

### Size

Signals intended for aircraft must be substantially larger than hand-held signals.

### Contrast

Use materials that contrast strongly with the surrounding terrain.

### Location

Place signals in:

* Open clearings.
* Beaches.
* Snow fields.
* Bare ground.
* Other locations visible from above.

### Permanence

Construct signals so that wind and weather do not easily destroy them.

## Technology

Ground-to-air signaling converts available materials into large visual symbols.

The signal should communicate at least:

* Human presence.
* Location.
* Need for assistance.

Additional standardized symbols can be used when known.

## Tools

* Rocks.
* Logs.
* Fabric.
* Knife or cutting tool.
* Fire-making equipment.

## Process

1. Select the most visible nearby location.
2. Clear or prepare the surface.
3. Choose material with strong contrast.
4. Construct a large recognizable signal.
5. Keep the signal unobstructed.
6. Add smoke or reflective signaling when aircraft are observed.
7. Remain near the signal when safe.

## Notes

* A large ground signal is more useful than a small detailed message that cannot be seen from altitude.
* Fire and smoke can supplement ground markings.
* The signal should not create an unnecessary wildfire hazard.
