# Terrain Navigation: Navigating by Landscape

## Objective

The objective of terrain navigation is the ability to move purposefully through unfamiliar landscape, find resources, avoid hazards, and reach a destination without getting lost. Navigation by landscape provides the ability to move without technology when the sky is overcast and a compass is absent.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

Prerequisites include knowing how to read landscape features, understanding the fundamental principles of navigation, and the ability to observe and interpret water, elevation, linear features, vegetation, and man-made remnants.

## Material

The materials used for terrain navigation are the physical elements of the landscape that encode directional information:

**Water:** Flows downhill and leads to civilization.
**Elevation:** High ground provides visibility and context.
**Linear Features:** Rivers, ridges, coastlines, and roads serve as handrails that guide movement.
**Vegetation:** Responds to sunlight, moisture, and wind, encoding directional information.
**Man-Made Features:** Roads, fences, ruins, power lines, and railroad grades persist and indicate populated areas.

## System

The system involves actively constructing a mental map of the terrain while traveling, integrating observations from various features to determine direction and route.

**Building a Mental Terrain Model Steps:**
1. Before leaving any high point, study the terrain ahead and identify the next three landmarks and the route between them.
2. Note major terrain features and their spatial relationships (e.g., “The river runs left to right. The ridge is behind the river. The burned area is between the two hills”).
3. Periodically look back, as the return route looks completely different from the reverse angle.
4. Estimate distances using known references (e.g., a 20-minute walk is about 1.5 km).

## Technology

The primary technology utilized in terrain navigation without technology is the ability to read the physical landscape itself, as there is no compass or technological aid.

## Tools

Tools are the specific skills used to interpret the material:

*   **Water Interpretation:** Identifying debris accumulation, bank erosion, vegetation, and animal trails.
*   **Elevation Interpretation:** Assessing what is visible from high ground (water, roads, smoke, buildings).
*   **Linear Feature Interpretation:** Identifying which features act as effective handrails (e.g., distinguishing reliable handrails like rivers from less reliable ones like fence lines).
*   **Vegetation Interpretation:** Recognizing sun-influenced growth and wind-shaped trees to confirm direction.
*   **Man-Made Feature Interpretation:** Assessing road width, pavement quality, intersections, and utility lines to determine destination importance.

## Process

**1. Following Water (The Drainage Hierarchy):**
*   **Step 1:** Find moving water (trickle, seep, wet gully).
*   **Step 2:** Follow it downhill (trickles merge into streams, streams into creeks, creeks into rivers).
*   **Step 3:** Rivers lead to floodplains, which are where humans build farms, towns, and roads.
*   **Water Hazards Rule:** Do not walk in the waterway; stay on the bank. If the bank becomes impassable, move uphill to a parallel ridgeline and continue downstream, rejoining the water when terrain permits.

**2. Using Elevation:**
*   **When to Climb:** When lost, needing a direction, needing to find water/roads/settlements, or needing to signal for help.
*   **What to Look for:** Reflective surfaces (water/lake), straight lines (roads/trails), vertical columns (smoke), geometric shapes (buildings), and horizon lines (coastlines).
*   **Ridge Running:** Follow a ridgeline for continuous sightlines, easier walking, and better wind, but plan water resupply by noting drainages on both sides.

**3. Utilizing Linear Features (Handrails):**
*   **Step 1:** From the current position, identify a linear feature running in the desired direction of travel.
*   **Step 2:** Move to that feature and follow it (staying within sight of it is sufficient).
*   **Step 3:** If the feature changes direction, leave it and look for a new handrail or landmark.

**4. Reading Vegetation:**
*   **Sun-Influenced Growth (Mid-Latitudes):** South-facing slopes receive more sun, leading to denser vegetation and earlier snowmelt in the Northern Hemisphere.
*   **Wind-Shaped Trees (Flagging):** Look for isolated trees on exposed ridges. Branches extend away from the prevailing wind, giving an east-west reference if the prevailing wind direction is known.

**5. Reading Man-Made Features:**
*   **Interpreting Remnant Infrastructure:** Assess the feature’s importance based on width, pavement quality, intersections, and utilities to determine the likely destination.
*   **Road Hierarchy:** Wider roads connect more important destinations; better pavement indicates higher traffic.

**6. Navigating Featureless Terrain:**
*   **Step 1:** Establish a heading using the sun or stars BEFORE entering the terrain.
*   **Step 2:** If crossing during the day, keep the sun at a consistent angle relative to the path, adjusting by about 15 degrees per hour.
*   **Step 3 (Desert Terrain):** Use sand dune ridges to align perpendicular to prevailing winds for an east-west or north-south reference.
*   **Step 4 (Snow/Sand):** Use the shadow of one's own body to maintain a constant angle relative to the direction of travel.
*   **Step 5:** Leave trail markers behind for retracing the route.

## Notes

*   **Reliability Warning:** Vegetation patterns are tendencies, not rules. Local factors (wind exposure, soil type, elevation, nearby water) can override solar influence, so never use vegetation alone for navigation.
*   **Key Takeaways Summary:**
    *   Water flows downhill to civilization (follow drainage downstream).
    *   High ground is the most valuable asset (climb to see resources).
    *   Linear features serve as handrails.
    *   Man-made remnants always lead somewhere significant.
    *   Always look back; the return route looks different from the reverse angle.
    *   In featureless terrain, establish a heading first, and use shadow angle to maintain it.
