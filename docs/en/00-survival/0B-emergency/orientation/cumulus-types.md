# Cumulus Types | Survival Wiki

## Objective
> Part of Weather Forecasting. The goal is to understand the progression from fair weather puffs to full thunderstorms and time the sequence.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
> Understanding the spectrum of cumulus clouds and the progression sequence.

## Material
> Cumulus clouds exist on a spectrum from harmless to lethal: humilis to mediocris to congestus to cumulonimbus.

> **Stage 1: Cumulus Humilis**
> *   Appearance: Small, flat, wider than tall. Bases are flat and well-defined. Tops are gently rounded.
> *   Height: Typically 1,000-3,000 feet. Tops stay below 10,000 feet.
> *   Meaning: The atmosphere is capped. Warm air rises, condenses at the base, rises a bit more, then hits a stable layer that stops further growth (the “lid”).
> *   Timing: Appears mid-morning as the sun heats the ground. Peaks early afternoon. Dissolves by sunset as surface cooling cuts off the thermals.
> *   Action: Safe to work, travel, and plan outdoor activities. No storms.

> **Stage 2: Cumulus Mediocris**
> *   Appearance: Taller than humilis but not towering. Height roughly equals width. Tops show cauliflower-like bumps. Bases remain flat but may darken slightly.
> *   Height: Reaches 5,000-8,000 feet. Tops push to 15,000-20,000 feet.
> *   Meaning: The stable cap is weakening. Thermals are punching higher before being stopped.
> *   Timing: If seen by late morning, the cap may break by early afternoon. If not seen until mid-afternoon, the cap will likely hold.
> *   Action: Monitor closely. Mediocris is the decision point. Check every 30 minutes. Have shelter plans ready.

> **Stage 3: Cumulus Congestus — Towering Cumulus**
> *   Appearance: Unmistakable. Towering vertical columns, tops are brilliant white, sharply defined, with a cauliflower texture of bulging turrets. Bases are flat and noticeably darkened.
> *   Height: Exceeds 10,000 feet. Tops reach 25,000-35,000 feet. May tower above surrounding clouds.
> *   Meaning: The cap has broken. The atmosphere is fully unstable. Warm air is rising rapidly without being stopped, containing enormous energy, and producing heavy rain and potential lightning.
> *   Critical Detail: Tops are still sharply defined with hard edges, indicating strong updrafts. Transition to cumulonimbus occurs when edges become fuzzy and fibrous (ice crystals forming).
> *   Timing: Once the first tower appears, expect storm conditions within 30-90 minutes. The cloud may already be producing weather.
> *   Action: Seek shelter now. Lightning can strike 10 miles ahead of visible rain. Move immediately if on exposed terrain.

> **Stage 4: Cumulonimbus**
> *   Appearance: The top spreads into a flat anvil shape. Edges become fibrous and wispy (ice crystals). The anvil may extend 20-50 miles downwind. The base is dark, sometimes rotating.
> *   Height: Tops reach 40,000-60,000 feet, hitting the tropopause. The anvil is made entirely of ice.
> *   Meaning: A mature thunderstorm containing violent updrafts and downdrafts, heavy rain, lightning, hail, and potentially tornadoes. A single cloud can release energy equivalent to a nuclear weapon.
> *   Timing: Can maintain itself for 1-3 hours. Supercell thunderstorms can last 4-8 hours.
> *   Action: Should already be in shelter. Avoid trees (lightning), hilltops (lightning and wind), and low-lying areas near streams (flash flooding). Crouch low if lightning is immediate.

> **Timing the Progression (Typical Timeline)**
> | Time        | Cloud Stage                                 | Hours to Storm |
> | ----------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------- |
> | 8-9 AM      | First cumulus humilis                       | 4-6 hours      |
> | 10-11 AM    | Humilis growing to mediocris                | 3-4 hours      |
> | 11 AM-12 PM | Mediocris tops bubbling, darkening bases    | 2-3 hours      |
> | 12-1 PM     | First congestus tower                       | 1-2 hours      |
> | 1-2 PM      | Congestus tops going fibrous, anvil forming | 30-60 minutes |
> | 2-3 PM      | Full cumulonimbus, storm in progress        | 0 — take cover |

## System
> The Cumulus Ladder, which maps cloud progression based on atmospheric instability.

## Technology
> Weather predictions based on observing the predictable sequence of cumulus types, allowing for timing of storm development without instruments.

## Tools
> Observation and timing.

## Process
> 1. Observe the progression from fair weather puffs to full thunderstorms.
> 2. Identify the current cumulus stage.
> 3. Apply the timing timeline to predict the storm arrival.
> 4. Execute the appropriate action based on the stage.

## Notes
> *   **The Flat Base Rule:** All cumulus clouds have flat bases because rising air cools to its dew point there. The base height is consistent because dew point is a property of the air mass.
> *   **Base Height Implications:**
>     *   Higher bases (above 6,000 feet): Drier air. Storms less likely, but increased wind shear and downdraft risk when storms do form.
>     *   Lower bases (below 3,000 feet): More humid air. Storms more likely, and rain reaches the ground more efficiently, utilizing more atmospheric moisture.
> *   **Night and Morning Cumulus:** Cumulus driven by surface heating usually dissolves after sunset. Development at night or early morning indicates a front or convergence zone, signaling that daytime heating will result in significant storms.
