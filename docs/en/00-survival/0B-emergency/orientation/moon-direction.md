# Moon Direction: The Crescent Tip Line Method

## Objective
To provide methods for navigation without technology using the moon and stars as references for direction.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Visibility of the moon (visible on roughly 25 nights per month and often during daylight hours).
*   Basic knowledge of cardinal directions.
*   For the Shadow Method: Bright moonlight (gibbous to full phases).

## Material
*   The Moon: Used as a reliable reference for direction because its illumination pattern reveals the sun's position, which defines east-west.
*   Stars: Used as a cross-reference when moonlight is washed out.

## System
*   Celestial Navigation:
    *   The moon's position tells something about direction at all times.
    *   The core principle relies on the fact that the moon is always illuminated by the sun.
*   Survival/Navigation Without Technology.

## Technology
*   No technology is required for the primary methods.

## Tools
*   A straight stick (for the Shadow Method).
*   A small stone (to mark the shadow tip).

## Process
### The Crescent Tip Line Method
*   **Applicability:** Works with any crescent or partial moon (not a full moon, and not during the new moon phase when the moon is invisible).
*   **How It Works:** Draw an imaginary line connecting the two tips (horns) of the crescent. Extend this line downward to the horizon. The intersection point indicates approximate south (Northern Hemisphere) or north (Southern Hemisphere).
*   **Step-by-Step:**
    1.  Locate the moon and identify the phase (crescent, quarter, or gibbous).
    2.  Identify the two tips of the illuminated crescent.
    3.  Draw an imaginary straight line connecting both tips.
    4.  Extend this line downward to the horizon to find the intersection point.
    5.  Use this bearing to orient: if south, north is behind, east is to the left (facing south), and west is to the right.

### Moon Phase Recognition
| Phase | Appearance | Rises | Sets |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| New Moon | Invisible | Sunrise | Sunset |
| Waxing Crescent | Thin sliver, lit on RIGHT (N. Hem) | Mid-morning | Mid-evening |
| First Quarter | Right half lit | Noon | Midnight |
| Waxing Gibbous | Mostly lit, dark on LEFT | Mid-afternoon | Before dawn |
| Full Moon | Fully lit | Sunset | Sunrise |
| Waning Gibbous | Mostly lit, dark on RIGHT | Mid-evening | Mid-morning |
| Last Quarter | Left half lit | Midnight | Noon |
| Waning Crescent | Thin sliver, lit on LEFT (N. Hem) | Before dawn | Mid-afternoon |

*   **Memory Aid (Northern Hemisphere):** If the moon is lit on the right side, it is waxing (growing). If lit on the left, it is waning (shrinking). In the Southern Hemisphere, this is reversed.

### Full Moon Method
*   **Principle:** The full moon is opposite the sun. At midnight, it is due south (N. Hemisphere) or due north (S. Hemisphere).
*   **Position Reference:** Rising at sunset (east) and setting at sunrise (west).

### Quarter Moon Method
*   **First Quarter (right half lit):**
    *   At sunset: due south (N. Hem)
    *   At midnight: setting in the west
*   **Last Quarter (left half lit):**
    *   At midnight: rising in the east
    *   At sunrise: due south (N. Hem)

### The Shadow Method (Moon Shadows)
*   **Application:** Used on bright moonlit nights (gibbous to full). Plants a stick vertically, marks the moon shadow, waits 20-30 minutes, marks the new shadow tip, and draws a line between them.
*   **Direction:** This line runs approximately east-west (the first mark is the west end in the Northern Hemisphere).

### Lunar Navigation by Time Estimation
*   **Key Estimates:**
    *   Rising: East.
    *   Setting: West.
    *   Highest Point: South (N. Hem) / North (S. Hem).
    *   Full, overhead: South (N. Hem).
    *   First quarter, overhead: 6 PM (N. Hem).
    *   Last quarter, overhead: 6 AM (N. Hem).
*   **Angular Rate:** The moon crosses the sky at approximately 15 degrees per hour.

### Combining Moon and Stars
*   **Partial Clouds:** Use the crescent tip method for a rough heading.
*   **Bright Moonlight:** Use the Big Dipper and Cassiopeia for Polaris, cross-referencing with the full moon’s position.
*   **Moon Sets Mid-Night:** Take a heading from the moon while visible, identify a landmark, then switch to star navigation after moonset.

### Special Cases and Complications
*   **Near the Equator:** Crescent tips may appear nearly horizontal, making the tip-line method less precise; the lit-side method is preferred.
*   **High Latitudes:** The moon's path is low and elongated, making horizon intersection less distinct.
*   **Moon Near the Horizon:** Atmospheric refraction can add 5-10 degrees of error; account for this by slightly adjusting the estimate.

### Practice Drill
*   **Week 1:** Waxing Crescent to First Quarter. Practice the tip-line method and check accuracy against known directions.
*   **Week 2:** First Quarter to Full. Practice the shadow-stick method and compare the moon-shadow east-west line with star navigation.
*   **Week 3:** Full to Last Quarter. Note the full moon's midnight position and track its setting position.
*   **Week 4:** Last Quarter to New. Apply the tip-line method to the waning crescent and verify against the coming sunrise.

## Notes
*   The Crescent Tip Line Method offers a rough direction, typically accurate to within 15-30 degrees, making it reliable enough to prevent catastrophic navigation errors, but it requires cross-referencing.
*   Moon shadows are only distinct enough to use during gibbous and full phases.
*   Moon shadows have lower accuracy than sunlight because the moon moves against the star background (about 13 degrees per day).
*   The lit side of the moon always faces the sun, providing an east-west reference even without precise technique.
