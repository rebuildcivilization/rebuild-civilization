# Water Crossings

## Objective

To cross rivers and other water obstacles while minimizing the risk of drowning, injury, and loss of equipment.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

Before attempting a crossing, evaluate:

* Water depth.
* Current speed.
* River width.
* Bottom conditions.
* Weather.
* Upstream conditions.
* Downstream hazards.
* Available flotation.
* Alternative routes.

## Material

Potential equipment includes:

* Rope or cordage.
* Staff or walking stick.
* Flotation material.
* Waterproof containers.
* Pack or load that can be released quickly.

## System

### Site Selection

Do not automatically cross at the shortest point.

Look for:

* Wider, shallower sections.
* Lower current velocity.
* Stable banks.
* Fewer downstream obstacles.
* Suitable entry and exit points.

Avoid crossings above:

* Waterfalls.
* Rapids.
* Large submerged obstacles.
* Other areas where a fall could have severe consequences.

### Load Management

Protect essential equipment from water.

A load should not become an uncontrolled anchor that can pull a person underwater.

When possible, make equipment buoyant or waterproof before entering the water.

## Technology

### Staff

A staff can provide additional stability when crossing shallow moving water.

It can be used to probe the bottom and detect changes in depth or footing.

### Rope

A rope may assist a crossing in some situations, but it can also create an entanglement hazard.

It should not be assumed that attaching a person to a rope automatically makes the crossing safer.

### Improvised Flotation

Containers and other buoyant objects may provide additional flotation.

Their buoyancy and stability must be assessed before relying on them.

## Tools

* Staff.
* Rope or cordage.
* Waterproof containers.
* Flotation equipment.
* Footwear appropriate for the terrain.

## Process

1. Observe the water before entering.
2. Identify hazards downstream.
3. Identify alternative crossing points.
4. Select the safest practical route.
5. Check the entry and exit points.
6. Secure essential equipment against water.
7. Remove or manage unnecessary loads.
8. Test the bottom before committing your full weight.
9. Enter the water carefully.
10. Maintain stable contact with the bottom when possible.
11. Cross deliberately without unnecessary movements.
12. Maintain awareness of downstream hazards.
13. Exit at a stable bank.
14. Move away from the water before reorganizing equipment.
15. Dry and inspect equipment after the crossing.

## Notes

* Avoid a water crossing whenever a safer alternative route exists.
* A river that appears shallow can still have a dangerous current.
* Fast-moving water can make standing or walking difficult even at relatively low depth.
* Cold water greatly increases the consequences of a fall.
* Do not attempt to cross above waterfalls, rapids, or other major downstream hazards.
* Water crossings should be treated as a distinct risk-management problem rather than simply as a navigation task.
