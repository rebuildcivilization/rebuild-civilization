# Dead Reckoning

## Objective
To track one's position while moving by combining three inputs: a known starting point, a held direction of travel, and a counted distance.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   A known starting point (e.g., camp, river crossing, hilltop).
*   A held direction of travel (a bearing).
*   A counted distance (measured by pace counting, time, or terrain estimation).
*   Discipline.

## Material
*   Charcoal (for recording information).
*   Birch bark (for recording information).
*   Beads, knots, or pebble-transfer systems (for pace counting).

## System
Dead reckoning means calculating the current position based on three continuous inputs: starting point, direction of travel, and distance traveled.

## Technology
No instruments are strictly required, though methods like sun position, Polaris, and improvised compass are available.

## Tools
*   Shadow stick (for bearing).
*   Compass (improvised or standard).
*   Pace counting system (beads, knots, or pebble-transfer).

## Process
### Core Method
1. **Establish Your Starting Point:** Fix a known location.
2. **Set Your Bearing:** Determine a direction to hold.
3. **Hold Your Direction:** Maintain the chosen direction against drift.
4. **Count Your Paces:** Measure the distance traveled.
5. **Record Your Route:** Document the bearing, pace count, and terrain notes for each leg of travel.

### Direction-Holding Techniques
*   **Leapfrog landmarks:** Pick a landmark on your bearing, walk to it, pick the next, repeat. (This eliminates most direction drift).
*   **Back-bearing checks:** Every few hundred paces, check if the line from the current position back to the previous one is opposite the direction of travel.
*   **Shadow alignment:** Check the shadow angle periodically.
*   **Wind as a reference:** Note where the wind hits the body and check it against the bearing every 15-20 minutes.

### Terrain Correction
| Terrain | Pace Adjustment | Rationale |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Flat ground | Baseline (no correction) | Normal stride |
| Gentle uphill (5-15°) | Add 10% more paces per unit distance | Shorter stride going up |
| Steep uphill (15-30°) | Add 20-30% more paces | Much shorter stride, more effort |
| Gentle downhill | Subtract 5% paces | Slightly longer stride |
| Steep downhill | Add 10% paces | Short, cautious steps |
| Thick vegetation | Add 15-25% paces | Weaving, stepping over obstacles |
| Snow or sand | Add 20-30% paces | Shorter stride, sinking |

### Detour Handling
*   **The Box Detour (around obstacles):**
    1. Turn 90° right from bearing.
    2. Walk counted paces to clear obstacle.
    3. Turn 90° left (back to original bearing).
    4. Walk past obstacle.
    5. Turn 90° left again.
    6. Walk the same number of paces (Step 2).
    7. Turn 90° right (back on original bearing).
    (Steps 2 and 6 must have the same pace count).
*   **The 60-Degree Detour (for visible obstacles):**
    1. Turn 60° away from the obstacle.
    2. Walk until past it, counting paces.
    3. Turn 120° (back across original line).
    4. Walk the same number of paces.
    5. Turn 60° back to original bearing.

### Aiming Off
When targeting a point on a linear feature, deliberately aim 5-10 degrees to one side. Hitting the feature allows you to determine the correct side by knowing whether the target was to the left or right.

### Night Dead Reckoning
If traveling at night, use Polaris or the Southern Cross for bearing, pick the closest visible landmark, count paces, and add a 15-20% error margin to the pace count. Stop at terrain changes if visibility is poor.

## Notes
*   **Error Accumulation:** Errors compound relentlessly. After 5 km, expect to be 200-500 meters off in good conditions, and up to 500-1,500 meters off in poor conditions.
*   **Key Takeaways:** Dead reckoning requires all three inputs (start + direction + distance). Landmarks are crucial; the leapfrog technique is the best method for reducing direction error.
*   **Accuracy Threshold:** Dead reckoning is imprecise; it bridges the gap between being "roughly there" and "completely lost."
*   **Memory Failure:** The process of recording every leg (bearing, pace count, terrain notes) is essential because memory alone will fail after four or five route changes.
*   **Dominant-Side Drift:** Right-handed people tend to drift right (clockwise), and left-handed people tend to drift left.
