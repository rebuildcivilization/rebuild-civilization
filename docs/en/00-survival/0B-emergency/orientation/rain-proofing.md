# Pitch Angles and Drip Lines

## Objective
To achieve rain-proofing for a shelter roof by correctly managing pitch angles and implementing drip line management to ensure water runs off the shelter and away from the sleeping area.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Climate assessment: Determine the necessary pitch angle based on rainfall.
*   Material suitability: Select roofing materials that shed water effectively.
*   Water path analysis: Understand that water follows the path of least resistance.

## Material
**Roofing Materials:
*   Bark slabs (birch, cedar, poplar bark peels)
*   Large broad leaves (banana leaves, palm fronds, burdock leaves)
*   Thatched grass/reeds (requires 15-20 cm minimum thickness to be waterproof)
*   Pine/spruce boughs (dense needle clusters)
*   Debris (leaves, needles, requires 30+ cm thickness on a lattice frame)

**Natural Sealants:
*   Pine resin/pitch (collected hardened sap from pine or spruce trees)
*   Clay slip (clay-rich soil mixed with water)
*   Moss/mud chinking (wet moss or clay-mud packed into gaps)

## System
**Pitch Angle System:
Water follows the path of least resistance. Steeper surfaces allow water to run off quickly, while shallower surfaces cause pooling and seepage.

**Water Management System (Drip Line):
*   Function: The drip line is the edge of the roof where water falls off. Management ensures water lands away from the shelter.
*   Components: Roof overhang, drip trench, ground grading, and a splash guard.

**Waterproofing System (Double-Layer Technique):
*   Structure: Two separate roof layers with an air gap between them.
*   Layer 1 (Outer): Primary water shedding (shingled material).
*   Air Gap: 5-10 cm to create space.
*   Layer 2 (Inner): Backup water catcher to redirect drips to the drip edge.

## Technology
**Pitch Angle Mechanics:
*   Rule of Thumb: Build at 45 degrees or steeper if the area experiences significant rainfall.
*   Angle Definition: 45 degrees is the angle where the horizontal distance and vertical height of the roof are equal.

**Material Effectiveness:
*   Shingle Principle: Every waterproof roof uses the principle of starting from the bottom and overlapping upward.
*   Natural Sealants: Used to fill gaps that layering alone cannot close.

## Tools
*   Container (for testing water flow).
*   Measuring tools (implied for calculating overhangs and pitch).
*   Shovels/Digging tools (for building the drip trench).
*   Heat source (implied for liquefying pine resin/pitch).
*   Brushes (for applying sealant).

## Process
**Building a Rain-Shedding Roof Process:
1. **Material Selection:** Choose materials based on shedding capability.
2. **Frame Construction:** Build the frame with rafters spaced 15-20 cm apart, running from ridge to ground edge.
3. **Lattice Creation:** Lay thin horizontal stringers (finger-thick sticks) across the rafters every 10-15 cm to create a lattice.
4. **Initial Layering:** Start at the bottom edge. Lay the first row of roofing material along the lowest horizontal stringer, hanging it over the drip edge by 5-10 cm.
5. **Layer Overlap:** Move up one stringer. Lay the next row so it overlaps the top of the first row by at least one-third of the material length (e.g., 10 cm for bark).
6. **Continue Upward:** Continue row by row, maintaining consistent overlap.
7. **Ridge Treatment:** At the ridge, fold or drape material over the top, adding extra layers here as it is the most leak-prone point.

**Setting Up Drip Lines Process:
1. **Extend the Roof Edge:** Ensure the roof overhangs the shelter walls by at least 30 cm (45-60 cm in heavy rain).
2. **Dig a Drip Trench:** Directly below the drip line, dig a trench (10-15 cm deep, 15-20 cm wide) following the roof edge.
3. **Drainage Channel:** Dig a shallow V-shaped diversion channel in an arc around the uphill side, at least 60 cm from the walls, to channel water past the shelter downhill.
4. **Ground Grading:** Grade the ground inside and around the shelter to slope slightly away from the sleeping area.
5. **Splash Guard:** Build a splash guard (rocks, log, or bark strip) just inside the drip trench to deflect splash.

**Water Testing Process:
1. **Flow Test:** Pour water slowly along the ridge to observe smooth flow off the drip edge.
2. **Visual Inspection:** Crawl inside during light rain to mark drip points and add material from outside.
3. **Trench Check:** Verify that water flows away from the shelter rather than pooling.

## Notes
*   A single layer of any natural material will leak; overlap is mandatory.
*   The difference between a 20-degree pitch and a 45-degree pitch is the difference between a dry shelter and a flooded one.
*   Drainage paths must be checked for evidence of past water flow (leaf debris lines, exposed roots).
*   For long-term protection, the double-layer technique adds 30-45 minutes of build time but significantly improves waterproofing.
*   A shelter that leaks is worse than no shelter at all.

### Pitch Angle Suitability Table
| Roof Pitch | Water Behavior | Suitability |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Less than 20 degrees | Pools and seeps through | Unacceptable for rain |
| 20-30 degrees | Sheds light rain, leaks in heavy rain | Minimum for dry climates |
| 30-45 degrees | Sheds moderate to heavy rain reliably | Good for most climates |
| 45-60 degrees | Sheds heavy rain and snow load | Best for wet/tropical climates |
| Over 60 degrees | Excellent water shedding but hard to build | Snow country, monsoon zones |
