# Weather Forecasting Guide

## Objective
The objective of weather forecasting is to predict atmospheric conditions to provide advance notice, allowing survival situations to be managed, shelter to be secured, or dangerous travel (like river crossings) to be delayed. The core purpose is to mitigate the risk, as weather kills more people in survival situations than almost anything else.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Instruments: Not always required; natural indicators can be used.
- Stable environment: For frost prediction, the sky must be clear at sunset, wind must be calm, and the terrain a low spot.

## Material
### Cloud Types
- Fair Weather Clouds:
    - Cumulus (cotton balls): White, puffy, flat bases, rounded tops. Form in the morning, dissolve by evening.
    - Cirrus (wisps): Thin, wispy streaks at high altitude, made of ice crystals.
    - Cirrocumulus (ripples): Tiny white puffs in rows at high altitude ("mackerel sky").
    - Warning Clouds:
        - Cumulus congestus (towering cumulus): Fair-weather cumulus growing tall, indicating instability, capable of developing into thunderstorms within 1-3 hours.
        - Cumulonimbus (thunderhead): King of storm clouds, towering from low to upper atmosphere, often spreading into an anvil shape. Produces lightning, heavy rain, hail, and sometimes tornadoes.
        - Nimbostratus (grey blanket): Thick, dark, featureless layer producing steady, continuous rain or snow.

### Pressure Indicators
- Smoke behavior: High pressure results in smoke rising straight up and dispersing. Falling pressure results in smoke swirling, hanging low, and drifting back down.
- Sound carries farther: Low pressure bends sound waves downward.
- Smells intensify: Low pressure releases gases, making the forest smell stronger.
- Campfire behavior: Bright, hot fire indicates high pressure; smoldering, popping fire with heavy smoke suggests falling pressure.

### Sky Color Signs
- Red Sky Rules:
    - "Red sky at night, sailor’s delight": Clear western sky means fair weather is coming.
    - "Red sky in the morning, sailor’s warning": Clear eastern sky means moisture-laden clouds to the west, indicating approaching storms.
- Halos Around the Sun and Moon:
    - Single halo: Indicates rain in 12-18 hours (65% accurate).
    - Corona (tight colored ring near the moon): Indicates fair weather if large and growing; shrinking means deteriorating weather.
- Other Sky Signs:
    - Green-tinted sky: Extremely dangerous; often indicates large hail or tornado conditions.
    - Pink or purple sunsets: Indicates high moisture in the upper atmosphere.

### Dew and Frost Indicators
- Reading Morning Dew:
    - Heavy dew on grass: Fair weather ahead (clear, calm nights).
    - No dew on a cool morning: Suggests clouds or wind overnight.
- Predicting Frost:
    - Conditions for frost: Sky clear at sunset, calm wind, dry air, and terrain being a low spot. If all four are met, expect frost by dawn.

### Wind Indicators
- Reading Wind Direction:
    - Wet finger method: Lick the index finger; the cold side faces the wind (wind direction within 30 degrees).
    - Grass or dust toss: Shows wind direction.
    - Smoke: Watch ground-level smoke.
    - Flag method: Cloth points downwind.

### Animal and Insect Indicators
- Bird Behavior:
    - Flying low: Indicates rain is 6-12 hours away (insects driven low by falling pressure).
    - Roosting early: Seabirds heading inland is a strong storm indicator.
    - Singing patterns: Birds sing more vigorously after a pressure drop stabilizes.
- Insect Indicators:
    - Crickets chirp faster in warm air: Indicates temperature trends.
    - Ants build up mound walls: Rain likely within 24 hours.
    - Bees return to the hive: Heavy traffic inward with few departures means a storm is coming.
    - Mosquitoes/gnats swarm more aggressively: Indicates heavy, humid air before a storm.

## System
The system relies on tracking the progression of clouds, monitoring pressure trends, observing wind patterns, and interpreting animal/insect behavior, often using multiple indicators for reliable forecasts.

## Technology
- Water Barometer: A crude but functional device built with a bottle and water to detect pressure trends.
- Simplified Beaufort Scale: Used for estimating wind speed based on visual cues.

## Tools
- Reading Clouds:
    - The Flat Base Rule: Used to assess air stability based on cumulus cloud bases.
    - The Cloud Progression Sequence: The most reliable 12-24 hour forecast pattern.
- Reading Pressure:
    - Smoke behavior observation.
- Reading Wind:
    - Wet finger method.
- Wind Speed Estimation:
    - Simplified Beaufort Scale.

## Process
### Cloud Progression Sequence (12-24 Hour Forecast)
| Stage | Cloud Type | What It Means | Time to Rain | 
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | 
| 1 | High cirrus appears | Warm front approaching | 24-36 hours | 
| 2 | Cirrus thickens to cirrostratus | Front getting closer | 12-24 hours | 
| 3 | Halo appears around sun/moon | Ice crystals at high altitude | 12-18 hours | 
| 4 | Clouds lower to altostratus | Front is near | 6-12 hours | 
| 5 | Sky darkens to nimbostratus | Rain imminent | 1-3 hours | 
| 6 | Rain begins | Front is here | 0 hours | 

### Calibration
- Water Barometer Calibration:
    - Rising water level = falling pressure = weather deteriorating.
    - Falling water level = rising pressure = weather improving.

### Storm Safety Process
- Lightning Safety (The 30-30 Rule):
    - If thunder count is 30 seconds or less (6 miles / 10 km), seek shelter immediately.
    - Wait 30 minutes after the last thunder before moving.

## Notes
- The most reliable forecast relies on combining multiple indicators; a single sign can be misleading.
- The Red Sky Rules are most reliable in mid-latitudes (30-60 degrees north or south) where weather moves west to east; less reliable in the tropics.
- The Towering Cumulus Exception: If cumulus clouds build vertically on a hot afternoon, skip the progression sequence, as thunderstorms from convective heating can develop in 30-60 minutes with no warning sequence.
- Wind shifts: Veering (clockwise shift) indicates improving weather; backing (counterclockwise shift) indicates deteriorating weather.
- Seasonal Trends: Spring is most unstable with the highest tornado risk; Summer is dominated by afternoon convective thunderstorms; Autumn becomes more stable; Winter is stable cold punctuated by frontal systems.
- Application: Weather forecasting skills allow for planning growing seasons, designing water management systems, timing food preservation, and building appropriate shelter.
