# Sunrise and Sunset Colors | Survival Wiki

## Objective
> Part of Weather Forecasting
> Detailed breakdown of dawn and dusk colors as weather predictors — what each shade means and why.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Understanding of prevailing westerly winds.
*   Knowledge of latitude effects on reliability.

## Material
### The Direction Principle
All sunrise/sunset weather prediction depends on the fact that in mid-latitudes, weather systems generally move from west to east. 
*   At sunset, look west toward incoming weather.
*   At sunrise, look east toward departing weather.

### Red Morning Sky — Detailed Analysis
*   **Deep, vivid red across the eastern horizon:** Clearest warning sign. Dry air has moved east, reflecting off moisture-laden clouds overhead or approaching from the west. Rain likely within 12-24 hours.
*   **Narrow band of red at the horizon with gray above:** Dry air has almost entirely passed. The gray overcast is the leading edge of an approaching system. Rain may arrive within 6-12 hours.
*   **Red-orange glow spreading across the entire eastern sky:** Indicates a large area of clear air to the east with significant moisture or cloud layers overhead. A broad weather system is approaching, potentially preceding 1-2 days of unsettled weather.
*   **Pale red or pink sunrise:** Indicates less contrast between air masses, suggesting light rain or overcast rather than a major storm.

### Red Evening Sky — Detailed Analysis
*   **Deep red sunset with a sharp, clear horizon line:** Strongest fair-weather signal. The western air mass is dry. Fair weather for at least 12-24 hours, often longer.
*   **Red sunset with some cloud bands turning orange and purple:** Generally fair-weather, but clouds suggest moisture at upper levels. Fair weather tomorrow, watch for changes in 24-48 hours.
*   **Red sun but hazy horizon:** Red comes from moisture or pollution close to the ground. Less reliable as a fair-weather signal.
*   **Vivid red that intensifies after the sun goes below the horizon (afterglow):** Caused by upper-atmosphere particles and ice crystals catching light. Indicates significant upper-level moisture; fair weather for 12 hours but a frontal system may be 24-48 hours out.

### Dawn Colors and Fog Prediction
*   **Gray dawn with no color:** Thick low cloud or fog present. If uniform and low, fog will likely burn off by mid-morning if temperatures rise. If high and textureless, overcast may persist all day.
*   **Golden-pink dawn:** Light filtering through moderate moisture and thin cloud. Often indicates morning fog will dissipate into a partly cloudy but dry day; generally favorable.
*   **Bright orange band at the eastern horizon, dark sky above:** Classic fog setup. Fog should form overnight and be present at dawn, burning off by mid-morning.
*   **No color at dawn, just gradual brightening:** Thick overcast blocking all direct sunlight. Expect continued overcast with possible precipitation.

### How Latitude Affects Reliability
*   **30-60 degrees latitude (most of Europe, North America, southern Australia, Patagonia):** Red-sky rules work well because westerly prevailing winds dominate, and weather systems track reliably from west to east. These rules were developed by and for mid-latitude cultures.
*   **Tropical latitudes (0-30 degrees):** Less reliable. Weather can approach from any direction; trade winds move from east to west, reversing the logic. Monsoon systems have their own patterns, making red-sky rules essentially useless near the equator.
*   **High latitudes (above 60 degrees):** Partially reliable. Weather systems still move generally west to east but can come from polar directions. Long sunrise/sunset periods in summer make color interpretation difficult.
*   **Southern hemisphere:** Physics are identical, but weather approaches from the west, applying the red-sky rules in the 30-60 degree band.

### Color Intensity and Timing
*   **Sunset color development:** A gradual transition (blue to gold to red over 30-45 minutes) indicates smooth, stable atmospheric layers—good weather.
*   **Sunset color persistence:** Colors fading within 10-15 minutes indicate dry, clear air—a strong fair-weather signal.
*   **Sunset color persistence:** Colors lingering for 30+ minutes indicate particles and moisture at high altitude—moisture approaching.
*   **Pre-dawn color:** The first colors appear 30-45 minutes before sunrise. Steel blue or gray suggests overcast; purple and pink suggest clear upper levels; red suggests moisture being illuminated overhead.

### Practical Application
1.  **Sunset check (primary forecast):** Look west. Deep red/orange = fair tomorrow. Pale yellow or no color = watch for change. 
2.  **Sunrise check (verification):** Look east. Red = incoming weather, potentially revising the sunset prediction. Clear colors = yesterday’s fair forecast holds.
3.  **Note the horizon clarity:** Sharp line = dry air. Hazy = moisture in the lower atmosphere.
4.  **Track color duration:** Quick, intense colors = stable atmosphere. Lingering, diffuse colors = upper-level moisture.
5.  **Combine with wind:** If sunset is red but wind has shifted from the south or southeast (northern hemisphere), the red sky may be misleading if weather approaches from non-western directions.

## System
*   **Fundamental Principle:** Weather systems move from west to east in mid-latitudes due to prevailing westerly winds (30 to 60 degrees latitude).
*   **Directional Logic:** West (sunset) looks toward incoming weather; East (sunrise) looks toward departing weather.

## Technology
*   **Weather Prediction Mechanism:** Interpretation of light refraction and scattering based on atmospheric moisture and particle content to predict rain, clear skies, or frontal systems.

## Tools
*   Observation of sunrise and sunset colors.
*   Wind direction knowledge (to contextualize the color reading).

## Process
1.  Observe sunset colors to gauge incoming weather.
2.  Observe sunrise colors to verify departing weather.
3.  Analyze color intensity and duration to assess atmospheric stability (quick colors = stable; lingering colors = moisture).
4.  Determine the implication based on latitude and wind direction to confirm reliability.

Notes
*   The red-sky rules are generally reliable in the 30-60 degree latitude band but become less reliable near the equator (tropical latitudes) where weather approaches from any direction.
*   The presence of a sharp horizon line during sunset indicates dry air, whereas a hazy horizon indicates moisture in the lower atmosphere.
*   The practical application emphasizes combining color observations with wind direction for the most accurate forecast.
