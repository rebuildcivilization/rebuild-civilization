# Seasonal Patterns | Survival Wiki

## Objective
Part of Fishing Understanding when and where fish move throughout the year is the difference between a reliable food source and wasted effort. The objective is to understand fish movement to secure reliable food sources.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Knowledge of water temperature and its effect on fish metabolism.
- Natural indicators (or a thermometer) to gauge water temperature.

## Material
### Core Principle
The core principle is: Fish follow temperature, and temperature follows the sun.

### Water Temperature and Fish Activity
| Water Temperature | Fish Behavior | Best Approach |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Below 4°C (39°F) | Near dormant, barely feeding | Not worth fishing — focus on traps in deep, slow pools |
| 4–10°C (39–50°F) | Sluggish, feeding slowly | Use small bait, fish deep, move bait slowly |
| 10–18°C (50–65°F) | Active, feeding well | Best overall fishing — use any method |
| 18–24°C (65–75°F) | Very active, aggressive feeding | Peak time for hook-and-line, spearfishing |
| Above 24°C (75°F) | Stressed, seeking cooler water | Fish early morning/late evening, target shaded spots and deeper pools |

### Seasonal Breakdown
**Spring: The Spawning Season**
- **Activity:** Fish become active as water warms past 10°C (50°F) after months of dormancy.
- **Spawning Runs:** Many species migrate upstream to spawn, leading to the largest concentrations of fish in small spaces.
- **Best Methods:** Fish weirs, spearfishing, gill nets, and basket traps.
- **Spawning Timing by Species:**
    - Trout, salmon: 6–12°C (43–54°F) - Early spring or fall
    - Bass (largemouth): 15–21°C (60–70°F) - Late spring
    - Carp, suckers: 15–20°C (60–68°F) - Mid to late spring
    - Catfish: 21–27°C (70–80°F) - Late spring to early summer
    - Perch, walleye: 7–12°C (45–54°F) - Early spring

**Summer: Dawn and Dusk Fishing**
- **Patterns:** Fish move to deeper, cooler water during midday, feeding at the edges of the day (dawn and dusk).
- **Night Activity:** Catfish, eels, and carp feed actively at night, requiring strong-smelling bait.
- **Strategies:** Move effort to deeper water (below thermoclines), fish near springs/stream inflows, set trot lines in the evening.

**Fall: The Second Peak**
- **Activity:** Fish feed aggressively throughout the day as water cools toward 10–18°C (50–65°F).
- **Priorities:** Bulk preservation (smoking, drying, salting), setting gill nets and weirs in migration streams near creek mouths.

**Winter: Minimal Effort, Maximum Patience**
- **Ice Fishing:** Cut holes at least 10 cm (4 inches) thick. Fish directly below the hole with small bait on short lines, sitting near the bottom.
- **Open Water:** Target the warmest water (deep pools, spring-fed streams, water below dams). Fish during the warmest part of the day (midday).

### Daily Weather Effects
| Condition | Effect on Fish | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Falling barometric pressure (storm approaching) | Fish feed aggressively before storms | Fish hard — prime time |
| Rising pressure (clearing weather) | Fish slow down after storms | Reduce effort, use passive methods |
| Overcast skies | Fish roam more freely in shallows | Good all-day fishing |
| Bright sunshine | Fish hide in shade and depth | Target shade, go deeper |
| Rain (light) | Washes insects into water, stimulates feeding | Excellent fishing |
| Rain (heavy) | Muddies water, disrupts feeding | Poor — wait it out |
| Wind | Pushes surface food to one bank | Fish the downwind shore |

### Tidal Patterns (Coastal)
- **Incoming tide (flood):** Fish move into estuaries, tidal pools, and shallow flats to feed (Best fishing).
- **High tide:** Fish are spread out and harder to target.
- **Outgoing tide (ebb):** Fish retreat to deeper channels; set nets across channels.
- **Low tide:** Harvest tidal pools; set weirs at low water to trap fish.
- **Tidal Swings:** Full moon and new moon produce the biggest tidal swings (spring tides).

## System
The system governing fish behavior is metabolism, which is driven by water temperature, light levels, and reproductive cycles.

## Technology
- Thermometer (or natural indicators like ice on the water surface) to gauge temperature.
- Various fishing methods (hook-and-line, spearfishing, gill nets, basket traps, trot lines).
- Strong-smelling bait for night fishing (fish guts, rotten meat, crushed insects).

## Tools
- Ice pick or axe (for testing ice thickness).
- Hooks and lines (various sizes, small hooks for cold water).
- Nets and weirs.

## Process
1. Determine the water temperature to assess fish activity.
2. Observe the seasonal timing to determine the correct fishing window (Spring spawning runs, Summer dawn/dusk, Fall bulk preservation, Winter midday).
3. Based on the water condition (temperature, weather), select the appropriate fishing location (shallow vs. deep, near springs vs. creek mouths).
4. Apply the correct method for the season (e.g., weirs for spawning runs, trot lines in the evening).
5. Adjust effort based on daily weather patterns (e.g., fishing hard before a storm).

## Notes
- **Sustainability Warning:** Spawning fish are easily caught; harvest no more than a third of what is seen to ensure population sustainability.
- **Ice Safety:** Never walk on ice thinner than 10 cm (4 inches). Test ice thickness before standing on it.
- **Bait Choice:** Live bait outperforms dead bait significantly in warm water.
