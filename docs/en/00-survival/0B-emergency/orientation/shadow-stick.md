# Shadow Stick

## Objective
To give cardinal directions, local solar noon, and a crude sundial without any manufactured equipment.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Flat, level ground with clear sun exposure.
- 15–20 minutes of patience.

## Material
- One straight stick, 2-3 feet (60-90 cm) tall.
- Two small stones or scratch marks.
- Cord or string.
- A flat stone or board (for building a permanent sundial).

## System
The system exploits the physics that govern the sun’s apparent motion across the sky, which is mirrored in reverse by the motion of shadows on the ground. In the Northern Hemisphere, the shadow traces a curve from west (morning) through north (noon) to east (afternoon).

## Technology
The shadow stick is the oldest precision navigation tool on Earth, providing true cardinal directions, local solar noon, and a crude sundial.

## Tools
- Morning Mark Method (fast, approximate).
- Noon Line Method (precision technique).
- Simple Hour-Line Sundial.
- Hour Angle Approximation.
- Geometric construction for finding the perpendicular (North-South Line).

## Process

### Morning Mark Method
1. **Clear a flat area** about 3 feet (1 m) in diameter and remove debris.
2. **Plant the stick vertically** in the center and check vertical alignment.
3. **Mark the shadow tip** (Mark A).
4. **Wait 15–20 minutes**.
5. **Mark the new shadow tip** (Mark B).
6. **Draw a line between Mark A and Mark B** (runs approximately east-west; Mark A is toward the west).
7. **Find north**: Stand with the left foot on Mark A (west) and the right foot on Mark B (east).

### Noon Line Method
1. **Set up the stick** on flat, clear ground, starting at least 2-3 hours before estimated noon.
2. **Measure the morning shadow length** using cord.
3. **Draw a circle** on the ground using the stick as the center and the shadow length as the radius.
4. **Mark the morning intersection point** where the shadow tip touches the circle (Morning Intersection Point).
5. **Wait** for the afternoon.
6. **Watch for the afternoon intersection** (Afternoon Intersection Point).
7. **Connect the two intersection points** with a straight line (runs exactly east-west).
8. **Draw a perpendicular line** through the stick base to create the **noon line** (runs exactly north-south).

### Sundial Construction
Once the noon line is established, the stick is kept in place, the noon line is marked as the 12:00 reference, and the shadow tip position is marked at each hour.

### Hour Angle Approximation
If a full day is not available, approximate hour lines using the fact that the sun moves 15 degrees per hour:

| Hours from Noon | Shadow Angle from Noon Line | Clock Time |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0 | 0 degrees (noon line) | 12:00 PM |
| 1 | ~15 degrees east | 11:00 AM / 1:00 PM |
| 2 | ~30 degrees east | 10:00 AM / 2:00 PM |
| 3 | ~45 degrees east | 9:00 AM / 3:00 PM |
| 4 | ~60 degrees east | 8:00 AM / 4:00 PM |

### Finding the Perpendicular (North-South Line)
If a perpendicular is difficult to draw by eye:
1. Mark the midpoint of the east-west line between the two stones.
2. From each stone, scratch two arcs of equal radius that intersect above and below the east-west line.
3. A line through these two arc intersections will pass through the stick base and run true north-south.

## Accuracy and Error Sources

| Error Source | Effect | Magnitude |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Time of day | Greater error near noon when shadow moves slowly | 5-15 degrees |
| Season | Summer/winter sun paths are asymmetric | 5-20 degrees |
| Stick not vertical | Shadow arc is distorted | Variable, up to 10 degrees |
| Ground not level | Shadow length reading is wrong | 3-8 degrees |
| Wait time too short | Insufficient shadow movement to measure | 10-20+ degrees |

**Near-Noon Inaccuracy:** Do not use the morning mark method within 1 hour of solar noon, as errors can exceed 20 degrees.

## Advanced Applications

**Determining True South (or North) for Shelter Orientation:**
- **Cold climate shelter**: Orient the opening toward true south (Northern Hemisphere) to capture low winter sun for passive heating.
- **Hot climate shelter**: Orient the opening toward true north (Northern Hemisphere) to avoid direct sun.

**Surveying and Layout:**
The noon line method is used to orient structures. The Great Pyramid of Giza is aligned to true north within 0.05 degrees, likely using a variant of this technique.

**Tracking the Seasons:**
By marking the noon shadow tip daily, a record of the sun’s altitude is created:
- Shadows get shorter = approaching summer solstice.
- Shadows get longer = approaching winter solstice.
- Shadow length matches a previous mark = completion of a half-year cycle.

## Key Takeaways
- **Morning mark method**: 15 minutes, approximate east-west, best for quick travel bearings.
- **Noon line method**: 3-6 hours, true north-south within 1-3 degrees, the gold standard of field navigation without instruments.
- **The noon shadow is true north**: when the shadow is shortest, it points exactly to true north (Northern Hemisphere).
- **Build on the method**: A noon line allows for sundials, building orientation, surveying, and season tracking.
- **Works everywhere**: The method works at any latitude, in any season, and in either hemisphere, unless consistent cloud cover defeats it.
- **Practice at home**: Verify the noon line with a compass and understand the potential errors.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Solution |
| :--- | :--- | 
| No clear shadow (overcast) | Wait for a break in clouds; even thin overcast often allows a faint shadow.
| Shadow edge is fuzzy | Use a stick with a pointed tip or place a small object (nail, thorn) at the top to create a sharper shadow point.
| Ground is too rough to draw a circle | Clear and smooth an area with a flat stone, or use a board/flat rock as a drawing surface.
| Cannot tell when shadow hits the circle in afternoon | Check every 10 minutes as the shadow lengthens; mark the first point where the tip clearly touches or crosses the circle.
| Wind keeps moving the stick | Brace the stick with rocks at its base; pack soil tightly around it.
| Shadow moves too fast to mark precisely | Pre-position your marker stone and slide it to the shadow tip quickly.
