# Sun Methods | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To find direction using the sun as the most reliable daytime direction finder on Earth.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   A straight stick (2-3 feet / 60-90 cm)
*   Flat ground with sun exposure
*   Two small stones or markers
*   An analog watch (for the Watch Method)
*   Knowledge of the hemisphere (Northern or Southern)

## Material
*   The sun
*   A straight stick
*   Stones or markers
*   A watch

## System
The sun follows a predictable arc across the sky daily. The system exploits two physical facts:
1. Shadows point away from the sun.
2. The sun is due south (or north) at local solar noon.

## Technology
*   Shadow Stick (Fastest daytime tool)
*   Watch Method (Instantaneous direction finding)
*   Equal-Length Shadow Method (Most accurate method)

## Tools
| Tool | Primary Use | Accuracy/Benefit |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Shadow Stick | Quick estimation of east-west line. | Within 10-15 degrees |
| Watch | Instant direction finding. | Works best above 40 degrees latitude |
| Cord/Measure | Used in Equal-Length Method to set radius. | Within 1-3 degrees |

## Process
### Method 1: The Shadow Stick (Quick Version)
1. Push the stick vertically into level ground in a sunny area.
2. Mark the tip of the shadow with the first stone (West mark in the Northern Hemisphere).
3. Wait 15-20 minutes.
4. Mark the new shadow tip with the second stone (East mark).
5. Draw a line between the two stones (the east-west line).
6. Stand with your left foot on the first mark and the right foot on the second mark to face North (Northern Hemisphere) or South (Southern Hemisphere).

### Method 2: The Watch Method
*   **Northern Hemisphere:** Point the hour hand at the sun, bisect the angle to 12 o’clock. The line points South; the opposite is North.
*   **Southern Hemisphere:** Point the 12 o’clock mark at the sun, bisect the angle between the 12 and the hour hand. The line points North.

### Method 3: Equal-Length Shadow Method (Most Accurate)
1. Plant a vertical stick on flat ground in the morning (at least 2-3 hours before noon).
2. Mark the shadow tip and measure its length with a piece of cord.
3. Draw a circle on the ground with the stick as the center and the shadow length as the radius.
4. Wait for the shadow tip to touch the circle again in the afternoon, and mark that point.
5. Draw a line between the morning mark and the afternoon mark (this line runs precisely east-west).
6. A perpendicular line through the stick base gives the true north-south direction.

### Method 4: Sun at Known Times
Estimate direction based on known local solar times:
*   6:00 AM (sunrise): East (both hemispheres)
*   9:00 AM: Southeast (N. Hemisphere), Northeast (S. Hemisphere)
*   12:00 PM (noon): Due South (N. Hemisphere), Due North (S. Hemisphere)
*   3:00 PM: Southwest (N. Hemisphere), Northwest (S. Hemisphere)
*   6:00 PM (sunset): West (both hemispheres)

### Seasonal Corrections
| Season | Sunrise Direction | Sunset Direction | Noon Height |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Spring/Fall equinox | Due east | Due west | Moderate |
| Summer solstice | Northeast | Northwest | Highest |
| Winter solstice | Southeast | Southwest | Lowest |

### Combining Sun Methods
| Situation | Best Method |
| :--- | :--- |
| Need direction quickly, traveling | Quick shadow stick (15 min) |
| Have a working watch, mid-latitudes | Watch method (instant) |
| Stationary camp, need accurate bearing | Equal-length shadow (3-5 hours) |
| Tropical region, near equator | Equal-length shadow only |

## Common Errors
| Error | Consequence | Prevention |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Stick not vertical | Skewed east-west line | Use a plumb weight (stone on string) to check vertical |
| Ground not level | Shadow length distorted | Find flat ground or clear and level a small area |
| Moving during equal-shadow method | Inconsistent circle radius | Mark the stick base permanently; do not disturb |
| Forgetting hemisphere | North-south reversed | Know which hemisphere you are in — this is non-negotiable |
| Using watch method in tropics | Large directional error | Switch to shadow stick for latitudes below 25 degrees |

## Practice Exercises
1. Quick shadow stick: Set up, mark the line, and verify with a compass. Note the error and the cause.
2. Watch method: Compare results to a compass bearing at different times. Note when accuracy is best (early morning and late afternoon are worst).
3. Equal-length shadow: Run the full method on a clear day. Compare to a compass. Accuracy should be within 3 degrees.

## Key Takeaways
*   The shadow stick is the workhorse: 15 minutes gives approximate east-west; the full equal-length method gives true east-west within 3 degrees.
*   The watch method is fastest but only reliable above 40 degrees latitude.
*   The sun is always there; shadows are often faintly visible even in light overcast.
*   Know your hemisphere: every method reverses between north and south of the equator.
*   Season affects accuracy: equinox methods are most accurate; summer/winter require the equal-length method for precision.
*   Practice before you need it: sun navigation is a motor skill.

Notes
- The watch method degrades in accuracy near the tropics (between 23.5°N and 23.5°S) because the sun passes nearly overhead.
- For greater accuracy with the shadow stick, the equal-length method is recommended.
- The sun is the most reliable daytime direction finder, rising in the east and setting in the west.
