# Cloud Reading

## Objective
Identifying cloud types by shape, altitude, and behavior to predict what weather is coming.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Ability to observe cloud types, shape, altitude, and behavior.

## Material
### The Three Cloud Families
1. **Cumulus** — the heap clouds: Puffy, cauliflower-like, with flat bases and rounded tops. Form when warm air rises in columns (thermals).
2. **Stratus** — the layer clouds: Flat, featureless sheets that cover the sky like a grey blanket. Form when air cools uniformly across a wide area.
3. **Cirrus** — the wisp clouds: Thin, white, hair-like streaks high in the sky. Made entirely of ice crystals at altitudes above 20,000 feet.

### Altitude Classifications
- **High clouds (above 20,000 ft / 6,000 m):** Cirrus, cirrostratus, cirrocumulus. Made of ice crystals; thin, white, often transparent; do not produce rain that reaches the ground.
- **Mid-level clouds (6,500-20,000 ft / 2,000-6,000 m):** Altostratus, altocumulus. The prefix “alto” means middle. Altostratus is a grey sheet that may dim the sun. Altocumulus appears as patches or rolls of white/grey cloud.
- **Low clouds (below 6,500 ft / 2,000 m):** Stratus, stratocumulus, nimbostratus. These are the clouds that can almost be touched from a hilltop. Nimbostratus is the rain-bearing layer cloud—thick, dark, and wet.
- **Vertical development (any altitude):** Cumulus and cumulonimbus. A cumulonimbus can stretch from 2,000 feet to over 50,000 feet.

### Cloud Color Meanings
- **White clouds:** Thin; sunlight passes through; no significant precipitation.
- **Grey clouds:** Thicker; block more sunlight; light rain or drizzle possible.
- **Dark grey to black bases:** Very thick clouds with lots of water; heavy rain likely.
- **Yellow or green tint:** Sunlight filtering through clouds with large hail stones; take cover immediately.
- **Pink or red at sunrise/sunset:** Red sunsets mean dust particles in dry air to the west (fair weather coming). Red sunrises mean that dry air has passed and moisture is approaching from the west.

### Cloud Sequences
**Warm front approaching (12-36 hours out):**
1. Cirrus streaks appear, thickening over hours.
2. Cirrostratus forms a halo around the sun or moon.
3. Altostratus thickens, sun becomes a dim disc.
4. Nimbostratus arrives—steady rain begins.

**Cold front approaching (6-12 hours out):**
1. High cirrus may appear briefly.
2. Cumulus builds rapidly from the west/northwest.
3. Dark cumulonimbus develops with anvil tops.
4. Heavy rain, possible thunderstorms, then rapid clearing.

**Fair weather holding:**
1. Small cumulus forms mid-morning, stays flat-topped.
2. Clouds dissipate by late afternoon.
3. Clear skies at sunset.
4. No cirrus thickening on the horizon.

## System
- **Cloud Families:** Cumulus, Stratus, Cirrus.
- **Altitude System:** High, Mid-level, Low, and Vertical development.

## Technology
- The underlying physics governing cloud formation (thermals, uniform cooling, ice crystal presence).

## Tools
- **Observation Routine:**
    - Dawn: Note cloud types, coverage, and wind direction at cloud level.
    - Mid-morning: Watch for cumulus development (growing vs. staying small).
    - Noon: Maximum solar heating; if cumulus is flat, storms are unlikely.
    - Late afternoon: If no storms developed, the evening will be clear.
    - Sunset: Check the western horizon.
- **Wind Analysis:** Watching the direction of cloud movement at different altitudes to determine weather changes within 24 hours.

## Process
### The Vertical Development Rule
- **Critical Principle:** Clouds building vertically mean storms are coming.
- **Progression:**
    - **Morning:** Scattered small cumulus, flat tops (stable, fair weather).
    - **Late morning:** Cumulus growing taller, tops starting to look lumpy (instability increasing).
    - **Early afternoon:** Towering cumulus, cauliflower tops reaching high (storms likely within 2-4 hours).
    - **Afternoon:** Tops flattening into anvil shapes (thunderstorms imminent or in progress).
- **Stability Check:** If cumulus clouds stop growing vertically and stay small all day, the atmosphere is stable and no storms will develop (all-clear signal).

## Notes
- The three basic shapes define the fundamentals of cloud reading.
- Cumulus clouds growing taller indicate warm air rising fast and unstable atmosphere.
- The vertical development rule is the single most important principle in cloud reading.
- Cloud bases lowering through the day indicate dropping pressure and deteriorating weather.
- Cloud bases rising indicate improving conditions.
- High clouds show the direction of the next air mass approaching.

## Practical Observation Routine Summary
- Check the sky at Dawn, Mid-morning, Noon, Late afternoon, and Sunset.
- Analyze cloud bases for pressure changes.
- Analyze cloud movement direction across altitudes for forecasting.
