# Pressure Signs

## Objective
To read barometric pressure changes without instruments by observing environmental clues to predict short-term weather.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

Observation skills and the ability to correlate environmental clues with atmospheric pressure changes are prerequisites. No instruments are required.

## Material

The material consists of environmental clues used for forecasting, including:

*   Smoke behavior
*   Sound propagation
*   Odors
*   Cloud behavior (height and thickness)
*   Insect flight patterns
*   Morning dew or frost
*   Visibility clarity

## System

The system is the relationship between atmospheric pressure and short-term weather prediction. Atmospheric pressure is considered the single most reliable predictor of short-term weather.

## Technology

The primary technology involves the detection and interpretation of barometric pressure changes through sensory observation rather than instrumental measurement.

## Tools

The primary tools are the senses (sight, hearing, smell) used for environmental clue detection.

## Process

1.  **Observe Change:** Monitor the rate of change in pressure.
2.  **Assess Rate:** Determine how fast the pressure is changing.
3.  **Correlate Clues:** Cross-reference at least three environmental indicators before making a prediction.
4.  **Determine Trend:** Identify whether pressure is falling (bad weather) or rising (fair weather).
5.  **Apply Timing:** Check if the pressure drop occurs outside of normal 12-hour cycles for accuracy.

## Notes

*   **Rate of Change Criticality:** The rate of pressure change is more critical than the absolute pressure value. A rapid drop (2-4 hours) signifies a serious storm, while a slow drop over 24 hours might bring light rain.
*   **Moderate/Dangerous Thresholds:** A pressure drop of roughly 1 millibar per hour is moderate. A drop of 2+ millibars per hour signals dangerous weather.
*   **Post-Storm Indicator:** A rapid pressure rise after a storm indicates the clearest, most stable weather of the week.
*   **Practical Reliability:** Combining the three key checks (smoke behavior, sound carrying distance, and smell intensity) yields a 6-12 hour forecast that is roughly 70% accurate for predicting rain within the next day.
