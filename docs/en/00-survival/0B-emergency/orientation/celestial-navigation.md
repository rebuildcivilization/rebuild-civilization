# Celestial Navigation

## Objective
Celestial navigation is the process of finding direction (north, south, east, west) and approximate latitude, and estimating time and season, without the use of technology.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Humans need eyes, hands, and knowledge to perform celestial navigation.

## Material
The sky is conceptualized as a compass, a clock, and a calendar.

## System
The system relies on observing celestial bodies (Sun, Moon, Stars) and interpreting their positions relative to the horizon and each other.

## Technology
- None explicitly identified in the source text.

## Tools
- Eyes
- Hands
- Knowledge
- Two sticks (as a sighting device for bearing)

## Process
### Finding Direction by Stars
**Northern Hemisphere (Polaris):**
1. Locate the Big Dipper (Ursa Major).
2. Identify the two ‘pointer stars’ (Dubhe and Merak).
3. Draw an imaginary line through the pointer stars, extending upward.
4. Measure five times the distance between the pointer stars along this line.
5. Polaris is the moderately bright star at the end of this line (true north).

**Southern Hemisphere (Southern Cross):**
1. Find the Southern Cross (Crux).
2. Extend the long axis of the cross (top star through the bottom star) approximately 4.5 times its length.
3. That point is approximately the South Celestial Pole (true south is directly below it).
4. Confirm with the Pointer Stars (Alpha and Beta Centauri).

**Finding East and West:**
1. Pick any bright star near the horizon.
2. Watch it for 15-20 minutes.
3. If it moves upward and to the right: roughly south (Northern Hemisphere) or north (Southern Hemisphere).
4. If it moves upward and to the left: roughly north (Northern Hemisphere) or south (Southern Hemisphere).
5. If it moves straight up: east.
6. If it moves straight down: west.

### Determining Latitude
**Northern Hemisphere (Polaris Altitude Method):**
1. Measure the angle of Polaris above the horizon.
2. The angle equals your latitude.
3. Hand measurements correlate angles to degrees:
    - Width of pinky finger: 1 degree
    - Width of three middle fingers: 5 degrees
    - Width of closed fist: 10 degrees
    - Spread between pinky and index finger: 15 degrees
    - Spread between thumb and pinky (full span): 20-25 degrees

**Southern Hemisphere Latitude:**
1. Use the South Celestial Pole point (found via the Southern Cross method).
2. Measure its angle above the southern horizon.
3. This angle equals your southern latitude.

### Telling Time
1. Face north and locate the Big Dipper.
2. Imagine a clock face centered on Polaris, with 12 o’clock straight up.
3. The pointer stars of the Big Dipper act as the hour hand.
4. The Big Dipper makes one full rotation counter-clockwise every 24 hours (approximately 23 hours 56 minutes).

### Seasonal Awareness
Different constellations dominate different seasons:
- Winter: Orion, Taurus, Gemini.
- Spring: Leo, Virgo.
- Summer: Scorpius, Sagittarius, Lyra.
- Fall: Pegasus, Andromeda, Cassiopeia.

### Moon Methods
**Crescent Moon Method:**
1. Draw an imaginary line connecting the two tips (horns) of the crescent moon.
2. Extend this line downward to the horizon.
3. The meeting point indicates approximate south (Northern Hemisphere) or approximate north (Southern Hemisphere). (Accuracy: within 20-30 degrees).

**Full Moon Method:**
- Rises in the east at sunset.
- Is due south at midnight (Northern Hemisphere) or due north (Southern Hemisphere).
- Sets in the west at sunrise.

## Notes
- Celestial navigation fails under overcast skies; therefore, cloud cover contingency requires backup methods such as terrain reading, prevailing wind patterns, and river following. 
- Celestial north (true north) may differ from magnetic north by 5-25 degrees, requiring the accounting for magnetic declination.
- Practice in known locations is recommended to learn the home-area sky before navigating unfamiliar territory.
