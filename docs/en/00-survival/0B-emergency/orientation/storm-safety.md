# Storm Safety

## Objective
Part of Weather Forecasting: Recognizing dangerous weather and taking correct shelter action for lightning, floods, and tornadoes.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

To successfully navigate a storm, one needs the ability to recognize warning signs and practice a pre-established plan. In a fixed camp or settlement, this requires establishing designated shelter locations, warning signals, a meeting point, and supply caches.

## Material

### Lightning: The Most Common Killer
**The 30/30 Rule:**
*   If the time between lightning flash and thunder is **under 30 seconds**, the individual is in the danger zone and must seek shelter immediately.
*   Wait **30 minutes** after the last thunder before resuming outdoor activity.

**Actions:**
*   Get inside a substantial building (not a shed, tent, or open pavilion).
*   If no building: get inside a vehicle with a metal roof (metal conducts around the person).
*   If caught in the open: move to low ground, crouch low on the balls of your feet, and minimize ground contact.
*   Stay away from isolated tall objects (single trees, poles, hilltops).

**Avoidances:**
*   Open fields, ridges, hilltops.
*   Water (swimming, boating, fishing).
*   Metal objects (fences, poles, tools, bicycles).
*   Sheltering under a single tall tree (lightning targets the tallest object).
*   Lying flat on the ground (increases ground current exposure).

### Flash Floods: The Deadliest Storm Hazard
**Warning Signs:**
*   Heavy rain upstream, even if the location is dry.
*   Rising water in streams and rivers.
*   Water changing color (brown, muddy—indicating upstream erosion).
*   Unusual sounds (roaring from upstream in a quiet stream).
*   Rain lasting more than 30 minutes at high intensity.

**Survival Rules:**
*   Never cross flowing water above ankles (six inches of fast-moving water can knock you down).
*   Move to high ground immediately when water rises; do not wait to see if it stops.
*   Never camp in dry washes, arroyos, or canyon bottoms (these can flood from unseen rain).
*   Flooding is more dangerous at night because water depth and speed are harder to judge.

**After Flooding:**
*   Do not walk through standing floodwater (risk of contamination, hidden hazards, downed power lines).
*   Do not return to flooded structures until water has receded and foundations are inspected.
*   Discard any food contacted by floodwater.

### Tornado Recognition
**Warning Signs (in order of urgency):**
1.  **Dark, greenish sky** (hail suspended in the storm).
2.  **Large hail** (tornado risk is elevated if hail arrives).
3.  **Wall cloud** (a low, rotating cloud base where tornadoes form; look for rotation).
4.  **Roaring sound** (described as a freight train; indicates proximity).
5.  **Sudden calm after intense storm** (the eye passing over; danger is not over).
6.  **Debris cloud at ground level** (confirms the tornado may be rain-wrapped and invisible).

**Actions:**
*   Get to the lowest floor of a sturdy building.
*   Move to an interior room away from windows (closet, bathroom, hallway).
*   Get under heavy furniture or cover yourself with a mattress.
*   If outdoors with no shelter: lie flat in a ditch or depression, protecting the head.
*   If in a vehicle: Stop, get out, and lie in a low area away from the vehicle.

**What NOT to Do:**
*   Do not open windows.
*   Do not shelter under highway overpasses (wind accelerates through the opening).
*   Do not stay in a mobile home or trailer (they are destroyed by even weak tornadoes).

### Reading Storm Direction
Storms generally move from west to east (or southwest to northeast) in the mid-latitudes, but local topography creates exceptions.
**Track a storm’s movement:**
1.  Pick a fixed reference point (tree, hilltop, building).
2.  Note where the storm front sits relative to that point.
3.  Check again in 5 minutes.
4.  If the storm moves left or right of the reference: it will pass to your side.
5.  If the storm does not move left or right but gets larger: it is coming directly at you.

### Shelter Priority by Storm Type
| Storm Type | Best Shelter | Acceptable | Avoid |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Lightning | Interior of building | Metal-topped vehicle | Open areas, tall objects, water |
| Tornado | Interior room, lowest floor | Ditch or depression | Vehicles, mobile homes, overpasses |
| Flash flood | High ground | Upper floors of solid buildings | Low ground, basements, vehicles in water |
| Severe wind | Lee side of solid building | Dense forest (no dead trees) | Open areas, tents, under single trees |
| Hail | Any roofed structure | Vehicle | Open areas (large hail is lethal) |

### Building a Storm Plan
In any fixed camp or settlement, establish:
1.  **Designated shelter locations** for each storm type.
2.  **Warning signals** that everyone recognizes (bell, horn, whistle pattern).
3.  **Meeting point** after the event for headcount.
4.  **Supply cache** in shelter locations (water, first aid, light source).
