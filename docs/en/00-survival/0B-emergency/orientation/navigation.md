# Navigation Without Technology

## Objective
To navigate without technology.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Flat, open ground with direct sunlight for the shadow stick method.
- Clear sky for celestial navigation (star/sun methods).
- Still water for the improvised compass method.
- A watch with hands (optional but helpful) for the watch method.

## Material
- A straight stick (30–60 cm / 1–2 feet)
- Two small rocks or markers
- A needle or any small piece of steel (sewing needle, safety pin, staple)
- A leaf or small piece of bark
- A puddle, cup, or any still water
- Silk, fur, or your own hair (for magnetizing)
- A watch with hands (optional but helpful)

## System
Navigation relies on cross-referencing multiple methods. The core principle is to cross-reference at least two methods before committing to a direction.

## Technology
- None explicitly identified in the source text.

## Tools
- Shadow stick
- Rocks/markers
- Needle/steel object
- Leaf/bark
- Still water container
- Watch

## Process
### Method 1: The Shadow Stick (Daytime)
1. Find flat, open ground with direct sunlight. Push a straight stick vertically into the ground.
2. Mark the shadow tip with a small rock (First Point, W in Northern Hemisphere).
3. Wait 15–20 minutes. Mark the new position with a second rock (East Point).
4. Draw a straight line between the two rocks (runs roughly east-west).
5. Stand with the left foot on the first mark and the right foot on the second mark to face roughly north (in the Northern Hemisphere).

### Method 2: North Star (Polaris) — Northern Hemisphere (Night)
1. Find the Big Dipper (Ursa Major).
2. Locate the two stars at the “pouring edge” (pointer stars).
3. Draw an imaginary line through the pointer stars and extend it about five times the distance between them to find Polaris.
4. Face Polaris to determine North.

### Method 3: Southern Cross — Southern Hemisphere (Night)
1. Find the Southern Cross (Crux).
2. Identify the long axis (the two stars farthest apart).
3. Extend the long axis 4.5 times its length downward to mark the South Celestial Pole.
4. Find the two bright pointer stars (Alpha and Beta Centauri). Draw a line between them and find the midpoint.
5. Draw a line from the midpoint perpendicular (90 degrees) toward the extended cross point. Drop a vertical line from that point straight down to the horizon; this spot is due south.

### Method 4: The Sun (Basic Rules)
1. The sun rises in the east and sets in the west.
2. At solar noon, it is due south in the Northern Hemisphere or due north in the Southern Hemisphere.
3. Combine this with shadow direction for a quick heading.

### Method 5: Watch Method (Analog)
- Northern Hemisphere: Point the hour hand at the sun, bisect the angle between the hour hand and the 12 o’clock mark to find roughly south.
- Southern Hemisphere: Point the 12 o’clock mark at the sun, bisect the angle between the 12 and the hour hand to find roughly north.

### Method 6: Improvised Compass
1. Magnetize the needle by stroking it 50–100 times in one direction against silk, fur, or hair.
2. Place a leaf or piece of bark on the surface of still water.
3. Gently lay the magnetized needle on the leaf; it will point roughly north-south.
4. Determine which end points north by cross-referencing with the sun's position (east in morning, west in evening).

### Method 7: Reading Terrain
1. Water flows downhill (streams lead to rivers, rivers lead to floodplains).
2. Ridgelines give visibility; climb to high ground to spot features.
3. Animal trails lead to water sources; follow trails downhill.
4. Man-made features (straight lines, cut stumps, fencing) are unmistakable.
5. Listen for running water, traffic, machinery, dogs, and trains for 60 seconds.

### Leaving Trail Markers
- Stack three rocks in a small cairn at each turning point.
- Break branches at head height, bending them to point in the direction of travel.
- Scratch arrows into tree bark or dirt at eye level.

## Notes
- **Shadow Stick Accuracy:** The longer the wait between marks, the more accurate the line. 15 minutes yields a rough heading; an hour yields a solid one.
- **Polaris Misconception:** Polaris is not the brightest star; Sirius, Vega, and Jupiter are brighter. Confirmation should use the Big Dipper’s pointer stars.
- **Moss Rule:** The rule that "moss grows on the north side of trees" is unreliable. Moss grows on the wet/shady side, depending on local moisture, canopy, and wind.
- **Drift Mitigation:** Without a fixed reference point, humans naturally drift in circles (usually to the dominant-hand side). Always pick a distant landmark and walk toward it.
- **Hemisphere Awareness:** The Sun and shadow methods reverse between Northern and Southern hemispheres, requiring awareness of the current location.
- **Magnetization Maintenance:** The magnetization of the needle lasts a few hours to a few days; re-stroke the needle daily.
- **Core Rule:** Always cross-reference methods; use at least two before committing to a direction.
