# Sky Color Rules

## Objective
Part of Weather Forecasting: To explain what sky colors mean for incoming weather by detailing the physics behind the folklore.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- None explicitly identified in the source text.

## Material
Sunlight (containing all visible wavelengths), atmosphere (composed of particles and molecules), water droplets, ice crystals, dust, pollution, and specific atmospheric conditions (moisture content, ice crystals, dust).

## System
Atmospheric composition and light scattering within the atmosphere.

## Technology
Rayleigh scattering (responsible for the blue sky), scattering of wavelengths at sunrise/sunset (producing red/orange), and scattering by moisture and particles.

## Tools
Practical Sky Reading techniques, including checking the western sky, checking the eastern sky, monitoring overcast color, noting color transitions, and combining color readings with other indicators (wind direction, cloud type, animal behavior).

## Process
1. Check the western sky each evening.
2. Check the eastern sky each morning.
3. Monitor overcast color (White overhead suggests possible rain later; Dark gray suggests rain soon; Green suggests take shelter now).
4. Note color transitions (e.g., blue to white to gray over hours, which is a textbook approaching warm front).
5. Combine sky color information with other indicators for reliable 12-48 hour forecasts.

Notes
- Rayleigh scattering causes the blue sky because nitrogen and oxygen molecules scatter short wavelengths (blue, violet) more than long wavelengths (red, orange).
- Red sky at night, shepherd’s delight, means the western sky is clear/dry, indicating fair weather tomorrow.
- Red sky in morning, shepherd’s warning, means the eastern sky is red, indicating the dry air has passed, with moisture-laden air approaching from the west.
- Green sky is a serious warning, indicating large amounts of water and ice in tall storm clouds, strongly correlating with hail and tornado-favorable conditions.
- Deep orange sunset with a clear western horizon indicates dry weather continuing due to dust particles.
- Pale yellow sunset suggests thin cirrus clouds and may precede warm fronts by 24-48 hours.
- Gray overcast (nimbostratus or thick stratus) indicates rain or drizzle is happening or imminent.
- Uneven gray with texture is more favorable than uniform dark gray as it suggests gaps in the cloud layer.
- Vivid pink and purple at sunset occur with clean air containing fine particles (like volcanic aerosols) scattering light between red and blue.
