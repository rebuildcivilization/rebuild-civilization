# Bottle Barometer

## Objective
To build a functional water barometer from a sealed bottle, a narrow tube, and basic sealant material, tracking pressure changes and predicting weather.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- A sealed bottle.
- A narrow tube or hollow reed (6-8 mm diameter, 30-40 cm long).
- Sealant material (tree resin, beeswax, clay, or pine pitch).
- Water (clean, to prevent algae growth).
- A mounting board or flat surface.
- A location with stable temperature.
- Consistent reading times.

## Material
- One clear glass or plastic bottle (500 mL to 1 L works well).
- One narrow tube or hollow reed (6-8 mm diameter, 30-40 cm long).
- Sealant: tree resin, beeswax, clay, or pine pitch.
- Water (clean, to prevent algae growth).
- A mounting board or flat surface.

## System
The water barometer exploits the same physics as mercury barometers but uses water. A sealed bottle is partially filled with water and connected to a tube open to the atmosphere. It works by atmospheric pressure pushing down on the water in the open tube, forcing water up inside the sealed bottle. When pressure falls, the trapped air in the bottle expands, pushing water out into the tube.

## Technology
The instrument relies on the principle that rising atmospheric pressure pushes down on the water, and falling pressure allows the trapped air in the bottle to expand, signaling weather changes.

## Tools
- Tools for marking the tube with scratches or paint.
- A log or board for recording readings.

## Process
### Construction Steps
1. Fill the bottle approximately half full with water. Add a small amount of charcoal dust or plant dye if available.
2. Insert the tube through the neck of the bottle so it reaches about 2 cm below the water surface inside the bottle. The tube should extend 15-20 cm above the bottle opening.
3. Seal completely around the tube where it enters the bottle. The seal is the most critical part; test it by gently pressing on the bottle to ensure the water level in the tube does not change.
4. The tube must remain open at the top for atmospheric pressure to act on the water.
5. Mount the barometer vertically in a location with stable temperature.

### Reading the Barometer
- **Water rises in the tube:** Atmospheric pressure is falling. The air trapped in the sealed bottle is expanding relative to outside pressure, pushing water up and out. Bad weather is likely approaching.
- **Water falls in the tube:** Atmospheric pressure is rising. Outside air is pressing harder on the water in the tube, compressing the trapped air in the bottle. Fair weather is likely.
- **Water level stable:** Pressure is steady. Current conditions will likely persist for 6-12 hours.

### Calibration
- On a day with clearly fair, stable weather, mark the water level as the “fair weather baseline.”
- Create at least three marks over several weeks:
    - **High mark:** observed during the worst storm conditions (water highest in tube).
    - **Baseline:** fair weather level.
    - **Low mark:** observed during the clearest, most stable weather (water lowest).

### Temperature Problem
- A 5-degree C temperature increase causes trapped air to expand as much as a significant pressure drop, leading to false readings.
- Mitigation strategies:
    - Place the barometer in the most temperature-stable location available (cave, root cellar, north-facing wall, or insulated shelter).
    - Read it at the same times each day (dawn and dusk).
    - Compare readings at the same time across days rather than within a single day.
    - If two bottles are used, place one indoors and one outdoors to isolate pressure from temperature effects.

### Accuracy and Expectations
- A well-built, temperature-compensated water barometer can detect pressure changes of 2-3 millibars, enough to predict major weather shifts 6-18 hours in advance.
- It will not match a mercury barometer’s precision but outperforms pure observational methods.
- Check the barometer twice daily at consistent times and record readings with simple marks.

### Maintenance
- Refill water as it evaporates; the level in the bottle must always cover the tube’s submerged end.
- Re-seal any cracks in the sealant as they appear.
- Replace water every few weeks to prevent algae buildup.
- In freezing conditions, bring the barometer indoors or use a water-alcohol mix to lower the freezing point.

## Notes
- The biggest limitation of water barometers is temperature sensitivity, where a 5-degree C change mimics a significant pressure shift.
- A functional barometer requires consistent measurement times and temperature-stable placement to yield reliable results.
- The maintenance schedule includes refilling water, re-sealing cracks, replacing water to prevent algae, and managing freezing conditions.
- Calibration involves establishing a fair weather baseline and marking highs (storms) and lows (stable weather).
- Accuracy is limited to detecting pressure changes of 2-3 millibars.
