# Chemical Contamination

## Objective

To reduce exposure to hazardous chemical contamination and prevent contaminated material from being carried into clean areas.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

Chemical contamination may affect:

* Air.
* Soil.
* Water.
* Vegetation.
* Buildings.
* Clothing and equipment.

The identity and toxicity of a contaminant may not be apparent from its appearance.

## Material

Useful resources include:

* Protective clothing.
* Gloves.
* Respiratory protection when appropriate and available.
* Sealed water.
* Sealed food.
* Cleaning materials.
* Shelter.
* Communication equipment.

## System

### Avoidance

Avoid suspected contaminated areas whenever possible.

If movement away is necessary, reduce the time spent inside the contaminated area.

### Wind

Wind direction can influence the movement of airborne contaminants.

Recommends moving crosswind or upwind to reduce exposure when escaping a contaminated area.

### Shelter

If shelter is unavoidable:

* Keep contaminated material outside.
* Limit openings.
* Avoid bringing contaminated vegetation or soil inside.
* Keep the shelter clean.

## Technology

### Contamination Control

The fundamental principle is to prevent transfer:

**Contaminated area → clothing/equipment → shelter → person**

Breaking this chain reduces secondary exposure.

### Water Protection

Sealed water is preferred.

Other sources should be treated as potentially contaminated until evaluated and appropriately treated.

## Tools

* Protective clothing.
* Gloves.
* Shelter material.
* Water containers.
* Cleaning materials.
* Communication equipment.

## Process

1. Avoid the contaminated area when possible.
2. Determine wind direction.
3. Move away from the contamination using the safest available route.
4. Avoid touching contaminated surfaces unnecessarily.
5. Protect water and food.
6. Establish a clean shelter if necessary.
7. Prevent contaminated clothing and equipment from entering clean areas.
8. Decontaminate according to current authoritative guidance.
9. Seek medical assistance if exposure occurs.

## Notes

* This document does not replace current hazardous-material emergency procedures.
* Do not rely on smell or appearance alone to identify a chemical hazard.
* Do not burn contaminated materials; combustion can create additional hazardous products.
* The safest strategy is generally avoidance and rapid movement out of the contaminated area when this can be done safely.
