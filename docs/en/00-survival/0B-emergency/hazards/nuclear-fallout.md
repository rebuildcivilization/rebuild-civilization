# Nuclear Fallout

## Objective

To reduce exposure to radioactive fallout following a nuclear detonation or other radioactive contamination event.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

The main factors controlling external radiation exposure are:

* Time.
* Distance.
* Shielding.

Fallout can contaminate surfaces, soil, food, and water.

## Material

Useful resources include:

* Enclosed shelter.
* Dense shielding material.
* Sealed food.
* Sealed water.
* Protective clothing.
* Cleaning materials.
* Communication equipment.
* Radiation detection equipment when available.

## System

### Time

Reduce the amount of time spent in contaminated or potentially contaminated areas.

### Distance

Increase distance from radioactive sources and contaminated areas whenever this can be done safely.

### Shielding

Place as much appropriate dense material as practical between people and the radioactive source.

Suitable shelter locations are generally those that place substantial material between the occupants and the outside environment.

### Contamination Control

Prevent radioactive material from being carried into clean areas.

Separate potentially contaminated clothing and equipment from living areas.

## Technology

### Shelter

An enclosed structure or underground location can provide significantly more shielding than open terrain.

The most protected location depends on the construction and surrounding material.

### Food and Water

Prefer supplies that were protected from environmental contamination.

Sealed containers are preferable to exposed food or water.

External surfaces of containers may themselves require cleaning before they are brought into a clean area.

## Tools

* Shelter.
* Water containers.
* Sealed food.
* Protective clothing.
* Cleaning supplies.
* Radiation detector when available.

## Process

1. Move into the best available shelter.
2. Increase distance from the suspected source when doing so does not increase exposure.
3. Place dense material between occupants and the outside environment.
4. Minimize unnecessary movement outside.
5. Keep contaminated material out of clean areas.
6. Protect food and water from contamination.
7. Remove potentially contaminated outer clothing when appropriate.
8. Clean exposed skin using safe decontamination practices.
9. Monitor official emergency information when communications are available.
10. Remain sheltered until reliable information indicates that movement is safer.

## Notes

* Fallout is radioactive material deposited after a nuclear event; it is not the same thing as the initial blast.
* Radiation cannot be reliably detected by sight, smell, or ordinary senses.
* This should not be treated as the current authority for radiation dose limits, medical treatment, or evacuation thresholds.
* This document focuses on the durable survival principles of time, distance, shielding, contamination control, and protected supplies.
