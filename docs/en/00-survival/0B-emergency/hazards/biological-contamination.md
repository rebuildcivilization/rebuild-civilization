# Biological Contamination

## Objective

To reduce exposure to biological hazards and prevent the spread of infectious agents through people, water, food, surfaces, and vectors.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

Biological hazards may be transmitted through:

* Airborne particles.
* Contaminated water.
* Contaminated food.
* Wounds.
* Insects.
* Rodents and other animals.
* Contaminated surfaces.

## Material

Useful resources include:

* Soap.
* Clean water.
* Protective clothing.
* Gloves.
* Face covering when appropriate.
* Clean shelter.
* Sealed food.
* Sealed water.
* Waste-containment materials.

## System

### Hygiene

Maintain high standards of personal hygiene.

Particular attention should be given to:

* Hands.
* Hair.
* Teeth and mouth.
* Clothing.
* Wounds.
* Food preparation areas.

### Vectors

Reduce contact with disease-carrying insects and animals.

Use physical barriers such as clothing and netting when available.

### Shelter

Keep the shelter clean.

Avoid locations where airborne contamination or contaminated material is likely to accumulate.

## Technology

### Contamination Control

Separate clean and contaminated materials.

Do not allow contaminated clothing, equipment, waste, or hands to contaminate food and water.

### Water

Prefer protected or sealed water sources.

Water from uncertain sources must be treated using an appropriate current purification method.

### Food

Prefer food protected from the environment.

Do not consume food that may have been contaminated unless current authoritative guidance confirms that it is safe.

## Tools

* Soap.
* Water.
* Gloves.
* Protective clothing.
* Face covering.
* Mosquito netting.
* Cleaning materials.
* Waste containers.

## Process

1. Avoid visibly or potentially contaminated areas.
2. Protect the mouth, nose, skin, and wounds as appropriate.
3. Wash hands frequently.
4. Keep food and drinking water protected.
5. Control insects and rodents.
6. Keep clothing clean.
7. Clean wounds promptly.
8. Separate contaminated clothing and equipment.
9. Clean living areas regularly.
10. Follow current public-health instructions when available.

## Notes

* The personal hygiene and sanitation are important defenses against biological hazards.
* Sealed food and water are preferable to exposed supplies.
* The manual recommends several specific historical procedures for biological contamination. These should not automatically be treated as current public-health guidance.
* Modern infectious-disease control measures should take precedence when authoritative information is available.
