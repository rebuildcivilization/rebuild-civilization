# Antiseptic Sources

## Objective
To reduce the bacterial load in wounds to a level the immune system can handle by using natural antiseptics when manufactured disinfectants are gone.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Boiled water (cooled)
- Raw honey
- Charcoal
- Spirit above 60% alcohol content
- Plant-based substances (Garlic, Yarrow, Plantain, Thyme, Oregon grape/Barberry)

## Material
- Boiled Water
- Raw Honey
- Activated Charcoal (or regular wood charcoal)
- Alcohol (above 60%)
- Garlic
- Yarrow (*Achillea*)
- Plantain (*Plantago*)
- Thyme
- Oregon grape / Barberry

## System
The best defense involves layering multiple antiseptic approaches, as no single natural antiseptic matches modern antibiotics.

## Technology
- Osmotic effect (Honey): Extreme sugar concentration draws water out of bacterial cells.
- Hydrogen peroxide production (Honey): An enzyme (glucose oxidase) slowly produces hydrogen peroxide when diluted by wound fluid.
- Acidity (Honey): pH ranges from 3.2-4.5, creating an environment too acidic for most bacteria.
- Adsorption (Charcoal): Charcoal adsorbs toxins and bacteria onto its porous surface.
- Surface Sterilization (Alcohol): Alcohol above 60% is an effective surface disinfectant.

## Tools
- Boiling pot or heat source (for boiling water).
- Rocks or mortar and pestle (for crushing charcoal).
- Clean dressings.

## Process
### Wound Irrigation Protocol
1. Bring water to a rolling boil and maintain for at least 1 minute (3 minutes above 2,000 meters elevation).
2. Allow the water to cool until warm (not hot enough to scald tissue, not cold).
3. Pour or squirt the water over and into the wound with pressure to flush out debris.
4. Use at least 500 ml per wound (more for contaminated wounds).
5. Repeat irrigation at each dressing change.

### Honey Application Protocol
1. Clean the wound first with boiled water.
2. Apply a layer of honey 3-5 mm thick directly to the wound surface.
3. Cover with a clean dressing.
4. Change the dressing and reapply honey every 24 hours, or when the dressing becomes saturated with wound drainage.
5. Continue until the wound shows healthy pink granulation tissue with no signs of infection.

### Charcoal Paste Protocol
1. Take charcoal from a hardwood fire (avoid treated/painted wood, softwoods heavy in resin, or briquettes with additives).
2. Crush the charcoal to a fine powder using two rocks or a mortar and pestle.
3. Mix the powder with a small amount of boiled water to form a thick paste (consistency of toothpaste).
4. Apply the paste to the wound surface or surrounding infected skin.
5. Cover with a clean dressing.
6. Replace charcoal poultices every 4-6 hours.

### Alcohol Usage
- Surface sterilization: Pour over tools, blades, needles before use, and allow to air dry for 30 seconds.
- Skin preparation: Wipe the area around a wound (not inside the wound) before suturing or bandaging.
- Hand cleaning: Rub over hands when soap and water are unavailable.

### Garlic Poultice Protocol
1. Peel 3-4 cloves and crush thoroughly.
2. Let sit 10 minutes for allicin to form.
3. Apply the crushed garlic around (not inside) the wound.
4. Cover with a dressing.
5. Remove after 20-30 minutes.

### Plant-Based Antiseptic Application
- Garlic: Crush fresh cloves, apply as a poultice for surface infections or fungal infections. For irrigation, steep crushed garlic in boiled water for 30 minutes, strain, and use the cooled liquid as a wash.
- Yarrow: Crush fresh leaves onto the wound for bleeding control and mild antiseptic.
- Plantain: Chew or crush leaves, apply directly for insect bites or minor wounds.
- Thyme: Brew strong tea or use as a wash for wound irrigation and skin infections.
- Oregon grape / Barberry: Boil root bark to use the yellow liquid as a wash for wound irrigation and eye infections.
- Pine resin: Apply directly or dissolved in fat to seal wounds or draw splinters.
- Tea tree (if available): Apply leaves as a poultice for fungal infections or minor wounds.

## Notes
- **Key Takeaways:**
    - Boiled water (cooled) is the most important antiseptic tool for flushing every wound.
    - Raw honey applied 3-5 mm thick is clinically proven to kill bacteria and promote healing; reapply every 24 hours.
    - Charcoal paste adsorbs toxins and bacteria from superficial wounds and bites; replace every 4-6 hours.
    - Alcohol above 60% sterilizes tools and intact skin but should never be poured into open wounds.
    - **What Does NOT Work:**
        - Urine: Not sterile; contains bacteria and waste products.
        - Saliva: Contains over 600 species of bacteria; risk outweighs benefit.
        - Mud or dirt poultices: Soil contains tetanus spores (*Clostridium tetani*) and other deadly anaerobic bacteria.
        - Kerosene or gasoline: Causes chemical burns, tissue necrosis, and systemic toxicity through skin absorption.
    - **Combining Approaches:** Layering is the best defense: Irrigate with boiled water or saline, apply honey as a dressing, use garlic or yarrow poultice if infection appears, change dressings daily, and monitor relentlessly for spreading redness, pus, fever, or red streaks.
