# Hypothermia Recognition and Rewarming

## Objective
To recognize hypothermia early and know how to reverse it to save lives in a survival situation.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Absence of a medical thermometer in most survival situations.

## Material
**Definition of Hypothermia:**
Hypothermia occurs when the body’s core temperature drops below 35°C (95°F). The body loses heat faster than it can produce it, leading to the shutdown of organ systems.

**Recognition Signs (The "Umbles"):**
Watch for:
- Stumbles: Loss of coordination, tripping, inability to walk straight.
- Mumbles: Slurred or slow speech, inability to finish sentences.
- Fumbles: Dropping things, inability to use fingers, struggle with zippers.
- Grumbles: Irritability, combative nature, refusal of help, irrational anger.

**Critical Warning:
** When shivering suddenly stops in someone who is still cold, they have gotten worse, not better.

**Paradoxical Undressing:**
In moderate to severe hypothermia, victims sometimes strip off clothing because blood vessels near the skin suddenly dilate, creating a false sensation of burning heat.

**Stages of Hypothermia:**
| Stage | Core Temp | Signs | Urgency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mild | 32-35°C (90-95°F) | Shivering, cold hands/feet, fumbling, slurred speech, poor decisions | Act now — fully reversible in the field |
| Moderate | 28-32°C (82-90°F) | Shivering stops, confusion, drowsiness, slow pulse, stumbling | Serious — requires careful rewarming |
| Severe | Below 28°C (82°F) | Unconscious, very slow/absent pulse, rigid muscles, appears dead | Life-threatening — gentle handling critical |

**Field Assessment Without a Thermometer:**
1. **Touch the torso:** Place a warm hand on the abdomen or chest (under clothing). If the trunk feels cold, the person is hypothermic.
2. **Check mental state:** Ask them to count backward from 100 by 7s. Hypothermic people cannot do simple math.
3. **Test coordination:** Have them touch their nose with their finger. Failure indicates at least moderate hypothermia.
4. **Check pulse:** Feel the carotid artery (side of neck) for 60 full seconds. A very slow or irregular pulse indicates moderate-to-severe hypothermia.

**Common Mistakes That Kill:**
- Putting the victim near a roaring fire (causes afterdrop).
- Rubbing the extremities (pushes cold blood toward the heart).
- Giving alcohol (causes vasodilation and accelerates heat loss).
- Letting them walk or exercise (circulates cold peripheral blood).
- Assuming someone is dead (Severe hypothermia mimics death; attempt rewarming first).

**Prevention Strategies:**
- Stay dry and change wet socks/clothing immediately.
- Eat regularly (high-fat foods provide sustained warmth).
- Stay hydrated (dehydration accelerates heat loss).
- Assign buddy checks in cold weather.

## System
The body's core temperature drops below 35°C (95°F), leading to heat loss exceeding heat production, which causes organ systems to shut down.

## Technology
- None explicitly identified in the source text.

## Tools
- Warm drinks (sweet drinks like sugar water or honey water).
- Warm water bottles.

## Process
**Phase 1: Stop the Heat Loss (Immediate Field Response)**
1. **Get them out of wind and wet:** Move the victim into a shelter, behind a windbreak, or into a vehicle.
2. **Remove wet clothing:** Cut off wet fabric if necessary, as wet fabric pulls heat from the body 25 times faster than dry air.
3. **Insulate from the ground:** Use pine boughs, a foam pad, dry leaves, or a backpack between the victim and the earth.
4. **Cover the head and neck:** Up to 40% of heat loss occurs from the head in cold conditions.

**Phase 2: Create a Heat Cocoon**
Wrap the victim in every layer of dry insulation available, including dry blankets, sleeping bags, spare clothing, or emergency space blankets (using the reflective side facing the person).

**Phase 3: Add External Heat**
- **For Mild Hypothermia:**
    - Administer warm drinks (not hot).
    - Use skin-to-skin contact as the single most effective field rewarming method.
    - Apply warm water bottles to the neck, armpits, and groin.
- **For Moderate-to-Severe Hypothermia:**
    - **Rewarm slowly:** Rapid rewarming can cause ‘afterdrop’ (cold blood rushing to the core, dropping core temperature further).
    - **Focus heat on the core:** Target the trunk, armpits, and groin. Avoid rubbing or heating the arms and legs.

## Notes
- The critical rule for severe hypothermia is that the heart is extremely irritable; rough handling can trigger fatal cardiac arrest. Move severe hypothermia victims slowly and gently, keeping them horizontal.
- Mild hypothermia caught early takes approximately 30 minutes to reverse.
- The best treatment is prevention, focusing on staying dry, eating calories for fuel, and staying hydrated.
