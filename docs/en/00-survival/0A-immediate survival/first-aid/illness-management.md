# Illness Management

## Objective
Part of First Aid. Without pharmacies or hospitals, treating common diseases with available resources becomes a core survival skill.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Available resources.
- Sanitation and clean water (for prevention of diarrheal diseases).
- Basic tools (e.g., clean, sharp blade for wound treatment).

## Material
### Diarrheal Diseases
- Cholera
- Dysentery
- Common gastroenteritis

### Respiratory Infections
- Pneumonia
- Bronchitis
- Influenza

### Fever
- The underlying cause of the fever (treat the cause, not the fever itself).

### Parasitic Infections
- Intestinal Worms (e.g., roundworms, tapeworms)

## System
The body's defense mechanism against pathogens. Pathogens replicate less efficiently at elevated temperatures.

## Technology
- Basic physical intervention (fluid replacement, cooling, steam inhalation).

## Tools
- Clean, sharp blade (for wound treatment).
- Boiling water (for purification/sterilization).
- Garlic and honey (for pneumonia treatment).
- Willow bark (for natural antipyretics).
- Pumpkin seeds or papaya seeds (for intestinal worms).

## Process
### Diarrheal Diseases Process
**Recognition:**
| Symptom | Mild (Gastroenteritis) | Moderate (Dysentery) | Severe (Cholera-like) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Stool frequency | 3-5 times/day | 6-15 times/day | Near continuous |
| Stool appearance | Watery, no blood | Blood and/or mucus | "Rice water" — clear fluid |
| Fever | Low or none | Moderate to high | Often absent |
| Dehydration | Mild | Moderate | Severe, rapid |
| Abdominal pain | Cramping | Severe cramping | Minimal (painless purging) |

**Treatment:**
1. Fluid replacement is the treatment (oral rehydration solution).
2. Give small, frequent sips (large volumes trigger vomiting).
3. Continue feeding if the patient can eat (bland foods: rice, boiled potatoes, plain bread, bananas).
4. Isolate the patient from others.
5. Bury feces at least 30 meters from water and 60 cm deep.
6. Caregivers must wash hands thoroughly after every contact.

### Respiratory Infections Process
**Recognition:**
| Symptom | Common Cold | Bronchitis | Pneumonia |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Fever | Low/none | Moderate | High (39°C+) |
| Cough | Dry or mild | Productive (mucus) | Productive, may be rusty/bloody |
| Breathing | Normal | Mild difficulty | Rapid, labored, painful |
| Chest pain | None | Mild | Sharp, worse on breathing |
| Duration | 5-10 days | 1-3 weeks | Weeks without treatment |
| Danger level | Low | Moderate | High — can be fatal |

**Treatment (All Respiratory Infections):**
- Rest.
- Fluids (at least 2-3 liters per day). Warm fluids help loosen mucus.
- Steam inhalation (boil water, drape cloth, breathe steam for 10-15 minutes; add thyme, eucalyptus, or pine needles).
- Elevate the head and chest during sleep.
- Isolate the patient if possible.

**Treatment (Pneumonia Specific):**
- Monitor breathing rate (Normal adult: 12-20 breaths per minute; >30 is a danger sign).
- Keep the patient sitting upright or propped at a 45-degree angle.
- Garlic-honey preparation: crush 4-5 cloves of garlic into 3 tablespoons of honey; give 1 teaspoon every 4 hours.
- Watch for blue lips or fingertips (cyanosis).

### Fever Management Process
**When to Treat Fever:**
- Below 38.5°C (101.3°F): Generally beneficial; keep comfortable, ensure hydration. Do not actively cool.
- 38.5-40°C (101.3-104°F): Monitor closely; use cooling measures if uncomfortable; ensure aggressive hydration.
- Above 40°C (104°F): Dangerous; active cooling is necessary; brain damage risk above 41°C.

**Cooling Methods:**
1. Tepid sponging (lukewarm water on forehead, armpits, groin, neck).
2. Remove excess clothing and blankets.
3. Fan the patient or move to a well-ventilated area.
4. Cool fluids (small, frequent sips of cool, not ice-cold, water).

**Natural Antipyretics:**
- Willow bark tea: Boil a thumb-sized piece of inner bark in 500 ml of water for 15 minutes, drink one cup every 4-6 hours. (Salicin precursor to aspirin).
- Elderflower tea: Steep dried or fresh flowers in boiled water for 10 minutes (promotes sweating).
- Meadowsweet tea: Steep dried flowers in boiled water (gentler on the stomach than willow bark).

### Wound Infections (Progressed) Process
**Signs of Systemic Infection (Sepsis):**
- Fever above 38.5°C or temperature below 36°C.
- Heart rate above 100 beats per minute.
- Breathing rate above 20 per minute.
- Confusion, disorientation.
- Red streaks extending from the wound toward the heart.

**Treatment:**
1. Open the wound and drain pus collection (using a clean, sharp blade; cut along the axis of the limb through the most swollen, fluctuant area).
2. Irrigate aggressively with boiled saline.
3. Pack the wound open (do not close it); stuff loosely with honey-soaked clean cloth.
4. Apply garlic poultice to the surrounding skin.
5. Force fluids (at least 3 liters per day).
6. Keep the patient resting with the infected area elevated.

### Parasitic Infections Process (Intestinal Worms)
**Treatment Options:**
- Pumpkin seeds: Eat 30-50 raw seeds on an empty stomach for 3 days (contains cucurbitin).
- Raw garlic: Eat 2-3 cloves daily on an empty stomach for 1 week (allicin).
- Papaya seeds: Chew 1 tablespoon on an empty stomach (proteolytic enzymes).
- Wormwood tea (*Artemisia absinthium*): Steep 1 teaspoon of dried leaves in boiled water for 10 minutes, drink once daily for 5 days.

**Prevention:**
- Always boil drinking water.
- Cook all meat thoroughly (internal temperature above 70°C).
- Wash hands before eating and after defecation.
- Wear footwear (hookworms enter through bare feet).
- Do not use human feces as fertilizer without composting at high temperature for at least 6 months.

### Quarantine Principles Process
1. Isolate the patient in a separate shelter or room, at least 2 meters from others.
2. Designate one caregiver, who should minimize contact with the rest of the group.
3. Ventilate the sick room to reduce airborne pathogen concentration.
4. Separate all eating utensils, bedding, and clothing; wash with boiling water.
5. Wash hands with soap (or wood ash and water) after every patient contact.
6. Monitor the rest of the group for symptoms for 14 days after the patient recovers.

## Notes
- Diarrheal diseases kill through dehydration, and the primary treatment is fluid replacement, not stopping the diarrhea.
- Fever management requires careful distinction between beneficial (below 38.5°C), monitorable (38.5-40°C), and dangerous (above 40°C) temperatures.
- Natural antipyretics like willow bark tea are effective, but dosage restrictions (e.g., for children or ulcer patients) must be observed.
- For wound infections progressing to sepsis, the combination of aggressive irrigation, packing with honey, garlic poultice, and forced fluids is critical, even without antibiotics.
