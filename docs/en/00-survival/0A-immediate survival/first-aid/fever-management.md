# Fever Management

## Objective
> Part of First Aid
> When pharmaceuticals are gone, managing fever becomes a critical skill — one that can mean the difference between recovery and organ damage.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Knowledge of danger thresholds for fever temperatures.
- Access to fluids for rehydration.
- Ability to perform physical cooling methods.

## Material
- Oral rehydration solution components: 6 level teaspoons of sugar (or honey), 1/2 level teaspoon of salt, clean water.
- Willow bark (inner bark from young branches).
- Elderflower (dried flowers or fresh).
- Meadowsweet (dried flowering tops).

## System
- The immune system, which uses core temperature to slow bacterial reproduction and accelerate white blood cell activity.

## Technology
- Modern pharmaceuticals (as a benchmark).
- Natural antipyretics (Willow Bark, Meadowsweet, Elderflower).

## Tools
- Thermometer (used to check temperature).
- Cloths for tepid sponging.
- Fans for evaporative cooling.
- Cool environment or raised surface.

## Process
### Understanding Fever
Fever is the body's defense mechanism. The management process involves determining the severity and applying appropriate interventions based on temperature and symptoms.

**Temperature Danger Thresholds:**
| Temperature | Status | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 100.4-102°F (38-39°C) | Mild fever | Monitor, hydrate, rest |
| 102-104°F (39-40°C) | Moderate fever | Active cooling, fluids, willow bark |
| 104-106°F (40-41°C) | High fever | Aggressive cooling, constant monitoring |
| Above 106°F (41°C) | Medical emergency | Risk of brain damage, seizures, death |

**Hydration Protocol:**
- The first priority is addressing fluid loss from sweat and metabolic demand.
- Oral rehydration solution: Sip continuously, aiming for at least 1 liter every 2 hours during a high fever. If intake is difficult, give small sips every 2-3 minutes.
- Danger Signs of Dehydration: Stopping sweating, dry mucous membranes, no urine for 6+ hours, confusion, disorientation.

**Physical Cooling Methods:**
1. **Tepid Sponging:** Use water roughly 85°F (29°C).
    - Steps: Strip patient, soak cloths, apply to high blood-flow areas (neck, armpits, groin, wrists, ankles), replace cloths every 3-5 minutes, continue for 20-30 minutes.
2. **Evaporative Cooling:** Fan the patient while skin is damp to draw heat via evaporation.
3. **Cool Environment:** Move the patient to shade or the coolest shelter, or elevate them off the ground.

**Herbal Intervention:**
- **Willow Bark:** Use 1-2 tablespoons of shredded inner bark per cup of water, simmer 15-20 minutes, dose one cup every 4-6 hours for adults.
- **Elderflower Tea:** Steep 2 tablespoons of dried flowers in hot water for 10 minutes. Safe for children over 2 in half-doses.
- **Meadowsweet:** Steep 1-2 teaspoons of dried flowering tops in hot water for 15 minutes, a gentler alternative when willow bark causes nausea.

**Fever in Children:**
- Risk: Febrile seizures (common 6 months to 5 years).
- Management: Hydration is paramount, tepid sponging is the safest cooling method, use elderflower tea in small doses, and avoid wrapping children in blankets to sweat them out.
- Seizure Management: Turn child on their side, protect the head, and time the event.

**Infection Signals:**
Fever patterns that signal serious infection require treatment of the underlying cause:
| Pattern | Possible Cause | Additional Signs |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sudden high fever with chills | Bacterial infection, malaria | Rigors (uncontrollable shaking) |
| Fever with stiff neck | Meningitis | Light sensitivity, headache, rash |
| Fever with abdominal pain | Appendicitis, peritonitis | Guarding, rebound tenderness |
| Fever recurring every 48-72 hours | Malaria | Sweating phase between fevers |
| Fever with productive cough | Pneumonia | Chest pain, rapid breathing |

**Monitoring and Record Keeping:**
- Track temperature, time of day, treatments given, and fluid intake.
- Interpretation: Fever dropping naturally with sweating and regained appetite indicates recovery. Sudden drop without sweating and cold/clammy skin indicates potential shock.

## Notes
- Without a thermometer, the back of the hand can be used to check temperature at the forehead, neck, and chest.
- Willow bark thins the blood, making caution necessary before any surgical procedure.
- Cold or ice water for sponging is ineffective as it constricts blood vessels and traps heat.
