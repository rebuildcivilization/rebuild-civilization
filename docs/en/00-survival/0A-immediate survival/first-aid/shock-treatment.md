# Shock Treatment | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To fix the underlying cause (stop bleeding, cool burns, rehydrate) while simultaneously supporting circulation.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Early recognition during compensated shock (pale skin, rapid pulse above 100, mild anxiety) gives the best chance of survival.

## Material
*   Insulation (underneath the patient to prevent hypothermia).
*   Blankets, coats, tarps, or sleeping bags for covering.

## System
The circulatory system fails, diverting blood from skin and limbs to the brain and heart. When compensation fails, organs shut down.

## Technology
*   **Blood Pressure Test:** Press a fingernail until it goes white, then release; if color takes more than 2 seconds to return, circulation is compromised.
*   **Improvised Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS):** 1 liter of clean water, 6 level teaspoons of sugar (or honey), 1/2 level teaspoon of salt.

## Tools
*   Stable objects (log, backpack, rolled clothing) for elevating legs.
*   CPR technique and Recovery Position.

## Process
### Step 1: Address the Underlying Cause
*   **Bleeding:** Apply direct pressure, use a tourniquet if necessary.
*   **Burns:** Cool the burn, cover loosely, prevent fluid loss.
*   **Dehydration:** Begin oral rehydration if conscious.
*   **Infection:** Clean and dress wounds; septic shock requires antibiotics.
*   **Allergic reaction:** Remove the trigger; keep the airway open.

### Step 2: Position the Patient
*   **Standard Position:** Lay flat on the back, elevate legs 20-30 cm (8-12 inches) to redirect 500-750 ml of blood to the heart and brain.
*   **Exceptions (Do NOT elevate legs if):** Suspected spinal injury, head injury (elevate head instead), chest injury/difficulty breathing (prop at 45 degrees), broken leg/pelvis (keep flat), or abdominal wound with exposed organs (bend knees/keep flat).
*   **If Vomiting/Unconscious:** Roll into the Recovery Position, then resume shock position after clearing the airway.

### Step 3: Maintain Body Temperature
*   Place insulation UNDERNEATH the patient to prevent heat loss through ground contact.
*   Cover the patient completely (head included).
*   Position near radiant heat sources (within 1 meter) but avoid hot water bottles or direct heat on the skin.

### Step 4: Manage Fluids
*   **If conscious and alert:** Offer small, frequent sips (50-100 ml) of ORS.
*   **Warning:** Do NOT give fluids if unconscious, vomiting, or with an abdominal wound.

### Step 5: Monitor and Reassess (Check every 5 minutes)
*   Pulse rate and strength (wrist or neck).
*   Breathing rate and depth.
*   Level of consciousness.
*   Skin color and temperature.
*   Capillary refill time (must return to pink in under 2 seconds).
*   Track the trend of improvement or deterioration.

### Step 6: Psychological Support
*   Speak calmly and reassuringly.
*   Maintain physical contact.
*   Shield the patient from seeing their own injuries.
*   Keep the environment calm and quiet to reduce stress hormones.

### Anaphylactic Shock: A Special Emergency
*   **Timeline:** Symptoms begin within seconds to 30 minutes; death can follow within minutes from airway closure.
*   **Treatment without Epinephrine:** Remove the trigger, position the person sitting upright (if breathing is difficult), apply a cold compress to the site, and use strong black coffee/tea for marginal bronchodilation.

### After the Crisis
*   Keep the patient warm and lying down for several hours.
*   Continue slow oral rehydration over 24 hours.
*   Monitor wound sites for infection leading to septic shock.
*   Require rest for at least 48 hours after a significant event.

## Key Takeaways
*   Shock kills by depriving organs of blood and oxygen: Address the cause while supporting circulation.
*   Positioning: Lay flat, elevate legs 20-30 cm unless contraindicated.
*   Insulation: Insulate from the ground to combat rapid hypothermia.
*   Fluids: Use ORS (1L water + 6 tsp sugar + 1/2 tsp salt).
*   Recognition: Early recognition during compensated shock offers the best chance of survival.

## Types of Shock You Will Encounter
| Type | Cause | Key Feature |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Hypovolemic | Blood loss, severe dehydration, burns | Most common in survival settings. Not enough fluid in the system. |
| Cardiogenic | Heart damage, cardiac arrest recovery | Heart cannot pump effectively. |
| Distributive (Septic) | Severe infection spreading to bloodstream | Blood vessels dilate, pressure drops. |
| Anaphylactic | Severe allergic reaction (insect stings, foods) | Rapid onset. Airway swelling, hives, breathing difficulty. |
| Neurogenic | Spinal cord injury | Loss of nervous system control over blood vessels. |

## Notes
- Shock is a medical emergency where the circulatory system fails to deliver enough blood and oxygen to vital organs.
- Shock victims look pale and feel cold due to blood diversion to the brain and heart.
- In a post-collapse world, common causes include traumatic injuries, blood loss, burns, dehydration, severe infections, and allergic reactions.
- Fear and pain accelerate shock by triggering adrenaline, which stresses the cardiovascular system.
- The ORS recipe (1L water + 6 tsp sugar + 1/2 tsp salt) is based on the concentration used in modern oral rehydration therapy.
