# Heat, Cold, Water | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To provide first aid guidance for environmental injuries caused by Heat, Cold, and Water, emphasizing that without climate-controlled buildings, central heating, or air conditioning, these injuries are leading causes of death.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Recognition and treatment of environmental injuries, including recognizing the critical differences between heat exhaustion and heat stroke, and understanding the stages of hypothermia and frostbite.

## Material
### Heat-Related Injuries
*   **Heat Exhaustion:** The body’s warning that the cooling system is overwhelmed; survivable and reversible if caught early.
*   **Heat Stroke:** A life-threatening emergency where the cooling system has failed.

### Cold-Related Injuries
*   **Hypothermia:** Occurs when the body’s core temperature drops below 35 degrees Celsius (95 degrees Fahrenheit).
    *   **Stages:** Mild (35-32 C), Moderate (32-28 C), Severe (Below 28 C).
    *   **Key Phenomenon:** Paradoxical Undressing (feeling burning hot in moderate to severe hypothermia).
*   **Frostbite:** The freezing of tissue, most commonly affecting fingers, toes, ears, nose, and cheeks.
    *   **Stages:** Frostnip, Superficial frostbite, Deep frostbite.

### Water-Related Injuries
*   **Near-Drowning:** A risk where secondary drowning can occur hours later as fluid in the lungs causes inflammation and breathing difficulty.
*   **Cold Water Drowning:** Cold water rapidly causes hypothermia and triggers the mammalian diving reflex.

## System
The overall system involves recognizing the severity of environmental injuries and applying specific, time-critical first aid treatments.

## Technology
*   **Core Temperature Thresholds:** 35-32 C (Mild Hypothermia), 32-28 C (Moderate Hypothermia), Below 28 C (Severe Hypothermia).
*   **Heat Stroke Threshold:** Core temperature above 40 degrees Celsius (104 degrees Fahrenheit).
*   **Frostbite Rewarming Temperature:** 37-40 degrees Celsius.

## Tools
*   Cooling resources (shade, cool water, wet cloths).
*   Warm drinks (sweet fluids).
*   Insulation materials (blankets, dry leaves/grass).
*   Pain management options (willow bark tea, alcohol).

## Process
### Heat Exhaustion Treatment
1. Move to shade immediately.
2. Lay person down with legs elevated 15-20 cm.
3. Remove excess clothing.
4. Cool actively: wet cloths on neck, armpits, and groin. Fan if possible.
5. Give water with salt (1/4 teaspoon salt per liter), small, frequent sips (250 ml every 15 minutes).
6. Rest for at least 24 hours before exertion.

### Heat Stroke Treatment (Life-Threatening Emergency)
1. Begin cooling immediately.
2. Immerse the person in cool water if available (fastest method).
3. If immersion is not possible, pour water over them continuously while fanning, concentrating on the head, neck, armpits, and groin.
4. Pack wet cloths around the neck, armpits, and groin.
5. Continue aggressive cooling until mental status improves or shivering begins.
6. Once responsive, give fluids slowly (salted water).
7. Do not leave them alone for 48 hours.

### Hypothermia Treatment
*   **Mild:** Move to shelter, replace wet clothing, give warm sweet drinks, apply insulation, skin-to-skin contact.
*   **Moderate:** Handle gently, lay horizontal, apply heat to the core (warm water bottles/heated stones) at the chest, armpits, and groin. Insulate thoroughly, warm humidified air.
*   **Severe:** Handle with extreme gentleness, slow rewarming of the core, begin CPR if no pulse after 60 seconds of checking.

### Frostbite Treatment
1. Move to a warm environment before rewarming.
2. Remove wet clothing and constricting items.
3. Rewarm by immersing in water at 37-40 degrees Celsius (20-40 minutes).
4. Provide pain management.
5. Do not rub or massage tissue.
6. Do not rewarm with direct heat (fire, hot stones).

### Near-Drowning Treatment
1. Rescue: Extend an object first, approach from behind and tow the person.
2. Open the airway and clear debris.
3. Check for breathing for 10 seconds.
4. If not breathing, begin rescue breathing (2 full breaths), check pulse, and proceed to CPR (30 compressions, 2 breaths).
5. Do not attempt to drain water from the lungs mechanically (aspirated water is absorbed).
6. Place the person in the recovery position.
7. Monitor for 24-48 hours for secondary drowning symptoms.

### Prevention
*   **Hydration:** 250 ml water every 30 minutes during physical work in heat.
*   **Work Schedule:** Hard labor only in early morning and evening; rest during midday.
*   **Headwear:** Wide-brimmed hat, wet cloth over head and neck.
*   **Salt Intake:** Add 1/4 teaspoon salt per liter of drinking water during heavy sweating.
*   **Acclimatization:** Gradually increase heat exposure over 7-10 days when moving to a hotter environment.
*   **Water Safety:** Never swim alone; use poles/group when crossing rivers; ensure children are supervised.

## Notes
*   Heat stroke is the most time-critical environmental emergency.
*   Hypothermia victims must be handled gently, especially in moderate to severe stages, as rough handling can trigger cardiac arrest.
*   The key principle for frostbite treatment is avoiding thaw-refreeze cycles.
*   The saying for severe hypothermia is: “Nobody is dead until they are warm and dead.”
