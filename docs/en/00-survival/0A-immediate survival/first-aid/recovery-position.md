# Recovery Position

## Objective
To prevent an unconscious person lying on their back from choking to death on their own vomit or tongue in minutes.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- An unconscious person who IS breathing on their own.
- Zero equipment required for the basic technique.

## Material
An unconscious person.

## System
The critical principle is gravity. The recovery position uses gravity to allow the tongue to fall forward and fluids to drain out of the mouth rather than pooling at the back of the throat.

## Technology
The recovery position solves the problem by using gravity, which allows the tongue to fall forward and facilitates the draining of fluids from the mouth.

## Tools
- Body positioning (kneeling beside the person).
- Hands to act as braces and levers.
- Jaw thrust (alternative for airway opening without neck movement).

## Process
### Before You Position: The Safety Check
1. Confirm the person is breathing (watch chest rise/fall, listen near the mouth, feel for air on the cheek). Spend no more than 10 seconds.
2. If the person is NOT breathing, begin CPR immediately. The recovery position is only for unconscious people who are breathing.
3. Check for spinal injury: Do NOT roll unless the airway is compromised and cannot be cleared otherwise. A jaw thrust can open the airway.
4. Remove glasses, bulky items, and check the mouth quickly for visible obstructions (food, broken teeth, dentures), removing them with a finger sweep, but do NOT blindly probe deep into the throat.

### The Standard Recovery Position: Step by Step
1. Take the arm nearest to you and place it at a right angle to the body, elbow bent, palm facing upward (to prevent rolling onto the face).
2. Take the far arm and bring it across the chest, holding the back of the far hand against the cheek nearest to you to cushion the head.
3. Reach across and grab the far knee, pulling the leg up so the foot is flat on the ground and the leg is bent.
4. Use the bent knee as a lever to pull the person toward you; they will roll onto their side.
5. Adjust the top leg so the hip and knee are both bent at right angles to act as a kickstand.
6. Tilt the head back slightly and angle the face downward so the mouth is the lowest point for fluids to drain.
7. Check that the airway is open, look for chest movement, listen for breathing, and confirm air is flowing.

### Monitoring in Recovery Position
- **Every 2 minutes:** Confirm breathing, check airway patency (no gurgling sounds), ensure the position has not shifted, and check the pulse.
- **If breathing stops:** Roll the person onto their back immediately and begin CPR.
- **If they vomit:** The position should allow drainage. If not, use fingers to clear the mouth, then reposition the head for drainage.
- **Every 30 minutes:** If unconscious for an extended period, roll the person onto their opposite side to prevent nerve damage and circulation restriction.

### Special Situations
- **Pregnant Women:** Position on the LEFT side. The weight of the uterus on the right side compresses the inferior vena cava, which can reduce blood flow by up to 25%.
- **Chest or Abdominal Injuries:** Position the person with the INJURED SIDE DOWN to allow the uninjured lung to expand fully and use gravity/body weight to compress the wound.
- **Suspected Spinal Injury with Compromised Airway:** If helpers are available, one person stabilizes the head/neck while two or more people perform a log-roll technique, keeping the spine straight.
- **Children and Infants (under 1 year):** Hold the baby face-down along your forearm with the head slightly lower than the body, supported by your hand.

### The Jaw Thrust Alternative
Used when suspecting a spinal injury but needing to open the airway without neck movement:
1. Kneel above the person’s head, looking down.
2. Place fingers behind the angles of the jaw.
3. Push the jaw forward and upward to lift the tongue.
4. Maintain the position.

### Common Mistakes
| Mistake | Risk | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Head tilted too far back | Neck hyperextension, worse in spinal injury | Gentle tilt only — enough to open the airway |
| Face pointing upward | Vomit pools in throat, causing aspiration | Angle face slightly downward toward the ground |
| Bottom arm tucked under body | Cuts off circulation, causes nerve damage | Arm out at 90 degrees, palm up |
| Top leg straight | Person rolls onto face or back | Bend hip and knee to 90 degrees as a brace |
| Not checking after positioning | Airway may reclose, breathing may stop | Monitor every 2 minutes |
| Rolling a spinal injury patient | Spinal cord damage, paralysis | Use jaw thrust instead; roll only if airway cannot be maintained |

## Notes
- The recovery position is specifically for unconscious people who ARE breathing. If breathing stops, CPR must be performed.
- The key principle is gravity: mouth lower than throat, tongue falling forward, fluids draining out.
- Prolonged pressure on one side requires switching sides every 30 minutes.
- When monitoring, gurgling sounds indicate fluid in the airway.
- The jaw thrust is an exhausting alternative to the roll when a spinal injury is suspected but the airway needs opening.
