# One-Handed Bowline Tie

## Objective
The one-handed bowline is a self-rescue technique that lets you tie a non-slip loop around your own body using a single hand — critical when one arm is injured, pinned, or holding you in place.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Before attempting the one-handed bowline, one must be able to tie a standard two-handed bowline automatically without thinking about the steps. If the ‘rabbit’ mnemonic is still needed, practice the standard bowline another 100 times before moving on.

## Material
*   Rope
*   Natural fiber cordage (bark, grass, vine)
*   Work gloves
*   Cold water (for soaking rope)

## System
The system revolves around a self-rescue mechanism utilizing a single hand to create a non-constricting loop around the body, functioning as a definitive self-rescue knot.

## Technology
The core technology is the one-handed bowline, distinguished by the critical wrist twist required to form the knot correctly, which determines if a slip knot or a proper bowline is formed.

## Tools
*   Two hands (one working, one stabilizing/holding)
*   Teeth, knee, or a fixed object (to pull the standing part)
*   Pre-made loops or carabiners (for when both hands are lost)

## Process
### The Technique: Right Hand Method
**Setup:**
1. Grasp the working end (short free end) with the right hand. The standing part hangs free.
2. Pass the working end around the body from right to left, behind the back.
3. The working end is in the right hand on the right side; the standing part is on the left side.

**Forming the Knot:**
4. With the right hand, bring the working end across the body and lay it *over* the standing part, forming an X. Pinch the crossing point between the right thumb and index finger. The working end should be on top.
5. Rotate the right wrist *toward* the body (clockwise for a right-hander). This action forms a small loop around the working end within the standing part.
6. Use the right fingers to route the working end behind the standing part, grabbing it again.
7. Pull the working end back through the small loop (the ‘rabbit goes back in the hole’).
8. Pull the standing part while holding the working end to tighten the knot. The loop around the body should be snug but not tight.

**Left Hand Method:**
Mirror all instructions, passing the rope around the body from left to right, and twisting the left wrist toward the body (counterclockwise).

**Verification:**
Check that the tail runs parallel to the rope on the *inside* (body side), the small loop is snug around the collar, and pulling the standing part does not cause the waist loop to tighten.

**Training Progression:**
*   **Phase 1: Understanding (Day 1-3):** Practice slowly with both hands available but only using one hand. Keep the other hand behind the back. Goal: complete the knot correctly 10 out of 10 times without time pressure.
*   **Phase 2: Fluency (Day 4-10):** Begin timing, starting at 60 seconds and working down to 30 seconds. Practice with both dominant and non-dominant hands while standing, sitting, and lying down.
*   **Phase 3: Stress Inoculation (Day 11-20):** Add stress factors such as darkness, cold hands (soak hands for 2 minutes before tying), wearing gloves, physical exhaustion (e.g., 20 pushups before tying), awkward positions, and wet rope.
*   **Phase 4: Maintenance (Ongoing):** Tie one one-handed bowline per day with each hand to maintain skill sharpness.

**Situational Variations:**
*   **Tying Around a Post or Tree (One-Handed):** Pass the working end around the tree. Grab both the working end and standing part in one hand (working end on top). Perform the same wrist twist to form the small loop, then complete the bowline.
*   **Tying While Hanging:** Wedge the standing part against the body or a surface with an elbow, knee, or chin to create tension. Use teeth to hold the working end momentarily during the wrist twist. Work against gravity.

**When One-Handed Is Not Enough:**
If both hands are lost, options include having someone else tie the knot, using a pre-tied loop dropped over the body, stepping into a pre-made loop, or using a carabiner or toggle clip if available.

## Notes
- The direction of the wrist twist is critical: twisting the wrong way produces a slip knot that tightens under load.
- The one-handed bowline is defined as the definitive self-rescue knot because it creates a non-constricting rescue loop using a single hand.
- Skill degradation occurs within weeks without consistent practice.
