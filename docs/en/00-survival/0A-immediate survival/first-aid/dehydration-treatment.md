# Dehydration Treatment

## Objective
To treat dehydration using oral rehydration solution (ORS) and prevent dehydration through proper daily water intake.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Water:** Must be cooled and boiled.
*   **Ingredients:** Salt (sodium chloride) and Sugar (glucose).
*   **Assessment:** Ability to assess dehydration severity using the skin turgor test and monitoring urine color.

## Material
### Oral Rehydration Solution (ORS)
**WHO Standard Formula (for 1 liter of clean water):**
*   Salt (sodium chloride): 3 grams (roughly half a level teaspoon).
*   Sugar: 18 grams (roughly 4 level teaspoons).

**Alternative Recipes:
1. The “pinch and scoop” method:
    *   1 liter boiled water.
    *   A three-finger pinch of salt.
    *   A fistful of sugar.
2. Cereal-based ORS (effective for diarrhea):
    *   Cook 50 grams of rice (or other starch) in 1 liter of water until soft.
    *   Strain the water.
    *   Add half a teaspoon of salt to the starchy liquid.
3. Coconut water (natural electrolyte source).

### Electrolytes
Critical electrolytes include Sodium, Potassium, Chloride, and Bicarbonate.
*   **Sodium:** Lost through sweat, diarrhea, vomiting. Deficiency signs include confusion, muscle cramps, headache.
*   **Potassium:** Lost through diarrhea, vomiting. Deficiency signs include muscle weakness, irregular heartbeat, fatigue.
*   **Chloride:** Lost through sweat, vomiting. Usually paired with sodium loss.
*   **Bicarbonate:** Lost through severe diarrhea. Deficiency signs include rapid breathing, lethargy.

## System
### Recognition of Dehydration
Dehydration severity is assessed based on symptoms:

| Sign | Mild (3-5% fluid loss) | Moderate (6-9% fluid loss) | Severe (10%+ fluid loss) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Thirst | Present | Intense | May be absent (too weak) |
| Mouth/lips | Slightly dry | Very dry, cracked | Parched, no saliva |
| Skin turgor (pinch test) | Normal | Slow return (2-3 sec) | “Tenting” — stays pinched |
| Eyes | Normal | Slightly sunken | Deeply sunken |
| Urine | Dark yellow, reduced | Very dark, scant | None for 6+ hours |
| Pulse | Normal | Rapid | Rapid and weak |
| Mental state | Normal | Irritable, restless | Lethargic, confused |
| Blood pressure | Normal | Low-normal | Low (shock imminent) |

### Mechanism of ORS
The gut absorbs water most efficiently when specific ratios of glucose and sodium are present, as they share a co-transport mechanism in the intestinal wall, pulling water with them.

### Prevention
Daily water requirements depend on conditions:
*   Resting in shade, temperate climate: 2-3 liters/day.
*   Light activity, temperate climate: 3-4 liters/day.
*   Heavy labor, temperate climate: 4-6 liters/day.
*   Any activity, hot climate (30°C+): 5-8 liters/day.
*   Fever (per degree above 37°C): Add 0.5 liters.
*   Diarrhea or vomiting: Add 1-2 liters per episode.

### Monitoring Hydration
Monitoring is done via urine color:
*   Pale yellow to clear: Well hydrated.
*   Dark yellow: Mildly dehydrated — drink more.
*   Amber or brown: Significantly dehydrated — begin active rehydration.
*   No urine for 6+ hours: Emergency — begin ORS immediately.

## Technology
Oral Rehydration Therapy (ORS) is considered the single most important medical intervention in a survival scenario.

## Tools
*   Spoon (for children's dosing).
*   Flexible tubing (rubber tubing, cleaned garden hose section, hollowed reed for rectal administration).

## Process
### Administration Technique
*   **Fluid Delivery:** Sip, don’t gulp, to prevent vomiting.
*   **Children's Technique:** Use a spoon for children, administering one teaspoon (5 ml) every 1-2 minutes.
*   **Vomitus Protocol:** If vomiting occurs, wait 10 minutes, then restart with smaller, more frequent sips.
*   **Infant Dosing:** 75 ml per kilogram of body weight over the first 4 hours.
*   **Rectal Fluid Administration (Proctoclysis):** A last-resort technique when oral intake fails. Involves preparing ORS, lubricating a flexible tube, inserting 5-10 cm into the rectum, slowly introducing 100-200 ml over 15-20 minutes, and allowing 30 minutes between doses, targeting 500-1000 ml per hour.

### ORS Preparation and Bulk Making
*   **Batch Preparation:** Prepare fresh batches every 24 hours, as ORS left at room temperature grows bacteria after one day. In hot climates, replace every 12 hours.
*   **Bulk Ratios:**
    *   1 liter batch: 3g salt (½ tsp) + 18g sugar (4 tsp).
    *   5 liters batch: 15g salt (2½ tsp) + 90g sugar (20 tsp / ~6 tbsp).
    *   10 liters batch: 30g salt (5 tsp) + 180g sugar (40 tsp / ~12 tbsp).

## Notes
*   The ratio of glucose and sodium in ORS is critical; too much salt causes hypernatremia, and too much sugar causes osmotic diarrhea.
*   Cereal-based ORS is explicitly stated to be more effective than sugar-based ORS for diarrheal dehydration because the complex carbohydrates provide slow-release glucose.
*   The skin pinch test is the fastest field assessment for dehydration severity.
*   The goal of treatment is to replace lost fluid, not to stop diarrhea.
*   Breast milk is recommended for infants in addition to ORS because it provides both fluid and nutrition.
