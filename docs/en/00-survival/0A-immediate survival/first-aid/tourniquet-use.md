# Tourniquet Use

## Objective
To apply a tourniquet when direct pressure and wound packing fail to stop life-threatening limb bleeding.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Application is appropriate when direct pressure, wound packing, and pressure bandages have all failed to control life-threatening limb bleeding. Specifically, it is appropriate when:

*   Arterial bleeding from an arm or leg that doesn’t stop with 10 minutes of firm direct pressure.
*   Amputations (partial or complete loss of a limb).
*   Multiple casualties (to stop one person’s bleeding quickly to help others).
*   Inability to reach the wound (crushed limb under debris or wound location impossible to control with direct pressure).
*   Mass hemorrhage (blood pooling on the ground, rapid soaking through dressings, and signs of shock).

**Crucially: Tourniquets are for limbs only. Never apply to the neck, torso, or head, as they work by compressing an artery against a bone.**

## Material
### Commercial
*   Commercial tourniquets (CAT, SOF-T, SWAT-T) are the gold standard.

### Improvised Materials Needed
1.  **Band**: A strip of strong material at least **1.5 inches (4 cm) wide** (wider is better). Narrow materials (rope, wire, paracord) must be folded to achieve the required width.
    *   Good options: Folded triangular bandage or cravat, torn strip of shirt or pants (folded to double thickness), belt (if wide and flexible), scarf, or strap from a backpack.
2.  **Windlass**: A rigid stick, rod, or tool roughly 6-8 inches (15-20 cm) long, strong enough not to break under twisting force. 
    *   Good options: Sturdy stick (roughly thumb thickness), screwdriver or wrench, tent stake, or folded metal strip.
3.  **Securing mechanism**: Something to hold the windlass in place after tightening (a second strip of cloth, tape, or the tail end of the band).

## System
### Technology
Tourniquets work by compressing an artery against a bone.

### Tools
*   Rigid windlass (stick, rod, wrench).
*   Material for the tourniquet band.
*   Marker, charcoal, or blood for time marking.

### Process

**Assembly**
1.  Fold or roll the band material to roughly 1.5-2 inches (4-5 cm) width.
2.  Make the band long enough to wrap around the limb at least twice, with extra length for tying.
3.  Have the windlass and securing strip ready.

**Placement**
1.  Apply the tourniquet **2-3 inches (5-7 cm) above the wound** (between the wound and the heart).
2.  If the wound location is unclear, apply it **high and tight** (upper arm near the armpit or upper thigh near the groin).
3.  **Never** place it directly over a wound, over a joint (elbow or knee), or below the wound.

**Step-by-Step Application**
1.  **Wrap the band** around the limb twice. Tie a simple overhand knot (half-knot), keeping it tight against the skin.
2.  **Place the windlass** on top of the knot. Tie a second overhand knot over the windlass, locking it in place (square knot with the windlass trapped).
3.  **Twist the windlass** to tighten, rotating it like winding a clock. Each turn increases compression.
4.  **Keep twisting until bleeding stops** and you can no longer feel a distal pulse. Do not stop short.
5.  **Secure the windlass** so it cannot unwind (tie the loose ends or use a separate securing strip).
6.  **Note the time.** Write “TK” and the time on the patient’s forehead, chest, or the tourniquet itself.

**Verification**
*   Check that **bleeding has stopped** (look at the wound).
*   Check that **no pulse is below the tourniquet** (radial pulse for arms, dorsalis pedis for legs). If a pulse is present, tighten further.
*   Check that the **windlass is secured** and cannot unwind.

**Post-Application Care**
1.  Treat for shock (lay flat, elevate legs unless the leg is the injury, keep warm).
2.  Splint the limb if possible.
3.  **Do not cover the tourniquet**; it must be visible.
4.  Monitor: Check every 15-30 minutes that the windlass hasn’t slipped and bleeding hasn’t restarted.
5.  Give water if the person is conscious and alert (unless abdominal injuries exist).
6.  Pain management and reassurance are necessary.

**Two-Tourniquet Rule**
If one tourniquet fails to stop bleeding (especially on the thigh), apply a second tourniquet directly above the first to distribute force over a wider area and occlude the femoral artery.

## Technology
*   The effectiveness of the tourniquet relies on compressing an artery against a bone to completely cut off blood flow.

## Tools
*   Band material (minimum 1.5 inches wide).
*   Rigid windlass.
*   Securing mechanism.
*   Marker/charcoal/blood for time marking.

## Process
**Duration and Limb Viability**
*   **Up to 6 hours** of proper application before the risk of irreversible limb damage becomes significant.
*   **Under 2 hours**: Almost no risk of permanent damage; temporary numbness is normal.
*   **2-6 hours**: Increasing risk of nerve and muscle damage.
*   **Over 6 hours**: Significant risk of irreversible damage; amputation may be necessary.
*   **In survival settings**: Leave the tourniquet on until a controlled environment is reached.

**Common Mistakes and Fixes**
| Mistake | Consequence | Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Not tight enough | Occludes veins but not arteries; increases bleeding | Tighten until bleeding stops AND distal pulse disappears |
| Too narrow a band | Tissue damage, pain, incomplete occlusion | Use material at least 1.5 inches (4 cm) wide |
| Placed over a joint | Cannot compress artery against bone at the joint | Move above (closer to torso) the joint |
| Placed too low | Bleeding continues from vessels above the tourniquet | When in doubt, go high and tight |
| Loosening to “check” | Restarts hemorrhage, washes out clots | Never loosen once applied in the field |
| Not securing the windlass | Windlass unwinds, tourniquet loosens | Tie, tape, or strap the windlass firmly |

## Notes
*   **Critical Error**: Undertightening is the most common error.
*   **Material Constraint**: The requirement for material width (1.5 inches / 4 cm) is critical to prevent tissue damage.
*   **Field Protocol**: The cardinal rule is: **Never loosen in the field**; mark the application time and leave it for qualified care to remove.
*   **Limitation**: Tourniquets work by compressing an artery against a bone, making them unsuitable for junctional bleeding (groin, armpit, neck), where wound packing and direct pressure are required.
