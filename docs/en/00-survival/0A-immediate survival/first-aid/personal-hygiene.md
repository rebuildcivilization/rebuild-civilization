# Personal Hygiene and Sanitation

## Objective

To reduce infection, disease, parasites, and contamination by maintaining personal hygiene when normal sanitation infrastructure is unavailable.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

Personal hygiene becomes more important when living without reliable water supply, sewage systems, waste collection, or medical services.

Water used for hygiene should not compromise drinking-water supplies.

## Material

Useful materials include:

* Water.
* Soap.
* Cloth.
* Clean clothing.
* Clean socks.
* Toothbrush or improvised cleaning material.
* Comb.
* Container for washing water.
* Ash or sand when soap is unavailable.

## System

### Priority Areas

When water is limited, pay particular attention to:

* Hands.
* Feet.
* Armpits.
* Groin.
* Hair.
* Mouth and teeth.
* Areas around wounds.

### Hands

Keep hands clean because contaminated hands can transfer microorganisms to food, drinking water, utensils, and wounds.

Wash hands:

* After defecating or urinating.
* After handling potentially contaminated material.
* After caring for sick people.
* Before handling food.
* Before handling food utensils.
* Before handling drinking water.

Keep fingernails short and clean.

### Hair

Keep hair clean and regularly combed or trimmed to reduce the accumulation of microorganisms and parasites.

### Clothing and Bedding

Keep clothing and bedding as clean and dry as practical.

Change underclothing and socks regularly.

When water is scarce, clothing can be shaken, aired, and exposed to sunlight.

Sleeping bags and similar bedding should be aired after use.

### Feet

Keep feet clean and dry.

Inspect them regularly for:

* Blisters.
* Cuts.
* Irritation.
* Fungal problems.
* Signs of prolonged moisture exposure.

## Technology

### Washing

A cloth and soapy water can replace a conventional shower.

When water is extremely limited, exposure of the body to clean air and sunlight can provide an additional hygiene measure, but sunburn must be avoided.

### Soap

An expedient soap-making process using rendered animal fat and an alkaline liquid produced from wood ashes.

The basic concept is:

**Animal fat + alkaline solution + heat → soap**

This process requires care because concentrated alkaline solutions can cause severe burns.

## Tools

* Cloth.
* Soap.
* Water container.
* Comb.
* Toothbrush.
* Cooking pot.
* Clean storage container.

## Process

### Basic Hygiene Routine

1. Wash hands at critical times.
2. Clean the face and body regularly.
3. Pay particular attention to feet, armpits, groin, hands, and hair.
4. Keep clothing and bedding clean.
5. Change wet clothing as soon as practical.
6. Clean teeth and mouth regularly.
7. Inspect skin for wounds and irritation.
8. Keep wounds protected from contamination.
9. Keep human waste away from living and food-preparation areas.

### Water-Limited Hygiene

When water is scarce:

1. Prioritize drinking water.
2. Use a cloth with a small amount of water for cleaning.
3. Concentrate cleaning on high-risk areas.
4. Air and sun clothing when appropriate.
5. Avoid contaminating shared water sources.

## Notes

* Poor hygiene can increase the risk of infection and disease.
* Hands are a particularly important contamination pathway.
* Clean water should not be unnecessarily consumed for low-priority washing.
* Soap-making procedure should be treated as a chemical process rather than improvised casually because alkaline solutions can be hazardous.
* This document describes hygiene principles rather than replacing modern medical or public-health guidance.
