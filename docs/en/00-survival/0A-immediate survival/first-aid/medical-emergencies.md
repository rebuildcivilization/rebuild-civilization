# Medical Emergencies: Life-Threatening Situations

## Objective
To provide immediate, life-saving first aid protocols for situations where someone stops breathing, has a heart attack, goes into shock, or suffers a severe allergic reaction, utilizing methods that require no medical equipment.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Medical equipment: Protocols work without any medical equipment.
- Training: Rescue breaths can be added if the rescuer is trained and willing.
- Knowledge: Awareness of known severe allergies within a survival community.

## Material
### Triage
Prioritize treatment based on the severity of injuries to maximize survival when resources are limited.

| Category | Signs | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Immediate** (Red) | Not breathing but has a pulse, severe bleeding, shock | Treat first — life-saving interventions only |
| **Delayed** (Yellow) | Broken bones, moderate wounds, burns, conscious and stable | Treat second — painful but not immediately fatal |
| **Minor** (Green) | Walking, talking, minor injuries | Treat last — can wait or help themselves |
| **Expectant** (Black) | No pulse and not breathing, massive unsurvivable injury | Do not treat — direct resources to those you can save |

### Cardiac Arrest and CPR
- Recognition:
    - Person collapses or is found unresponsive.
    - No breathing or only gasping (agonal breathing is irregular, noisy gasps).
    - No pulse at the carotid artery (check for 10 seconds maximum).
- Hands-Only CPR Protocol:
    1. Place the person on a hard, flat surface.
    2. Position hands: Heel of one hand on the center of the chest (lower half of the breastbone), other hand on top, fingers interlaced.
    3. Lock elbows straight, position shoulders directly above hands.
    4. Compress hard and fast: Depth 5-6 cm (2-2.5 inches); Rate 100-120 compressions per minute. Allow full chest recoil.
    5. Do not stop compressions.
    6. Rotate rescuers every 2 minutes.
- Rescue Breathing (Optional):
    - Add 30 compressions then 2 breaths (tilt head back, lift chin, seal mouth, blow for 1 second until chest rises).
- Duration:
    - Continue until the person starts breathing on their own, moves, or groans.
    - Realistic survival rate after 30-45 minutes of good CPR is very low, except for hypothermia victims who require CPR until warmed.

### Choking (Airway Obstruction)
- Conscious Adult:
    1. Ask if choking. Encourage forceful cough if possible.
    2. If unable to speak/cough: Perform abdominal thrusts (Heimlich maneuver) – wrap arms around waist, fist above navel, pull sharply inward and upward in a J-shaped motion.
    3. For very large persons or late-pregnancy: use chest thrusts (fist on center of breastbone, compress straight backward).
- Unconscious Adult:
    1. Lower to the ground and check mouth for object (sweep out, do not blind finger sweep).
    2. Attempt 2 rescue breaths.
    3. Begin CPR (30 compressions, check mouth, attempt 2 breaths). Continue until airway clears.

### Anaphylaxis (Severe Allergic Reaction)
- Recognition:
    - Swelling of face, lips, tongue, throat.
    - Difficulty breathing, wheezing, stridor.
    - Widespread hives or red, itchy skin.
    - Rapid pulse, dropping blood pressure.
    - Nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps.
    - Feeling of impending doom.
- Emergency Treatment Without Medication:
    1. Position: Sit upright if breathing is difficult; lay flat with legs elevated 30 cm if blood pressure is dropping.
    2. Remove the trigger if possible.
    3. Monitor the airway constantly; consider emergency surgical airway (cricothyrotomy) if breathing stops.
    4. Keep them calm.
    5. Cold compress on the exposure site may help.

### Shock
- Recognition:
    - Pale, cold, clammy skin.
    - Rapid, weak pulse (over 100 bpm).
    - Rapid, shallow breathing.
    - Confusion, anxiety, agitation.
    - Thirst.
    - Decreased or absent urine output.
- Treatment:
    1. Treat the cause (stop bleeding, cool burns, replace fluids).
    2. Position: Lay flat, elevate legs 20-30 cm above the heart (unless head/spinal injury).
    3. Maintain body temperature: Cover with blankets, insulate from the ground.
    4. Fluids: Small sips of water with salt and sugar if conscious and no abdominal injury.
    5. Keep them calm and still.
    6. Monitor continuously every 5 minutes.

### Recovery Position
- Application:
    - For any unconscious person who is breathing.
    - Procedure: Kneel beside the person, extend the nearest arm straight, bring the far arm across the chest (holding the hand against the cheek), bend the far knee up, roll toward you, and adjust the top knee to a right angle. Tilt the head back slightly to keep the airway open.

### Seizures
- During a Seizure:
    - Do not restrain.
    - Clear the area of hard objects.
    - Do not put anything in the mouth.
    - Protect the head.
- After a Seizure:
    - Place in the recovery position.
    - The person will be confused and exhausted (postictal state).
    - Life-threatening if seizure lasts more than 5 minutes or if a second seizure follows without full recovery (status epilepticus).

## System
- The entire system relies on applying time-sensitive interventions across multiple simultaneous life-threatening conditions.

## Technology
- Protocols are designed to be effective without medical equipment.
- Specific tools mentioned:
    - Epinephrine auto-injectors (for anaphylaxis).
    - Rescue breaths (optional component of CPR).
    - Defibrillators (mentioned as a factor in survival rate for cardiac arrest).

## Tools
- Hands and body weight for CPR compressions.
- A hard, flat surface for positioning during CPR.
- A flat surface, board, or floor to provide a hard, flat surface.
- Cold compresses (for anaphylaxis).
- Water, salt, and sugar (for oral rehydration).

## Process
1. **Assessment (Triage):** Determine the priority based on signs (Breathing, Pulse, Severity).
2. **Cardiac Arrest Management:** Recognize arrest (unresponsive, no breathing/pulse). Initiate CPR (Hands-Only or with Rescue Breaths).
3. **Choking Management:** Assess consciousness. Apply Heimlich (conscious) or CPR cycle (unconscious).
4. **Anaphylaxis Management:** Recognize symptoms. Position and remove trigger. Monitor airway.
5. **Shock Management:** Identify the cause and treat it. Position the patient for blood flow optimization. Maintain temperature.
6. **Post-Event Care:** Apply Recovery Position for unconscious, breathing victims. Assess seizure duration.

## Notes
- Cracked ribs are acceptable during CPR compression depth; cracked ribs heal, a dead brain does not.
- The rhythm of the song “Stayin’ Alive” is the correct tempo for CPR rate (100-120/min).
- The decision to stop CPR after 30-45 minutes of good CPR without response is difficult but rational, unless the victim is hypothermic.
