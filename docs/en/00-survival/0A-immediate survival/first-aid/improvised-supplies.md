# Improvised Supplies: A Guide to Field Medical Gear

## Objective
To build improvised medical supplies, the primary objective is to prioritize sterilization above everything else.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Having a base camp to begin assembling a dedicated medical supply cache.
*   Materials found in nature or scavenged from ruins.

## Material
### Bandages and Dressings
*   **Fabric Sources:** T-shirts, bedsheets, pillowcases, curtains, towels, undershirts. Cotton is ideal.
*   **Absorbent Materials:** Sphagnum moss, cotton cloth (folded), sanitary pads/tampons, dried grass, paper, spider silk.
*   **Closure Materials:** Adhesive tape or duct tape, tree resin (pine pitch).
*   **Splint Materials:** Straight sticks/branches, bark slabs (birch, poplar), boards, plywood, rolled magazines/newspapers, trekking poles, metal rods, pipes.
*   **Stretcher Materials:** Blanket or tarp, 2 sturdy poles, jackets or shirts.

### Antiseptic Solutions
*   Raw honey (direct from comb).
*   Boiled saline solution (1 teaspoon of salt per 1 liter of boiled water).
*   Dilute alcohol (Any spirit above 60% ABV, diluted to ~70%).
*   Garlic (raw cloves).
*   Plantain leaves (fresh broadleaf).
*   Charcoal (hardwood charcoal powder).

## System
**The Mindset: Clean Over Clever**
Every improvised supply must follow the rule that it must be as clean as possible. The order of preference for sterilization is:

1.  **Boiling:** Submerge in a rolling boil for 10 minutes to kill virtually all bacteria, viruses, and parasites.
2.  **Dry heat:** Hold near fire (not in flame) until too hot to touch (useful for tools and needles).
3.  **Alcohol:** Soak or wipe with spirits above 60% concentration (effective for surfaces and small items).
4.  **Sunlight:** Spread materials in direct UV sunlight for 6+ hours (partially effective).
5.  **Nothing:** Use the material anyway if sterilization is impossible, provided the alternative (uncontrolled bleeding) is worse.

## Technology
*   **Sterilization Technique:** Boiling fabric strips for 10 minutes is the superior method over elaborate unsterilized dressings.
*   **Wound Closure Technique:** Using butterfly closures (tape/resin) to hold edges together while allowing drainage.
*   **Immobilization Technique:** Splints must be rigid enough to span the joint above and below the fracture, with padding to prevent pressure sores.
*   **Irrigation Technique:** Using a syringe to create a pressurized stream to flush debris from wounds.
*   **Transport Technique:** The Pole-and-Shirt Stretcher uses poles sliding through sleeves of jackets to form a bed.

## Tools
*   **Improvised Needles:** Any thin, rigid, curved piece of metal (safety pin, fish hook (barb filed off), sewing needle, thorn).
*   **Improvised Thread:** Fishing line (monofilament, ideal), dental floss, thin natural cordage, hair from a horse’s tail.
*   **Tweezers:** Two thin, flat sticks bound tightly at one end with cord, with a small wedge between them.
*   **Irrigation Syringe:** A punched hole (2-3 mm) in a plastic bottle or animal bladder filled with clean water.

## Process
### Bandage Preparation
1.  **Fabric Selection:** Choose clean fabric, prioritizing cotton.
2.  **Preparation:** Tear or cut fabric into strips 5-8 cm wide and at least 60 cm long.
3.  **Sterilization:** Boil the strips for 10 minutes, then wring out and air dry.
4.  **Storage:** Store dry strips rolled up in a clean container.

### Splinting Process
1.  **Material Selection:** Choose a rigid material (stick, board, metal rod).
2.  **Application:** Ensure the splint is long enough to span the joint above and below the fracture.
3.  **Padding:** Always pad between the splint and the skin, especially on bony prominences.

### Antiseptic Application
1.  **Honey/Garlic/Plantain:** Apply as a thin layer directly to the wound or as a poultice, changing dressings daily or every 8-12 hours.
2.  **Saline Irrigation:** Irrigate wounds by pouring or squirting through a small hole in a container.

### Stretcher Assembly (Pole-and-Shirt Example)
1.  Button or zip the jackets closed.
2.  Turn sleeves inside out (push them into the jacket body).
3.  Slide the poles through the sleeves so each pole runs through the sleeves of all jackets.

## Notes
*   **Pre-preparation is essential:** Pre-make at least 20 cloth strips, 10 absorbent pads, and a triangular sling before an emergency arises.
*   **Absorption Comparison:** Sphagnum moss is the best natural option (20x its weight) and naturally antibacterial. Cotton cloth folded 4-6 layers thick is the next-best alternative.
*   **Material Caution:** Avoid synthetic materials like nylon or polyester for bandages as they do not absorb fluids and can stick to wounds.
*   **Suturing Philosophy:** Suturing is a last resort; most wounds heal better open and clean than closed with improvised sutures that trap infection.
*   **Key Takeaway:** Sterilization is more important than sophistication; boiled cotton strips outperform elaborate unsterilized bandages.
*   **Key Takeaway:** Honey, garlic, and plantain are recommended field antiseptics.
*   **Key Takeaway:** Build a pre-built medical supply cache.
