# Infection Prevention

## Objective
Part of First Aid. Stopping the bleeding is only half the battle — keeping a wound clean in the days that follow determines whether you heal or die slowly from sepsis.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Clean water (for irrigation).
- Time (for initial cleaning within the first hour).
- Clean hands (to prevent introducing infection).

## Material
- Water (boiled and cooled, or the cleanest available).
- Raw honey.
- Garlic cloves.
- Spirits above 40% ABV.
- Salt.
- Cloths (boiled and dried fabric preferred).
- Sterilized blade or improvised tweezers (two thin sticks, fire-sterilized thorns).

## System
The infection strategy rests on three pillars in a post-collapse scenario without pharmaceutical antibiotics: cleaning the wound aggressively at the start, keeping it clean while it heals, and recognizing infection early enough to intervene.

## Technology
- Mechanical removal of bacteria and debris via pressurized water flow (Irrigation).
- Osmotic action and hydrogen peroxide production (Raw honey).
- Allicin (from garlic) as a broad-spectrum antimicrobial.
- Mild antimicrobial effects of naturally acidic substances (Sphagnum moss).

## Tools
- Water container or plastic bottle (to create pressure for irrigation).
- Sterilized blade or improvised tweezers (for debridement).
- Cloths (for dressing).

## Process
### Initial Wound Cleaning
1. **Irrigation:** Within the first hour, aggressively clean the wound using pressurized water.
2. **Debridement:** Visually inspect and remove visible dirt, gravel, splinters, and cloth fragments using clean fingers or improvised tweezers.
3. **Debridement (Tissue):** Gently trim away loose dead tissue (gray, white, or black tissue that does not bleed when touched) with a sterilized blade, ensuring deeply embedded objects are stabilized.

### Ongoing Wound Management
1. **Dressing Changes:** Change dressings every 12-24 hours, or immediately if they become wet, dirty, or saturated.
2. **Re-irrigation:** Before applying a new dressing, irrigate the wound again with clean water or boiled salt water.
3. **Antiseptic Reapplication:** Reapply honey or antiseptic with each dressing change.
4. **Dressing Material:** Use the cleanest available cloth; boiled and dried fabric is ideal.
5. **Securing:** Secure dressings firmly enough to stay in place but loose enough to slide a finger underneath.

### Wound Closure Decisions
- **Close the wound if:** The wound is less than 6 hours old, thoroughly cleaned/irrigated, edges are clean-cut, and there is no visible contamination.
- **Leave the wound OPEN if:** It is more than 6-8 hours old, caused by a bite, a puncture wound, visibly contaminated, or shows signs of infection.

### Recognizing Infection
Infection develops on a predictable timeline:
- **12-24 hours:** Increasing pain, mild redness at edges (Normal inflammatory response).
- **24-48 hours:** Redness spreading beyond 2 cm from the wound edge, increasing warmth, swelling (Early infection; increase cleaning frequency).
- **48-72 hours:** Pus (yellow, green, or cloudy discharge), foul smell (Established infection; open the wound if closed).
- **3-5 days:** Red streaks extending from the wound toward the heart (Lymphangitis; life-threatening emergency).
- **5-10 days:** Fever, chills, rapid heart rate, confusion, general malaise (Systemic sepsis; high mortality without antibiotics).

### Emergency Measures for Advancing Infection
If red streaks appear or fever develops:
1. Open the wound completely, removing all sutures or closures.
2. Irrigate aggressively with boiled salt water.
3. Drain abscesses by incising at the lowest point with a sterilized blade.
4. Pack the wound cavity with honey-soaked cloth.
5. Apply a warm, wet compress over the dressing for 20-30 minutes, 3-4 times daily (to increase blood flow and immune cell delivery).
6. Ensure the patient is hydrated and resting.
7. Oral consumption of garlic (3-4 raw cloves daily) offers mild systemic antimicrobial properties if available.

### Hand Hygiene
- Wash hands with soap and water for at least 30 seconds before and after touching any wound.
- Substitute soap with wood ash mixed with water (lye solution) if soap is unavailable.
- Scrub under fingernails.
- Wash hands between patients to avoid cross-contamination.
- Designate clean cloths for wound care only.

## Natural Antiseptics
| Antiseptic | How to Prepare | How to Apply | Effectiveness | 
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | 
| Raw honey | Use unprocessed, unheated honey directly | Spread a 3-5 mm layer over the wound surface | Excellent — osmotic action dehydrates bacteria, hydrogen peroxide production, acidic pH | 
| Garlic poultice | Crush 3-4 fresh cloves, let sit 10 minutes to activate allicin | Apply around (not deep inside) the wound, cover with cloth | Good — allicin is a broad-spectrum antimicrobial. Burns on contact | 
| Dilute alcohol | Spirits above 40% ABV, diluted to roughly 50-70% with clean water | Pour over wound surface — one-time use during initial cleaning | Good for surface bacteria. Damages tissue with repeated use | 
| Yarrow | Crush fresh leaves into a paste | Pack gently into wound | Moderate — anti-inflammatory, mild antimicrobial, promotes clotting | 
| Sphagnum moss | Harvest from clean bogs, squeeze out excess water | Apply as a wound dressing directly over the wound | Good — naturally acidic (pH 3-4), absorbs 20x its weight, antimicrobial | 
| Boiled salt water | 1 teaspoon of salt per 500 ml of boiled water, cooled | Use for irrigation and dressing soaks | Moderate — osmotic effect inhibits bacterial growth |

## Key Takeaways
- Aggressive irrigation within the first hour is the single most effective infection prevention measure (use at least 500 ml of pressurized clean water).
- Honey is the best field-available antiseptic for ongoing wound management (osmotic, acidic, and produces hydrogen peroxide).
- Not every wound should be closed; contaminated, old, or bite wounds heal safer when left open.
- Red streaks extending from a wound toward the heart mean infection has entered the lymphatic system and is a life-threatening emergency.
- Clean hands prevent more infections than any antiseptic applied to the wound itself.

Notes
- Stopping bleeding is only half the battle; keeping a wound clean in the days that follow determines healing or death from sepsis.
- The historical mortality rate for untreated infection reaching the bloodstream exceeded 80% without antibiotics.
- Alcohol and garlic should be used once during initial cleaning, then switch to honey or clean dressings for ongoing care to prevent tissue damage.
- The primary vector for introducing infection is the hands, making hand hygiene critical.
