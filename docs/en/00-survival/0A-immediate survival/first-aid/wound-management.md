# Wound Management | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To teach critical survival skills for wound management in a world without hospitals, emphasizing that every cut, scrape, and puncture is a potential death sentence if it becomes infected. Knowing how to clean, close, and care for wounds is one of the most critical survival skills.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Clean water (for irrigation).
*   Basic tools (fingers, improvised tweezers, water container, needle/thread for sutures).
*   Knowledge of wound assessment criteria.

## Material
*   Water (Boiled and cooled, filtered, or clean running water).
*   Clean cloth (for contact and absorbent layers).
*   Salt (optional for mild saline solution).
*   Raw, unprocessed honey (for antimicrobial properties).
*   Adhesive strips or medical tape.
*   Sterilized needle and thin thread or fishing line (for improvised sutures).

## System
The biological system being managed is the tissue and the body responding to injury and potential infection.

## Technology
The primary 'technology' utilized is the application of correct technique to mitigate the absence of modern medicine (antibiotics).

## Tools
*   Improvised tweezers (two sticks, thorns, or bent wire).
*   Water container with a small hole or squeeze bottle.
*   Clean cloth.
*   Adhesive strips or tape.
*   Sterilized needle and thin thread/dental floss.

## Process

### 1. Wound Assessment
*   **Determine Severity:** Check bleeding severity (life-threatening?), depth (fat, muscle, tendon, bone), location (joints, face, hands/feet), contamination (dirt, debris), and mechanism (bite, crush, cut).

### 2. Wound Cleaning (The Most Important Step)
*   **Water Quality Check:** Use the cleanest water available (Boiled/cooled > Filtered > Clean running > Any clean water).
*   **Irrigation Technique:**
    1. Remove visible debris with clean fingers or improvised tweezers.
    2. Flush the wound with pressure, aiming for at least 250 ml (1 cup) per inch of wound length.
    3. Scrub if necessary for embedded debris.
    4. Flush again after scrubbing.

### 3. Wound Closure
*   **Decision Matrix:**
    *   **Close If:** Clean cut, sharp edges align well, cleaned within 6-8 hours, no infection signs, location benefits from closure (face, hands, joints).
    *   **Leave Open If:** Bite wound, puncture wound, heavily contaminated, older than 8-12 hours, signs of infection present.
*   **Closure Methods:**
    *   Adhesive strips (butterfly bandages): Apply perpendicular, space strips 3-5 mm apart.
    *   Improvised sutures: Use sterilized needle and thin thread, pass needle 5 mm from edge, tie stitches 5-8 mm apart (last resort).
    *   Hair ties: For scalp wounds.

### 4. Dressing and Bandaging
*   **Layers:** Contact layer (clean cloth), Absorbent layer (thicker cloth), Securing layer (bandage/tape).
*   **Maintenance:** Change dressings every 12-24 hours, or sooner if soaked through. Clean gently with each change.

### 5. Recognizing and Treating Infection
*   **Recognition Timeline:**
    *   Early Warning (24-48 hours): Increasing pain, spreading redness.
    *   Moderate Concern (24-72 hours): Warmth, swelling beyond the wound area.
    *   Active Infection (48-96 hours): Pus (yellow/green/gray drainage), red streaks (lymphangitis), fever, chills, foul smell.
*   **Treatment if Infection Develops:**
    1. Open the wound.
    2. Irrigate aggressively with clean water or saline.
    3. Apply warm compresses (clean cloth soaked in warm water) for 20 minutes, 3-4 times daily.
    4. Elevate the injured area.
    5. Seek available medical help.
    6. Apply raw honey directly to the wound if available.

### Special Cases
*   **Puncture Wounds:** Do NOT close. Soak in warm water for 20 minutes to encourage flushing from inside out, watching for tetanus risk.
*   **Animal Bites:** Irrigate immediately and aggressively (at least 5 minutes). Leave open. Risk of rabies.
*   **Burns:** Cool with clean, cool (not cold) water for 10-20 minutes. Do not apply butter, oil, or toothpaste. Do not pop blisters.

## Notes
*   Irrigation is explicitly stated as the single most effective step to prevent infection, superseding the importance of medication or technique.
*   The decision to close a wound is critical; puncture wounds and bite wounds are specifically highlighted as 'Leave Open' cases due to high infection rates.
*   The source emphasizes that infection progresses fast without antibiotics, necessitating early recognition.
