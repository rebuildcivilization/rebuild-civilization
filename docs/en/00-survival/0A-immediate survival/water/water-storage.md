# Water Storage: Safe Long-Term Storage

## Objective
To ensure purified water remains safe during long-term storage by mitigating the risks of recontamination, which is considered the second half of water purification.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Purified water (the foundation).
- Safe storage containers.
- A cool and dark location for storage.
- Proper management of temperature.

## Material
Purified water.

## System
### Recontamination Mechanisms
The three primary mechanisms of recontamination are:
1. Biological regrowth (bacteria/algae spores multiplying).
2. External introduction (pathogens carried by insects, dust, dirty ladles, wind).
3. Chemical leaching (toxins released by the wrong container material).

### Temperature Management
Temperature is the single largest factor, as every 10 degrees Celsius of warming roughly doubles the bacterial growth rate.

### Storage Duration Guidelines
| Condition | Safe Duration |
| :--- | :--- | 
| Sealed glass or food-grade plastic, cool and dark | 2-4 weeks |
| Sealed container, warm environment (25-35C) | 5-7 days |
| Sealed container in direct sunlight | 2-3 days |
| Open container, covered with cloth | 12-24 hours |
| Open container, uncovered | 4-8 hours |
| Clay pot with lid, cool shade | 1-2 weeks |

## Technology
### The 24-Hour Rule
If a sealed, shaded storage environment cannot be guaranteed, all stored water must be re-purified after 24 hours.

### The Wet-Cloth Trick
Wrapping a container in a wet cloth and placing it where wind can reach it pulls heat from the water inside, achieving a cooling effect of 5-10°C below ambient temperature. This requires rewetting the cloth as it dries.

## Tools
### Container Selection
Containers must be sealed tightly (screw cap, cork, fitted lid) to prevent entry of insects, dust, or rain splash.

### Bulk Storage Methods
- **Underground Cistern:** Dig a pit approximately 1 meter deep, line it with clay, plastic sheeting, or stone, and cover with a solid lid. Capacity ranges from 200-500 liters.
- **Barrel or Drum System:** Use scavenged 200-liter food-grade drums, cleaned thoroughly, stacked in shade, elevated on pallets or logs, and fitted with a spigot.
- **Clay Pot Battery:** Use multiple clay pots (10-30 liters each) stored in a shaded, ventilated structure, sealed with beeswax or pine pitch if necessary.

## Process
### Step-by-Step: Storing Water Safely
1. **Start clean:** Wash and rinse the container before filling, rinsing with purified water if soap is unavailable, and swirling the rinse water around interior surfaces.
2. **Fill immediately after purification:** Pour water into the storage container while it is still warm from boiling.
3. **Fill completely:** Minimize air space; leave only 1-2 cm of headspace.
4. **Seal tightly:** Use appropriate sealing mechanisms to prevent entry of contaminants.
5. **Store in shade:** Find the coolest, darkest location available, ideally underground.
6. **Elevate off the ground:** Place containers on shelves or rocks to prevent ground contact heat transfer.
7. **Label or mark:** Use a system to track fill dates; always use the oldest water first.

### Rotation and Maintenance
- **Use-first rule:** Always drink from the oldest container first.
- **Inspection schedule:** Every 2-3 days, check for cloudiness, color change, unusual smell, or slime.
- **Container cleaning:** Scrub interior surfaces with sand and water when emptying, and thoroughly rinse before refilling to remove biofilm.

### Emergency Indicators: When to Re-Purify
| Sign | Likely Cause | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Green tint or green film | Algae growth (light exposure) | Filter and boil |
| Cloudy when it was clear | Bacterial bloom | Boil vigorously |
| Sour or musty smell | Anaerobic bacterial activity | Boil; discard if smell persists |
| Slimy container walls | Biofilm formation | Scrub container, boil water |
| Floating particles | Mold, insect contamination | Filter, then boil |
| Tastes metallic or chemical | Container leaching | Discard; switch containers |

## Seasonal Considerations
- **Summer:** Storage life is shortest. Prioritize underground storage and evaporative cooling.
- **Winter:** Cold temperatures significantly extend storage life; prevent freezing only if the container can withstand expansion (glass, rigid plastic).
- **Rainy season:** Humidity promotes mold on exteriors; inspect lids frequently; use rain to facilitate frequent rotation.

## Key Takeaways
- Storage is the second half of purification.
- Seal tightly, store in the coolest/darkest location, and minimize air space.
- The 24-hour rule is critical for unguaranteed storage environments.
- Temperature management is primary; every 10°C of warming doubles bacterial growth.
- Rotation (using the oldest water first) and container cleaning are mandatory.
- Trust your senses for emergency indicators.

Notes
- The source text explicitly states that purifying water solves only half the problem; storage management is equally critical.
- The key takeaway emphasizes that recontamination is as effective as never purifying at all.
- The process includes a crucial step (Step 2) to pour water into the storage container while it is still warm from boiling to help sterilize the container walls.
