# Dew Point Reading

## Objective
To provide practical dew point estimation without instruments and explain why dew point matters more than humidity for weather prediction.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Equipment for measurement (for the Metal Cup Method):
    - Polished metal cup or can (shiny surface)
    - Thermometer (any type that reads to 1 degree)
    - Cold water source (stream, well, ice if available)
    - Something to stir with

## Material
- Dew Point:
    - The temperature at which air becomes saturated and water begins to condense.
- Relative Humidity:
    - A measure that is misleading when comparing moisture content across different temperatures.
- Dew Point Spread (or depression):
    - The difference between air temperature and dew point, used as a primary rain prediction tool.

## System
- Moisture Prediction Model:
    - Dew point provides an absolute measure of moisture content, making it the single most useful number for weather prediction at ground level.

## Technology
- Differential Humidity Measurement:
    - The system relies on the fact that relative humidity changes with temperature, whereas dew point remains constant unless moisture is added or removed.

## Tools
- Metal Cup Method:
    - A physical method using a metal cup to observe condensation.
- Observable Indicators (for estimation without instruments):
    - Breath visibility
    - Surface condensation timing
    - Grass and metal comparison
    - Fog formation

## Process
### The Metal Cup Method
1. Fill the cup half full with room-temperature water.
2. Place the thermometer in the water.
3. Slowly add cold water (or ice) while stirring constantly.
4. Watch the outside of the cup carefully.
5. Read the thermometer the instant fog or condensation forms on the cup’s exterior. That temperature is the dew point.

### Estimating Dew Point Without Instruments
- **Breath visibility:** Check when exhaled breath becomes visible (approaching 35C).
- **Surface condensation timing:** Note when dew begins forming; earlier formation means a higher dew point.
- **Grass and metal comparison:** Observe if dew forms on metal but not grass to determine moderate dew point.
- **Fog formation:** Ground fog near the overnight low indicates a dew point near the overnight low.

### Dew Point Spread Interpretation
- **Over 15C:** Very dry, no precipitation likely.
- **10-15C:** Fair weather, low rain chance.
- **5-10C:** Clouds forming, rain possible.
- **2-5C:** Rain likely within hours.
- **Under 2C:** Fog, drizzle, or rain imminent.
- **Critical Rule:** When the spread is narrowing (dew point rising or temperature falling), precipitation is approaching. When it is widening, conditions are clearing.

## Notes
- **Humidity vs. Dew Point:** Relative humidity is misleading because 50% humidity at 30C contains more water than 50% humidity at 10C. The dew point eliminates this temperature dependency.
- **Practical Applications:**
    - *Agriculture:* Irrigate when spread is wide; spray fungicides when spread is narrow; harvest grain when spread is wide.
    - *Construction/Crafts:* Avoid painting or drying when dew point is within 3C of air temperature to prevent adhesion failure.
    - *Fire Management:* Wide spread means low fuel moisture (high fire risk); narrow spread means damp fuel (lower wildfire risk).
    - *Health:* Dew points above 20C reduce the body’s ability to cool through sweat evaporation; above 24C is dangerous for sustained outdoor work.
- **Long-Term Tracking:** Building a dew point log by tracking readings at the same time (dawn) helps in pattern recognition regarding local moisture trends and wind direction.
