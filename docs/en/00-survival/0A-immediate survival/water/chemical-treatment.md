# Chemical Treatment | Survival Wiki

## Objective
Chemical disinfection uses oxidizing agents to kill pathogens in water when boiling is not possible, providing a fast alternative that requires no fire, no equipment, and almost no effort.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   A supply of disinfecting agents (chlorine, iodine, potassium permanganate).
*   Pre-filtering for cloudy water is necessary because chemicals cannot reach pathogens hiding behind sediment particles.
*   For cold water (below 10°C), double the dose and double the contact time.

## Material
*   **Chlorine (Sodium Hypochlorite / Bleach):** Ordinary household bleach (5-8% sodium hypochlorite).
*   **Calcium Hypochlorite (Pool Shock):** Concentrated calcium hypochlorite granules (65-73%).
*   **Iodine:** Tincture or povidone-iodine.
*   **Potassium Permanganate (KMnO4):** Crystals.

## System
Chemical disinfection is a method of water purification that employs oxidizing agents to destroy pathogens.

## Technology
*   **Oxidizing Agents:** The core technology used to kill pathogens (chlorine, iodine, potassium permanganate).
*   **SODIS:** Solar Disinfection method (used as a secondary kill step).
*   **Boiling:** The gold standard, serving as the baseline comparison for chemical treatment.

## Tools
*   Timer (ideal for setting contact time).
*   Cloth, bandana, or improvised sand filter (for pre-filtering).

## Process
**Field Procedure Steps:**
1.  **Assess the water source:** Determine clarity and temperature to dictate the dose and contact time.
2.  **Pre-filter if needed:** Pour water through a cloth or filter to remove visible particles.
3.  **Measure your dose:** Use the provided tables based on water clarity and temperature.
4.  **Add chemical to water and mix thoroughly:** Shake vigorously or stir for 30 seconds to ensure contact.
5.  **Treat the threads (if using a bottle):** Tip the bottle to let treated water run over the threads and rim.
6.  **Wait the full contact time:** This step is non-negotiable.
7.  **Smell/taste check (chlorine only):** If no smell, the dose was insufficient; retreat.

**Chemical Specific Dosing Examples:**

**Chlorine (Liquid Household Bleach 5-8%):**
| Water Clarity | Dose per Liter | Dose per Gallon | Contact Time |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Clear water | 2 drops | 8 drops | 30 minutes |
| Cloudy water | 4 drops | 16 drops | 60 minutes |
| Very cold water (<10°C) | 4 drops | 16 drops | 60 minutes |

**Calcium Hypochlorite (Pool Shock, 65-73%):**
*   **Stock Solution:** Dissolve 1 level teaspoon (approx. 7g) in 2 liters of water (0.35% chlorine).
*   **Treatment Dose:** Add 5 ml (1 teaspoon) of stock solution per liter of drinking water.
*   **Contact Time:** 30-60 minutes.

**Iodine (Tincture 2%):**
*   Clear water: 5 drops for 30 minutes.
*   Cloudy/cold water: 10 drops for 60 minutes.

**Potassium Permanganate (KMnO4):**
*   **Dosing Rule:** Add crystals one at a time until the water turns light pink. Purple or dark pink means too much.
*   **Contact Time:** 30 minutes for clear water at this concentration.

## What Chemicals Cannot Do
*   **Does not remove sediment:** Chemicals disinfect but do not clarify the water.
*   **Does not remove chemical pollutants:** Pesticides, heavy metals, and fuel contamination are unaffected.
*   **Cryptosporidium resistance:** Oocysts resist chlorine and iodine at normal doses.
*   **Temperature dependent:** Cold water slows the reaction, necessitating double dose and double contact time below 10°C.
*   **Turbidity reduces effectiveness:** Particles shield pathogens, requiring pre-filtering.

## Notes
*   **Shelf Life:** Liquid bleach degrades roughly 20% per year at room temperature, potentially losing potency after 2 years. Calcium hypochlorite powder stores for 10+ years if kept dry and is superior for long-term stockpiling.
*   **Iodine Limitations:** Do not use for more than 3 consecutive weeks, as prolonged intake can disrupt thyroid function. Pregnant women and those with thyroid conditions should avoid it.
*   **Taste Neutralization:** After contact time, adding vitamin C (ascorbic acid) powder can neutralize the medicinal taste and residual iodine.
*   **Safety Protocol:** The safest protocol involves pre-filtering, chemical treatment with full contact time, and optionally a second kill step like SODIS or boiling.
*   **Improvisation:** Sources to look for include pool supply sheds, municipal water treatment plants, and veterinary clinics.
