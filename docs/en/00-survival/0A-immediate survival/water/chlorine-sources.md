# Chlorine Sources | Survival Wiki

## Objective
This article catalogs every practical source of chlorine compounds for water disinfection — where to find them, how to assess potency, correct dosing for each form, and long-term storage to maintain a reliable supply.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- For manufacturing chlorine post-collapse: Salt (sodium chloride), Water, and a DC power source (battery, solar panel, hand-crank generator).
- For Calcium Hypochlorite: Must make a stock solution first before treating drinking water to ensure correct concentration.
- For long-term storage: Containers must be cool, dry, and dark.

## Material
*   Household bleach (liquid)
*   Concentrated bleach
*   Pool shock (granular: Calcium hypochlorite, Sodium dichloro-s-triazinetrione)
*   Bleach tablets (Sodium hypochlorite, Sodium dichloroisocyanurate)
*   Bleach powder (Calcium hypochlorite)
*   Municipal treatment chemicals (Chlorine gas, sodium hypochlorite, Ca(OCl)2)
*   Salt (sodium chloride)
*   Water

## System
All chlorine disinfection works through the same chemistry: hypochlorous acid (HOCl) forms when chlorine compounds dissolve in water. This acid oxidizes cell membranes and destroys microbial DNA.

## Technology
- **Disinfection Mechanism:** Hypochlorous acid (HOCl) oxidation of cell membranes and microbial DNA.
- **Manufacturing Method:** Electrolysis of salt water to produce sodium hypochlorite at the anode and hydrogen gas at the cathode.

## Tools
*   Dropper or measuring spoon (for standard dosing).
*   Scent test (to assess liquid bleach potency).
*   Testing for faint chlorine smell in treated water (to verify dose effectiveness).
*   Sealed, cool, dry, and dark containers (for long-term storage).

## Process
### Chlorine Compound Reference
Different chlorine products are delivery vehicles for generating HOCl.

### Dosing by Source
**Liquid Household Bleach (Standard 5-8% NaOCl):
*   Clear, room temperature: 2 drops/liter (8 drops/gallon), Contact Time: 30 minutes.
*   Cloudy or cold (< 10°C): 4 drops/liter (16 drops/gallon), Contact Time: 60 minutes.
*   Very turbid (pre-filter first!): 4 drops/liter (16 drops/gallon), Contact Time: 60 minutes.
*   Verification: Water must smell faintly of chlorine after contact time. If not, add 2 more drops/liter and wait 15 minutes. Repeat if necessary.

**Concentrated Bleach (8.25%):
*   Clear, room temperature: 2 drops/liter, Contact Time: 30 minutes.
*   Cloudy or cold: 3 drops/liter, Contact Time: 60 minutes.

**Calcium Hypochlorite (Pool Shock):
*   Stock Solution Preparation (0.35% chlorine):
    1. Dissolve 1 level teaspoon (~7g) of granular calcium hypochlorite in 2 liters of clean water.
*   Treating Drinking Water (Stock Solution):
    *   Clear: 5 ml (1 teaspoon) per liter, Contact Time: 30 minutes.
    *   Cloudy or cold: 10 ml (2 teaspoons) per liter, Contact Time: 60 minutes.
*   Stock Solution Shelf Life: Use within 1-2 weeks.

**Water Purification Tablets (NaDCC):
*   Dosing Rule: Follow package instructions; tablets are formulated for specific volumes (typically 1L or 5L per tablet).
*   General Rule: One 1L tablet provides approximately 3-5 mg/L of free chlorine, within the effective range.

### Assessing Bleach Potency in the Field
*   **Smell Test:** Sniff the bottle from arm’s length. A sharp chlorine smell indicates meaningful potency.
*   **Dose-and-Check Method:** Treat 1 liter of water with the standard dose. Wait 30 minutes. If faint chlorine smell is present, the bleach works.
*   **Degradation Timeline (Liquid Bleach, Room Temperature):** Fresh (< 3 months) is 95-100%; 6 months is ~80%; 1 year is ~60-70%; 2 years is ~40-50%; 3+ years is unreliable.

### Priority Scavenge Locations
1. Pool supply stores and sections (highest value for Calcium Hypochlorite).
2. Hardware stores (Pool chemicals section, well treatment supplies).
3. Municipal water treatment plants (Industrial quantities).
4. Janitorial supply closets (Standard bleach bottles).
5. Household kitchens and laundry rooms (Most accessible).

### Long-Term Storage of Chlorine
*   **Calcium Hypochlorite (Dry Granules):** Store in the original sealed container. Keep cool, dry, and dark. Avoid organic materials, acids, ammonia, or petroleum products.
*   **Liquid Bleach:** Store in the original opaque bottle. Keep cool and dark. Useful for 6-12 months.
*   **Water Purification Tablets:** Store in original sealed packaging or waterproof containers. Shelf life 3-5 years typical. Once opened, use promptly due to faster degradation.

## Notes
- **Do NOT Use These Sources:** Scented bleach, splash-less or thick bleach, color-safe bleach (contains hydrogen peroxide, not chlorine), and bleach with added cleaners (contain surfactants and other chemicals).
- **Fire/Explosion Risk:** Calcium hypochlorite is an oxidizer. It can cause spontaneous combustion if it contacts organic materials (oil, grease, cloth, wood) or acids.
- **Key Takeaway:** Chlorine provides residual disinfection, making it superior to boiling or UV for stored/transported water.
- **Manufacturing Note:** Electrolysis produces sodium hypochlorite and hydrogen gas; the solution concentration is unpredictable without testing.
