# Well Digging: Hand-Dug Water Wells

## Objective
To build a hand-dug well is one of the most important infrastructure projects a post-collapse community can undertake. It provides reliable, protected groundwater access that can serve dozens of people for decades.

**Benefits of a Well:**
*   **Volume:** 200-2,000+ liters per day, depending on the aquifer.
*   **Protection:** A lined, capped well prevents surface contamination from entering the water supply.
*   **Reliability:** Wells reach below seasonal water table fluctuations, providing water year-round.
*   **Community scale:** A single well can serve 50-200 people.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
**Site Selection Prerequisites:**
1.  **Geological Suitability:** Start with indicators from Ground Sources (vegetation, terrain, test holes) to suggest shallow groundwater.
2.  **Test Pit:** Dig a 1-meter test pit. The site is viable if water or very damp soil is hit within 1–2 meters; if dry at 1.5 meters, move on.
3.  **Soil Assessment:** The ideal profile digs through soil and sand/gravel to reach an aquifer, with enough clay or stable material in the walls to prevent collapse.
4.  **Contamination Avoidance:** Select a site slightly elevated to prevent surface runoff. Minimum distances required:
    *   Latrine/outhouse: 30 meters (well must be uphill).
    *   Animal enclosure: 30 meters (well must be uphill).
    *   Garbage or compost: 50 meters (well must be uphill).
    *   Cemetery: 50 meters (well must be uphill).
    *   Flooded area/swamp: 15 meters.
    *   Building foundations: 5 meters (prevent structural undermining).

**Safety Prerequisites:**
*   **Lining:** Must be lined before construction begins.
*   **Air Quality Test:** Before entering wells deeper than 3 meters, test the air with a candle. If the flame dims or goes out, do not enter.
*   **Ventilation:** If the candle test fails, ventilate by fanning for 30 minutes before retesting. For wells deeper than 5 meters, continuous ventilation is required.

## Material
**Lining Materials (Best to Acceptable):**
| Material | Advantages | Disadvantages |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Fired brick or stone with morite | Strongest, longest-lasting, best contamination barrier | Requires brick-making or stone-cutting capability |
| Dry-stacked stone | Strong, no mortar needed, natural material | Takes skill to fit tightly; gaps allow some soil entry |
| Timber cribbing (log rings) | Fast to build, strong when new | Rots within 5-15 years depending on wood species; must be replaced |
| Woven bamboo or wicker rings | Lightweight, easy to build | Short lifespan (2-5 years), not a contamination barrier |
| No lining | — | Well will collapse. |

## System
**Water Yield System:**
*   A properly constructed hand-dug well provides reliable, protected groundwater access.
*   The aquifer zone includes a deeper sump (roughly 50 cm deeper than the floor) to act as a collection point and increase yield.
*   **Yield Estimation:** Sustainable daily yield is measured by the overnight recovery volume after bailing the well dry.

**Contamination Control System:**
*   The wellhead (above-ground structure) is the most critical contamination barrier.
*   The wellhead must extend at least 50 cm (ideally 80 cm) above ground level.
*   An apron (concrete, stone, or packed-clay platform) extending 1-2 meters in all directions must be built to divert spilled water and rain.
*   A drainage channel must be installed from the apron, leading at least 5 meters away from the well.

**Safety System (Air Quality):**
*   A candle test serves as the initial check for oxygen depletion (CO2/methane).
*   Continuous ventilation (fanning or a cloth chute) is necessary for wells deeper than 5 meters to displace oxygen.
*   A hauling mechanism (windlass or pulley) prevents contamination from handling the rope.

## Technology
- Hand tools (shovels, picks, mattocks, sharpened hardwood digging sticks).
- Rope: Strong cordage, at least twice the planned depth of the well.
- Hauling System components: Simple windlass, tripod, or cross-beam.
- Lining components: Stones, bricks, fired clay rings, or timber.

## Tools
**Essential Tools:**
*   Digging tools: shovels, picks, mattocks, or sharpened hardwood digging sticks.
*   Buckets or baskets for hauling excavated material.
*   Rope: Strong cordage, at least twice the planned depth of the well.
*   A simple windlass, tripod, or cross-beam for lifting buckets from depth.

**Highly Recommended Tools:**
*   Chisels (metal or hardened stone) for breaking through compacted material or rock.
*   A ladder or footholds carved into the wall for entering and exiting.

## Process
**Phase 1: Planning and Layout**
1.  Mark a circle on the ground with a diameter of 1.0-1.5 meters.
2.  Clear all vegetation and loose topsoil from a 3-meter radius around the well.
3.  Set up the hauling system (e.g., tripod with a rope and bucket, or a horizontal log over the opening).

**Phase 2: Digging**
1.  Begin excavating within the marked circle, keeping walls as vertical as possible.
2.  Remove all loose material from the bottom before deepening.
3.  Work in shifts (one person digging at the bottom, one or two people hauling at the top), rotating every 30-60 minutes.
4.  When encountering the water table, continue deepening at least 1-2 meters below the first water entry.
5.  In the aquifer zone, dig an additional sump, roughly 50 cm deeper than the floor.

**Phase 3: Lining the Well**
1.  Dig in 1-meter increments.
2.  Line the walls in 1-meter increments after each meter of depth.
3.  For stone lining: build a ring of fitted stones, packing clay or mud behind the stones to fill gaps.
4.  For timber cribbing: build a square or hexagonal frame from logs at each 1-meter level, stacking frames and filling gaps with packed clay.
5.  The lining must extend from the bottom of the well to at least 0.5 meters above ground level.

**Phase 4: Wellhead Construction**
1.  Extend the lining at least 50 cm (ideally 80 cm) above ground level.
2.  Build an apron: a concrete, stone, or packed-clay platform extending 1-2 meters in all directions, sloped away from the well.
3.  Install a cover: A removable wooden or stone lid to keep out animals, insects, leaves, and children.
4.  Build a drainage channel from the apron leading at least 5 meters away from the well.
5.  Install a permanent hauling mechanism (windlass or pulley) if possible.

## Notes
**Key Takeaways and Observations:**
*   **Collapse Hazard:** The most common well-digging deaths come from cave-ins; thus, lining must happen as you dig, not after.
*   **Air Quality is Deadliest:** Carbon dioxide and methane displace oxygen. The candle test is critical for wells over 3 meters. Rescue protocol dictates ventilate first before descending.
*   **Labor Investment:** A team of 3-4 people may spend 1-4 weeks reaching 5-15 meters.
*   **Maintenance Schedule:**
    *   Check and repair cover: Weekly.
    *   Clean apron and drainage: Monthly.
    *   Inspect lining: Every 6 months.
    *   Disinfect the well: Annually or after contamination.
    *   Replace timber lining: Every 5-15 years.
*   **Yield Measurement:** Sustainable yield is determined by measuring the overnight recovery volume after bailing the well dry.
