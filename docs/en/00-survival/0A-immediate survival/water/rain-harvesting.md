# Rain Harvesting: Catchment and Storage

## Objective
To secure water supply by catching, storing, and purifying natural rainfall, making the cleanest naturally occurring water source a reliable resource.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Catchment System:** Having gutters, a screened downspout, and sufficient covered storage in place before rain arrives.
*   **First-Flush Diverter:** A mechanism to discard the initial dirty runoff.
*   **Storage Sizing:** Containers must be sized to bridge the longest expected dry period.
*   **Hygiene:** Every storage vessel must be covered and screened to prevent disease.

## Material
*   **Catchment Surfaces:** Metal, tile, thatch, plastic sheeting, wood, concrete, clay, compacted earth, or paint (safe). 
*   **Gutter Materials:** Split bamboo (8+ cm diameter), hollowed logs, bent sheet metal, PVC pipe, bark.
*   **Sealing Agents:** Pine pitch, beeswax, clay, strips of plastic.
*   **Storage Vessels:** Tank, barrel, cistern, lined pit.
*   **Screening Materials:** Mesh, cloth, woven basket, woven wire, woven bamboo strips.

## System
### Permanent Catchment System Components
| Component | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | 
| Roof surface | Collects rain over a large area |
| Gutters | Channels water from roof edge to downspout |
| Downspout | Carries water from gutter to storage |
| First-flush diverter | Discards first dirty water |
| Screen/filter | Removes leaves and debris |
| Storage vessel | Holds collected water |

### Community/Ground Catchment Options
*   **Ground catchments:** Pave or line a sloped area of ground (concrete, clay, compacted earth, or plastic sheeting) to channel runoff into a central cistern.
*   **Multiple roof networks:** Connect gutters from several buildings to a shared storage tank via a common drainage line.
*   **Subsurface cisterns:** Dig a large pit, line it with clay, stone, or concrete, and cover it to prevent evaporation and contamination.

## Technology
### The Math of Rainwater
**Formula:** Liters collected = Catchment area (m2) x Rainfall (mm) x Efficiency factor

**Efficiency Factor:** Accounts for splash loss, evaporation, and first-flush diversion. Use 0.8 for a well-designed system, or 0.5-0.6 for a rough improvised system.

**Rainfall Calculation Examples:**
| Catchment Area | 10 mm Rain | 25 mm Rain | 50 mm Rain |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 5 m2 (small tarp) | 40 L | 100 L | 200 L |
| 10 m2 (medium roof) | 80 L | 200 L | 400 L |
| 25 m2 (large roof) | 200 L | 500 L | 1,000 L |
| 50 m2 (building roof) | 400 L | 1,000 L | 2,000 L |

### Storage Calculation
*   **Daily Water Need:** Minimum survival: 3 L/person/day. Comfortable: 10-15 L/person/day. Community with agriculture: 50+ L/person/day.
*   **Dry Period Identification:** Determine the longest expected dry period (e.g., 2-4 weeks in temperate climates).
*   **Calculation:** Multiply Daily Need by the duration of the dry spell.

### Water Quality Rules
*   **Covering:** Every storage vessel must have a tight-fitting lid or cover.
*   **Darkness:** Use opaque containers or cover transparent ones to prevent algae growth.
*   **Screening:** Fine mesh over every opening prevents insects from laying eggs.

## Process
### Improvised Catchment: Fast Setup
1.  **Find the largest waterproof sheet:** Tarp, plastic sheeting, poncho, split trash bags, or vehicle hood.
2.  **Create a collection point:** Drape the tarp so all water flows to one corner or edge. Use sticks, poles, or rope to create a slope to reduce splash loss.
3.  **Position a container at the low point:** Place a bucket, pot, barrel, or clean trash can.
4.  **Stabilize against wind:** Anchor the tarp edges with rocks, stakes, or by tying to trees.
5.  **Divert the first flush:** Let the initial 1-2 minutes of rain wash dust off the surface before redirecting the flow into storage.

### Building Gutters From Scratch
1.  **Measure your roof edge:** The gutter runs along the lower edge (eave) and must slope slightly (1-2 cm drop per meter) toward the downspout end.
2.  **Choose your gutter material** and shape it accordingly.
3.  **Mount the gutter:** Attach it to the roof edge using wooden brackets, lashed supports, or bent wire hangers. The lip must sit just below the edge.
4.  **Seal joints:** Seal where sections meet with pine pitch, beeswax, clay, or plastic strips.
5.  **Install a leaf screen:** Place a coarse mesh over the gutter entrance or at the downspout to prevent debris.

### The First-Flush Diverter
**Simple Standpipe Diverter Process:**
1.  **Install a vertical pipe (standpipe)** at the base of the downspout. Calculate volume: 1-2 liters per square meter of roof area (e.g., 10 m2 needs a 10-20 liter standpipe).
2.  **Connect the downspout** to the top of the standpipe.
3.  **Install a slow-draining outlet** (hole, loose plug, or drip valve) at the base of the standpipe.
4.  **Connect overflow to storage:** Add a T-junction or overflow pipe to direct water to storage once the standpipe is full.

**Manual Alternative Process:**
1.  Leave the downspout disconnected from storage at the start of the rain event.
2.  Watch the water color; when it runs clear (after 2-5 minutes on a clean roof), manually redirect the flow into the storage container.

## Notes
*   **Water Source Purity:** Rain is the cleanest naturally occurring water source because it has not passed through soil contaminated with animal waste, industrial chemicals, or parasites.
*   **Mosquito Prevention Criticality:** An uncovered water container can produce hundreds of mosquitoes per week; screening and covering EVERY container is non-negotiable in post-collapse scenarios.
*   **Safe Catchment Surfaces:** Bare metal, clean plastic, tile, slate, and wood are safe; avoid asphalt shingle roofs, lead-painted surfaces, and heavily corroded galvanized metal.
*   **Storage Rotation:** Practice first-in, first-out rotation to ensure the oldest water is used first.
