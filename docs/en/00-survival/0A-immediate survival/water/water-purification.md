# Water Purification

## Objective
To kill all bacteria, viruses, and parasites in water to ensure survival, as dirty water can kill within roughly three days without water.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Fire (needed for boiling method).
- A reliable water source.

## Material
- Containers (metal, clay pot, single-wall steel bottle).
- Cloth, bandana, or t-shirt (for pre-filtering).
- Sand (fine-grained).
- Gravel or small pebbles.
- Charcoal from a hardwood fire (NOTite briquettes).
- Clear plastic sheet or PET plastic bottle (for solar still and SODIS).
- Clear PET plastic bottle (not colored).

## System
Water Purification employs several methods, including Boiling, Filtration, Solar Still (Evaporation/Condensation), and UV Treatment (SODIS).

## Technology
- **Heat-based Disinfection (Boiling):** Uses high heat to kill pathogens.
- **Ultraviolet Radiation (SODIS):** Uses the sun’s ultraviolet radiation to kill pathogens.
- **Evaporation and Condensation (Solar Still):** Uses solar energy to extract moisture from soil or water.
- **Adsorption (Charcoal Filtration):** Charcoal adsorbs chemicals and many organic contaminants.

## Tools
- Fire (to boil water).
- A metal container, clay pot, or steel bottle.
- Plastic bottle, birch bark tube, or hollow log (for filter setup).
- Digging tool (for solar still).
- Small rock or weight (for solar still).
- Clear plastic sheet or tarp (for solar still).

## Process
### Water Source Finding (In order of preference)
1. Springs (cleanest natural source).
2. Fast-moving streams (better than still water; look upstream).
3. Rainwater (collected directly, needs to run off to wash dust).
4. Morning dew (drag cloth through grass at dawn).
5. Lakes and ponds.
6. Transpiration bags (tie plastic bag over leafy branches).
7. Snow and ice (MELT FIRST, then purify).

### Water Purification Methods

**Method 1: Boiling (Most Reliable)**
1. Collect water from the cleanest source available (flowing water preferred).
2. If cloudy, pre-filter through cloth to remove sediment.
3. Pour water into a container and place it over a strong fire.
4. Bring to a full, rolling boil (aggressive bubbles).
5. Maintain a rolling boil for at least **1 full minute** (3 minutes above 2,000 meters altitude).
6. Remove from heat and store in a clean container with a cover.

**Method 2: Sand, Gravel, and Charcoal Filter**
1. Prepare the filter vessel (plastic bottle cut at the bottom, neck pointing down, plug with cloth).
2. Layer materials from bottom (neck) to top:
    1. Cloth or grass plug.
    2. Fine charcoal (5-8 cm layer).
    3. Fine sand (5-8 cm layer).
    4. Coarse sand or fine gravel (5-8 cm layer).
    5. Larger gravel or pebbles (5-8 cm layer).
3. Pour water through the top. Run water through 2-3 times for best results.
4. Replace the charcoal layer every few days or when taste changes.

**Method 3: Solar Still (Emergency Water from the Ground)**
1. Dig a bowl-shaped hole (approx. 60 cm deep, 90 cm across) and place moist soil or vegetation in the bottom.
2. Place the collection container in the center.
3. Stretch a clear plastic sheet over the hole and seal edges with rocks/soil to create an airtight seal.
4. Place a small rock in the center to form an inverted cone over the container.
5. Wait for condensation to drip into the container. Run a drinking tube to sip water.

**Method 4: UV Treatment (SODIS — Solar Disinfection)**
1. Use a clear PET plastic bottle, removing labels.
2. Fill the bottle, pre-filter if cloudy.
3. Shake vigorously for 20 seconds to oxygenate, then cap tightly.
4. Lay the bottle on its side on a reflective surface in direct sunlight.
5. Leave for **6 hours in full sun**, or **2 full days if overcast**.

### Best Practice Sequence
The best sequence for purification is: pre-filter through cloth, then run through the sand/charcoal filter, then boil.

## Common Mistakes
| Mistake | Why It’s Dangerous | What to Do Instead |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Drinking from clear-looking streams without treatment | Clear water can still contain giardia, crypto, and bacteria | Always purify, no matter how clean it looks |
| Not boiling long enough | Some pathogens survive brief heating | Full rolling boil for 1 minute minimum |
| Eating snow directly | Lowers core body temperature, accelerates hypothermia, and snow may contain contaminants | Melt it first, then boil or treat |
| Drinking from stagnant pools | Standing water breeds mosquito larvae, algae, and bacteria | Use only as a last resort, always filter AND boil |
| Using the charcoal filter alone | Filters improve water but don’t guarantee pathogen removal | Always combine filtering with boiling or UV |
| Collecting water downstream of settlements | Human and animal waste flows downstream | Walk upstream of any camps, farms, or animal areas |

## Notes
- **Rule of 3:** The survival benchmark is approximately 3 days without water.
- **Boiling Altitude Adjustment:** Boil for 3 minutes above 2,000 meters (6,500 feet).
- **SODIS Reliability:** If the sky is more than 50% cloudy, SODIS is unreliable; boiling is the preferred alternative.
- **Pre-filtering Importance:** Pre-filtering cloudy water before boiling or filtering makes the subsequent steps more effective.
- **Solar Still Efficiency:** Output is low (0.5-1 liter per day), but the water produced is distilled and clean.
- **Best Combination:** The most reliable purification sequence is to pre-filter through cloth, then run through the sand/charcoal filter, and finally boil.
- **Water Quality Check:** Flowing water is preferred over still water; upstream is preferred over downstream.
- **Post-Purification Storage:** Store purified water in a clean container with a cover to prevent recontamination.
