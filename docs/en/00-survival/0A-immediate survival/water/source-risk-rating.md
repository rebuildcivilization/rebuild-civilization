# Source Risk Rating

## Objective
To rate water sources on a five-level risk scale and match the appropriate treatment to the source's contamination profile to save fuel, filter life, and time.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

*   Senses (for sensory evaluation).
*   Ability to survey the area within 200 meters upstream and uphill.
*   Community stabilization (for building and maintaining a source map).

## Material

Every water source carries a different contamination profile, which includes pathogen load and chemical contamination potential, along with seasonal variability.

## System

**The Five Risk Categories:**

| Risk Level | Category | Examples | Treatment Required |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 - Low | Protected underground | Deep wells (>15m), capped springs | Minimal — filter for sediment |
| 2 - Moderate | Moving surface water, remote | Mountain streams above habitation, rainfall collection | Standard — filter + boil or chemical treat |
| 3 - Elevated | Surface water, populated area | Rivers below settlements, shallow wells near agriculture | Aggressive — sediment settle + filter + boil |
| 4 - High | Stagnant or contaminated | Ponds, swamps, water near latrines or graves | Maximum — multiple treatment stages required |
| 5 - Extreme | Industrial or unknown chemical | Water near factories, refineries, mines, discolored water | Avoid if any alternative exists |

**Chemical Contamination Rule:** Boiling concentrates heavy metals and industrial chemicals; these cannot be removed by boiling or standard filtration.

## Technology

*   Sensory evaluation (Color, Smell, Taste, Surface).
*   Laboratory analysis (implied necessity to validate sensory findings).

## Tools

*   Cloth filter for sediment.
*   Rolling boil apparatus.
*   Coagulation agents (alum/moringa seed).
*   Community source map.

## Process

### Step-by-Step Source Assessment

**Step 1: Observe the Surroundings**
Survey the area within 200 meters upstream and uphill for red flags (human/animal remains, latrine pits, agricultural runoff, industrial buildings, algae blooms, dead animals, absence of aquatic life).

**Step 2: Assess Water Characteristics**
Examine water using senses. High risk indicators include brown/green/milky color, sulfur/chemical/sewage/sweet/fruity smell, oily sheen, metallic/bitter/soapy/sweet taste, and heavy sediment.

**Step 3: Evaluate Flow Dynamics**
*   **Springs emerging from rock:** Lowest pathogen risk (Level 1 if capped, Level 2 if exposed).
*   **Fast-flowing streams:** UV and oxygenation reduce pathogen survival (Level 2 in remote areas, Level 3 near habitation).
*   **Slow rivers:** Pathogens survive longer; sediment carries contaminants (Level 3 minimum).
*   **Standing water (ponds, puddles):** Pathogen breeding grounds (Level 4).
*   **Rainwater:** Relatively clean if collected properly (Level 2).

**Step 4: Consider Seasonal and Weather Factors**
*   **After heavy rain/snowmelt:** All surface sources increase by one risk level due to runoff contamination and turbidity.
*   **Late summer/drought:** Stagnant pools form, increasing pathogen concentration.
*   **Winter:** Frozen surface may trap gases, but deep cold slows pathogen reproduction.

**Step 5: Check for Upstream Activity**
Reconnaissance upstream can reveal contamination sources. Key minimum safe distances:

| Contamination Source | Minimum Safe Distance |
| :--- | :--- |
| Latrine/outhouse | 30 meters downhill, 60 meters on flat ground |
| Animal pen/pasture | 60 meters |
| Grave or remains | 60 meters |
| Agricultural field | 100 meters |
| Road or vehicle wreckage | 50 meters (fuel/oil leakage) |
| Industrial site | 500+ meters — consider avoiding entirely |

**Treatment Requirements by Risk Level**

| Risk Level | Minimum Treatment | Recommended Treatment |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Level 1** | Cloth filter for sediment | Filter through sand/charcoal for taste |
| **Level 2** | Rolling boil for 1 minute (3 min above 2000m) | Filter + boil, or filter + chemical (chlorine/iodine) |
| **Level 3** | Settle 2+ hours, filter, then boil | Settle + coagulate (alum/moringa seed) + filter + boil |
| **Level 4** | Settle + coagulate + filter + boil + solar disinfect if possible | Double treatment: boil AND chemical treat after filtration |
| **Level 5** | Do not use | Seek alternative source; distillation only as absolute last resort |

**Building a Source Map**
Document location, risk rating (reassess seasonally), flow reliability, volume, and treatment notes.

## Notes

*   **Key Takeaway:** Rate every water source on a 1-5 scale before collection to match treatment intensity to actual risk.
*   **Common Mistakes:** Assuming clear water is safe; ignoring seasonal changes; collecting too close to the bank; trusting a single treatment for Level 4 water.
*   **Waterborne Disease:** In a post-collapse scenario, waterborne disease (Cholera, giardia, cryptosporidium, dysentery) kills more people than dehydration.
*   **Last Resort Taste Test:** Only taste-test water that has already been visually and smell-assessed as non-chemical. Metallic or bitter taste may indicate heavy metals.
