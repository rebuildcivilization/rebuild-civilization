# Surface Sources: Rivers, Lakes, and Springs

## Objective
To locate, evaluate, and safely collect from surface water (rivers, lakes, streams, and springs) as the most accessible category of freshwater before purification.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Knowledge of water purification methods.
*   Understanding of the risks associated with different surface water types.
*   Survival scenario context.

## Material
### Types of Surface Water
| Source Type | Key Characteristics | Advantages | Risks |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Rivers and Streams** | Flowing water; movement limits buildup. | Constant flow refreshes supply; higher dissolved oxygen levels; easier to find.
| **Lakes and Ponds** | Standing bodies of water; higher pathogen loads due to stagnation. | Large volume means they rarely dry up; easy to locate on maps.
| **Springs** | Water emerging from underground; natural filtration by soil and rock. | Natural filtration removes many pathogens; consistent temperature; steady flow.

### Contamination Indicators
*   **Oily sheen or rainbow film:** Petroleum or chemical contamination.
*   **Blue-green algal mats:** Cyanobacteria (can cause liver failure, survive treatment).
*   **Sulfur smell (rotten eggs):** Hydrogen sulfide, usually from anaerobic decomposition.
*   **Orange or rust-colored sediment:** Iron or acid mine drainage (indicates heavy metals).
*   **Dead fish or animals near the water:** Toxic contamination.
*   **Foam that does not dissipate:** May indicate detergent or chemical pollution.
*   **No visible life:** Suggests something is wrong with the water.

### Seasonal Effects
| Season | Effect on Surface Water |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Spring (snowmelt)** | High flow; water is cold and diluted but carries agricultural runoff and animal waste. Turbidity increases. |
