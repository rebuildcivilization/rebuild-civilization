# Rolling Boil Method | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To make water safe to drink by killing every waterborne pathogen using the single most reliable field method available when fire and a container are present.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
* Fire
* A container capable of withstanding direct heat

## Material
* Water
* Vessel (Metal pot/pan, single-wall steel water bottle, aluminum can, cast iron, fired clay pot)
* Fuel (for sustained heat)

## System
The core system is the heat-based disinfection achieved through a rolling boil, which results in large, vigorous bubbles across the entire water surface that cannot be stopped by stirring. This process kills every waterborne pathogen.

## Technology
* Rolling Boil Definition: Vigorous, continuous boil where large bubbles break rapidly across the entire surface of the water.
* Temperature Thresholds: 100°C (212°F) at sea level for a rolling boil.
* Altitude Adjustment: Boiling points are lower above 2,000 meters.
* Pre-filtration: Necessary for cloudy or turbid water to improve boiling efficiency.

## Tools
* Fire
* Container (Vessel)
* Timing reference (for measuring boil duration)
* Cloth/Bandana/Sock/Sand (for quick pre-filtration)
* Shielding materials (rocks, dirt bank, stacked logs) for wind protection

## Process
### Step 1 — Collect Water from the Best Available Source
Choose in this order of preference:
1. Flowing streams and rivers (upstream of any settlements or animal activity)
2. Springs emerging from the ground
3. Collected rainwater
4. Lakes and ponds (from the deepest accessible point)
5. Standing puddles and ditches (absolute last resort)
*Avoid water with visible chemical contamination (oily sheens, unusual colors, foam, or chemical odors).

### Step 2 — Pre-Filter If Needed
If the water is cloudy, silty, or contains visible debris, filter it before boiling. This step is crucial because sediment insulates pathogens from heat and reduces boiling efficiency.
*Quick pre-filtration methods include:
    * Pouring through a folded cloth, bandana, or t-shirt (removes large particles).
    * Pouring through a sock filled with sand (removes finer sediment).
    * Letting water sit for 30-60 minutes in a container, then pouring off the top.
*Note: Pre-filtration does NOT make water safe to drink.

### Step 3 — Transfer to a Boiling Vessel
Use a container that can withstand direct heat. The vessel should be filled no more than two-thirds full to prevent boiling over.
*Suitable Vessel Types and Notes:
    * Metal pot or pan: Excellent (durable, conducts heat well).
    * Single-wall steel water bottle: Good (remove plastic parts; lid off during boiling).
    * Aluminum can: Fair (thin walls conduct heat fast but can collapse; support from sides needed).
    * Cast iron: Excellent (heavy, indestructible, retains heat).
    * Clay pot (fired): Good (may crack with thermal shock).
    * Glass jar: Poor (extreme crack risk).
*Warning: Double-wall insulated bottles (vacuum flasks) CANNOT be used for boiling because trapped air/vacuum will expand and cause an explosion. Only single-wall metal containers are safe over fire.

### Step 4 — Build an Appropriate Fire
Build a small, sustained fire with focused flame directly under the vessel. The goal is a hot bed of coals with focused flame.
*Fire Setup for Boiling:
    * Build a small fire between two parallel rows of rocks or logs that support the vessel.
    * Let the fire burn down to a thick coal bed, then add smaller fuel to produce direct flame.
    * Position the vessel 5-10 cm (2-4 inches) above the flame tips.
    * Shield from wind on three sides using rocks, a dirt bank, or stacked logs, as wind is a major fuel waster.
    * If using a tripod, adjust height so the bottom of the vessel sits just above the flame tips.

### Step 5 — Bring to a Rolling Boil
Place the vessel over the fire and wait. Resist the urge to check or remove the lid constantly.
*Confirmation of Boiling:
    * The rolling boil is reached when large bubbles break vigorously across the entire surface, meaning the water is roiling and cannot be calmed by stirring.
*Typical Times to Reach Rolling Boil (for 2 liters of water):
    * Hot coal bed with active flame: 8-12 minutes
    * Moderate campfire: 12-20 minutes
    * Small survival fire: 20-30 minutes
    * Marginal fire (wet fuel, wind): 30-45 minutes

### Step 6 — Maintain the Boil
The duration of the boil depends on the elevation.
*   **Below 2,000 meters (6,500 feet):** Maintain the rolling boil for **1 full minute**.
*   **Above 2,000 meters (6,500 feet):** Maintain the rolling boil for **3 full minutes**.
*   **Boiling Points and Required Times:
    * Sea level (100°C / 212°F): 1 minute.
    * 1,000 m (97°C / 207°F): 1 minute.
    * 2,000 m (93°C / 200°F): 3 minutes.
    * 3,000 m (90°C / 194°F): 3 minutes.
    * 4,000 m (87°C / 189°F): 3 minutes.
    * 5,000 m (83°C / 181°F): 3 minutes (minimum; consider 5 minutes).

### Step 7 — Cool and Store
Remove the vessel from the fire using a stick, cloth, or improvised handle and place it on a stable surface.
*Critical Storage Rules:
    * Do not pour hot water into a dirty container (recontamination risk).
    * Cover the vessel with a clean lid, cloth, or plate to prevent debris, insects, or airborne contaminants.
    * Let it cool naturally, or use evaporative cooling (surrounding the vessel with wet cloth) to cool faster.
    * Do NOT add untreated water to the boiled water.
    * Consumption timeline: Within 24 hours if stored without a sealed lid; within 2-3 days if sealed and kept cool.

## Common Mistakes
| Mistake | Why It’s Dangerous | Correct Approach |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Stopping at first bubbles | Small bubbles form well below 100°C; pathogens may survive | Wait for large, vigorous bubbles across the entire surface |
| Not timing the boil | Pulling water off heat too early, especially at altitude | Count to 60 (or 180 at altitude) from the start of a full rolling boil |
| Storing in a contaminated container | Reintroduces the pathogens just killed | Use the same vessel you boiled in, or a vessel cleaned with boiled water |
| Adding ice or cold stream water | Contaminates the purified water | Cool the sealed container externally or simply wait |
| Boiling in a closed, sealed container | Pressure buildup can cause explosion | Always leave the lid loose or slightly ajar, never fully sealed |
| Boiling chemically contaminated water | Heat does not remove chemicals; may concentrate them | Filter through charcoal first; use distillation if chemical contamination is severe |

## Boiling for Groups
Batch processing is more fuel-efficient for multiple people.
*Efficient Group Protocol:
    1. Collect all water at once using the largest available clean containers.
    2. Pre-filter the entire batch through cloth or sand.
    3. Boil in your largest fireproof vessel in sequential batches.
    4. Transfer boiled water to clean storage containers with lids.
    5. Clearly mark or separate treated water from untreated water.
    6. Assign one person as the water steward to manage the boiling schedule and prevent cross-contamination.
*Fuel Estimate: A group of 10 people needs roughly 20-30 liters of drinking water per day (2-3 liters per person), requiring 4-6 boiling cycles per day (20-30 minutes each), with roughly 1-2 kg of dry hardwood per boiling cycle.

## When You Cannot Boil
If fire is impossible, alternative heat-based disinfection methods include:
*   **SODIS (Solar Disinfection):** Clear PET bottle in direct sunlight for 6 hours.
*   **Pasteurization:** Any heat source reaching 65°C for 6 minutes.
*   **Chemical Treatment:** Iodine tablets, chlorine drops (8 drops of unscented household bleach per gallon, wait 30 minutes).
*   **Ceramic Filtration:** If a fired clay filter is accessible.

When fire and a container are available, the rolling boil is the most reliable and universally applicable method.

## Notes
*   **Visual Identification:** A rolling boil is defined as large, vigorous bubbles across the entire surface that cannot be stopped by stirring.
*   **Pathogen Kill:** The rolling boil is the only stage with absolute certainty to kill every waterborne pathogen with no equipment beyond fire and a container.
*   **Chemical Treatment:** Boiling kills biological pathogens but does not remove chemicals; combining it with charcoal filtration offers comprehensive treatment.
*   **Vessel Rule:** The critical warning is that double-wall insulated bottles explode when used for boiling.
