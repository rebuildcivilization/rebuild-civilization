# Well Lining: Stabilizing Well Walls

## Objective
To stabilize hand-dug well walls to prevent collapse, which occurs without warning below 3 meters, and to prevent constant surface contamination by ensuring a sanitary seal.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Material Availability:** The choice of lining material depends entirely on what is available.
*   **Skill Level:** Required skills range from Low (Wattle lining) to High (Mortared stone or Fired brick).
*   **Depth Management:** Digging must be done in short sections (no more than 1 meter) before lining to prevent cave-ins.
*   **Safety:** A spotter with a rope must always be present when working in a well deeper than 2 meters.

## Material
### Lining Options
| Material | Availability | Durability | Skill Required | Best Depth |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Dry-stacked stone | Common near rivers, hills | 20+ years | Moderate | Up to 15 m |
| Mortared stone | Requires lime or clay morite | 50+ years | High | Up to 20 m |
| Fired brick | Requires kiln and clay | 50+ years | High | Up to 20 m |
| Woven branches (wattle) | Common in wooded areas | 1-3 years | Low | Up to 5 m |
| Split logs / timber | Forested areas | 3-10 years | Moderate | Up to 8 m |
| Stacked sod/turf | Grasslands | 1-2 years | Low | Up to 3 m |
| Salvaged concrete pipe | Urban ruins | 50+ years | Low (heavy lifting) | Up to 15 m |

### Sealing Materials
*   **Clay pack:** Puddle clay kneaded with water and packed 10-15 cm thick for the top 2-3 meters.
*   **Lime mortar:** Used in the joints of the upper section.
*   **Concrete apron:** Salvaged concrete or hand-mixed concrete spread around the well, sloped away.

### Wood Requirements
*   **Preferred Species (for durability):** Hardwoods (oak, locust, cedar) last far longer than softwoods (pine, spruce).
*   **Avoid:** Treated wood, railroad ties, and painted wood due to leaching chemicals.

## System
### Well Mechanics
*   **Collapse Cause:** Soil loses structural integrity when exposed, leading to lateral pressure on the walls. Water seeping in saturates the walls, turning firm earth into mud, which causes predictable cave-ins.
*   **Critical Depth:** The collapse risk increases dramatically below 3 meters, where a cave-in can dump several hundred kilograms of soil.

### Liner Function
*   **Dry-Stacked Stone:** A permeable lining that allows groundwater to seep in while holding back soil, requiring no mortar.
*   **Above-Ground Seal:** The top section (2-3 meters) must be sealed to block surface runoff and pathogens.

## Technology
*   **Dry-Stacked Stone:** A traditional, durable method utilizing flat-sided stones staggered like bricklaying, backfilled with gravel, and leaving gaps below the water table to allow groundwater entry.
*   **Wattle Lining:** A temporary method using woven branches to hold back walls.
*   **Timber Crib Lining:** A strong support system using squared or halved logs stacked in alternating layers.

## Tools
*   Shovel, pick, or sharpened stick and flat rock (for digging).
*   Rope and bucket (for hauling soil and lowering stones).
*   A straight stick or plumb line (for checking vertical alignment).
*   Hardwood stakes (for wattle lining).
*   Digging tools (shovel, pick, or sharpened stick and flat rock).

## Process
### Method 1: Dry-Stacked Stone Lining (Most Common)
1.  **Dig the shaft:** Dig a circular shaft approximately 1.2 meters in diameter.
2.  **Shape the walls:** Trim walls to be as smooth and vertical as possible, using a plumb line for alignment.
3.  **Lay the first course:** Lay the flattest stones at the base, ensuring flat faces contact the soil wall and inner faces form a smooth cylinder, leaving small gaps for water seepage.
4.  **Stack upward:** Lay the second course on top, staggering joints like bricklaying, ensuring each stone bridges the gap between two below.
5.  **Backfill with gravel:** Pack gravel into the gap between the stone lining and the earth wall to stabilize stones and filter water.
6.  **Continue downward:** Dig another 1-meter section and line it, repeating until the water table is reached.
7.  **Build above ground level:** Extend the lining at least 50 cm above ground. Seal this section with clay pack, lime mortar, or concrete.

### Method 2: Wattle Lining (Quick and Temporary)
1.  **Cut stakes:** Cut hardwood stakes (5-8 cm diameter, 20 cm longer than the section depth).
2.  **Drive stakes:** Drive stakes vertically at approximately 15 cm intervals around the circumference, forming a ring about 10 cm inside the shaft wall.
3.  **Weave branches:** Weave flexible green branches horizontally between the stakes, alternating inside/outside successive stakes, and packing each row tightly.
4.  **Backfill:** Pack gravel or coarse sand between the wattle wall and the earth.
5.  **Replace when needed:** Replace the lining when the wattle begins to rot (1 to 3 years of service).

### Method 3: Timber Crib Lining
1.  **Prepare timbers:** Cut logs to equal length (matching shaft diameter plus 10 cm overlap on each side), splitting or hewing them to have at least one flat face, preferably rot-resistant species.
2.  **Build the crib:** Lay two parallel timbers across the bottom, then two perpendicular timbers on top, alternating layers upward.
3.  **Trim interior:** Trim or adze protruding interior faces to create a roughly smooth inner wall.
4.  **Backfill:** Pack gravel between the crib and the shaft wall.

### Wellhead Construction
1.  **Cover:** Build a removable cover from timber planks, flat stones, or metal sheeting, ensuring it is heavy enough to withstand wind and animals.
2.  **Drainage apron:** Grade the ground around the well so it slopes AWAY from the opening for at least 1.5 meters.
3.  **Paving:** Pave the drainage apron with stone, clay, or packed gravel to prevent erosion.
4.  **Dedicated bucket:** Use a single, dedicated bucket, storing it on a hook or shelf, not on the ground.
5.  **Drainage channel:** Dig a shallow channel leading away from the well to carry spilled water at least 5 meters away.

## Notes
*   **Contamination Risk:** Without a sealed upper section, surface runoff, soil, animal feces, and surface bacteria are washed directly into the water supply, causing cholera, typhoid, and dysentery outbreaks.
*   **Common Mistakes:**
    *   Digging too deep before lining, resulting in a cave-in.
    *   Using only soil for backfill, allowing soil to wash through lining gaps.
    *   Lack of a surface seal, leading to contaminated water.
    *   Having the well too close to a latrine (minimum 30 m separation required).
*   **Key Takeaways:** The sanitary seal is non-negotiable. Never work alone in a well deeper than 2 meters. Always have a spotter with a rope attached to the digger.
*   **Material Preference:** Always use untreated, natural wood (hardwoods preferred) to prevent groundwater contamination from treated lumber.
