# Sea Survival

## Objective

To remain alive and detectable after becoming stranded on or near the sea by managing flotation, water, exposure, food, and signaling.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

Sea survival is dominated by:

* Flotation.
* Water.
* Protection from exposure.
* Signaling.
* Avoiding unnecessary energy expenditure.
* Understanding local sea hazards.

## Material

Useful equipment includes:

* Flotation device or improvised flotation.
* Water containers.
* Signaling mirror.
* Whistle.
* Waterproof fire-starting equipment.
* Shelter or raft material.
* Fishing equipment when available.

## System

### Flotation

Maintain flotation before attempting other tasks.

Protect flotation equipment from unnecessary damage.

### Water

Do not treat seawater as drinking water.

Freshwater sources may include:

* Stored drinking water.
* Rainwater.
* Certain naturally occurring freshwater sources.
* Fresh liquid from appropriate coconuts when available.

### Exposure

Protect the body from excessive sun, wind, and cold.

A floating or improvised platform can reduce prolonged contact with water.

### Signaling

A sea survivor should maximize the probability of being seen or heard.

Use:

* Signaling mirror.
* Whistle.
* Smoke or other visible signals.
* Large visual markers.

## Technology

### Improvised Flotation

Any improvised flotation must be tested cautiously because buoyancy can change when materials become saturated.

### Rainwater Collection

Rain provides a potential freshwater source and should be collected whenever practical.

## Tools

* Flotation device.
* Water container.
* Signaling mirror.
* Whistle.
* Waterproof fire starter.
* Raft repair material.
* Fishing equipment.

## Process

1. Secure flotation.
2. Account for all survivors.
3. Check immediate injuries.
4. Protect available drinking water.
5. Establish a method for collecting rainwater.
6. Reduce unnecessary exertion.
7. Protect yourself from sun and cold.
8. Prepare signaling equipment.
9. Signal at appropriate intervals.
10. Avoid consuming seawater.
11. Use fishing or other food procurement only after water and exposure needs are addressed.

## Notes

* Sea survival as one of the more difficult survival environments.
* Food is generally a lower immediate priority than water, flotation, and protection from exposure.
* The manual contains specific survival-kit equipment intended for military aviation; the repository should preserve the underlying capabilities rather than the military equipment lists.
