# Tropical Survival

## Objective

To maintain health, shelter, water, mobility, and protection from insects and infection in tropical environments.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

Tropical survival is strongly influenced by:

* Rain.
* Humidity.
* Insects.
* Rapid wound infection.
* Water availability.
* Dense vegetation.
* Heat.

## Material

Useful equipment includes:

* Waterproof shelter material.
* Mosquito netting.
* Insect protection.
* Machete or cutting tool.
* Water containers.
* Fire-starting equipment.
* First-aid supplies.
* Navigation tools.

## System

### Shelter

Shelter must protect against:

* Heavy rain.
* Sun.
* Insects.
* Wet ground.

Keep sleeping areas as dry as possible.

### Insects

Mosquitoes and other insects can represent a major health hazard.

Use:

* Clothing that covers exposed skin.
* Mosquito netting.
* Physical barriers.
* Appropriate insect protection.

### Wounds

Small cuts and scratches may become serious in tropical conditions.

Clean and protect wounds promptly.

### Water

Water may be abundant but unsafe.

Possible sources:

* Vines.
* Roots.
* Palm trees.
* Rain.
* Condensation.
* Water collected near river or lake margins.

All questionable water must be treated before drinking.

## Technology

### Condensation and Collection

Tropical humidity and vegetation can provide opportunities for collecting water, but the resulting water still requires appropriate treatment.

### Natural Materials

Tropical vegetation can provide:

* Shelter material.
* Cordage.
* Food.
* Water in specific species.
* Materials for insect protection.

Positive identification is required before consuming plant material or fluids.

## Tools

* Cutting tool.
* Water container.
* Shelter material.
* Mosquito net.
* Fire starter.
* First-aid supplies.
* Navigation equipment.

## Process

1. Establish shelter from rain, sun, and insects.
2. Protect the sleeping area from wet ground.
3. Protect exposed skin.
4. Treat even minor wounds.
5. Locate potential water sources.
6. Purify collected water.
7. Keep clothing and equipment as dry as practical.
8. Establish a fire when useful.
9. Mark routes through dense vegetation.
10. Monitor for insect bites, infection, and dehydration.

## Notes

* Dense tropical vegetation can make rescue and navigation difficult.
* Protecting against insects and rain as immediate priorities.
* The manual contains numerous region-specific plants and water sources; these should not be generalized to every tropical environment.
* Navigation and water-purification techniques already present elsewhere in the repository should be referenced rather than duplicated.
