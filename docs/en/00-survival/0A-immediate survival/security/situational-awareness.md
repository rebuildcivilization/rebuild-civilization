# Situational Awareness

## Objective
Situational awareness is the practice of continuously monitoring your environment, understanding what you observe, and predicting what is about to happen based on that information. In a post-collapse world without police, emergency services, or communication networks, the objective is to develop the single most important survival skill: the ability to notice things before they become crises.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

*   The ability to continuously monitor the environment.
*   The skill to observe, understand, and predict based on observations.
*   The ability to establish a baseline behavior.

## Material

### Observation Fundamentals
*   **Visual Scanning:** Utilizing both the narrow cone of sharp focus (3 degrees) and peripheral vision. Effective scanning involves a near-to-far scan (10m radius, 10-100m, 100m to horizon).
*   **Night Observation:** Requires understanding that night vision takes 20-30 minutes to develop and is destroyed by bright light. Techniques include off-center viewing, silhouette detection, and prioritizing sound over sight.
*   **Reading Environments:** Specific indicators for different areas:
    *   **Urban Environments:** Watching for windows, doors (cracks, scuff marks), rooftops, intersections, and signs of habitation (tire tracks, food waste).
    *   **Rural and Wilderness:** Watching for game trails, bird behavior (flocking or alarm calls), water source tracks, and vegetation disturbance.
    *   **Transitional Zones:** These areas (forest edge, riverbank, road entry points) are the most information-rich, acting as cover and natural ambush points.

### Cooper Color Code System
This system dictates awareness levels:
*   **White — Unaware:** Relaxed, only acceptable inside a secure shelter with others.
*   **Yellow — Relaxed alert:** Default state; scanning, listening, and processing; noticing subtle changes.
*   **Orange — Specific alert:** A threat has triggered attention; evaluating the threat and identifying exits/cover.
*   **Red — Action:** A threat is confirmed and execution of a plan (evacuation, hiding, or confrontation) occurs.

## System

### Baseline Establishment
*   **Building Your Baseline:** Spend initial days observing to determine 'normal' by noting:
    *   Bird singing times.
    *   Animal visitation patterns (e.g., deer at dawn).
    *   Wind patterns.
    *   Normal sounds (creaking trees, water flowing).
    *   Night sky appearance.

### Deviation Detection
Once a baseline is established, deviations are detected as:
*   **Absent sounds** (e.g., creek stopping).
*   **New sounds** (engine noise, gunshots; distance estimated by sound travel rate of 340 m/s).
*   **New smells** (wood smoke, diesel, human waste).
*   **Visual changes** (new tracks, broken branches).

### Awareness Fatigue Management
Strategies to prevent degradation of awareness:
*   Rotate observation duties (e.g., two hours on, four hours off).
*   Use technology multipliers (tripwires, noise makers, dogs) to extend the perimeter while resting.
*   Schedule deliberate rest periods (dropping to Condition White).
*   Eat and hydrate (as the brain consumes 20% of calories).
*   Recognize degradation immediately (staring blankly, missing obvious things) and rotate off watch.

## Technology

*   **Technology Multipliers:** Tools used to extend the perimeter and allow rest while maintaining warning include tripwires, noise makers, and dogs.

## Tools

| Tool/Drill | Purpose | Technique Summary |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Kim’s Game | Build observational memory | Place objects, study for one minute, cover, list memory, increase object count. |
| Baseline Walk | Establish 'normal' | Walk the same route daily to spot changes instantly. |
| Sound Mapping | Categorization | Sit with eyes closed for 10 minutes to map sounds by direction and distance (natural, animal, human, mechanical). |
| Back-trail Check | Follower detection | Stop every 100 steps to observe the back trail for 30 seconds to check for parallels. |

## Process

1.  **Observe Continuously:** Implement the near-to-far scan, paying attention to edges and gaps.
2.  **Establish Baseline:** Spend initial days observing all normal environmental factors.
3.  **Detect Deviations:** Compare current observations against the baseline to identify anomalies (new sounds, smells, visual changes).
4.  **Assess Threat Level:** Determine the required level using the Cooper Color Code System (Yellow to Orange to Red).
5.  **Manage Fatigue:** Rotate duties and use multipliers to sustain awareness, scheduling deliberate rest.

**Pre-departure / pre-movement check**
- Determine expected duration.
- Estimate food and water requirements.
- Check clothing against climate.
- Check medical supplies.
- Check terrain-specific equipment.
- Identify alternative routes.
- Define regrouping points.
- Establish what to do if weather, terrain or transport conditions change.

## Notes

*   The human eye has a narrow cone of sharp focus (roughly 3 degrees).
*   Sound travels farther in cool night air.
*   Red light preserves night vision better than white light.
*   Assumption of safety (absence of evidence) is a common failure; anomalies must be investigated rather than explained away.
*   Tunnel vision and task fixation are common failures that must be countered by regular pausing and scanning.
