# Cutting Tools

## Objective
To select, use and maintain cutting tools safely for firewood processing, shelter construction, food processing and fabrication of survival equipment.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Basic understanding of cutting-tool geometry.
- Stable working position.
- Clear working area.
- Appropriate tool for the task.

## Material
- Fixed-blade knife.
- Folding knife with a secure lock.
- Folding or bow saw.
- Axe or hatchet.
- Sharpening stone.
- Spare handle/haft material where appropriate.
- Protective covers.

## System
- Use the smallest and safest tool capable of completing the task.
- Keep cutting tools sharp rather than forcing a dull blade.
- Maintain a clear safety zone around cutting operations.
- Process wood according to size instead of using unnecessary force.
- Never use a knife as a substitute for an axe or saw when doing so increases the risk of injury or damage.

## Technology
- Cutting.
- Splitting.
- Batoning.
- Sawing.
- Sharpening.
- Hafting and handle replacement.

## Tools
- Knife.
- Saw.
- Axe/hatchet.
- Sharpening stone.
- File.
- Wooden wedges.

## Process
1. Inspect the tool before use.
2. Check the cutting edge and handle.
3. Establish a clear working area.
4. Position the material securely.
5. Use controlled strokes away from the body.
6. Use wedges when splitting wood where appropriate.
7. Stop when fatigue reduces control.
8. Clean and dry the tool after use.
9. Sharpen before storing.
10. Protect the cutting edge.

## Notes
- Cutting tools are disproportionately valuable because many other survival items can be manufactured with them.
- Tool maintenance is part of survival capability, not merely equipment care.
- A damaged or dull tool can consume more energy and increase injury risk.
