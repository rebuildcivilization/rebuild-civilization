# Igloo Principles

## Objective
To construct a self-supporting dome shelter using snow blocks to achieve cold weather protection and thermal efficiency.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- **Snow Requirement:** Dense, wind-packed slab snow that holds block shape when cut and lifted.
- **Testing Requirement:** Snow must be dense enough to cut into blocks that hold their shape.
- **Build Condition:** Snow is wind-packed or dense slab (common on open tundra, prairies, exposed ridgelines).
- **Experience:** Having built one before or having time to learn is recommended.

## Material
- Snow blocks (for the structure).
- Loose snow (for chinking/sealing gaps).

## System
- **Core Principle:** The blocks are laid in a continuous ascending spiral, not in discrete horizontal rows, which creates the dome shape.
- **Structural Outcome:** A self-supporting dome where the structure distributes loads as compression forces through the blocks, resulting in no tension.
- **Durability:** The igloo gains strength over time as the blocks sinter together, eventually becoming a monolithic dome.

## Technology
- **Structural Technology:** The spiral stacking method leverages the leaning of blocks to create the necessary dome curve and structural integrity.
- **Thermal Technology:** The interior finishing step (lighting a candle) accelerates sintering, creating a thin ice glaze that strengthens the dome and reflects body heat.

## Tools
- Snow saw
- Machete
- Long knife
- Flat piece of wood (for block cutting/lifting)
- Snow boot (for foot stomp test)
- Hand/Gloved hand (for chinking)

## Process
### Block Cutting
1. **Find Quarry:** Locate a quarry area of suitable snow within 10 meters of the build site.
2. **Vertical Cuts:** Use a snow saw, machete, or long knife to make vertical cuts outlining each block from above.
3. **Horizontal Cut:** Make a horizontal cut at the base of the block by inserting the saw parallel to the ground at the desired depth (15-20 cm). Connect this cut with the vertical walls.
4. **Lever Out:** Lever the block out by inserting the saw blade at the base and gently prying upward. Do not grab edges.
5. **Transport:** Transport blocks by carrying them flat on forearms or on a sled/tarp; do not stack blocks on top of each other.

### Spiral Stacking Method
1. **Mark the Base Circle:** Stamp or scratch a circle in the snow based on intended occupants (e.g., 1-2 occupants require a 2.5-3.0 m base circle diameter).
2. **Lay the First Row:** Place blocks end-to-end around the circle, standing upright on their longest edge. Trim blocks to fit snugly against neighbors.
3. **Create the Spiral Ramp:** Cut a diagonal slope across 2-3 blocks to create a ramp from ground level up to the top of the first row.
4. **Lean and Lock:** Place each new block so it leans against the previous block’s end AND leans inward toward the center. Trim the bottom edge at a slight angle so it sits flush against the inclined surface.
5. **Close the Dome (King Block):** Close the final opening with a single block, trimming it slightly larger than the opening, passing it through vertically, rotating it, and lowering it in.
6. **Chink and Seal:** Fill all gaps between blocks from outside with loose snow and pack it firmly.
7. **Cut the Entrance:** Cut a low arch through the base blocks on the downwind side AFTER the dome is complete.
8. **Interior Finishing:** Smooth the interior, carve a sleeping platform, poke a ventilation hole (8-10 cm diameter), and light a candle inside.

### Snow Quality Testing
- **Foot Stomp Test:** If the print holds a clean edge with vertical walls, the snow is probably dense enough. If it crumbles or collapses, it is too loose.
- **Block Cut Test:** Cut a block (approx. 50x25x20 cm) and lift it by the ends. If it holds together without cracking or crumbling, it is suitable.
- **Squeeze Test:** Dense building snow compresses slightly but springs back; powder snow compresses and stays compressed; wet snow balls up and feels heavy.

### Comparison: Igloo vs. Quinzhee
| Factor | Igloo | Quinzhee |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Snow requirement | Dense, wind-packed slab snow | Any snow |
| Structural strength | Excellent — self-supporting dome | Good — cohesive but weaker |
| Build time (experienced) | 2-3 hours | 3-4 hours (with settling) |
| Build time (first attempt) | 4-6 hours | 3-4 hours |
| No settling wait needed | Correct — blocks are already dense | Must wait 1-2 hours |
| Interior temperature | -2 to +2 C with body heat | -2 to 0 C |
| Wind resistance | Excellent — aerodynamic dome | Good |

## Notes
- **Critical Failure Point:** The most common mistake is not ensuring blocks lean inward enough, which prevents the upper rows from curving to form the dome. Blocks should lean inward roughly 10-15 degrees more than their vertical position.
- **Structural Integrity:** The entrance should be cut only after the dome is complete to preserve structural integrity during construction.
- **Key Takeaway:** A finished igloo behaves as a monolithic dome that can support a person’s weight.
