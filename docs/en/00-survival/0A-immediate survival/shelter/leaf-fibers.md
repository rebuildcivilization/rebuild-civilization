# Leaf Fibers: A Guide to Natural Cordage Production

## Objective
To find the fastest path from plant to functional cordage using leaf fibers, with the goal of producing material adequate for bindcraft, lashing, snares, fishing line, and general camp cordage.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Tools required for processing include a flat rock or log section, a dull edge, a knife (optional), a smooth rock, and a wooden baton (optional).
- Water is required for rinsing, soaking, and re-wetting fibers.

## Material
Leaf fibers derived from various plants:
- Yucca
- Cattail (*Typha* species)
- Iris (Wild Iris / Flag Iris)
- New Zealand Flax (*Phormium*)
- Palm Fronds (specifically coconut palm fronds)
- Agave (Century Plant)

## System
The system relies on harvesting mature outer leaves, processing the pulp to extract usable fibers, working the fibers at a partially dried state, and managing moisture during processing and storage.

## Technology
The primary processing technologies are:
1. Pounding and Scraping (Fastest)
2. Soaking and Scraping (Better Fiber Quality)
3. Cooking (Fastest Bulk Processing)

## Tools
| Tool | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- |
| Flat Rock / Log Section | Laying leaves flat for pounding/processing. |
| Dull Edge (Knife/Stone Edge/Split Stick) | Scraping the crushed pulp off the fibers. |
| Smooth Rock / Wooden Baton | Pounding the leaf to crush the pulp. |
| Knife (Optional) | Cutting leaves at the base.

## Process
### Harvesting
1. Select mature outer leaves, which are darker, stiffer, and have more developed fiber.
2. Cut leaves at the base with a knife or bend the leaf sharply side to side until it tears free if no knife is available.
3. Harvest 2-3 times more leaves than initially estimated.

### Processing Methods
**Method 1: Pounding and Scraping (Fastest)**
1. Lay a leaf flat on a hard surface.
2. Pound the leaf with a smooth rock or wooden baton along its entire length to crush the pulp without cutting the fibers.
3. Flip and pound the other side.
4. Scrape the crushed pulp away from the fibers using a dull edge.
5. Work from the center outward toward each end.
6. Rinse the exposed fibers in water to remove remaining pulp.
7. Hang to dry (2-4 hours in sun, longer in shade).
*Time:* 10-20 minutes per leaf.

**Method 2: Soaking and Scraping (Better Fiber Quality)**
1. Soak harvested leaves in water for 24-48 hours to soften the pulp.
2. Lay soaked leaves on a flat surface and scrape pulp away with a dull edge.
3. Rinse and dry the fibers.
*Time:* 5-10 minutes per leaf after soaking.

**Method 3: Cooking (Fastest Bulk Processing)**
1. Simmer leaves in water for 1-2 hours, ensuring gentle simmering to preserve fiber strength.
2. Remove leaves and let them cool enough to handle.
3. Scrape pulp away (pulp practically falls off after cooking).
4. Rinse and dry.

### Working with Leaf Fibers
**Moisture Management**
- **Green/wet fibers:** Pliable and easy to twist but shrink as they dry, loosening cordage.
- **Dried fibers:** Stiff and hard to work but maintain cord tension when twisted.
- **Best practice:** Twist cordage from partially dried fibers—flexible enough to work but past the major shrinkage phase. Alternatively, twist from dry fibers after re-wetting, then allow the finished cord to dry under tension.

**Splicing**
- Leaf fibers tend to be shorter than bast fibers, requiring frequent splicing.
- When reverse-wrapping (two-ply twist), stagger splices—never add new fiber to both plies at the same point.
- Overlap each new fiber by at least 3 inches with the outgoing fiber.

**Storage**
- Dried leaf fibers store well if kept dry.
- Bundle processed fibers loosely and hang them in a dry, ventilated area.
- Avoid tight binding to prevent trapped moisture and mold.

## Notes
- **Yucca & Agave Strength:** Produce the strongest leaf fibers, comparable to sisal twine.
- **Cattail Limitations:** Cattail leaf cordage is significantly weaker; a two-ply cord holds roughly 15-30 pounds, suitable only for light lashing and binding.
- **Needle and Thread:** Leaving the terminal spine attached during processing results in a natural needle threaded with fiber, useful for sewing.
- **Quick Reference Summary:**
    - Yucca processing time: 10-20 min/leaf.
    - Cattail processing time: 2-5 min/leaf.
    - Processing comparison shows Yucca and Agave as having Very High to High fiber strength, while Cattail is Low-moderate.
- **Key Takeaways:**
    - Yucca and Agave are the strongest leaf-fiber cordage sources.
    - Cattail provides the most abundant quick cordage.
    - The optimal working state is partially dried fibers.
    - Splice staggering is critical to avoid weak spots.
