# Raw Materials for Rope

## Objective
The objective is to find fibers, assess their strength, and turn them into cordage. This guide covers the full range of natural fiber sources: plant, animal, and scavenged, with practical tests and selection criteria for each. Rope is one of the foundational technologies, essential for building shelter, setting traps, making bows, fishing with lines, lashing tools, climbing, hauling, or binding loads.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
The prerequisite for making rope is the ability to find fibers, assess their strength, and turn them into cordage. Successful rope-making requires testing fibers (pulling, twisting) and understanding the properties that matter for rope-making: tensile strength, length, flexibility, durability (wet), and availability.

## Material
### Category 1: Plant Fibers
*   **Bark Fibers (Bast Fibers):** Inner bark fibers from trees.
    *   *Best sources include:* Basswood (linden), Willow, Cedar (western red), Elm, Mulberry, Hibiscus, and Tulip poplar.
*   **Leaf and Stem Fibers:** Strong fibers stored in leaves or stems.
    *   *Sources include:* Yucca (leaves), Cattail (leaves), Stinging nettle (stems), Dogbane (stems), Milkweed (stems), Flax (stems), Agave/sisal (leaves), and Palm (leaves, leaf sheaths).
*   **Grass and Rush Fibers:** The most universally available, but weakest.
    *   *Materials include:* Dried grass, Sedge, Rush, and Raffia palm.

### Category 2: Animal Fibers
*   **Sinew:** Tendons of deer, elk, etc.
*   **Rawhide strips:** Dried untanned hide.
*   **Hair/mane:** Horse, yak, human hair.
*   **Wool:** From sheep or goat (used for yarn, not strong enough for rope).
*   **Gut (intestine):** Sections from any large animal.

### Category 3: Scavenged Materials
*   **Paracord:** Military gear, outdoor equipment (Excellent potential).
*   **Electrical wire:** Buildings, vehicles, appliances (Good potential).
*   **Seatbelts:** Vehicles (Excellent potential due to nylon webbing).
*   **Plastic bags:** Everywhere (Moderate potential when cut and braided).
*   **Clothing strips:** Abandoned clothing, curtains, bedding (Variable potential).
*   **Fishing line:** Tackle shops, boats (Excellent for light applications).
*   **Cable/wire rope:** Construction sites, industrial areas (Extremely strong but heavy).
*   **Twine/string:** Hardware stores, farms (Good potential, often sisal or polypropylene).

## System
The system revolves around finding fibers, assessing their properties, and selecting the appropriate material for the task. The overall system prioritizes scavenging manufactured materials over making cordage from raw fibers.

## Technology
*   Knots and Cordage: Rope is one of the foundational technologies.

## Tools
*   Hands (for testing fibers).
*   Measuring tools (for fiber length).
*   Soaking vessels (for durability testing).

## Process
### Fiber Selection Criteria
Fibers must be assessed based on:
*   **Tensile strength:** Determines how much weight the rope can hold before breaking.
*   **Length:** Longer fibers require fewer splices (30 cm+ is good; 60 cm+ is excellent).
*   **Flexibility:** Stiff fibers twist poorly and make brittle rope.
*   **Durability (wet):** Rope must retain strength after soaking.
*   **Availability:** The quantity of material gathered in a given time.

### Quick Test
Pull a fiber taut between hands and twist it. If it twists without snapping and resists a firm tug, it is a candidate for rope-making.

### Selection for Application
| Application | Minimum Strength Needed | Recommended Fiber(s) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Shelter lashing | Low | Grass, bark strips, any available |
| Snare loop | Moderate | Wire, sinew, rawhide, yucca |
| Bowstring | High | Sinew, rawhide, paracord |
| Climbing/hauling | High | Paracord, rawhide, bark rope (thick) |
| Fishing line | Low but must be thin | Sinew, gut, monofilament, hair |
| Sewing | Low but must be fine | Sinew, gut, nettle fiber |
| Trap triggers | Moderate | Bark cord, yucca, cattail |
| Binding tools | Moderate | Wet rawhide (shrinks tight), sinew, bark |

### Seasonal Considerations
*   **Spring:** Best for bark stripping (sap flow makes inner bark peel easily). Cattails sprouting, nettles growing.
*   **Summer:** Peak for leaf fibers (yucca, agave). Grass is abundant but often too green.
*   **Fall:** Dead nettle and dogbane stalks are ready for harvest. Grass is dry and usable.
*   **Winter:** Bark is harder to peel. Reliance on stored fibers, scavenged materials, and animal sources is necessary.

## Notes
*   **Fiber Strength Comparison:** Paracord (250 kg / 550 lb), Sinew (90-130 kg / 200-290 lb), Rawhide strip (80-120 kg / 175-265 lb), Yucca/Agave/sisal (50-80 kg / 110-175 lb), Bark fiber (30-50 kg / 65-110 lb), Nettle/dogbane (30-50 kg / 65-110 lb), Grass (10-20 kg / 22-44 lb), Plastic bag braid (20-40 kg / 44-88 lb).
*   **Quality Caveat:** Poorly twisted rope can be 50-70% weaker than its potential.
*   **Key Takeaway:** Plant fibers (bark, leaf, stem) are primary; animal fibers are scarce for high-value uses; manufactured materials should be scavenged first.
