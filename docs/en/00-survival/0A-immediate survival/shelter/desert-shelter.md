# Shade and Ground Cooling

## Objective
Part of Emergency Shelter. In arid environments, the sun and ground are trying to cook you. The shelter strategy reverses: instead of trapping heat, you are escaping it.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Arid environments.
- Water conservation (water is a critical resource).
- Ability to find or utilize natural features.

## Material
- Upright supports (for ramada).
- Cross-beams.
- Brush, leafy branches, fabric, or any opaque material (for shade layer).
- Logs or rock lines (for elevated sleeping).
- Flat rocks or branches (for platforms).
- Loose, light-colored clothing.

## System
### Heat Dynamics
- **Desert Heat Problem:** The environment actively adds heat from two directions:
    - Solar radiation from above (roughly 1000 watts per square meter).
    - Radiant heat from below (ground absorbs energy and re-radiates it, reaching 60-75 degrees C when air temperature is 40-45 degrees C).
- **Body Cooling Mechanism:** Sweating, which costs water.

### Shelter Design Systems
- **The Ramada (Brush Shade):** The simplest and most effective shelter, consisting of a flat or slightly angled roof on posts with open sides for maximum airflow.
- **The Double-Roof Shade:** The most effective improvement, consisting of two layers with an air gap:
    - Layer 1 (upper): Absorbs direct solar radiation, often reaching 50-60 degrees C.
    - Air gap (15-30 cm): Allows hot air to convect away.
    - Layer 2 (lower): Stays cooler, receiving only re-radiated heat from the outer layer. Temperature difference between layers can be 10-15 degrees C.

### Ground Cooling Systems
- **Excavation:** Digging a trench to access cooler temperatures beneath the surface.
- **Elevated Sleeping:** Creating a platform above the ground to separate the body from radiating heat.

### Behavioral System
- **Timing:** Work and travel at night or early morning; rest in shade during peak heat (10:00 to 16:00).
- **Exertion Control:** Minimize exertion; work in short bursts (e.g., 15 minutes work, 15 minutes rest) to conserve water.
- **Clothing Strategy:** Stay clothed in loose, light-colored clothing to trap sweat vapor and reduce water loss.
- **Dietary Strategy:** Minimize food intake, especially protein, if water is scarce.

## Technology
- **Solar Radiation:** Direct sunlight delivery (1000 watts/sqm).
- **Radiant Heat:** Ground absorption and re-radiation.
- **Convection:** Heat transfer through the air gap in double-roof design.
- **Thermal Conductivity:** The effectiveness of materials in conducting heat (e.g., rocks conduct heat poorly).

## Tools
- Sticks or branches (for upright supports, cross-beams, and elevated sleeping).
- Rocks (for support posts or platforms).
- Sharpened stakes (if soil allows).

## Process
### Shade Construction Process
1. **Find Supports:** Locate or cut four upright supports (1.2-1.5 meters tall), using natural posts (bushes/trunks) or piling rocks/driving stakes.
2. **Cross-beams:** Lay cross-beams across the tops of the uprights and secure them.
3. **Shade Layering:** Layer brush, leafy branches, fabric, or opaque material across the cross-beams, aiming for 80% direct sunlight blockage.
4. **Airflow Management:** Leave all sides open to allow critical airflow.
5. **Double-Roof Improvement (If applicable):** Build two sets of cross-beams 15-30 cm apart, cover each level separately.

### Ground Cooling Process
1. **Location Selection:** Choose a spot with existing shade (rock overhangs, north side of boulders) if possible.
2. **Excavation:** Dig a body-length trench 45-60 cm deep and 60-75 cm wide.
3. **Trench Shaping:** Angle sides outward in sand to prevent collapse; vertical walls for harder soil.
4. **Shade Layer:** Cover the trench with a shade layer propped on sticks or rocks at ground level or slightly above.
5. **Resting:** Rest in the trench during peak heat hours (10:00 to 16:00).

### Water Conservation Process
1. **Baseline Loss:** Walking in direct sun (40°C) costs 0.75-1.5 liters/hour.
2. **Shade Application:** Sitting in single-layer shade costs 0.25-0.5 liters/hour.
3. **Double-Shade Application:** Resting in a double-shade trench costs 0.1-0.25 liters/hour.

## Notes
- **Ground Temperature Data:** Digging 60 cm+ reduces temperature to 25-30 degrees C.
- **Flash Flood Warning:** Never shelter in dry washes, arroyos, or wadis, as flash floods can arrive with little warning.
- **Scorpion/Snake Warning:** Check for these animals before entering trenches or holes.
- **Key Takeaways:**
    - The double-roof design is the most effective improvement.
    - Digging 45-60 cm accesses 25-30 degrees cooler ground.
    - Work and rest timing is crucial for water management.
- **Water Multiplier:** A well-built desert shelter can stretch a limited water supply 3-4 times longer than no shelter.
