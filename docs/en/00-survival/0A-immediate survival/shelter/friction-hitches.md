# Friction Hitches and Mechanical Advantage Systems

## Objective
To provide the ability to haul, climb, tension, and secure loads far beyond what brute strength alone allows, utilizing dynamic grip systems and mechanical advantage.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Prusik Hitch:** The Prusik cord must be *thinner* than the rope it grips. A good ratio is the Prusik cord being 60-70% of the main rope’s diameter. Same-diameter combinations will result in the Prusik failing to grip.
*   **Safety:** Prusik climbing requires a proper harness or an improvised harness (rope around waist and thighs) to distribute the load; a single waist loop can cause serious internal injuries.

## Material
*   Rope
*   Cordage
*   Flat webbing
*   Fabric strips

## System
### Friction Hitches
Friction hitches grip a rope under load but slide freely when unloaded. They are dynamic, gripping when pulled and releasing when stopped.

### The Prusik Hitch
*   **Function:** The foundational friction hitch. Grips a larger-diameter rope when loaded in either direction, and slides when the load is removed.
*   **Application:** Essential for ascending a fixed rope (Prusik climbing) and as a ratchet in hauling systems (Z-Drag).

### The Klemheist Hitch
*   **Function:** A directional friction hitch that grips when loaded in one direction only, and slides when loaded in the other.
*   **Advantage:** Works with flat webbing and fabric strips, unlike the Prusik.

### The Trucker’s Hitch
*   **Function:** The most important tensioning system in ropework, creating a theoretical 3:1 mechanical advantage.
*   **Application:** Tensioning a tarp ridgeline, securing a load to a sled or cart, stretching a hide, and tensioning a fence line.

### The Z-Drag (3:1 Hauling System)
*   **Function:** A system used for hauling heavy loads (logs, stones, stuck vehicles) with rope alone, utilizing a Prusik hitch as a ratchet to lock the load while repositioning.

## Technology
*   **Friction Grip:** Grip under load, slide when unloaded.
*   **Mechanical Advantage:** The Trucker’s Hitch provides a 3:1 mechanical advantage, meaning 10 kg of pull applies 30 kg of tension.

## Tools
*   Rope
*   Cord
*   Harness (for safety in climbing scenarios)
*   Objects to be hauled (logs, stones, vehicles).

## Process
### Tying the Prusik Hitch
1.  Pass the Prusik loop behind the main rope. Push the knot (double fisherman’s) to one side.
2.  Open the loop and pass one end through the other end, wrapping around the main rope (one wrap).
3.  Repeat: pass the same end through the loop again, wrapping around the main rope in the same direction (second wrap).
4.  Repeat once more for a total of three wraps (use four for slippery rope).
5.  Tighten by pulling on the loop (loaded end). The wraps should nest neatly.
6.  Test: Pull down on the loop to lock; release the load and push the wraps up the rope to see them slide freely.

### Tying the Klemheist Hitch
1.  Wrap the Prusik loop around the main rope, spiraling upward, for 3-4 turns.
2.  Pass the lower end of the loop through the upper end.
3.  Pull down on the lower end to cinch and grip.
4.  To release, push the wraps up to uncinch and slide freely.

### Tying the Trucker’s Hitch
1.  Anchor one end of the rope to a fixed point using a bowline or clove hitch.
2.  Create a fixed loop at the midpoint (e.g., form a small bight, twist once to create a loop, and pull a small bight of the standing part through the loop).
3.  Run the working end around the second anchor point (the load).
4.  Thread the working end up through the inline loop from Step 2.
5.  Pull the working end downward; the inline loop acts as a pulley, achieving 3:1 mechanical advantage.
6.  Lock off the tension with two half hitches around the standing part, tied snug against the inline loop.

### Prusik Climbing Sequence
1.  Attach both Prusik loops to the fixed rope (Upper near the chest, Lower with a foot loop).
2.  Stand up in the lower Prusik’s foot loop, weighting it to grip.
3.  Slide the upper Prusik as high as reachable.
4.  Sit into the upper Prusik, lifting feet.
5.  Slide the lower Prusik up as high as possible.
6.  Stand up in the foot loop again. Repeat.

### Z-Drag Hauling Sequence
1.  Anchor one end of the rope to the object to be hauled (using a bowline or timber hitch).
2.  Run the rope to an anchor point and around it.
3.  Attach a Prusik hitch to the rope between the object and the anchor (the ratchet).
4.  Pull on the free end. The mechanical advantage moves the object. When to reset, the Prusik locks the load while repositioning.
5.  Reset the Prusik, pull again until the object reaches the anchor.

## Notes
*   **Prusik Requirements:** To ensure grip, the Prusik cord must be thinner than the main rope. Common mistakes include using the same diameter cord.
*   **Wrap Integrity:** For friction hitches, wraps must be neat, parallel, and not overlap to maximize grip.
*   **Klemheist Directionality:** The Klemheist only grips in one direction; loading it the wrong way results in sliding.
*   **Trucker’s Hitch Overload:** Overtensioning the system (due to 3:1 advantage) can lead to the snapping of cordage or the pulling out of anchors.
*   **Prusik Wrap Count:** Two wraps may slip; three wraps are the default, but four are recommended for wet or synthetic rope.
*   **Key Takeaway:** Hands-on practice with real loads is necessary to fully understand the physics of mechanical advantage and friction grip.

## Friction Hitch Selection Guide
| Scenario | Best Hitch | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Ascending a rope | Prusik (x2) | Bidirectional grip; slides both ways when unloaded |
| Hauling system ratchet | Klemheist | Directional; easy to reset |
| Tensioning a ridgeline | Trucker’s hitch | Mechanical advantage for maximum tension |
| Adjustable guy line | Taut-line hitch | Slides to adjust, holds under load |
| Gripping flat webbing | Klemheist | Works with flat material unlike Prusik |
| Emergency foot loop | Prusik | Fast to tie, reliable |

## Friction Hitch Tension Table
| Application | Tension Level | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Tarp ridgeline | Moderate | Should be taut with slight sag; overtightening rips grommets |
| Load securing | High | As tight as the rope and anchors can handle |
| Clothesline | Light-moderate | Only needs to prevent sagging under wet clothes |
| Hide stretching | Moderate-high | Even tension across all tie points |
| Fence line | High | Needs to resist animal pressure |
