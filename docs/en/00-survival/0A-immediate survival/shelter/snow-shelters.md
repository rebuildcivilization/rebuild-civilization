# Snow Shelters

## Objective
To maintain interior temperatures near 0 degrees C even when outside air drops to -40.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Snow (any type).
*   Available time.
*   Firm snow for the snow trench.
*   Wind-packed or dense slab snow for igloos.
*   Deep snow around evergreens for tree wells.

## Material
*   Snow (roughly 90% trapped air).
*   Packed snow.
*   Solid ice.
*   Pine boughs (for floor insulation).
*   A sleeping pad or foam pad.
*   A pack or fabric (for sleeping on top of the insulation).

## System
*   **Dead-Air Principle:** Air is a poor conductor of heat, so a wall of packed snow acts like a thick blanket between you and the killing cold outside.

## Technology
*   **Insulation:** Snow provides insulation through trapped air.
*   **Thermal Conductivity:**
    *   Thermal conductivity of snow: 0.05-0.25 W/m-K (depending on density).
    *   Thermal conductivity of still air: 0.025 W/m-K.
    *   Thermal conductivity of solid ice: 2.2 W/m-K.

## Tools
*   Digging implement.
*   Stick or ski pole (for ventilation checks and hollowing).
*   Gloved hand (for smoothing the ceiling).
*   Sticks of known length (30 cm) (to check wall thickness).
*   Upright stick, brightly colored cloth, or pile of dark objects (to mark the shelter).
*   Tarp or emergency blanket.

## Process
### Types of Snow Shelters
| Shelter Type | Snow Needed | Build Time | Best For | Skill Level |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Quinzhee | Any snow, 60 cm+ depth | 3-4 hours (includes settling) | Most conditions, any snow type | Beginner |
| Igloo | Wind-packed or dense slab snow | 2-3 hours | Arctic conditions, firm snow | Advanced |
| Tree well shelter | Deep snow around evergreens | 30-60 minutes | Emergency, heavy snowfall areas | Beginner |
| Snow trench | Firm snow, 1 m+ depth | 1-2 hours | Quick overnight, firm snowpack | Beginner |

### Universal Snow Shelter Principles
1. **Entrance Below Sleeping Platform:** Cold air is denser and sinks, so the entrance tunnel must be lower than the sleeping area to let cold air drain out.
2. **Dome the Ceiling:** A domed or arched ceiling forces meltwater to run down the walls instead of dripping.
3. **Ventilation Is Non-Negotiable:** Maintain at least one ventilation hole (fist-sized minimum) in the ceiling or upper wall to prevent carbon dioxide and carbon monoxide buildup.
4. **Wall Thickness:** Maintain a minimum of 30 cm (12 inches) wall thickness.
5. **Mark Your Shelter:** Mark the shelter to prevent collapse risk and aid in location.

### Specific Shelter Processes
**Snow Trench (Fastest Option):**
1. Dig a trench 60-75 cm wide (shoulder width), 90 cm deep, and long enough to lie in (about 2.5 meters).
2. Pile excavated snow along both sides.
3. Cut snow blocks or lean branches, skis, or poles across the top of the trench. Cover these with a tarp, emergency blanket, or more snow blocks.
4. Insulate the floor with pine boughs, a sleeping pad, or packed snow covered with any fabric.
5. Block one end completely and leave the other end as the entrance, partially blocked with a pack or snow block.

**Tree Well Shelter Process:**
1. Find a large spruce or fir with low-hanging branches and a visible well around the trunk (well should be at least waist-deep).
2. Carefully climb or slide into the well; the branches above serve as the roof.
3. Dig out the well floor to create more headroom and pack the walls.
4. Add boughs from nearby dead trees to the floor for insulation and pull lower branches down to anchor gaps with snow.

### Staying Dry Inside
*   Smooth the ceiling so water runs down walls.
*   Sleep on insulation (pine boughs, foam pad, pack), not bare snow.
*   Remove wet outer layers before entering.
*   Keep the entrance partially open for air circulation.
*   Do not cook inside unless the shelter is large with excellent ventilation.

## Notes
*   A sealed snow shelter traps exhaled CO2 at dangerous levels within hours.
*   A difference of even 30 cm (12 inches) between the entrance floor and the sleeping platform makes a measurable difference in temperature.
*   Loose, deep snow around trees can collapse inward if the snow is powdery and unconsolidated, making tree wells risky.
*   The key takeaway is that snow shelters maintain temperatures near 0 degrees C because packed snow traps 90% of the air.
