# Animal Fibers: Natural Cordage Materials

## Objective
To utilize the strongest natural cordage materials available (sinew, rawhide, and gut) for survival and critical applications where plant fibers are inadequate.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   An animal carcass or game is required to harvest sinew, rawhide, and gut.
*   Timing is critical: Harvest tissues as soon as possible after the kill, especially in warm weather, as decomposition begins within hours.

## Material
*   **Sinew (Tendon):** Premier animal cordage material, stronger than any plant fiber, naturally adhesive when wet, and separates into fine threads.
*   **Rawhide (Untanned Hide):** Serves heavy-duty lashing functions; not technically a fiber.
*   **Gut (Intestine):** Produces a strong, flexible, and water-resistant cord.

## System
The system relies on the inherent physical properties of animal tissues, specifically their tensile strength, elasticity, and natural adhesion when wet.

## Technology
*   **Natural Adhesion:** Wet sinew becomes pliable and sticky, shrinking as it dries to create a self-locking bond suitable for tool hafting without additional glue or knots.
*   **Tensile Strength Superiority:** Animal fibers measure strength in hundreds of pounds, compared to plant fibers measuring in pounds.
*   **Water Resistance:** Gut cord offers moderate resistance, while rawhide has poor resistance (softens when wet).

## Tools
*   Dull blade or thumbnail (for cleaning and separating sinew).
*   Smooth stone or bone (for pounding dried sinew).
*   Dull scraper (bone, stone, or knife back) (for fleshing the hide).
*   Hard surface or frame/peg (for drying the hide).
*   Hardwood ash (for dehairing rawhide).

## Process
### Sinew Processing
1.  **Harvesting:**
    *   **Back Sinew:** Make a shallow cut along the spine from neck to hip, peel the hide back, and carefully separate the sinew from the meat.
    *   **Leg Sinew:** Skin the lower leg below the knee/hock joint and cut the large tendon free at both ends.
2.  **Cleaning:** Remove all meat and membrane from the raw sinew.
3.  **Drying:** Lay flat or drape over a stick in a well-ventilated area. Sinew dries in 1-3 days, becoming hard and translucent.
4.  **Preparing for Use:** Pound dried sinew to separate into fibers, then moisten before twisting or wrapping. The resulting wet sinew shrinks and tightens upon drying.
5.  **Cordage Creation:** Separate dried sinew into fiber bundles, moisten, use the reverse-wrap (two-ply twist) method, and splice wet fibers by overlapping them to utilize natural adhesion.

### Rawhide Processing
1.  **Fleshing:** Lay the fresh hide hair-side down and scrape all meat, fat, and membrane from the inner surface.
2.  **Dehair (Optional):** Use the Ash method (soak in hardwood ash/water for 3-5 days) or the Folding method (fold hair-side in and keep damp for 2-3 days).
3.  **Rinse:** Wash thoroughly in clean water.
4.  **Drying:** Stretch the hide flat or peg it to the ground until stiff and board-like.
5.  **Cutting Lacing:** Cut strips spirally from a round piece of hide. Widths range from 1/8 inch (fine work) to 1/2 to 3/4 inch (heavy duty).

### Gut Processing
1.  **Cleaning:** Slit the intestine lengthwise and wash thoroughly to remove all contents and inner mucous lining.
2.  **Cutting:** Cut the cleaned intestine into strips 1/4 to 1/2 inch wide.
3.  **Twisting:** While wet, twist each strip tightly along its length to increase strength.
4.  **Drying:** Tie twisted strips between fixed points and pull taut. The gut dries, shrinking and hardening into a round, translucent cord.
5.  **Oil (Optional):** Rubbing dried gut cord with animal fat improves water resistance and flexibility.

## Comparison Table
| Material | Strength (relative) | Processing Time | Availability | Water Resistance | Best Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sinew | Very high | 1-3 days (drying) | Any large game | Good | Bowstrings, sewing, hafting |
| Rawhide | Very high | 1-5 days | Any large game | Poor (softens) | Heavy lashing, webbing |
| Gut | High | 1-2 days | Any game with intestines | Moderate | Fishing line, snares |
| Plant fiber | Low-moderate | Hours to weeks | Widespread | Varies | General cordage |

## Key Takeaways
*   **Sinew:** The strongest and most versatile animal fiber; harvest both back sinew (long pieces) and leg sinew (short pieces).
*   **Self-Locking Property:** Wet sinew is naturally adhesive and shrinks as it dries, creating a self-locking bond.
*   **Rawhide Caution:** Must be kept dry; softens dramatically when wet.
*   **Gut Utility:** Nearly invisible in water, making it excellent for fishing leaders.
*   **Efficiency:** A single deer-sized animal provides enough material for weeks of survival use across multiple applications.

Notes
*   Animal tissues begin decomposing within hours in warm weather, emphasizing the need for rapid processing.
*   Sinew cordage strength: A two-ply sinew cord the thickness of paracord inner strand (roughly 1mm) can hold 50-80 pounds.
*   Rawhide shrinkage is a key advantage for tight lashings, but its poor water resistance is a significant hazard.
*   Gut cord is particularly effective as a fishing leader due to its near-invisibility in water.
