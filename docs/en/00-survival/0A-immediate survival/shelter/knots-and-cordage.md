# Knots and Cordage

## Objective
Rope is one of the oldest human technologies and one of the most critical. Knowing how to make cordage from plants and tie knots that hold under load is a foundation skill that unlocks almost everything else in survival.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
### For Cordage
- Plant fibers: inner bark of dead branches (basswood, cedar, willow), dried grasses, stinging nettle stalks, cattail leaves, yucca leaves, or any long fibrous plant material.
- Water (soaking softens fibers).
- A flat rock or your thigh (for rolling fibers).

### For Knot Practice
- Any piece of rope, cord, shoelace, or vine (1–2 meters).
- A pole, branch, or post to tie to.
- Your hands and patience.

## Material
- Inner bark of dead branches (basswood, cedar, willow).
- Dried grasses.
- Stinging nettle stalks.
- Cattail leaves.
- Yucca leaves.
- Any long fibrous plant material.

## System
### Cordage Making System (Reverse Twist Method)
- The fundamental technique for turning loose fibers into strong rope, used in every piece of handmade cordage in human history.
- The reverse twist method: Fold a pencil-thick bundle of inner bark or nettle fibers roughly in half, pinch the fold, twist the near ply away from you 3–5 times, then cross it over the far ply and repeat. The opposing twists lock together.
- Splicing Rule: When one ply gets short, lay a new bundle alongside it, overlapping by at least 8 cm (3 inches). Stagger splices by never adding fibers to both plies at the same point.

### Knot System (8 Essential Knots)
- **Bowline:** Creates a fixed loop that won’t slip or tighten under load (Rescue Loop).
- **Clove Hitch:** Ties a rope to a pole or post quickly (Quick Attach).
- **Taut-Line Hitch:** Creates a loop that slides to tighten but grips under load (Adjustable Grip).
- **Square Knot (Reef Knot):** Joins two ends of the same rope for binding (Binding Knot). Critical sequence: Right over left, THEN left over right.
- **Figure-8 Knot:** A bulky knot at the end of a rope that prevents it from pulling through a hole or device (Stopper).
- **Trucker’s Hitch:** Creates a pulley-like system for extreme tightening, giving roughly 3:1 mechanical advantage (Mechanical Advantage).
- **Sheet Bend:** Joins two ropes of different thickness (Rope Joiner). A double sheet bend is more secure for slippery ropes.
- **Timber Hitch:** Grips a log, pole, or cylindrical object for dragging or hoisting (Drag and Haul).

### Lashing System (Square Lashing)
- Used to bind two poles at a right angle to build frames, shelters, tables, racks, and towers.
- Process: Start with a clove hitch on the vertical pole. Place the horizontal pole on top at a right angle. Wrap the rope: go over the horizontal pole, behind the vertical pole, over the horizontal pole on the other side, behind the vertical pole. Make 3–4 complete wrapping turns, keeping them tight and parallel. Perform 2–3 frapping turns between the poles. Finish with a clove hitch on the opposite pole.

## Technology
- Rope making and cordage are prerequisites for building shelters, setting traps, fishing, climbing, hauling, and binding splints.
- Cordage strength: Good plant-fiber cordage the thickness of a pencil should hold 10–20 kg (22–44 lbs).

## Tools
- A flat rock or your thigh (for rolling fibers).

## Process
### Part 1: Making Cordage from Plant Fibers
1. **Harvesting Fibers:** Find fibrous plant material (inner bark, dried grasses, stinging nettle stalks, cattail leaves, yucca leaves).
2. **Process Fibers:** Strip fibers into the longest, thinnest strands; remove woody bits. If stiff, soak them in water for 15–30 minutes.
3. **Reverse Twist Method:** Take a bundle, fold roughly in half, pinch the fold (creating two plies). Twist the near ply away from you 3–5 times. Cross the near ply over the far ply. Repeat, maintaining tension so the twists lock together. For splicing, overlap new bundles by at least 8 cm.

### Part 2: The 8 Essential Knots
Practice each knot until it can be tied with eyes closed.

### Part 3: Square Lashing
1. Start with a clove hitch on the vertical pole below the horizontal pole.
2. Place the horizontal pole on top at a right angle.
3. Wrap the rope in a square pattern (over horizontal, behind vertical, over horizontal, behind vertical), pulling each wrap tight.
4. Make 3–4 complete wrapping turns.
5. Perform 2–3 frapping turns between the poles, pulling the lashing even tighter.
6. Finish with a clove hitch on the opposite pole.

## Notes
- **Strength Test:** Good plant-fiber cordage should hold 10–20 kg (22–44 lbs).
- **Critical Error:** Using the square knot to join two ropes under tension will cause it to slip; a sheet bend should be used instead.
- **Cordage Failure Point:** The most common mistake in cordage is splicing fibers at the same point for both plies.
- **Practice Rule:** Practice knots when calm and with light, as they will be needed in difficult survival situations.
