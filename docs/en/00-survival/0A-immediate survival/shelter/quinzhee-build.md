# Quinzhee Build

## Objective
To construct a Quinzhee, which is a hollowed-out mound of piled snow, serving as a versatile emergency shelter for winter survival.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- **Snow:** Any snow type works (wet, dry, powdery, or dense).
- **Skill Level:** Beginner skill level required.
- **Time:** Varies based on the required build time.

## Material
- Snow (for the mound).
- Insulation materials for the floor (pine boughs, dry grass, bark).
- Firewood (for an outside fire).
- Guide sticks (12-15 sticks, exactly 30 cm or 12 inches long).
- Tarp or ground sheet.

## System
- **Principle:** Sintering. The snow crystals must bond together over time to achieve structural integrity.
- **Function:** Provides excellent insulation for winter survival.

## Technology
- **Quinzhee vs. Igloo:** A quinzhee requires less skill and material than an igloo, which needs dense, cuttable snow.

## Tools
**Minimum:**
- Something to dig with (shovel, cooking pot, flat piece of bark, snowshoe, or hands).

**Ideal:**
- A proper shovel or entrenching tool.
- 12-15 sticks, each exactly 30 cm (12 inches) long (to guide wall thickness).
- A saw or knife for trimming the entrance.
- A tarp or ground sheet for the sleeping platform.

## Process
### Phase 1: Mounding (45-60 minutes)
1. **Site Selection:** Select a flat or gently sloped site protected from wind, avoiding areas under heavy tree canopy or slopes steeper than about 10 degrees.
2. **Base Area:** Stamp out a circular base area roughly 3 meters (10 feet) in diameter for a 2-person quinzhee. Add 60 cm to the diameter for each additional person.
3. **Piling:** Pile snow into the center. Mix snow from different layers if possible to promote sintering.
4. **Shaping:** Shape the mound into a rough dome, avoiding steep sides to distribute structural loads.

### Phase 2: Settling (1-2 hours)
- **Crucial Step:** Do not skip this period. Freshly piled snow has no structural integrity.
- **Waiting:** Wait at least 1 hour. Wait 2 hours in very cold conditions (below -20 C).
- **Productive Use:** Gather insulation for the floor, collect firewood, prepare food/water, and insert guide sticks perpendicular to the surface to indicate when to stop hollowing.

### Phase 3: Hollowing (45-90 minutes)
1. **Entrance:** Choose the entrance location on the downwind side. Dig a tunnel entrance at ground level, angling slightly downward then back up.
2. **Interior Carving:** Begin hollowing upward and outward, working from the top down (remove ceiling snow first).
3. **Stopping Point:** Stop digging when the tips of the guide sticks (30 cm wall thickness) are exposed.
4. **Ceiling:** Shape the interior ceiling into a smooth dome to eliminate flat spots.
5. **Sleeping Platform:** Carve a raised sleeping platform, 30-45 cm (12-18 inches) higher than the entrance floor.
6. **Ventilation:** Poke a ventilation hole through the ceiling at the highest point, fist-sized (8-10 cm diameter), angled at roughly 45 degrees.

### Phase 4: Finishing
1. **Insulation:** Insulate the sleeping platform with pine boughs (tips up, stems down, layered like shingles), a tarp, or dry grass (a 10 cm layer of boughs makes a significant difference).
2. **Entrance Block:** Create an entrance block using a backpack, a cut snow block, or a stuff sack filled with snow to reduce airflow without sealing it completely.
3. **Marking:** Mark the outside of the quinzhee with a tall stick or dark objects.

### Emergency Variant: The Quick Quinzhee
For immediate shelter (less than 2 hours of daylight):
1. Pile snow for 20 minutes (even a 1-meter-high mound helps).
2. Pack it firmly.
3. Wait only 30 minutes (risky).
4. Hollow just enough space to curl up inside.
5. Dig the entrance tunnel at least 30 cm below body level.
6. Poke a ventilation hole immediately.

## Notes
**Key Takeaways:**
- The settling period (1-2 hours) is mandatory for structural integrity.
- Guide sticks pushed in from outside (30 cm length) prevent walls from being too thin.
- The entrance must be lower than the sleeping platform so cold air drains out.
- Always maintain a ventilation hole in the ceiling to prevent CO2 buildup.

**Common Failures and Fixes:**
- **Roof collapse during hollowing:** Insufficient settling time; solution is to wait longer (2+ hours in extreme cold).
- **Dripping ceiling:** Flat spots on the interior dome; solution is to smooth the ceiling into a continuous curve.
- **Cold interior:** Entrance too large or at the same level as the sleeping area; solution is to reduce entrance size or raise the sleeping platform.
- **Blocked ventilation:** Snow drift or frost plugging the hole; solution is to check every few hours and keep a stick nearby to clear it.
- **Wet sleeping gear:** Direct contact with snow walls; solution is to keep gear 5 cm from walls or use an insulation layer on the platform.
