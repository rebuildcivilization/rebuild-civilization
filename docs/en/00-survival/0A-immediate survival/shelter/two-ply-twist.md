# Two-Ply Twist

## Objective
To produce balanced, self-locking cordage using the reverse wrap technique, which is the single most important cordage technique in survival, ensuring the cord will not unravel.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Materials:** Loose fibers.
*   **Pre-preparation:** Spend time before starting to separate, clean, and organize fiber bundles by length and thickness.

## Material
*   Fibers (for cordage).

## System
*   **Core Principle:** The two-ply reverse wrap works by setting twist and wrap in opposing directions. Each ply is twisted in one direction, and the plies are wrapped around each other in the opposite direction. This opposing force results in a cord that locks itself and will not unravel.
*   **Fundamental Concept:** The basis of every rope, cord, thread, and yarn.

## Technology
*   **Reverse Wrap Method:** The core technique used to achieve the self-locking property.
*   **Four-Beat Cycle:** The fundamental motion cycle: Twist, Cross, Pinch, Repeat.

## Tools
*   Fingers (for manipulating fibers).
*   Thumb and forefinger (for pinching and twisting).
*   Dominant hand and non-dominant hand (to manage the work).
*   Thigh (for faster production via thigh rolling).

## Process
### Starting the Cord
Two methods exist:
1.  **Method A: The Loop Start (Preferred):**
    *   Take a bundle of prepared fibers (roughly 18-24 inches long and the thickness of a pencil lead).
    *   Find the approximate center and offset it by 2-3 inches so the legs are slightly different lengths (staggering the first splice).
    *   Pinch the bundle at this off-center point.
    *   Twist the short side away from you (clockwise for right-handed).
    *   Fold the bundle at the pinch point.
    *   **Method B: The Knot Start (Simpler):**
    *   Take two separate bundles of fiber.
    *   Hold them together and tie a simple overhand knot at one end, leaving 1-inch tails above the knot.

### The Reverse Wrap Motion
*   **Hand Position:** Hold the cord between the thumb and forefinger of the non-dominant hand (the “pinch hand”), about 1 inch below the last completed section.
*   **The Four-Beat Cycle:**
    *   **Beat 1 — Twist the far ply:** Twist the ply farthest from the body clockwise (away from you) by 3-5 twists depending on fiber thickness.
    *   **Beat 2 — Cross:** Bring the now-twisted far ply toward you, crossing it over the near ply.
    *   **Beat 3 — Advance the pinch:** Move the pinch hand forward to grip just below the crossover point, locking in the wrap.
    *   **Beat 4 — Repeat:** Twist the ply that is now farthest from you clockwise, cross it over, advance the pinch. Continue.

### Detailed Step-by-Step (For Right-Handed Example)
1.  Hold the work in the left hand, pinching the two plies between the left thumb and forefinger.
2.  Identify Ply A (upper ply, farther from the body) and Ply B (lower ply, closer to the body).
3.  With the right thumb and forefinger, grip Ply A about 1-2 inches from the pinch point.
4.  Roll Ply A clockwise, applying moderate twist.
5.  Bring Ply A down toward you, crossing it over Ply B (Ply A is now in the lower position).
6.  Advance the left-hand pinch to grip at the crossover point.
7.  Repeat the cycle for Ply B (now the upper ply).

### Maintaining Tension
*   **Rule 1: Even Ply Thickness:** Both plies must contain roughly the same amount of fiber. Add fiber to balance them if one ply looks thinner.
*   **Rule 2: Consistent Twist:** Each ply must receive the same amount of twist to prevent the cord from kinking or spiraling.

### Splicing (Adding Fiber)
*   **When to Splice:** Begin a splice when a ply has about 3-4 inches of fiber remaining; do not wait until the ply runs out.
*   **Splice Procedure:** Lay a new fiber bundle alongside the thinning ply, overlapping by the full remaining length of the old fiber (minimum 3-4 inches). Continue twisting normally for 2-3 twists until the splice is secure.
*   **Stagger Splices:** Never splice both plies at the same location. Maintain at least 3-4 inches between splices on different plies.
*   **Testing Splices:** Give the cord a sharp tug; a good splice holds without slipping.

### Finishing the Cord
*   **Overhand knot:** The simplest finish to prevent unraveling.
*   **Whipping:** Wrap a thin fiber tightly around the last inch of the cord, then tuck the tail under the wrapping for a neater, lower-profile finish.
*   **Back-splice:** Unlay the last 2 inches of each ply and tuck the loose fibers back into the body of the cord to create a tapered end.

### Speed and Rhythm
*   **Production Rate:** Beginners produce roughly 1-2 feet per hour; with practice, this can reach 4-8 feet per hour for thin cord.
*   **Tips for Faster Twisting:**
    1.  Develop rhythm: Twist-cross-pinch should be a fluid motion.
    2.  Pre-prepare fibers.
    3.  Use your thigh for rolling (thigh rolling).
    4.  Anchor the work to give resistance against twisting.

### Practice Drill
*   Practice with grass or jute twine.
*   Goal: Time how long it takes to produce 3 feet of functional cord (holds 10+ lbs), aiming for under 30 minutes.

## Notes
*   **Troubleshooting Summary:**
    *   *Cord unravels when released:* Apply more twist to each ply (4-5 rolls minimum).
    *   *Cord spirals/coils:* Reduce twist per ply to 2-3 rolls.
    *   *Cord has lumpy/uneven sections:* Add or remove fiber to balance plies.
    *   *Splices pull apart:* Increase overlap to at least 3 inches and twist tightly through the splice zone.
    *   *Cord feels stiff:* Lightly mist fibers with water before twisting.
    *   *Cord is limp and weak:* Let fibers dry slightly; increase twist count.
*   **Key Takeaways Summary:**
    *   The reverse wrap locks the cord by opposing twist and wrap.
    *   The motion is the four-beat cycle (Twist, Cross, Pinch, Repeat).
    *   Balance (equal thickness and equal twist) is critical for a straight cord.
    *   Splice when 3-4 inches remain, and always stagger splices by 3-4 inches.
    *   Consistent tension and rhythm are more important than speed.
