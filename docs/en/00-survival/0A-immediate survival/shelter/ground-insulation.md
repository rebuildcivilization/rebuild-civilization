# Ground Insulation

## Objective
To prevent the core temperature from dropping dangerously when lying directly on cold ground, making the sleeping surface life-support equipment.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   A survival shelter.
*   Cold ground (e.g., 5°C or 41°F).

## Material
### Bough Bed Materials
*   Conifer boughs (pine, spruce, fir, cedar).
*   Deciduous leafy branches (last resort).

### Grass Matting Materials
*   Long grass (30+ cm / 12+ inches).
*   Cordage, vine, or bark strips (for bundled mattresses).

### Other Ground Insulation Materials
| Material | Depth Needed | Pros | Cons |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Dry leaves | 25-30 cm (10-12 in) | Abundant in deciduous forests | Compress fast, rustle loudly, attract insects |
| Cattail fluff | 10-15 cm (4-6 in) | Outstanding insulator | Seasonal, limited quantity |
| Moss | 8-10 cm (3-4 in) | Good insulation, soft | Often damp — must dry first |
| Bark slabs | 2-3 layers with grass between | Durable, waterproof base | Hard to harvest without tools |
| Bracken fern | 15-20 cm (6-8 in) | Springy, good coverage | Seasonal availability |
| Corn stalks / straw | 15-20 cm (6-8 in) | Excellent if near fields | Location dependent |

## System
### Layered System for Cold Conditions
In temperatures below -5°C (23°F), a multi-layer system is recommended:
1. **Bottom**: Bark slabs or flat rocks to create a moisture barrier from the ground.
2. **Middle**: 15-20 cm (6-8 inches) of boughs or bundled grass for primary insulation.
3. **Top**: 5-8 cm (2-3 inches) of soft dry grass or moss for comfort and fine insulation.
4. **Cover**: If available, a layer of large leaves, bark, or fabric over the top to trap body heat.

## Technology
*   The ground conducts heat away from the body 25 times faster than cold air.
*   Lying directly on cold ground at 5°C (41°F) will drop the core temperature dangerously within 2–3 hours.

## Tools
*   Tools are implied for harvesting (cutting branches, harvesting bark slabs).

## Process
### Bough Bed Construction Steps
1. **Clear the ground**: Remove rocks, roots, and debris to create a flat surface roughly 60 cm × 200 cm (2 × 6.5 feet).
2. **Lay containment logs** (optional): Place two logs, 10-15 cm (4-6 inches) in diameter, parallel along the long edges to prevent spreading.
3. **Harvest boughs**: Cut branches 60-90 cm (2-3 feet) long from multiple trees.
4. **Lay the first course**: Place boughs with cut ends pointing toward the head of the bed, curved side facing up like shingles.
5. **Build up layers**: Add 3-4 more courses, alternating the angle slightly each time, pressing down firmly after each course. The bed must compress to at least 10 cm (4 inches) thick under full weight.
6. **Top layer**: Lay the final course with the soft needle side facing up for comfort and flatten poking stems.

### Grass Matting Construction Steps
**Loose Grass Fill Method**
1. Gather large armloads of dry grass, creating a pile roughly the size of a large garbage bag.
2. Create a depression or frame (logs, rocks) to contain the grass.
3. Stuff the grass loosely into the frame, building up 25-30 cm (10-12 inches) of depth.
4. Test by lying on it and add more until ground cold is no longer felt.

**Bundled Grass Mattress Method**
1. **Gather grass**: Pull or cut long grass stems, aiming for stems at least 30 cm (12 inches) long.
2. **Make bundles**: Compress a double-handful of grass into a tight cylinder about 10-12 cm (4-5 inches) in diameter. Tie bundles at two points with cordage.
3. **Arrange bundles**: Lay bundles side by side across the width of the sleeping area, needing roughly 15-20 bundles for a full bed.
4. **Lash together** (optional): Thread cordage through the bundles lengthwise to prevent separation.
5. **Add a loose top layer**: Scatter loose grass over the bundles for comfort.

### Testing Your Bough Bed
Lie down and press your hand between your body and the bed surface:
*   If you feel cold ground through the boughs: add more material.
*   If boughs poke uncomfortably: rearrange the top layer or add a grass covering.
*   If the bed bottoms out when rolling over: add containment logs and more filling.

## Notes
*   **Material Degradation**: Ground insulation degrades due to body moisture, compression, and biological breakdown.
*   **Maintenance**: The top layer must be replaced with fresh material every 2-3 days. Move the old top layer to the bottom.
*   **Moisture Warning**: If bedding gets wet, replace the entire layer, as wet insulation actively pulls heat from the body.
*   **Bough Bed Specifics**: Yew trees are highly toxic; if uncertain, use grass instead.
*   **Key Takeaway**: Start with 20-30 cm (8-12 inches) of loose material, as it compresses to half its original volume under body weight.
