# Tool Hafting

## Objective
To haft a stone or bone tool head onto a wooden handle, using a combination of adhesive and binding, as neither alone is sufficient for a reliable joint. Hafting transforms a cutting edge into a usable tool, providing leverage, reach, and safety.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Wood Selection:** Select a seasoned hardwood handle; green wood shrinks as it dries, loosening the head.
*   **Material Readiness:** Tools must have a head (stone or bone) and a handle (wood).
*   **Adhesive/Binding:** A combination of adhesive and binding materials is necessary.

## Material
### Adhesives
*   Pine pitch glue (Pine resin melted with roughly 1 part charcoal to 3 parts resin).
*   Hide glue (Animal skin/hooves boiled).
*   Birch bark tar (Birch bark heated without flame).
*   Beeswax blend (Beeswax + resin + charcoal).
*   No adhesive (relies entirely on binding).

### Binding Materials
*   Wet rawhide (Excellent wet strength, very high shrink factor).
*   Fresh sinew (Excellent wet strength, high shrink factor).
*   Plant cordage (Good wet strength, no shrink factor).
*   Salvaged wire (Excellent wet strength, no shrink factor).
*   Wet leather strips (Good wet strength, moderate shrink factor).

## System
Hafting is the most important technology in early human toolmaking, relying on the principles of mechanical fit, binding, and adhesive to create a secure joint between a tool head and a handle.

## Technology
### Hafting Methods
There are three primary approaches:

1.  **Socket Hafting (Wrap-and-Bind):**
    *   **Best for:** axes, adzes, hammers, spear points.
    *   **Process:** Prepare handle (14-24 inches long, 1-1.5 inches diameter). Shape a seat on the handle. Score contact surfaces. Apply adhesive. Seat the head. Bind tightly with wet rawhide, sinew, or strong cordage in a crossing pattern. Seal the binding with a final adhesive coat.

2.  **Split-Handle Hafting:**
    *   **Best for:** axes, tomahawks, large chisels.
    *   **Process:** Split the handle 3-4 inches deep. Insert the tool head into the split. Drive small wedges into the split on either side of the head. Bind below the split to prevent travel down the handle and above the head to clamp the split closed.

3.  **Socket-and-Peg Hafting:**
    *   **Best for:** ground stone axes, hammers, heavy chopping tools.
    *   **Process:** Create the socket (drill/carve for stone; carve/drill for bone). Shape the handle end to fit the socket snugly. Insert the handle from the bottom. Drive a wedge into the top of the handle to lock it in place. Soak briefly in water for the wood to swell.

### Testing Procedures
Before trusting a tool for heavy use, test it by:
1.  Visual inspection (look for gaps).
2.  Wobble test (grip the handle and rock the head).
3.  Light impact test (tap the tool against a log 10-15 times).
4.  Moderate use test (chop softwood for 5 minutes).
5.  Re-tighten (most hafts need re-tightening after the first hour of use).

### Tool-Specific Guides
*   **Axe / Hatchet:** Handle length 14-20 inches (35-50 cm). Attachment via split-handle or socket method. Critical: the cutting edge must be **parallel** to the handle for chopping.
*   **Adze:** Handle length 12-18 inches (30-45 cm). Attachment via socket or wrap-and-bind. Critical: the cutting edge must be **perpendicular** to the handle (like a hoe).
*   **Spear:** Handle length 5-7 feet (1.5-2 m). Attachment via split-handle with the point wedged into the split. Requires extensive binding. Recommended to add a foreshaft (short intermediate piece) to replace broken points.
*   **Knife:** Handle length 4-6 inches (10-15 cm). Attachment via wrap-and-bind, with the blade tang seated in a split or groove for stone blades.

## Tools
*   Bow drill (used for creating sockets).
*   Knife or wedge (used for splitting handles).
*   Charcoal (used as filler in glue).
*   Stone or shell container (for melting pitch).

## Process
### Making Pine Pitch Glue
1.  Collect Pine resin and Charcoal powder.
2.  Melt the resin over low heat (not direct flame).
3.  Stir in charcoal powder (roughly 1 part charcoal to 3 parts resin). Add fiber filler (optional).
4.  Mix thoroughly.
5.  Apply hot.
6.  Reheat to reapply or adjust.

### The Cross-Wrap Binding Pattern (Strongest General-Purpose Binding)
1.  Anchor the cord by trapping the end under the first wrap.
2.  Wrap 3-4 tight turns perpendicular to the handle, covering the base of the head.
3.  Cross diagonally over the head to the opposite side.
4.  Wrap 3-4 turns on the other side of the head.
5.  Cross diagonally back, forming an X over the head.
6.  Repeat the X pattern 2-3 more times.
7.  Finish with 3-4 perpendicular wraps and tuck the end under.
8.  If using rawhide or sinew, let dry completely before use (12-24 hours).

## Notes
*   **Key Takeaway:** Dry wood only must be used; green handles shrink and loosen.
*   **Core Principle:** The combination of adhesive and binding is necessary for a durable joint.
*   **Field Efficiency:** Pine pitch glue is the easiest and most effective field adhesive to make.
*   **Binding Advantage:** Wet rawhide and sinew shrink as they dry, creating self-tightening bindings that can hold for months.
*   **Risk Mitigation:** Testing the tool (especially the wobble test and light impact test) before heavy use is critical to prevent serious injury.
*   **Carry Requirement:** Repair materials such as extra cordage, pitch sticks, and a spare handle blank should be carried to allow for in-field re-hafting.
*   **Material Comparison Summary:**
    | Material | Wet Strength | Shrink Factor | Durability | Availability |
    | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
    | Wet rawhide | Excellent | Very high (10-15%)
    | Fresh sinew | Excellent | Very high |
    | Plant cordage | Good | None |
    | Salvaged wire | Excellent | None |
    | Wet leather strips | Good | Moderate |
