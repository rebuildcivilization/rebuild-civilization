# Wood Selection | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To determine whether smoked food is safely preserved with pleasant flavor or contaminated with toxic compounds based on the wood burned.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

*   A wood source for smoking.
*   A basic understanding of smoke quality indicators.

## Material

### Wood Types
*   **Hardwoods:** Must be used; specifically from deciduous trees.
*   **Softwoods:** Must be avoided; from coniferous trees (needles and cones).
*   **Toxic Woods (Never Burn):**
    *   All conifers: Pine, spruce, fir, cedar, hemlock, yew, juniper (wood).
    *   Oleander (extremely toxic).
    *   Rhododendron / Azalea (grayanotoxins).
    *   Black walnut (juglone).
    *   Poison sumac, poison oak, poison ivy (urushiol).
    *   Treated/painted wood (Arsenic, lead, chromium, industrial toxins).
    *   Plywood, particle board, MDF (Formaldehyde and glue compounds).
    *   Driftwood (toxic chlorine compounds when burned).

### Identification Markers
*   **Oak:** Bark: deeply furrowed, grayish-brown; Wood: heavy, tight grain, light brown; Leaves (if present): lobed edges, alternate arrangement.
*   **Hickory:** Bark: shaggy, peeling in long strips; Wood: very heavy, nearly white sapwood with brown heartwood; Nuts: thick-shelled, edible.
*   **Apple/Cherry/Fruit Trees:** Bark: smooth to slightly rough, often with horizontal lenticels; Wood: fine-grained, reddish-brown heartwood; Leaves (if present): opposite arrangement, palmate shape.
*   **Maple:** Bark varies; sugar maple has shaggy plates, red maple is smoother; Wood: light-colored, close-grained, moderate weight; Leaves (if present): opposite arrangement, palmate shape.
*   **Ash:** Bark: diamond-pattern ridges on mature trees; Wood: light, straight-grained, flexible; Leaves (if present): compound, opposite arrangement.

## System

### The Rule: Hardwood Only
The single most important rule is to use only hardwoods from deciduous trees. Never burn softwoods from coniferous trees.

### Flavor Intensity and Pairing Logic
*   **Light:** Alder, Ash, Apple $\rightarrow$ delicate foods (fish, poultry, cheese)
*   **Medium:** Cherry, Maple, Birch, Beech, Pecan $\rightarrow$ versatile, most meats
*   **Heavy:** Oak, Hickory $\rightarrow$ robust meats (beef, game, pork shoulders)
*   **Aggressive:** Mesquite $\rightarrow$ short exposure only, thin cuts

### Mixing Woods
*   **Base wood (60-70%):** Oak, ash, or beech for steady, consistent smoke.
*   **Flavor wood (30-40%):** Fruit woods or hickory for character.
*   **Limitation:** Avoid mixing more than 3 wood types to prevent flavors from becoming indistinct or unpleasant.

## Technology

### Smoke Quality Indicators
*   **Ideal:** Thin, blue-white smoke (clean combustion, good flavor compounds).
*   **Too Much Moisture/Restricted Airflow:** Thick, white smoke (bitter flavor).
*   **Incomplete Combustion/Too Little Oxygen:** Gray or black smoke (open dampers or reduce fuel load).
*   **Fire Too Hot:** No visible smoke, shimmer of heat (burns clean but lacks smoking compounds; add wood, reduce airflow slightly).

## Tools

### Chunk Size
| Form | Size | Burn Time | Best Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Chips | 1-2 inch pieces | 20-30 minutes | Quick bursts of smoke, small smokers |
| Chunks | 3-4 inch pieces | 1-2 hours | Standard smoking sessions |
| Split logs | 4-6 inch diameter | 3-5 hours | Large chambers, long sessions |
| Sawdust | Fine particles | 15-20 minutes | Cold smoking generators, smoldering |

### Preparation Tools
*   Splitting tool (to create chips/chunks).
*   Air gaps between pieces during stacking.

## Process

### Wood Preparation
*   **Seasoning:** Fresh-cut wood (40-60% moisture) produces excessive steam.
    *   **Properly Seasoned Wood:** 15-20% moisture content.
    *   **Seasoning Steps:**
        1.  Split logs into pieces 3-4 inches across (never burn whole logs).
        2.  Stack off the ground with air gaps.
        3.  Cover the top to shed rain but leave sides open for airflow.
        4.  Wait **6-12 months** depending on climate and density.
    *   **Emergency Drying:** Split into thin strips (1-inch thick) and place near a fire for 24-48 hours, turning regularly.
*   **Bark:** Remove the bark when possible, as it burns at a different rate and produces harsher smoke. Stripping bark improves smoke quality.
*   **Soaking Wood (Myth vs. Reality):** Soaking wood chips in water is counterproductive because surface water must evaporate before smoking begins, and steam dilutes smoke concentration, producing more creosote.

## Notes

*   The core principle is that the antimicrobial compounds in all safe hardwood smoke perform the same preservation function regardless of species; flavor differences are secondary to safety.
*   The target smoke quality is a **steady, thin stream of blue-tinted smoke**.
*   If smoke smells wrong (acrid, sharp, chemical-smelling), the wood is likely toxic or resinous.
