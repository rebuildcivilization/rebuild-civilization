# Fire Making in Rain

## Objective

To build and sustain a fire in heavy rain or wet conditions by protecting the fire site, preparing reliable tinder, and exposing dry fuel before ignition.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

Fire building in rain is significantly more difficult because tinder, kindling, and fuel rapidly absorb moisture. Success depends on completing the preparation before applying the ignition source.

The fire still requires heat, fuel, and oxygen. Wet conditions mainly make it difficult to provide sufficiently dry fuel and tinder.

## Material

### Tinder

Use tinder that can ignite under difficult conditions and continue burning long enough to ignite the first kindling.

Suitable materials include:

* Dry natural tinder protected from rain.
* Very fine wood shavings.
* Dry fibers.
* Commercial or prepared fire starters.
* Cotton with petroleum jelly when available.

### Kindling

Prefer:

* Thin, dry twigs.
* Matchstick- to pencil-thick wood.
* Fine wood splinters.
* Dry material collected from protected locations.

### Fuel Wood

Use progressively larger pieces of wood.

When the outside of larger wood is wet:

* Remove wet bark and moss.
* Split the wood.
* Continue splitting until dry interior wood is exposed.
* Produce long, thin splinters for the first stages of the fire.

## System

### 1. Protect the fire site

Before attempting ignition, establish protection from precipitation.

The preferred solution is an overhead rain fly or tarp positioned so that rain cannot fall directly onto the tinder and initial fire.

If no tarp is available, natural protection can be used, such as the area beneath overhanging evergreen branches.

The fire must still have sufficient ventilation and must be positioned safely with respect to combustible vegetation and the shelter.

### 2. Prepare everything before ignition

Gather and prepare:

1. Tinder.
2. Fine kindling.
3. Larger kindling.
4. Fuel wood.
5. Ignition source.

Do not start the ignition process until enough material is ready to sustain the fire.

### 3. Find dry material

In wet weather, thin dry twigs can often be found beneath the overhanging branches of larger trees, especially evergreen trees.

If dry small fuel cannot be found:

1. Collect available wood.
2. Remove wet bark and moss.
3. Split larger pieces.
4. Continue splitting until relatively dry wood is exposed.
5. Produce thin splinters.

### 4. Establish the fire progressively

Start with the driest and finest material.

Once the tinder is burning reliably, gradually add thin kindling and then progressively larger fuel.

Avoid placing large wet pieces directly onto a weak flame.

## Technology

The essential technology is not a different ignition method but **moisture management**.

The sequence is:

**Protection → Dry tinder → Dry kindling → Exposed dry wood → Ignition → Progressive fuel**

## Tools

Useful equipment includes:

* Rain fly or tarp.
* Knife.
* Fire starter.
* Waterproof storage container.
* Dry tinder.
* Cordage for constructing an overhead cover.

## Process

1. Select a site protected from falling rain.
2. Erect an overhead rain fly when necessary.
3. Collect tinder and keep it protected.
4. Collect thin dry twigs from protected areas.
5. Collect larger fuel.
6. Remove wet bark and moss from unsuitable wood.
7. Split larger pieces to expose drier interior wood.
8. Prepare a large supply of thin splinters.
9. Arrange the tinder and kindling so that the flame can spread progressively.
10. Ignite the tinder.
11. Protect the initial flame from rain and excessive wind.
12. Add progressively larger pieces as the fire becomes established.
13. Maintain a reserve of prepared fuel under protection.

## Notes

* Heavy rain can defeat even a good ignition method if the fire site and fuel have not been prepared.
* The fire site should be protected before ignition rather than attempting to shelter the fire after it has started.
* Dry twigs may remain available beneath tree canopies even when exposed material is wet.
* Splitting wet wood can expose a relatively dry interior.
* Preparation time is critical. Trying to gather fuel while a small tinder flame is already burning greatly reduces the chance of success.
* Cotton and petroleum jelly can be effective as prepared tinder. Excess petroleum jelly, however, can saturate the cotton and make the fibers difficult to ignite.
* In cold conditions, a petroleum-jelly cotton starter may become difficult to remove from its container and difficult to ignite if it is frozen.
