# Tinder Preparation

## Objective
To prepare tinder for fire-starting, which is the critical first link in the fire chain, bridging a tiny spark or ember to a sustainable flame.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Tinder must be bone-dry; moisture below 10% is ideal.
- For ember-based fire starting, a tinder bundle must be constructed.

## Material
- 100% cotton or linen fabric (for char cloth).
- A small metal tin with a tight-fitting lid (e.g., altoids tin, small paint can).
- Fatwood (pine wood saturated with resin).
- Fungal materials (Amadou, Chaga, Cramp Ball).
- Other natural materials: Birch bark, Cedar bark, Cattail fluff, Dried grass, Thistle/milkweed down, Pine resin, Dryer lint, Steel wool (fine).
- Wax coating (beeswax, candle wax, rendered tallow) for waterproof starters.

## System
Fire-starting follows the chain: ignition source → tinder → kindling → fuel. Tinder is the bridge between the ignition source and the sustainable flame.

## Technology
- Char Cloth: Cotton or linen fabric carbonized through pyrolysis.
- Fatwood: Pine wood saturated with resin (pitch).
- Fungal Tinders: Biological materials (e.g., *Fomes fomentarius*, *Inonotus obliquus*, *Daldinia concentrica*).

## Tools
- Knife (for splitting fatwood and processing fungus).
- Smooth rock or mallet (for pounding amadou).
- Metal tin (for char cloth preparation and storage).

## Process
### Char Cloth Making
1. Cut fabric into squares roughly 2x2 inches (5x5 cm).
2. Pack the tin with fabric pieces, leaving a little room.
3. Punch a small hole in the tin lid (about 1/16 inch / 1.5 mm).
4. Place the sealed tin on hot coals or in a fire (moderate heat).
5. Watch the vent hole until smoke stops (typically 10–20 minutes).
6. Immediately plug the vent hole with a small stick or pebble to prevent burning if air enters.
7. Let it cool completely before opening.

### Fatwood Processing
1. Find dead pine stumps that have stood for 1–5 years.
2. Split fatwood into thin sticks (matchstick to pencil thickness).
3. For spark-based starting, shave fine curls off a fatwood stick.

### Fungal Tinder Processing
- Amadou: Cut the fungus, slice away the hard outer shell and pore layer, select the middle trama layer, slice into thin sheets (1/8 inch / 3 mm or thinner), and pound the sheets.
- Chaga: Dry it, break it up, and strike sparks onto it.
- Cramp Ball: Slice thin, dry thoroughly.

### Tinder Bundle Building
For ember-based fire starting (bow drill, hand drill, amadou), construct a nest-shaped ball:
1. **Inner layer**: Finest material (shredded cedar bark, dried grass fibers, cattail down).
2. **Middle layer**: Slightly coarser material (thin bark strips, fine wood shavings, dried leaf crumbles).
3. **Outer layer**: Coarsest material (larger bark strips, small dry twigs, pine needles).
4. Place the ember in the center, fold loosely, and blow steadily.

### Keeping Tinder Dry
- Body carry a small tin of char cloth and fatwood shavings in an inner pocket.
- Use a waterproof container (sealed tin or plastic).
- Use wax coating (melted wax) for waterproof starters.
- Learn to find and process tinder on the move.

## Other Natural Tinder Materials
| Material | Spark Catch | Ember Catch | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Birch bark (shredded) | Fair | Excellent | Contains flammable oils; works even damp |
| Cedar bark (shredded) | Poor | Excellent | Best as tinder bundle around an ember |
| Cattail fluff | Good | Excellent | Burns fast; mix with slower material |
| Dried grass (fine) | Poor | Good | Must be bone dry; use as bundle |
| Thistle/milkweed down | Good | Fair | Catches sparks but burns out in seconds |
| Pine resin | Good | N/A | Sticky; use on other materials to boost ignition |
| Dryer lint | Excellent | Excellent | Scavenge from abandoned laundromats early on |
| Steel wool (fine) | Excellent | N/A | Catches battery sparks; single use |

## Notes
- Synthetic fabrics (polyester, nylon, blends) will melt and produce toxic fumes; use only natural fibers (cotton, linen, hemp, jute).
- Char cloth is the most reliable spark-catching tinder.
- Fatwood lasts indefinitely if kept dry due to its resin content.
- The difference between freezing and warmth depends on proper tinder preparation.
