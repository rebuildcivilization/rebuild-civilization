# Trigger Stick

## Objective
To serve as the release mechanism that holds a spring snare or deadfall trap in its set position, allowing stored energy to be released instantly when an animal disturbs it.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Material:** Dry hardwood (Oak, maple, ash, hickory) is ideal. Softwoods compress and lose their notch shape after one use.
*   **Tools:** A sharp blade for carving notches.
*   **Skill:** The ability to carve precise notches and calibrate sensitivity.

## Material
*   **Trigger Stick Material:** Dry hardwood (Oak, maple, ash, hickory).
*   **Trap Components:** Sticks for the trigger stick, base pegs, and bait sticks.
*   **Secondary Components:** Cordage or bent wire (for Toggle Trigger rings).

## System
*   **System:** Powered traps, including spring snares, deadfalls, and cage traps.
*   **Core Function:** Every powered trap requires a mechanism that holds the trap in a loaded state and releases it upon contact.

## Technology
There are three primary trigger mechanisms:

1.  **The Hook Trigger (Most Common for Spring Snares):**
    *   **Mechanism:** Two sticks with opposing hooks lock together under tension.
    *   **Function:** The leader line pulls the trigger stick, which catches the base peg. An animal's pull pops the hooks apart, releasing the sapling.

2.  **The Toggle Trigger:**
    *   **Mechanism:** The trigger stick passes through a hole or ring in a stake.
    *   **Function:** The angle of the trigger stick determines sensitivity; changing the angle allows the animal's pull to slip the stick free from the ring.

3.  **The Tension Trigger (for Deadfalls):**
    *   **Mechanism:** An upright stick supports the weight of the deadfall rock or log on its top end, with a bait stick extending horizontally from the base.
    *   **Function:** A notch where the bait stick meets the upright creates a friction lock; pulling the bait slips the notch, causing the upright to topple.

## Tools
*   Sharp blade (for carving notches).
*   Measuring tools (for stick length and notch depth).
*   A test object (long twig) for calibration.

## Process
### Carving Principles
*   **Notch Geometry:** Must be flat-bottomed, carved perpendicular to the stick’s axis (90 degrees). The back wall should be vertical or slightly backward-angled to prevent undercutting.
*   **Surface Finish:** Contact surfaces must be smoothed to prevent unpredictable friction from rough bark or torn wood fibers.

### Calibration Steps
1.  **Hold Test:** Assemble the mechanism without the snare noose. Bend the sapling, hook the trigger, and check that it holds steady for at least 30 seconds.
2.  **Release Test:** Push the trigger sideways with a light force (roughly 0.5-1 kg or 1-2 lbs lateral force) to ensure it pops free.
3.  **Adjustment (Too Stable):** If it doesn't release, shave a thin layer off the contact shelf to reduce engagement depth.
4.  **Adjustment (Too Sensitive):** If it releases too easily, deepen the notch by 1-2 mm.
5.  **Wind Test:** Watch the trap for 2-3 minutes to confirm it holds steady in a breeze.

### Wood Selection Criteria
| Property | Good | Bad |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Hardness | Oak, maple, ash, hickory | Pine, poplar, willow (too soft — notches compress) |
| Moisture | Dry or slightly green | Waterlogged or punky (rotten) |
| Grain | Straight, tight grain | Twisted, knotty (splits unpredictably) |
| Shape | Straight sticks, finger-thick | Curved, tapered, or branching |

### Troubleshooting
| Problem | Cause | Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Trigger will not hold at all | Notch too shallow or surface too smooth | Deepen notch; roughen contact surface slightly |
| Trigger holds but won’t release | Notch too deep or undercut | Shave the back wall to remove undercut; reduce depth |
| Trigger releases randomly | Wind, vibration, or notch wearing down | Deepen notch; add windbreak; use harder wood |
| Hooks slip when setting | Wrong angle between sticks | Realign so pull direction is perpendicular to the hook face |
| Trigger works once then fails | Soft wood — notch compressed after first use | Switch to hardwood; carve a new trigger |
| Wet weather jams trigger | Wood swelling increases friction | Smooth surfaces; widen notch; use wire components where possible |

## Field Expedients
When a knife is unavailable for carving:
*   **Break and split:** Use the rough, splintered end of a snapped stick as a natural hook surface against a forked stake.
*   **Rock notch:** Grind a notch into a stick by rubbing it against a sharp-edged rock.
*   **Wire hook:** Bend wire into a hook shape and lash it to the trigger stick, as wire hooks are smoother and more predictable.
*   **Cordage loop:** Tie a loop of cordage to the trigger stick to catch on a nail, thorn, or small peg.

## Notes
*   The trigger is the most critical and failure-prone part of any powered trap.
*   The sweet spot for notch depth for rabbit-sized game is 5-8 mm.
*   The Wind Test is essential: the trigger must withstand a breeze without tripping.
*   The distinction between a good trigger and a bad one is the ability to hold steady in wind while releasing with a gentle sideways push.
*   Spare trigger sticks should be carried as they are the first component to fail in the field.
