# Fishing in Survival: A Comprehensive Guide

## Objective
To acquire reliable sources of animal protein by utilizing various fishing methods, aiming to multiply food output per hour by a factor of 10 or more compared to rod-and-line, and to preserve the catch immediately.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Knots & Cordage Rope making, 8 key knots

## Material
- Cordage
- Sharp edge (knife, broken glass, sharp rock)
- Hooks: thorns, bone, safety pins, bent wire, carved wood, or any small curved hard material
- Bait: insects, worms, berries, small pieces of meat, shiny objects
- Rocks (for weirs)
- Flexible branches or saplings (for traps and nets)
- Inner strands of paracord
- Braided plant-fiber cordage
- Dental floss
- Unraveled fabric threads
- Long animal sinew

## System
- Rivers, lakes, ponds, streams, coastlines, and tidal pools (for fish habitat)
- Water currents
- Water refraction (for spearfishing)

## Technology
- **Passive Methods:** Gill Net, Fish Trap (Weir), Trapping Methods (Basket Traps, Basket Traps, Funnel Design)
- **Active/Semi-Passive Methods:** Improvised Hook and Line, Spearfishing, Hand Fishing (Noodling)
- **Preservation Techniques:** Smoking, salting, fermenting (though these are unlocks, they are crucial for the overall strategy).

## Tools
- Flexible branch or sapling (for a rod)
- Fishing spear (straight, light pole)
- Knife (for cleaning/gutting)
- Container for catch
- Stakes or heavy rocks (for anchoring)

## Process
### Method 1: Improvised Hook and Line
1. **Making Hooks:**
    - Thorn hooks: Break a thorn with a branch piece attached to form a natural hook.
    - Bone hooks: Split bone lengthwise, carve one end into a point, carve a notch at the other end for the line, and curve it over heat.
    - Wire hooks: Bend scavenged wire into a J-shape with a barb filed or bent at the tip.
    - Gorge hook: Use a straight piece of bone, wood, or thorn sharpened at both ends, with the line tied to the middle.
2. **Making Line:** Select line material based on strength (paracord inner strands, braided plant-fiber cordage, dental floss, unraveled fabric threads, long animal sinew).
3. **Tying:** Use a clinch knot to attach the hook to the line.
4. **Simple Rod Fishing:** Use a flexible branch (2–4 meters) as a rod. Tie the line to the tip, add a weight (15–20 cm above the hook), bait the hook, and wait for a sustained pull before setting the hook.
5. **Multiple Lines:** Tie baited lines to flexible branches (limb lines) and check every 30–60 minutes.

### Method 2: Gill Net
1. **Cordage Requirement:** Need 20–50 meters of thin, strong line.
2. **Mesh Size Determination:**
    - Small fish (minnow-size): 2 cm (3/4 inch)
    - Medium fish (trout-size): 5 cm (2 inch)
    - Large fish (bass/carp): 8–10 cm (3–4 inch)
3. **Weaving:** Stretch a top line (headline) between supports. Tie vertical drop lines at intervals equal to the mesh width.
4. **Grid Formation:** Connect drop lines horizontally with cross-ties at intervals equal to the mesh width, using sheet bend knots at junctions.
5. **Weighting:** Attach small floats (bark pieces, sealed bottles) along the top line and small weights (stones) along the bottom line.
6. **Setting:** Anchor both ends with stakes or heavy rocks.

### Method 3: Fish Trap (Weir)
1. **Structure Building:** Find shallow, moderately current water (30–60 cm deep).
2. **Wall Construction:** Build two V-shaped walls from rocks, pointing downstream. The wide end faces upstream.
3. **Funnel Opening:** The narrow end must have an opening of 15–20 cm (6–8 inches).
4. **Pen Construction:** Build an enclosed pen or basket behind the narrow opening.
5. **Basket Trap (Portable):** Weave flexible branches into a cone or cylinder shape with a funnel entrance.
6. **Maintenance:** Check the trap daily.

### Method 4: Hand Fishing (Noodling)
1. **Approach:** Wade slowly into shallow, calm water, moving quietly to avoid scaring fish.
2. **Locating Fish:** Look for hiding spots like undercut banks, submerged logs, rock crevices, and root tangles.
3. **Capture:** Reach in slowly. Locate the fish by touch, slide the hand along the belly toward the gills, grip firmly just behind the gill plates, and lift quickly.

### Method 5: Tidal Pool Harvesting
1. **Timing:** Visit pools at low tide.
2. **Harvesting:** Scoop small fish trapped in corners by hand or improvised nets (shirt stretched between two sticks).
3. **Shellfish:** Pry mussels, limpets, and periwinkles off rocks with a knife or flat rock.
4. **Crabs:** Lift rocks and grab crabs from behind (behind the claws).
5. **Preservation:** Cook all shellfish (boil, steam, or roast) until shells open and flesh is opaque.

### Method 6: Spearfishing
1. **Spear Construction:** Use a straight, light pole (2–3 meters / 7–10 feet). Split the end into 2–4 prongs, carving splits about 15 cm (6 inches) deep, and lash below the split.
2. **Sharpening:** Fire-harden the tips (hold over coals and rotate).
3. **Refraction Rule:** Aim lower than where the fish appears to be (e.g., aim 25% lower for a fish appearing 30 cm deep).
4. **Execution:** Wade slowly. Strike with a fast, straight downward thrust to pin the fish to the bottom.

### Cleaning and Gutting Fish
1. **Killing:** Kill humanely with a sharp blow to the top of the head between the eyes.
2. **Scaling:** Scrape scales from tail to head with the back of a knife, shell, or rock (do outdoors).
3. **Gutting:** Insert the knife tip into the belly near the vent and cut forward to just below the gills (shallow cuts).
4. **Extraction:** Pull out the guts and scrape along the spine to remove the dark bloodline (kidney). Rinse the cavity.
5. **Gill Removal:** Remove gills by cutting or pulling them out.
6. **Final Step:** Cook immediately or preserve using drying, smoking, or salting.

## Notes
- **Efficiency Principle:** Passive methods beat active methods. The most efficient starting method is a stone weir.
- **Timing:** Fish feed most actively at dawn and dusk; midday is the worst time.
- **Line Visibility:** Use the thinnest, lightest line possible; thick, dark line is seen by fish.
- **Hook Setting:** Wait for a strong, sustained pull before setting the hook; setting too early results in a missed catch.
- **Safety First:** Fish with scales are almost always safe when cooked. Puffer fish (blowfish) are deadly.
- **Freshwater Risk:** Freshwater fish commonly carry parasites (tapeworms, flukes) and must always be cooked.
- **Environmental Awareness:** Be aware of potential toxins from coral reefs (ciguatera) or polluted water (red tide).

**Fish Safety Quick Check**
| Feature | Safe? |
| :--- | :--- | 
| Has scales | Almost always yes (cook it) |
| No scales | Usually yes (cook thoroughly) |
| Puffs up / inflates | NO — deadly toxin |
| Bright colors + spines | NO — likely venomous |
| Large tropical reef fish | Risky — ciguatera |
| Shellfish in red/brown water | NO — algal toxin |

**The one rule:** Passive methods beat active methods. Build a trap or set a net, then go do something else. Your time is better spent on shelter, fire, and water while the fish catch themselves.
