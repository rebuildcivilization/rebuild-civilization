# Quick Preservation: Field Fish Preservation Guide

## Objective
To begin fish preservation within 2 hours of catching in warm temperatures (above 20°C or 68°F) to prevent bacterial growth, which doubles every 20 minutes on raw fish, and secure a shelf life for the catch.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Temperature:** Temperatures above 20°C (68°F) are the primary trigger for immediate preservation need.
*   **Salt:** Required for salt packing method.
*   **Fire/Heat:** Required for field smoking.
*   **Materials:** Fish, salt, hardwood/green wood, waterproof vessels, cloth, rock, or bark.

## Material
*   Fish (must be killed, bled, and gutted).
*   Salt (for packing or rubbing).
*   Hardwood for smoking (oak, hickory, maple, alder, willow).
*   Green wood or soaked hardwood chips for smoking.
*   Water (for finding salt, for soaking cured fish).
*   Waterproof vessels (clay pot, hollowed log, bark container, plastic bucket).
*   Fire pit or fire source.
*   Frame materials (poles, crossbars, sticks).
*   Cloth or bark for wrapping and storage.
*   Flat rocks or pieces of wood for weighting.

## System
The system involves three primary strategies to manage perishable fish in warm weather:
1.  **Field Smoking:** Combines heat, dehydration, and antimicrobial smoke.
2.  **Salt Packing:** Uses salt to draw moisture out through osmosis.
3.  **Combined Method:** Applying a salt rub followed by smoking for the longest shelf life.

## Technology
*   **Bacterial Action:** Bacteria double every 20 minutes on raw fish, making the 2-hour window critical in warm weather.
*   **Smoking Mechanism:** Heat cooks the fish while smoke provides antimicrobial chemicals and dehydration.
*   **Salt Packing Mechanism:** Osmosis, where salt draws water out of both the fish and microorganisms.

## Tools
### Field Smoking Tools
*   Fire pit.
*   Frame materials (poles, crossbars).
*   Green-wood sticks (for the grill).
*   Cordage or thin sticks (for hanging fish).

### Salt Packing Tools
*   Waterproof vessels (clay pot, hollowed log, bark container, plastic bucket).
*   Flat rock or piece of wood (for weighting).

## Process
### The Two-Hour Rule
Priority order after a large catch in warm weather:
1.  Kill and bleed all fish immediately.
2.  Gut all fish.
3.  Begin preservation within 1–2 hours—smoke or salt what you can’t eat today.

### Method 1: Field Smoking
**Building a Quick Smoker Options:**
*   **Option A — Tripod smoker (30 minutes):** Drive three poles into the ground in a triangle, lash them at the junction, tie horizontal crossbars between the poles (60–90 cm height), and dig a shallow fire pit below the crossbars. Drape the outside with bark slabs, tarp, wet cloth, or leafy branches, leaving the top partially open for airflow.
*   **Option B — Trench smoker (20 minutes):** Dig a trench about 60 cm deep, 30 cm wide, and 1 m long. Build the fire at one end, lay green-wood sticks across the trench above the fire end as a grill, and cover the trench loosely with flat rocks, bark, or branches, leaving gaps for ventilation.
*   **Option C — Ground rack (10 minutes):** Drive two Y-shaped forked sticks into the ground on either side of the fire. Rest a crossbar in the forks about 60 cm (2 ft) above the fire. Hang fish from the crossbar using cordage or thin sticks through the gill opening. (Open-air smoking).

**The Smoking Process:**
1.  **Prepare the fish:** Gut and clean. Butterfly fish under 500g (1 lb) or fillet and cut into 10–15 mm (1/2 inch) thick strips for larger fish.
2.  **Optional salt rub:** Rub a thin layer of salt (roughly 1 tablespoon per 500g/1 lb) into all flesh surfaces.
3.  **Start the fire:** Build a fire and let it burn down to a bed of **hot coals** with no open flames.
4.  **Add smoking wood:** Place green or soaked hardwood chips on the coals. Preferred woods include Oak, Hickory, Maple, Apple/cherry, Alder, and Willow.
5.  **Place fish on the rack:** Arrange pieces so they don’t touch. Skin-side down or hung by the tail or gill opening.
6.  **Maintain the smoke:** Keep a steady stream of thick smoke for **4–8 hours**. The target temperature inside the smoker should be 60–80°C (140–175°F).
7.  **Test for doneness:** Fish is done when the surface is dark golden to brown and glossy, flesh flakes easily, and no raw, translucent areas remain in the center.

### Method 2: Salt Packing
**Finding Salt Options:**
*   Seawater (boil until only crystals remain).
*   Salt licks (natural mineral deposits near game trails).
*   Inland salt deposits (white crystalline crusts near springs or dry lake beds).
*   Plant ash (burning Coltsfoot, glasswort, kelp) dissolved in water.
*   Trade.

**The Salt Packing Process:**
1.  **Prepare the fish:** Gut, scale, and open the fish flat (butterfly cut) or fillet into pieces. Maximum thickness: 3 cm (1.2 inches).
2.  **Calculate salt:** Use salt equal to **15–25% of the fish weight**.
3.  **Layer in a container:**
    *   Spread a 1 cm (1/2 inch) layer of salt on the bottom.
    *   Place the first layer of fish, flesh-side down.
    *   Cover completely with salt, filling every gap.
    *   Add the next layer of fish, flesh-side up.
    *   Continue until the container is full, ending with a thick cap of salt.
4.  **Press and weight:** Place a flat rock or piece of wood on top and weight it down to force moisture out.
5.  **Drain:** Within 12–24 hours, drain the liquid. Add more salt to exposed surfaces.
6.  **Cure time:** Small fish/thin fillets: **3–5 days**. Thick pieces: **7–10 days**. The fish is cured when the flesh feels firm throughout.
7.  **Dry after curing:** Remove fish, brush off excess, and hang in a breezy, shaded location for 1–2 days.

### Combining Methods: Smoke + Salt
1.  Salt-rub the fish (lighter application: 5–10% by weight).
2.  Let it sit for 2–4 hours to draw moisture.
3.  Rinse off excess salt.
4.  Smoke for 4–8 hours.

## Notes
*   **Wood Selection:** Use only hardwoods (oak, hickory, maple, alder, willow). Never use resinous softwoods like pine, spruce, fir, cedar, yew, or any evergreen with needles, as they produce acrid smoke.
*   **Salt Safety:** Do not over-salt; excess salt is soaked out. Before eating salt-cured fish, soak in fresh water for 4–12 hours, changing water 2–3 times, to draw out excess salt.
*   **Storage Requirements:** Preserved fish must be kept dry, cool, away from animals, and checked regularly for mold, fuzzy white growth, ammonia, or sourness.
*   **Shelf Life Summary:** Hot-smoked fish has a shelf life of 5–7 days in cool weather, 2–3 days in warm weather. Salt-packed fish can last 2–6 months.
*   **Emergency Shortcuts:**
    *   No smoker materials: Suspend fish directly over campfire smoke, turning frequently (2–3 days shelf life).
    *   No salt: Smoke longer (8–12 hours) until the fish is very dry and hard (1–2 weeks shelf life).
    *   No fire: Salt-pack only (weeks to months shelf life).
    *   No salt AND no fire: Cut into the thinnest possible strips, hang in direct sun and wind (1–3 days, weather dependent).
