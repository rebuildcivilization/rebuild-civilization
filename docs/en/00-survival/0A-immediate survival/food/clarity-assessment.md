# Clarity Assessment: Visual Quality Checks

## Objective
The primary objective of a clarity assessment is to determine the water quality by using visual indicators, which serve as the most accessible and immediate indicator of water quality. Clarity assessment reveals the sediment load, organic matter, algae growth, and the general state of the water, which dictates which purification methods are viable.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- A clear container (glass jar or clear plastic bottle) for testing.
- A white background or white container for accurate color assessment.
- For flocculation: Crushed Moringa seeds or Alum (Potassium Aluminum Sulfate).
- For monitoring: A log or record to track daily clarity.

## Material
Water samples from a stored water supply.

## System
Clarity assessment determines the necessary pre-treatment steps before applying purification methods. The system evaluates two properties of water: turbidity (suspended particles) and color (dissolved substances).

## Technology
**Clarity Indicator:** Visual assessment of water clarity.
**Turbidity Measurement:** Measured in NTU (Nephelometric Turbidity Units). The clarity determines the efficacy of purification methods:
- **UV/SODIS treatment:** Requires clear water because turbidity blocks UV penetration.
- **Chemical treatment (chlorine):** Requires low turbidity because particles shield bacteria from chemical contact.
- **Sand/charcoal filtration:** Works with cloudy water but clogs faster with high turbidity.

## Tools
- Hand for the Hand Test.
- Printed text (newspaper, book page) for the Newspaper Test.
- Bright light source for the Sunlight Test.
- A stick or straw for the Stick Test (to differentiate oil from iron bacteria).

## Process
### Turbidity Assessment Methods
**1. The Hand Test:**
- Hold a hand behind the container and look through the water at the fingers.
- Results determine classification (Low turbidity < 5 NTU, Moderate 5-20 NTU, High 20-50 NTU, Very high > 50 NTU).

**2. The Newspaper Test:**
- Place printed text flat on a surface.
- Hold a clear container of water above the text and look down.
- Results determine suitability for methods (e.g., Text easily readable < 5 NTU).

**3. The Sunlight Test:**
- Fill a clear bottle and hold it up against a bright light source.
- Observation determines turbidity (Light passes freely = Low turbidity; Light is diffused = High turbidity).

### Color Assessment
- Fill a clear container and hold it against a white background.
- Compare observations using the Color Interpretation Guide.

### Surface Film Analysis
- Allow water to sit undisturbed for 5-10 minutes.
- Look at the surface from a low angle.
- Gently disturb the surface with a stick or straw.
- Distinguish between film types:
    - Rainbow/iridescent sheen (Petroleum products/mineral oils).
    - Iron bacteria film (Crack into angular plates when disturbed; harmless).
    - White frothy film (Detergents/surfactants).
    - Green/blue-green scum (Cyanobacteria; Very High Risk).
    - Thin dust film (Airborne dust/pollen).

### Settling Test
- Fill a clear, straight-sided container and let it stand undisturbed (10 min, 30 min, or 2 hours).
- Interpretation of settling:
    - 0-5 minutes: Heavy sand, grit, visible debris.
    - 5-15 minutes: Fine sand, silt.
    - 15-60 minutes: Very fine silt, some clay.
    - 1-4 hours: Clay particles, some organic matter.
    - Does not settle: Colloidal clay, dissolved organics, algae.

### Flocculation
- Used when water remains cloudy after settling (particles too fine to sink on their own).
- **Method 1 (Moringa Seeds):** Shell and crush 1-2 seeds per liter, mix into a paste, stir vigorously for 2 minutes, stir gently for 5 minutes, let sit 1-2 hours, and siphon the clear water.
- **Method 2 (Alum):** Dissolve a pea-sized piece (approx. 1/8 teaspoon) per liter, stir thoroughly, let stand 30 min to 1 hour, and decant the clear water.
- **Key Rule:** Flocculation makes water clearer but does NOT kill pathogens; always follow with boiling, UV treatment, or chemical disinfection.

### Monitoring Clarity Over Time
- **Daily Monitoring Protocol:** Collect a sample at the same time each day, perform the hand test or newspaper test, and record the result.
- **Trend Analysis:**
    - Consistently clear: Storage conditions working.
    - Gradually becoming cloudier: Biological growth; shorten storage cycle.
    - Sudden cloudiness: Contamination event or container issue.
    - Clear at top, cloudy at bottom: Normal sediment settling.
    - Oscillating: Temperature fluctuations or inconsistent sealing.

### Practical Decision Framework
1. **Source environment:** Check for upstream contamination (dead animals, industrial sites, human waste).
2. **Surface check:** Look for oil sheen, foam, or blue-green scum (Avoid if present).
3. **Color check:** Assess chemical colors (red/black/iridescent) vs. organic colors (amber/green).
4. **Hand test:** Determine turbidity (Below 5 NTU = all methods viable; Above 20 NTU = pre-filter required).
5. **Smell test:** Avoid if chemical; proceed with caution if organic (earthy, musty).
6. **Purification Chain Decision:** Clear water $\rightarrow$ Boiling or SODIS. Cloudy water $\rightarrow$ Settling/Flocculation $\rightarrow$ Cloth filtration $\rightarrow$ Boiling.

## Key Takeaways
- Clarity dictates purification method viability (UV/SODIS and chemical treatment require clear water).
- The hand test is a quick turbidity gauge; clearly visible fingers mean low turbidity.
- Surface films reveal contamination (Rainbow films = petroleum; Iron bacteria films are harmless).
- Settling test informs pre-filtration needs based on particle size.
- Flocculation removes fine particles but requires a secondary disinfection step.
- Daily monitoring is essential to detect biological growth in storage.

Notes
- Water clarity is the most accessible and immediate indicator of water quality.
- Clarity assessment is the first step, not the last, as it defines the required pre-treatment.
- Turbidity (suspended material) and color (dissolved substances) are distinct properties.
- The distinction between harmless iron bacteria films and hazardous petroleum films based on the Stick Test is critical.
- Flocculation is a pre-treatment step, not a final purification step.
