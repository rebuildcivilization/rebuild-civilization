# Deadfall Traps | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To create the most mechanically simple lethal trap: a heavy weight propped by a trigger that collapses onto an animal that disturbs the bait, killing it instantly with no cordage, no moving parts, and nothing to fail.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- The weight must be at least five times the target animal’s body weight.
- The weight must have a flat bottom surface to land squarely.
- Bait must match the target species’ diet and be compelling enough to risk approaching the trap.

## Material
- Weight: Stone or log or slab.
- Trigger components: Carved sticks (for Figure-4), toggle (short stick), cord (string, sinew, or thin plant fiber), bait stick (thin twig with bait).
- Guide Walls: Small stones, sticks, or debris.
- Backstop: A flat stone, log, or hard surface.

## System
The principle is mechanical simplicity: a weight (rock, log, or slab) is propped up at an angle by a trigger mechanism. An animal disturbs the trigger while reaching for bait, the support collapses, and the weight falls directly onto the animal, resulting in immediate death from crushing force.

## Technology
Mechanical simplicity, gravity, and a trigger mechanism are the core technologies. The cord-and-toggle system amplifies small forces, making the Paiute deadfall extremely sensitive.

## Tools
- Knife or carving tools (implied for carving notches).
- Long twig (used to gently touch the bait during testing).

## Process
### Weight Selection
1. Choose the Right Stone or Log:
| Target Animal | Animal Weight | Minimum Deadfall Weight | Recommended Weight |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mouse | 20-40 g | 100-200 g | 300-500 g |
| Chipmunk | 70-100 g | 350-500 g | 750 g - 1 kg |
| Squirrel | 300-700 g | 1.5-3.5 kg | 4-5 kg |
| Rat | 200-500 g | 1-2.5 kg | 3-4 kg |
| Rabbit | 1-2.5 kg | 5-12 kg | 15-20 kg |
| Raccoon | 4-9 kg | 20-45 kg | 50+ kg |
| Marmot / Groundhog | 3-6 kg | 15-30 kg | 35-45 kg |

2. Verify the Shape:
- The weight must have a flat bottom surface.
- Uniform thickness is required to prevent wobbling.
- Test the fall: Prop the weight and confirm it lands flat where the bait will be.

### Deadfall Configurations
- Configuration 1: Figure-4 Deadfall (Versatile, uses three interlocking sticks. Best for weights from 500 g to 50+ kg).
- Configuration 2: Paiute Deadfall (Uses a toggle-and-cordage system. Extremely sensitive, requires cordage).
- Configuration 3: Split-Stick Deadfall (Simplest, requires a forked stick. Low sensitivity, best for bold feeders like rats and raccoons).
- Configuration 4: Box Deadfall (Samson Post) (Weight supported by a vertical post with bait attached, best for narrow spaces like burrow entrances).

### Kill Zone Optimization
1. Create a Backstop: Place a flat stone, log, or hard surface under the kill zone to crush the animal.
2. Build Guide Walls: Use small stones, sticks, or debris to create walls on three sides, leaving one opening for approach.

### Bait Selection
Bait must match the target species’ diet (e.g., Squirrels prefer nuts, Rats eat almost anything).

### Testing Before Leaving
- Use a long twig to gently touch the bait from the approach direction.
- Confirm the weight falls within 1 second of contact.
- Confirm the weight lands flat and completely covers the bait position.
- Reset and test twice more to confirm reliability.

### Placement Strategy
- Best locations: Against walls, fences, rock faces, fallen logs, near nut trees with feeding evidence, and at burrow entrances.
- Poor locations: Open ground with no cover, near strong human scent, on slopes (where weight slides), or in windy areas.

### Maintenance and Troubleshooting
| Problem | Cause | Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Trap not triggered, bait gone | Trigger too stiff | Recarve notches shallower; use lighter bait stick |
| Trap triggered, no animal | Animal too fast or approached from side | Add guide walls; make trigger more sensitive |
| Weight fell but animal escaped | Weight too light or not flat | Use heavier stone; ensure flat base |
| Trigger collapsed during setup | Notches too shallow | Deepen notch cuts; practice assembly technique |
| Rain or wind triggering trap | Trigger too sensitive | Slightly deepen notch engagement |
| Bait dried out or rotted | Too long between checks | Refresh bait daily; use durable baits (nuts) |

### Common Mistakes
- Weight too light (most common failure).
- No backstop (soft ground absorbs lethal force).
- Bait outside the kill zone (animal takes bait without being under the weight).
- Overcomplicated triggers (simpler triggers are better).
- Leaving traps unchecked (traps need checking twice daily).

## Notes
- The single most critical rule is weight: it must be at least five times the target animal’s body weight.
- The best deadfall in the world catches nothing if placed wrong; location matters more than design.
- The Paiute deadfall is faster to build and more sensitive than the figure-4, but requires a short length of cordage.
- A crushed animal left for 24+ hours in warm weather will be decomposed, fly-blown, and unsafe to eat.
