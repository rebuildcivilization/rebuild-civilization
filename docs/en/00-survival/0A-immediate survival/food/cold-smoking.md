# Cold Smoking

## Objective
To preserve food by running smoke through a long flue that cools it below 90°F (32°C) before it reaches the meat, infusing antimicrobial smoke compounds without cooking the meat.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Salt Cure:** Food must always be salt-cured before cold smoking, as smoke alone at low temperatures does not kill bacteria, creating ideal conditions for botulism without prior curing.
*   **Infrastructure:** Requires more infrastructure than hot smoking to separate the firebox from the chamber.

## Material
*   Meat (general), Fish (salmon, trout), Bacon (pork belly), Sausages, Hard cheese, Jerky strips.
*   Fuel: Sawdust or fine wood chips (preferred over chunks or logs).
*   Setup Components: Firebox, Smoking chamber (barrel, box, or brick structure), Flue (metal stovepipe, ductwork, or clay pipe), Metal trays or pans, Dividers (metal, foil, or wood).
*   Monitoring: Thermometers (oven, candy, or aquarium types).

## System
**The Cold Smoking System:**
This system relies on generating smoke without heat reaching the food, achieved by separating a firebox from the smoking chamber using a long flue.

*   **Core Principle:** The long flue cools the smoke as it travels, ensuring the meat temperature remains below the critical threshold.
*   **Fire Source:** Requires a smoldering fire, not a burning fire.

## Technology
*   **Cooling Mechanism:** Running smoke through a long flue to cool it below 90°F (32°C).
*   **Antimicrobial Action:** Infusing antimicrobial smoke compounds.
*   **Temperature Control:** Monitoring the meat temperature to ensure it stays below the maximum safe chamber temperature of 90°F (32°C).

## Tools
*   Thermometers (essential for monitoring temperature at meat level).
*   Metal tray or pan (for maze generator).
*   Metal strips, foil, or wooden dividers (for the maze generator).
*   Sawdust or fine wood chips (for fuel).
*   Rag-wrapped stick (for flue maintenance).

## Process
### Cold Smoking Setup Options
1.  **Option 1: Hillside Flue (Best for natural draft)**
    *   Firebox at the bottom of the slope.
    *   Flue trench running uphill (12-16 inches wide, 12 inches deep, covering 8-10 feet horizontal distance).
    *   Smoking chamber at the top of the slope.
2.  **Option 2: Flat Ground Pipe Flue
    *   Firebox: Small enclosed structure.
    *   Flue: Metal stovepipe, ductwork, or clay pipe (4-6 inches in diameter, running 8-12 feet horizontally), buried or covered with dirt.
    *   Smoking chamber: Positioned at the far end, flue entering through the bottom.
3.  **Option 3: Cardboard Box Cold Smoker (Emergency)
    *   Structure: Large cardboard box (refrigerator box ideal).
    *   Flue: Flexible tubing run 6-8 feet to a small fire pit.
    *   Meat Placement: Hung from dowels across the top.

### Fire Management
*   **Fuel Management:** Spread a thin layer (1/2 inch) of sawdust on a metal tray. Light one edge and let it slowly burn.
*   **The Maze Generator:** Create a maze pattern (12x12 inches) in a metal tray, fill channels with lightly packed sawdust, and light one end for 8-12 hours of continuous smoke.
*   **Airflow Control:**
    *   If the fire flames up: close the firebox door partially to restrict air intake.
    *   If the fire goes out: open airflow slightly and add dry chips to restart.

### Cold Smoking Schedule
*   **Standard Cycle:** Smoke for 6-8 hours (during coolest parts of the day or overnight in warm weather), followed by a rest period of 12-16 hours (no fire) to allow smoke compounds to penetrate and moisture to equalize.
*   **Duration by Product:**
    *   Fish: 1-3 days (12-24 hrs smoke).
    *   Bacon: 1-2 weeks intermittent.
    *   Whole Hams: 2-4 weeks intermittent after salt cure.

### Temperature Monitoring
*   **Placement:** Thermometer at meat level in the center of the chamber.
*   **Ideal Range:** 60-85°F (15-29°C).
*   **Maximum Safe Temperature:** 90°F (32°C).
*   **Minimum Practical Temperature:** 40°F (4°C).
*   **Action if Temp > 85°F:** Reduce fire to bare smoldering, open chamber exhaust vents wider, and partially block the flue entry.

## Notes
*   **Critical Safety Rule:** Never cold-smoke meat that has not been properly salt-cured first.
*   **Ambient Temperature:** Works best in cool weather (40-60°F or 4-15°C). In warm climates, cold smoke only at night or not at all.
*   **Humidity:** High humidity (>80%) slows drying, necessitating longer rest periods.
*   **Signs of Proper Cold Smoking:** Meat surface develops a glossy, amber to mahogany pellicle; texture firms progressively; pleasant smoky aroma without bitterness; no wet spots or slime after rest; 20-30% weight loss.
*   **Flue Maintenance:** Creosote and tar accumulate; clean the flue after every 3-4 sessions by pushing a rag-wrapped stick through it.
