# Funnel Design

## Objective
To create one-way fish entry systems that allow fish to swim in but prevent them from finding the exit.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Fish
- Water
- Materials (flexible wood, cordage/plant fiber, rocks, driven stakes)

## Material
- Flexible green sticks (willow, hazel, or any bendable wood) for woven funnels.
- Thin cordage or plant fiber for binding.
- Rocks for stone funnels.
- Driven stakes for stick fence funnels.

## System
Every funnel has four parts:
1. Wings — the wide, flared opening that guides fish inward.
2. Throat — the narrow passage that fish squeeze through.
3. Holding chamber — the enclosed space where fish accumulate.
4. Backstop — the far wall of the holding chamber that prevents fish from swimming straight through.

Design Variations include:
- Double Funnel: Two funnels in series, with the first leading to a small intermediate chamber before the second.
- Side-Entry Funnel: Positioned on the side wall to catch fish swimming along the bank or weir.
- Floor Funnel: The opening is flush with the streambed, with wings lying flat on the bottom.

## Technology
The principle relies on the fact that fish swim forward, following walls, current, and scent trails. A well-designed funnel exploits this behavior: the entrance is obvious and inviting, but the exit (the narrow throat) is nearly invisible from inside the holding area because turning around in a tight space is difficult and unnatural.

## Tools
- Sticks (for woven funnels).
- Rocks (for stone funnels).
- Stakes (for stick fence funnels).
- Cordage or plant fiber (for binding).

## Process
### Construction
- **Woven Funnel:** Form sticks into a cone shape; the narrow end (throat) must taper to 3-8 cm; bind the sticks at the narrow end to hold the shape; splay the wide ends outward and tie them to the trap entrance; extend the cone 15-25 cm into the trap interior.
- **Stone Funnel:** Extend two rows of rocks inward, angling them; the entrance between rows should match the weir gap width (30-50 cm); the exit (throat) should narrow to 8-15 cm; build a circular or semicircular stone pen behind the throat.
- **Stick Fence Funnel:** Drive two rows of stakes inward; weave flexible branches between the stakes to create walls; leave the throat gap between the innermost stakes.

### Positioning
- The funnel entrance must face into the current or toward the direction fish travel.
- In a stream: Point the funnel opening upstream.
- In a weir: Place the funnel at the downstream side of the gap.
- In still water: Orient the funnel toward the bank or toward a structure where fish patrol.

### Testing
1. Drop a stick or leaf into the entrance to check if it flows through the throat smoothly.
2. Push a stick through the throat from inside to see if it catches on the flexible tips or narrow walls.
3. Simulate a fish by pushing a fist through from the outside; the tips should flex inward, and pulling the fist back should result in resistance from the tips.
4. Look through the funnel from inside the holding chamber to ensure the exit is obscured.

## Notes
**Critical Measurements:
| Component | Dimension | Why It Matters |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Wing spread | 40-80 cm (16-32 in) | Wide enough to intercept fish following the wall |
| Wing angle | 30-45 degrees from center | Too steep and fish bounce off; too shallow and they swim past |
| Throat width | 3-8 cm (1-3 in) | Must fit target species but feel restrictive enough that fish don’t casually exit |
| Throat length | 15-25 cm (6-10 in) | Longer throat = harder to find the exit from inside |
| Chamber depth | At least 50 cm (20 in) behind throat | Fish must clear the throat fully before they can orient |

**Common Failures:**
- Throat too short: Fish poke their head through and back out. (Extend throat to at least 15 cm)
- Throat too centered: Fish swim along the walls and find a centered exit. (Offset the throat)
- Wings too rigid: In woven funnels, stiff wings do not guide fish smoothly. (Use green, flexible wood)
- No bait: In still water, an empty trap catches almost nothing. (Always bait the holding chamber)

**Key Takeaways:**
- The funnel works because fish swim forward through narrowing spaces but rarely reverse through them.
- Throat width of 3-8 cm accommodates most freshwater species while preventing escape.
- Longer throats (15-25 cm) dramatically reduce escape rates.
- Double funnels nearly eliminate escapes for critical food-production scenarios.
- Always test the funnel before deployment — the throat must flex open from outside but resist from inside.
