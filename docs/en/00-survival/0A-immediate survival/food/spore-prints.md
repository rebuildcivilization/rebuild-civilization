# Spore Prints: The Definitive Mushroom Identification Technique

## Objective
To reveal spore colour, which is one of the most reliable and consistent identification features in mycology, thereby narrowing down thousands of possible mushroom species to a manageable handful and catching deadly look-alikes.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- A mature mushroom cap (gills or pores must be fully developed, not still covered by a partial veil).
- A flat surface (paper, smooth bark, a flat stone, a piece of fabric, or a clean leaf).
- A cover (a bowl, cup, pot, or even a large leaf) to prevent air currents.
- Time: 4–12 hours (Overnight is ideal).

## Material
- Mature mushroom cap.
- Flat surface materials (paper, bark, stone, fabric, leaf, dried clay).
- Cover material (bowl, cup, pot, large leaf).
- Fixative options (hairspray or a thin coat of tree resin dissolved in alcohol for preservation).

## System
The spore print technique is the single most powerful tool for mushroom identification after physical examination. It relies on the fact that millions of microscopic spores form a visible deposit whose color is a fundamental taxonomic characteristic.

## Technology
- **Spore Printing:** Placing a mushroom cap gill-side down on a surface for 4–12 hours to reveal spore colour.
- **Consistency Check:** Spore color does not change with age, weather, or growing conditions, making it a reliable feature.

## Tools
- A surface for printing (must be half white and half dark for optimal results).
- A cover for humidity and air current control.
- Natural light for reading the print.
- Reference library (for building a personal collection).

## Process
### Spore Print Taking
1. **Harvest the cap:** Cut the stem flush with the bottom of the cap. Remove the ring (annulus) if present, as it can block spore release.
2. **Place cap gill-side down:** Set the cap directly on the flat surface with the spore-producing surface facing down.
3. **Cover:** Place a bowl, cup, or other cover over the cap to prevent air currents and maintain humidity.
4. **Wait:** Leave undisturbed for 4–12 hours (overnight is ideal).
5. **Remove the cap:** Carefully lift the cover, then gently lift the mushroom cap straight up to view the print.

### Reading the Print
Examine the spore color in good natural light. Artificial firelight can distort colors.

| Spore Print Color | Common Genera | Safety Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| White to cream | Amanita, Lepiota, Clitocybe, Pleurotus (oyster) | **Danger zone** — many deadly species produce white spores |
| Pink to salmon | Entoloma, Pluteus, Volvariella | Mixed safety — some edible, some toxic |
| Brown to rust | Cortinarius, Agrocybe, Pholiota | Some deadly Cortinarius species here |
| Dark brown to purple-brown | Agaricus (field mushroom), Stropharia | Many edible species, but confirm other features |
| Black to dark purple-black | Coprinopsis (ink caps), Psathyrella | Generally safer group, fewer deadly species |
| Yellow-green to olive | Chlorophyllum (false parasol) | Indicates green-spored parasol — toxic |

### Critical Interpretation Rules
1. **White spores + ring on stem + volva (cup at base) = Amanita.** This combination kills more people worldwide than any other mushroom genus. Must be discarded.
2. **Dark purple-brown spores + ring on stem + growth in grass = likely Agaricus.** Confirm the flesh does not turn bright chrome-yellow when cut.
3. **Rust-brown spores + cobweb-like partial veil = Cortinarius.** Avoid all species due to delayed-onset kidney toxins (orellanine).
4. **Spore color alone never confirms edibility.** Always combine with cap morphology, gill attachment, stem features, habitat, smell, and other data.

### Spore Prints Without Paper (Alternatives)
- Smooth flat stone: Excellent. Dark stone shows white spores; light stone shows dark spores.
- Smooth bark (birch, beech): Good. Light inner bark works well for dark spores.
- Large flat leaf: Good, but may wilt and curl overnight.
- Clean fabric (cotton, linen): Good, but harder to read fine color distinctions.
- Flat piece of dried clay: Excellent, smooth surface.
- Your own skin (back of hand): Emergency only, crude but functional.
- Another mushroom cap (turned upside down): Surprisingly good, contrasting surface shows spore color clearly.

### Preserving Spore Prints
- **Spray fixative:** Hairspray works in pre-collapse conditions.
- **Post-collapse preservation:** A thin coat of tree resin dissolved in alcohol (pine resin in grain alcohol) will fix spores to paper.
- **Press and dry:** Place the printed paper between two flat surfaces and let it dry completely.
- **Label immediately:** Note the date, location, habitat (type of tree, soil, season), and every physical feature of the mushroom.

### Using Spore Prints for Ongoing Food Security
- **Building a Species Reference Library:** Collect spore prints of positively identified edible species. This builds a personal field guide to compare against unfamiliar mushrooms.
- **Spore Propagation:** The technique is viable for cultivation:
    1. Take a thick spore print from a confirmed edible species.
    2. Scrape the spores into water.
    3. Pour the spore solution onto a suitable substrate (fresh hardwood logs for wood-loving species, composted straw for field mushrooms).
    4. Keep moist and shaded.
    5. Success rates are reliable for oyster mushrooms (Pleurotus), shiitake (Lentinula), and wine caps (Stropharia).

## Notes
- **Critical Mistake 1 (Immature Mushroom):** If the cap is immature and gills are covered by a partial veil, the print will be faint or absent. Wait for full expansion.
- **Critical Mistake 2 (Timing):** A 30-minute print may show nothing; 4–8 hours minimum is recommended.
- **Critical Mistake 3 (Lighting):** Firelight distorts colors; always read spore prints in daylight.
- **Critical Mistake 4 (Misinterpretation):** Gill color and spore color are different. The print is the primary indicator.
- **Critical Mistake 5 (Final Call):** A spore print narrows the field but does not confirm edibility; physical features (morphology, habitat, smell) must always be combined.
- **Key Takeaway:** The spore print is one of the most reliable features, and building a reference library is the most valuable outcome.
