# Hunting and Trapping

## Objective
To secure animal protein and fat, which are the most efficient calories in a survival situation. The primary objective is to hunt smart, not hard, by utilizing traps that work while sleeping.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Knots & Cordage Rope making, 8 key knots
- Fire capability

## Material
- Cordage or wire
- Knife, sharp stone, or any cutting edge
- Straight sticks and saplings (for triggers, spears, bows)
- Fire capability (for fire-hardening)
- Bone, antler, and metal (for spear tips)
- Feathers (for fletching)

## System
- Traps work 24 hours a day while the hunter sleeps. Trapping should be regarded as a passive collection method that frees up time for other activities.
- Snares have a roughly 10–20% daily catch rate per snare.
- Deadfall traps use a heavy weight held by a trigger mechanism to kill instantly when the trigger is disturbed.
- A properly set snare on an active trail catches game within 1–3 days.
- The number of traps must be proportionate to the capacity to monitor them.
- An unmonitored trap increases the risk of food loss, predation and unnecessary suffering for the animal
- Check the trap lines regularly and change the position or bait if a trap is not catching anything.

## Technology
- **Trapping Efficiency:** The numbers drive results; setting 10–20 snares across an area maximizes food odds.
- **Field Dressing Efficiency:** Saving the liver, heart, and kidneys, as they are the most nutrient-dense parts of the animal, prevents deficiency.
- **Spear Hardening:** Fire-hardening the spear tip drives out moisture and toughens the wood fibers, making the point penetrative.

## Tools
- Snares
- Spears (simple sharpened and tipped)
- Throwing stick
- Bow and arrow set (staves, arrows, fletching)
- Figure-4 trigger mechanism
- Knife and sharp stone

## Process

- Inspect the area before setting the traps.
- Identify water sources, feeding areas and pathways.
- Position the traps along active routes.
- Check them at least once in the morning and once in the evening.
- Remove any catch immediately.
- Relocate traps that are not yielding results.
- Avoid placing excessive pressure on the local wildlife.

### Tracking (Finding Animals Before Trapping)
1. **Look for tracks:** Categorize by size (Small: mouse to squirrel; Medium: rabbit to raccoon; Large: deer to boar).
2. **Look for scat:** Fresh scat indicates recent presence (Pellet-shaped = rabbit or deer; Segmented with hair/bone = predator; Berry-filled = bear).
3. **Find trails:** Look for compressed vegetation, tunnels, or bark rubbed off trees at consistent heights.
4. **Identify water sources:** Set traps near streams, ponds, and puddles.
5. **Look for feeding signs:** Chewed bark, stripped branches, dug-up roots, nutshells, scattered seeds.
6. **Time observations:** Most small game is active at dawn and dusk (crepuscular).

### Snare Construction (The Simple Snare)
1. Cut cordage/wire about 60–90 cm for rabbit-sized game.
2. Tie a small fixed loop at one end.
3. Pass the other end through the loop to create a running noose.
4. Size the noose: about 10 cm (4 inches) diameter for rabbits.
5. Position the snare on a known animal trail.
6. Anchor the snare firmly to a stake, rock, or branch.
7. Use small sticks to funnel the animal through the snare.
8. Check every 12 hours at minimum.

### Deadfall Trap Construction (The Deadfall Trap)
1. **Weight:** Find a rock at least 5 times the weight of the target animal.
2. **Trigger:** Build a figure-4 trigger from three sticks (Upright, Diagonal, Bait stick).
3. **Carving:** Carve the notches so the sticks interlock to form a “4” shape, stable enough to hold the rock but sensitive to the bait stick.
4. **Baiting:** Bait the end of the horizontal stick with the target animal's food.
5. **Placement:** Prop the heavy rock on the diagonal stick, positioning the bait stick under the rock's center.
6. **Testing:** Gently touch the bait stick from a safe distance to ensure the rock falls cleanly.

### Spear Making
**Simple Sharpened Spear:**
1. Select a hardwood sapling, 1.5–2 meters long, 3 cm thick.
2. Sharpen one end to a point, carving gradually to create a long tapered point.
3. **Fire-harden the tip:** Hold over hot coals, rotating slowly until the wood darkens and hardens.

**Tipped Spear:**
1. Lash a sharp point (stone flake, bone shard, or metal) to the shaft.
2. Split the end of the shaft about 10 cm (4 inches) deep.
3. Insert the point and lash tightly with cordage in an X-pattern.

### Bow and Arrow Construction
**Bow:**
1. Find a stave, 1.5 meters long, 3–4 cm thick (Yew, ash, hickory preferred).
2. Identify the natural curve; the belly faces the hunter.
3. Carve the belly to create a taper, ensuring both limbs flex evenly.
4. Cut notches near the tips for the bowstring.
5. String the bow with strong cordage, leaving a 15 cm (6 inches) gap between string and handle.

**Arrows:**
1. Find straight sticks, 60–75 cm long, pencil-thickness.
2. Straighten by heating over coals and bending.
3. Add a notch (nock) at the back end for the string.
4. Sharpen the front or add a point.
5. **Fletching:** Lash or glue three half-feathers equally spaced around the back of the arrow for stabilization.

### Field Dressing (Gutting) Game
1. **Cut shallow:** Make a cut through the skin and abdominal wall from the pelvis to the ribcage.
2. **Guide the blade:** Use two fingers to lift the skin away from the organs.
3. **Remove viscera:** Pull internal organs (viscera) out as a connected mass, cutting the windpipe and esophagus at the top and the colon at the bottom.
4. **Save:** Save the liver, heart, and kidneys.
5. **Check Liver:** A healthy liver is smooth and dark red; a spotted, discolored, or lumpy liver may indicate parasites.
6. **Cooling:** Wipe the cavity dry with grass or cloth; prop the body cavity open for air circulation.

## Notes
- **Trap Philosophy:** Set more traps (10–20) to compensate for low individual snare catch rates.
- **Scent Management:** Minimize scent by handling snares with gloves or rubbing them with mud.
- **Field Dressing Rule:** Never puncture the gut during field dressing; go shallow to prevent contamination.
- **Nutrient Density:** Organ meats (liver, heart, kidneys) are essential for preventing deficiency when eating only muscle meat.
- **Safety First:** Kill cleanly to prevent the animal from breaking traps or spoiling its own meat with stress hormones.
- **Environmental Awareness:** Check traps frequently (at least twice daily) and be aware of predators competing for the catch.
- **Throwing Stick Reality:** The throwing stick is a supplement to trapping, not a replacement, and success requires practice.
- Trapping techniques must be adapted to local laws and the need to conserve the environment;
- Traps can also be dangerous to people and non-target animals.
