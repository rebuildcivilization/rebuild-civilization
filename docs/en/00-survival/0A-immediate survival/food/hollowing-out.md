# Hollowing Out

## Objective
To convert a settled snow mound into a warm, structurally sound dome shelter by working methodically from the ceiling down to create a dome profile, ensuring the ceiling stays supported while carving.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- The mound must have settled for the required time (see Snow Mounding).
- Readiness must be tested by pressing an open hand firmly against the surface.
- If the snow sinks in more than 2-3 cm, the mound has not sintered enough, requiring a longer wait.
- Tools must be gathered before beginning.
- Guide sticks must be verified if inserted during mounding to confirm placement.

## Material
- Quinzhee snow shelter (settled snow mound).

## System
- Snow shelter construction method.

## Technology
- Principles of thermal insulation (cold air trap) and structural integrity (domed ceiling).

## Tools
- Digging tool (shovel, pot, flat piece of wood, or sturdy stick).
- Something to move snow out (tarp, sled, large bag, or shovel).
- A long stick (for poking the ventilation hole from inside).
- Headlamp or candle (for interior darkness).

## Process
### Entrance Tunnel
1. Choose the downwind side of the mound (the lee side). 
2. Begin digging a tunnel at ground level, aimed at the center of the mound’s base. 
3. Angle the tunnel floor slightly downward for the first 30 cm, then level off, then angle slightly upward as you enter the main chamber to create a cold air trap.
4. Keep the entrance as small as practically possible.

### Hollowing Sequence (Work from Ceiling Down)
**Step 1: Establish Ceiling Height**
Target ceiling heights depend on shelter size:
| Shelter Size | Target Ceiling Height | Minimum Clearance (sitting) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1-person | 60-75 cm (2-2.5 ft) | 50 cm above platform |
| 2-person | 75-90 cm (2.5-3 ft) | 60 cm above platform |
| 3-4 person | 90-120 cm (3-4 ft) | 75 cm above platform |

**Step 2: Shape the Dome**
- Work the ceiling into a smooth, continuous dome curve.
- Remove any bumps, ridges, or flat spots, as these are drip points.
- Test the dome by running a gloved palm over it; it should feel like the inside of a bowl.

**Step 3: Widen the Walls**
- Begin widening the interior by removing snow from the walls, working downward from the ceiling junction.
- Maintain the dome curvature where walls meet the ceiling; no sharp inside corners.

**Step 4: Carve the Sleeping Platform**
- Create a raised shelf of packed snow running along the sides, opposite the entrance.
- Dimensions:
    - Height above entrance floor: 30-45 cm (12-18 inches) minimum
    - Width: At least 60 cm (24 inches) per person
    - Length: Full body length — 180-200 cm (6-6.5 feet)
    - Surface: Flat and smooth, packed firm.

**Step 5: Create a Gear Niche**
- Carve a small alcove in the wall opposite the sleeping platform for gear storage.
- Gear must be kept off the sleeping platform and off the floor to maximize sleeping space and keep gear out of the cold air trap zone.

**Step 6: Ventilation Hole**
- From inside, push a stick (at least 50 cm long) through the ceiling at the highest point, angling it roughly 45 degrees.
- Twist the stick to create a clean hole 8-10 cm (3-4 inches) in diameter.
- Check from outside; use a small branch across the outer opening to prevent snow drift.

**Step 7: Smooth Everything**
- Perform a final pass to smooth all interior surfaces.
- Pay special attention to the ceiling dome, the junction between ceiling and walls, and the sleeping platform surface.

### Managing Excavated Snow
- **Method 1 (Best):** Have a partner outside pull snow out of the entrance while the user pushes it using a tarp or sled as a conveyor.
- **Method 2 (Solo):** Push snow toward the entrance with each stroke; periodically back out to clear the accumulation at the entrance.
- **Method 3 (Gear Available):** Fill stuff sacks, bags, or a pot with loose snow inside, pass or drag them to the entrance, and dump outside.
- **Critical Rule:** Never let excavated snow block the exit; check the entrance frequently, as a blocked entrance traps the occupant in an airtight space.

## Notes
- The counterintuitive rule for hollowing out is to work from the ceiling down to shape the dome before removing wall snow, which keeps the ceiling supported.
- Walls below 30 cm thickness are structurally weak and poorly insulating; digging should stop when guide sticks are hit.
- The cold air trap is established by ensuring the lowest point of the entrance tunnel floor sits below the sleeping platform.
- The goal of smoothing the ceiling is to ensure meltwater runs down the walls rather than dripping on the occupant.
