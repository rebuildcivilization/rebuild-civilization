# Natural Features | Survival Wiki

## Objective
Part of Emergency Shelter. Natural features such as rock overhangs, caves, fallen trees, and dense vegetation can provide immediate shelter with minimal construction, as nature has already done most of the work.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Time, energy, or daylight must be limited to make natural features viable for immediate shelter.

## Material
*   **Rock Overhangs:** Provide immediate shelter.
*   **Caves:** Offer superior protection from weather.
*   **Fallen Trees:** Provide an immediate wind break and structural framework.
*   **Dense Vegetation:** Provides natural wind protection and partial rain cover.

## System
Natural features act as primary, immediate shelter options.

## Technology
*   **Natural Features:** The foundational element, requiring minimal construction.
*   **Fire Management:** Essential for improving shelters (e.g., using fire reflection in overhangs).
*   **Structural Assessment:** Required for caves and fallen trees (checking stability, airflow, and water).

## Tools
- **Assessment Tools:** Torch or burning stick (to check for animal occupation in caves).
- **Physical Assessment:** Tapping the ceiling of caves to check for hollow sounds.

## Process
### Shelter Selection Process
1.  **Identify Need:** Use natural features when time, energy, or daylight is limited (15-30 minutes vs. 2-3 hours for a debris hut).
2.  **Assess Features:** Determine the suitability of the natural feature based on specific criteria (Depth, Height, Airflow, Stability).
3.  **Improve Shelter:** Implement modifications to enhance protection (e.g., building a wind wall, insulating the floor).

### Feature-Specific Processes
*   **Rock Overhang Improvement:**
    *   Block wind by stacking rocks, logs, or debris to create a wind wall.
    *   Build a fire near the drip line for heat reflection.
    *   Insulate the floor with a thick bed of debris (at least 15 cm).
    *   Extend shallow overhangs with lean-to branches.
*   **Cave Assessment Process:**
    *   Check for animal occupation (eyes, droppings, bones, smell).
    *   Check airflow by holding a flame at the entrance (flickers inward means ventilation exists).
    *   Check stability by tapping the ceiling.
    *   Check for water (flowing water or stains).
    *   **Usage:** Stay near the entrance and build a wind wall.
*   **Fallen Tree Shelter Process:**
    *   Assess the root ball’s stability before using it.
    *   Position the body on the leeward side of the root ball wall.
    *   Lean branches from the top of the root ball to create a lean-to.
    *   Layer debris over leaning branches and insulate the ground beneath.

## Notes
*   **Rock Overhang Warning:** Inspect rock above for cracks, loose flakes, or hollow sounds, as freeze-thaw cycles can loosen rock.
*   **Cave Warning:** Never build a fire deep inside a cave; smoke needs an escape route, and heat can cause ceiling collapses.
*   **Fallen Tree Warning:** Never shelter under a fallen tree propped up by supports unless those supports are solid, as the tree can shift.
*   **Universal Takeaway:** Every natural feature still requires floor insulation because the ground steals heat regardless of the overhead shelter.
*   **Key Takeaway:** Natural features offer shelter in 15-30 minutes, making them superior to building a debris hut from scratch when time or energy is scarce.
