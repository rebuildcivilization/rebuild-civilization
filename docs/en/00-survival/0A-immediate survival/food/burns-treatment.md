# Thermal Injury Care

## Objective
> Part of First Aid
> Burns from fires, boiling water, hot metal, and steam are among the most common injuries in a world where open flames are the primary energy source. Proper immediate treatment dramatically reduces scarring, infection, and death.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Immediate cooling is the single most important action.
*   Aggressive fluid replacement is critical for survival, especially in severe burns.
*   Hand hygiene and clean dressings are prerequisites for infection prevention.

## Material
*   Clean, running water (approximately 15-20 degrees Celsius).
*   Clean, non-adhesive material (e.g., boiled and dried cotton cloth).
*   Honey (raw, unprocessed).
*   Salt (for fluid replacement).
*   Willow bark (for pain management).
*   Meat, fish, eggs, beans, and nuts (for high-protein diet).

## System
*   **Cooling Protocol:** The core immediate treatment.
*   **Severity Assessment:** Used to determine the required treatment path (Minor, Moderate, Severe).
*   **Fluid Management System:** Based on body surface area percentage to estimate fluid needs.
*   **Infection Prevention System:** Focuses on destroying the skin barrier and keeping insects away.

## Technology
*   The effectiveness of cooling is based on reducing tissue damage and preventing deeper burn progression.
*   Honey is recognized as a proven antimicrobial dressing.
*   Willow bark tea is used for pain management.

## Tools
*   Cooling source (running water or cool water). 
*   Needle (for draining large blisters).
*   Measuring tools (to assess burn area percentage).

## Process
### The Critical First Minutes
1. **Remove the heat source:** Strip clothing unless it is stuck to the skin, in which case, leave it and cool around it.
2. **Cooling Protocol:** Cool with clean, running water at 15-20 degrees Celsius for a minimum of **20 minutes**.
    *   If running water is unavailable, immerse in a container of cool water or apply continuously refreshed wet cloths.
    *   **Never use ice or ice water.**
3. **After Cooling:**
    *   Assess the burn severity.
    *   Cover loosely with a clean, non-adhesive material (e.g., boiled and dried cotton cloth).
    *   Elevate the burned area above the heart if possible.
    *   Hydrate aggressively (aim for at least 500 ml per hour for the first several hours with significant burns).

### Treating Burns by Severity
**Minor Burns (First-Degree and Small Second-Degree):**
*   **Day 1-3:** Apply a thin layer of honey or aloe gel if available after initial cooling. Cover with a single layer of clean cloth, changed daily. Use willow bark tea for pain management.
*   **Day 3-14:** Continue dressing changes. Leave small blisters (under 2 cm) intact. Drain large, tense blisters (over 2 cm) from the edge with a fire-sterilized needle, leaving the roof in place.

**Moderate Burns (Larger Second-Degree):**
*   **Immediate Priorities:** Cool for 20 minutes. Begin aggressive fluid replacement (add a pinch of salt per liter). Immobilize burned joints in functional positions (Hands: fingers slightly curved, wrist slightly extended; Elbows: 90 degrees; Knees: straight or slightly bent).
*   **Ongoing Care:** Range-of-motion exercises 3 times daily once pain allows. High-protein diet is critical. Healing time is estimated at 2-4 weeks.

**Severe Burns (Third-Degree and Large Area):**
*   **Survival Priorities (in order):**
    1. **Airway:** Watch for swelling that closes the airway; keep the patient upright.
    2. **Fluid Replacement:** Critical. For 30% body surface area burns, approximate 4-6 liters of water in the first 8 hours, with salt added (1/4 teaspoon per liter).
    3. **Temperature Regulation:** Keep patients warm but not hot; cover unburned areas with blankets.
    4. **Wound Care:** Clean daily with boiled and cooled water. Apply honey liberally. Change dressings every 12 hours.
    5. **Nutrition:** Provide the highest-calorie, highest-protein diet.

### Estimating Fluid Needs
| Body Surface Burned | Fluid Needed (First 24 Hours) |
| :--- | :--- | 
| 10% | 2-3 liters |
| 20% | 4-6 liters |
| 30% | 6-9 liters |
| 40%+ | 8-12 liters |

### Infection Prevention
*   **Hand Hygiene:** Wash hands thoroughly before touching the burn or dressings (using soap/water or wood ash/water).
*   **Clean Dressings:** Boil cloth before use and allow to air-dry; change at least daily.
*   **Honey Application:** Apply a layer 3-5 mm thick directly to the burn surface.
*   **Insect Control:** Cover wounds at all times and keep the patient in a screened or enclosed area.
*   **Watch for Signs:** Redness spreading beyond edges, green/foul-smelling discharge, fever, or confusion.

### Special Situations
*   **Chemical Burns:** Flush with enormous quantities of water for at least 30 minutes. Do not attempt to neutralize the chemical; just dilute and wash it away.
*   **Steam Burns:** Treat aggressively with the standard cooling protocol. Watch for airway involvement if steam was inhaled.
*   **Tar or Molten Material:** Cool the area with water for 20 minutes. Material can be gradually softened and removed over days using oil or animal fat worked gently under the edges.

## Key Takeaways
*   Cool every burn with room-temperature water for 20 minutes—this is the single most effective treatment.
*   Never apply butter, oil, toothpaste, or any folk remedy to a burn.
*   Fluid replacement is the primary survival factor for major burns.
*   Honey is the best available antimicrobial wound dressing in a post-apocalyptic setting.
*   Burns over joints must be actively stretched during healing to prevent permanent contracture.
