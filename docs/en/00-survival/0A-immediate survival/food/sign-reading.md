# Sign Reading | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To interpret animal signs—scat, browse marks, beds, trails, and rubs—to determine which species are present, what they eat, and the exact locations to set traps.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Observation combined with basic biology; the ability to see, interpret, and act on physical evidence.

## Material
Physical evidence collected across the landscape, including:
*   Scat
*   Browse marks (chewed vegetation)
*   Beds (depressions where animals sleep)
*   Trails and Runs
*   Rubs and Scrapes (e.g., antler rubs, claw marks)
*   Sound and Smell (musk or urine, alarm calls)

## System
The core system involves identifying the daily triangle of water, feeding area, and bedding site. The highest-value trap locations are the narrow trails connecting these three zones.

## Technology
- Observation skills (to see signs at multiple heights).

## Tools
- Stick (to break apart scat for analysis).

## Process
### Step 1: Assess Freshness
Fresh scat is the priority, as it indicates recent animal presence and return.
*   **Very fresh (0-4 hours):** Moist, shiny surface, strong odor, may still be warm.
*   **Recent (4-24 hours):** Dull surface, slight crust forming, odor present but fading.
*   **Old (1-7 days):** Dry outer surface, crumbling edges, faint odor.
*   **Very old (1+ weeks):** Bleached, crumbling, no odor (useful for confirming presence, not timing).

### Step 2: Determine Diet
Breaking apart scat reveals the diet, which dictates the correct bait for traps.
*   **All plant fiber:** Herbivore (rabbit, deer).
*   **Mixed plant and animal material:** Omnivore (raccoon, bear, boar). Respond to a wide range of baits (fruit, nuts, meat scraps, fish).
*   **Fur, bones, feathers:** Predator (fox, coyote, wolf).

### Step 3: Identify Browse Patterns
Determining what the animal is eating by examining vegetation signs.
*   **Clean-cut stems at 45 degrees:** Rabbit or hare.
*   **Ragged, torn branch ends:** Deer or elk.
*   **Stripped bark (lower trunk):** Rabbit in winter (below 50 cm), deer (50 cm to 1.5 m), porcupine (any height).
*   **Dug-up ground with exposed roots:** Wild boar or bear.
*   **Gnawed nut shells at the base of trees:** Squirrel.
*   **Stripped pine cones:** Squirrel (look for middens).

### Step 4: Measure Browse Height

| Browse Height | Likely Animal |
| :--- | :--- |
| Ground level to 15 cm | Rabbit, vole, mouse |
| 15-50 cm | Rabbit, groundhog |
| 50 cm - 1.5 m | Deer, goat |
| 1.5 - 2.5 m | Elk, moose |
| Any height (on tree trunk) | Porcupine, bear |

### Step 5: Recognize Bedding
Identifying where animals feel safe.
*   **Deer beds:** Oval depressions in tall grass, leaf litter, or snow, roughly body-sized (90-120 cm long). Often on slight rises.
*   **Rabbit forms:** Shallow scrapes in grass or under bushes, near cover or burrow entrances.
*   **Wild boar wallows:** Muddy depressions near water, often with rubbing trees nearby.
*   **Predator dens:** Holes in the ground, under rock outcrops, or in hollow logs.

### Step 6: Map Beds Relative to Food and Water
Animals follow the route: Leave bedding at dawn/dusk -> Travel to water -> Travel to feeding areas -> Return to bedding. The trails connecting these three locations are the highest-value trap sites.

### Step 7: Build an Area Profile
Spend 1-2 hours walking slowly to catalog signs and create a mental map of:
1. Water sources
2. Feeding areas
3. Bedding areas
4. Connecting trails

### Step 8: Select Trap Sites
Based on the signs found, the best trap locations are:
*   Fresh scat near water: On the trail between water and nearest cover (Snare).
*   Active rabbit run through brush: In the tunnel, with guide sticks (Snare).
*   Squirrel midden under tree: At the base of the tree, baited with nuts (Deadfall).
*   Deer trail between bedding and browse: On the narrow section of the trail (Large snare, leg or neck).
*   Boar rooting area: Near the wallow or rooting edge, baited with heavy deadfall or a pit.

### Other Sign Types
*   **Antler rubs:** Stripped bark on small trees at 50-120 cm height, indicating a resident deer population.
*   **Claw marks on trees:** Bear (large, deep gouges) or bobcat/mountain lion (smaller, parallel scratches). Look for shredded wood from bears tearing logs for insects.
*   **Game trails:** Width indicates size (10-15 cm for rabbits, 30-40 cm for deer, 50+ cm for elk or bear).
*   **Tunnels through brush:** Rabbit or small mammal runs, ideal snare locations.
*   **Musk or urine odor:** Fox dens, cat spray marks, rutting deer.
*   **Alarm calls:** Jays, crows, and squirrels sound alarm calls when predators or large animals are present.

## Notes
*   **Safety Warning:** Never handle scat with bare hands due to potential diseases (roundworm, hantavirus, parasites). Use a stick to break apart scat. Wash hands thoroughly.
*   **Scat Interpretation:** Fresh scat tells *when* the animal was here; old scat tells *what* lives there.
*   **Predator Caution:** If large predator scat (wolf, bear, mountain lion) is found with fresh kill remains, exercise extreme caution. Avoid setting traps within 500 meters of active predator dens.
*   **Common Mistakes:** Ignoring old signs, confusing dog and fox scat, only looking at the ground (need to scan vertically), and rushing the observation process.

### Scat Reference Table

| Animal | Shape | Size | Contents | Where Found |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Rabbit | Round pellets | 8-12 mm diameter | Dry, fibrous plant matter | Open fields, near burrows, in clusters |
| Deer | Oval pellets or clumped mass | 10-15 mm each | Plant fiber, sometimes seeds | Along trails, near browse areas |
| Raccoon | Tubular, blunt ends | 5-8 cm long | Seeds, berries, insect parts, bone | At base of trees, on logs, on rocks |
| Fox | Tapered, twisted | 5-10 cm long | Fur, bone fragments, berry seeds | On prominent rocks, trail junctions |
| Coyote / Wolf | Rope-like, tapered | 10-15 cm long | Heavy fur/bone content, sometimes teeth | Along travel corridors, at territory edges |
| Bear | Large irregular mass | 10-25 cm across | Berries, seeds, insect parts, grass | On trails, near feeding areas |
| Squirrel | Small oval pellets | 5-8 mm | Plant fiber | At base of trees, near nests |
| Wild Boar | Irregular clumps | 8-15 cm | Roots, acorns, mixed plant/animal | Near rooting sites, mud wallows |
