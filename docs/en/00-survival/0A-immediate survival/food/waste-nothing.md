# Bone Tools, Sinew, Hides

## Objective
To utilize every component of an animal kill for survival, ensuring that nothing is wasted. Every component must have a survival function.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- A medium-sized game animal (deer-sized) is required to yield the components detailed.
- Basic skills for processing, skinning, and careful tool-making are necessary.

## Material
- **Meat:** 40-50% of body weight, used for food.
- **Bones:** 15-20% of body weight, used for tools, weapons, needles, fishhooks, glue, broth.
- **Hides:** 8-12% of body weight, used for leather, rawhide, containers, clothing, shelter.
- **Organs:** 8-10% of body weight, used for food (liver, heart, kidneys, lungs), containers (stomach, bladder).
- **Fat:** 5-15% (seasonal), used for cooking oil, lamp fuel, waterproofing, skin protection, and tanning.
- **Sinew:** 2-3% of body weight, the strongest natural thread/cordage.

## System
Survival in a post-collapse world where every animal killed represents hours of effort, calories spent, and risk taken.

## Technology
- **Bone Superiority:** Bone is harder than wood and holds an edge longer, making it superior for certain tools.
- **Sinew Strength:** Sinew possesses tensile strength 2-4 times stronger than plant fiber of the same diameter.
- **Leather Quality:** Brain-tanned leather is soft, flexible, breathable, and comfortable.

## Tools
### Bone Tools
- **Needles:** Used to make fitted clothing and repair gear.
    - *Process:* Requires selection of a dense bone splinter (e.g., cannon bone), grinding to a tapered point, and drilling the eye hole.
- **Awls:** Thick, pointed bone tools used to punch holes in leather before sewing.
    - *Process:* Split a leg bone lengthwise, select a thick splinter, and grind one end to a strong, sharp point.
- **Scrapers and Fleshers:** Flat bone pieces with one sharpened edge used for scraping hides (removing fat and membrane).
- **Other Bone Tools:
    - Fishhook (from small splinter, rib): Used for fishing.
    - Pressure flaker (from antler tine, dense leg bone): Used for flint knapping.
    - Chisel (from leg bone section): Used for woodworking/carving.
    - Buttons/toggles (from antler slices, flat bone): Used for clothing fasteners.
    - Digging stick tip (from dense leg bone): Used for digging roots.
    - Gorge hook (from small bone splinter): Used for primitive fishing.

### Sinew
- **Uses:** Thread (when moistened), lashing (when wrapped and dried), bowstring.

## Process
### Bone Tool Making
1. **Needle Creation:** Select splinter, grind to a tapered point, drill the eye hole, and smooth the eye with sinew or fine sand.
2. **Awl Creation:** Split leg bone lengthwise, select a thick splinter, grind one end to a sharp point, leave the other end thick for gripping.

### Sinew Harvesting and Use
1. **Harvesting:** Locate back sinew (along the spine) or leg sinew (Achilles tendon). Peel the strips free without cutting the sinew itself.
2. **Drying:** Lay strips flat to dry in 1-2 days into hard strips.
3. **Using as Thread:** Moisten dried sinew in the mouth to make it flexible; the shrinkage locks stitches tight.
4. **Using as Lashing:** Wrap wet strips around joints; as they dry, they contract into an iron-tight binding (apply resin/glue for waterproofing).

### Hide Processing
- **Rawhide (No Tanning):**
    1. Flesh the hide thoroughly.
    2. Stretch on a frame or stake it.
    3. Dry completely (2-7 days).
    4. *Uses:* Containers, shields, shoe soles, lashing material.
- **Brain Tanning (Soft Leather):**
    1. Remove hair; soak the hide in a wood-ash solution for 2-5 days.
    2. Prepare brain solution (mash brain into warm water).
    3. Soak the de-haired hide in the solution.
    4. Work the hide by stretching, pulling, and flexing continuously during drying.
    5. Smoke the finished hide over smoldering fire (1-2 hours) to waterproof and prevent stiffening.

### Fat Processing
- **Rendering:** Cut fat into small pieces and heat slowly until liquid (tallow or lard) forms. Strain solids.
- **Uses:** Cooking, lamp fuel, waterproofing, skin protection, candle making, pemmican mixture.

### Organ Processing
- **Stomach:** Cleaned and dried; used as a waterproof container or cooking vessel.
- **Bladder:** Used as a waterproof pouch for carrying water or storing rendered fat.
- **Intestines:** Cleaned, turned inside out, and dried; used as sausage casing or lightweight cordage.
- **Blood:** Mixed with fat and cooked as blood pudding; can bind soil.
- **Hooves and Antler Bases:** Boiled for hours to extract hide glue.

## Notes
- Nothing is waste: Every component has a function.
- Bone needles and awls are essential for making fitted clothing from hides; time spent making them well is critical.
- Bone needles are fragile and must be stored in a protective case (hollow reed or bone).
- Brain tanning is labor-intensive and requires constant attention during the working/drying phase.
- Rendered fat must be processed immediately as it spoils quickly.
