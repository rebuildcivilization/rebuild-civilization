# Refraction Aiming

## Objective
To correct for light bending (refraction) when light passes from water to air, ensuring the spearfisher correctly judges the fish's true depth and position to eat it.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Clear water for accurate depth judgment.
- A spearfishing tool (spear) and a light source (torch) for observation.
- Practice and patience for developing an intuitive correction skill.

## Material
- Water (clear and murky conditions).
- Fish.
- Light (torchlight).
- Distinctly colored stone for training.
- Straight stick for calibration.

## System
- Refraction: The physical phenomenon where light bends when passing from water to air.
- Perception Error: The brain incorrectly reconstructing the fish's position based on the refracted light rays.

## Technology
- Refraction Aiming (The core technique).
- Torchlight: Used to illuminate fish and aid in positioning.

## Tools
- Spear
- Colored Stone
- Straight Stick

## Process
### The Rule of Refraction
Light bends at the water surface, making fish appear higher and closer than reality. The fish is always deeper and slightly farther away than it appears.

### The Numbers That Matter
| Your Viewing Angle | Apparent Depth vs. Real Depth | How Much to Aim Lower |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 90 degrees (straight down) | Fish appears at 75% of real depth | Minimal — aim at what you see |
| 60 degrees | Fish appears at ~67% of real depth | Aim 1/3 below apparent position |
| 45 degrees | Fish appears at ~58% of real depth | Aim halfway between apparent position and bottom |
| 30 degrees | Fish appears at ~50% of real depth | Aim at double the apparent depth |
| 15 degrees (very shallow) | Fish appears at ~35% of real depth | Nearly impossible to correct — reposition |

### The Practical Correction Rules
1. **Rule 1: Aim Low**: Always aim below where you see the fish. If unsure, aim lower than perceived.
2. **Rule 2: Get Above the Target**: To minimize distortion, position the line of sight as close to vertical as possible (wade into water, stand on elevated banks, or lean directly over the target).
3. **Rule 3: Use Depth as Your Guide**: Apply specific depth corrections based on the fish's depth:
    - Fish at 15 cm depth: aim about 4-5 cm below.
    - Fish at 30 cm depth: aim about 8-10 cm below.
    - Fish at 60 cm depth: aim about 15-20 cm below.
    - Fish deeper than 1 meter: use traps or lines.

### Training Your Eye
- **Practice Method 1: Stone Targeting**: Place a stone on the bottom (20-40 cm deep), thrust the spear at the stone, and note the actual hit point relative to the stone. Repeat 20-30 times.
- **Practice Method 2: Shadow Verification**: Compare the fish's shadow on the bottom (shows true horizontal position) to where the fish appears to be. The gap reveals the refraction error.
- **Practice Method 3: Stick Calibration**: Plunge a stick in at the normal spear angle and note where it appears to bend at the surface to determine the shift in objects.

### Factors That Change Refraction
- **Water Clarity**: Murky water causes fish to appear closer to the surface, requiring aiming lower and reducing effective spearing range (strike targets within 30-40 cm of the surface).
- **Water Surface Conditions**: Dead calm offers the clearest view. Light ripples cause constant shifting; choppy water leads to severe distortion, often requiring waiting for calm moments.
- **Time of Day**: Midday offers best visibility. Morning/evening requires positioning the observer so the sun is behind to minimize glare. Overcast conditions are excellent.

### Combining Refraction with Spear Technique
1. **Pre-aim**: Position the spear tip below and slightly behind the fish's apparent position before thrusting.
2. **Commit fully**: Once the strike decision is made, drive straight through.
3. **Follow through into the bottom**: Aim to pin the fish against the substrate, compensating for depth error.
4. **Lead moving fish**: Add additional lead in the direction of travel to account for swimming.

### Night Spearing and Refraction
- Torchlight illuminates fish from a high angle, making them easier to see, but refraction still applies.
- Hold the torch to one side, not directly above the spear arm, to prevent the shadow from blocking the target.
- Fish often freeze in torchlight, providing time for careful aim correction.

## Notes
- Refraction correction is a learned skill requiring practice.
- The key to success is immediate feedback during training.
- Shadow verification is useful for left-right calibration, while depth rules handle up-down correction.
- The general correction factor for a fish in clear water viewed from directly above is an apparent depth of roughly 75% of its actual depth.
