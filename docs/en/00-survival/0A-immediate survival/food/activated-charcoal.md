# Making Activated Carbon

## Objective
To remove contaminants from water, including trace organics, dissolved heavy metals, and volatile compounds, by dramatically increasing the porosity and surface area of charcoal.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Charcoal (pre-made hardwood charcoal chunks).
*   Water (for steam activation or chemical solution).
*   A source of steam or a hot fire.
*   A metal container with a tight-fitting lid (for activation vessels).
*   Tongs or thick gloves for handling hot materials.
*   Chemical reagents for chemical activation (e.g., Calcium chloride, Lye).

## Material
| Source Material | Quality | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Coconut shell | Excellent | Highest micropore density, ideal for water filtration. Available in tropical regions. |
| Fruit pits (peach, olive, cherry) | Excellent | Very hard, dense carbon with fine pore structure. |
| Hardwood (oak, maple, beech) | Very good | Dense structure creates good pore network. Most universally available. |
| Bamboo | Very good | High silica content creates natural micropores. Excellent in tropical/subtropical areas. |
| Softwood (pine, spruce) | Fair | Lower density, resin can clog pores. Use only if hardwood is unavailable. |
| Bone | Fair | Produces “bone char”—better for fluoride removal than organic chemicals. |

## System
Activated carbon is charcoal processed to increase porosity, making it far more effective at adsorbing contaminants than regular campfire charcoal. The activation process opens millions of additional micropores.

## Technology
The core technology is the activation process, which creates additional micropores. The surface area of activated carbon can reach 1,000–2,000 square meters per gram, compared to 200–500 square meters per gram for plain charcoal.

## Tools
*   Metal container with a tight-fitting lid.
*   Tongs or thick gloves.
*   Heat source (fire).
*   Measuring tools (implied for concentration and time).

## Process
### Method 1: Steam Activation (Preferred)
1.  **Prepare the charcoal:** Start with fully carbonized hardwood charcoal; break it into pieces roughly 2-3 cm (about 1 inch) across.
2.  **Load the activation vessel:** Pack the charcoal loosely into the metal container, leaving space for steam circulation.
3.  **Introduce steam:** Options include direct steam injection via a tube, wet-packing cloth/moss, or using a water reservoir beneath the charcoal.
4.  **Heat the vessel:** Place the loaded container into or over a hot fire. Maintain sustained temperatures of 600-900 degrees Celsius (1,100-1,650 degrees Fahrenheit) for 3-6 hours.
5.  **Cool and test:** Remove the container and let it cool completely. Properly activated charcoal should be lighter in weight, very black and matte, brittle, and noticeably more porous.
6.  **Crush for use:** Crush to coarse granules for water filtration (size of coarse sand to small peas) or fine powder for emergency poison ingestion treatment.

### Method 2: Chemical Activation
1.  **Prepare the solution:** Dissolve the reagent in water at the specified concentration.
2.  **Soak the charcoal:** Soak crushed charcoal chunks (1-3 cm pieces) in the solution for 24 hours, stirring occasionally.
3.  **Transfer:** Drain the solution and transfer the soaked charcoal to a metal container.
4.  **Heat:** Heat in a fire at 450-600 degrees Celsius (850-1,110 degrees Fahrenheit) for 1-2 hours to catalyze pore formation.
5.  **Rinse:** Cool completely, then rinse the activated charcoal thoroughly with clean water (at least 5-6 rinses) to remove all residual chemical.
6.  **Dry:** Dry the rinsed charcoal in the sun or near a fire before use.

## Notes
*   **Pathogen Warning:** Even the best activated carbon does not kill pathogens; water must always be disinfected separately before drinking.
*   **Safety Warning:** A fully sealed container under heat WILL explode; position the vent away from people.
*   **Chemical Residue:** Thorough rinsing during chemical activation is critical, as residue will leach into the water.
*   **Testing:** Success is gauged by a clearer, cleaner, and less odorous output when filtered water is run through the carbon compared to regular charcoal.
*   **Storage and Shelf Life:** Store in airtight containers and keep dry. Use within weeks of production for best results. Do not store near strong-smelling substances as the carbon will adsorb these odors.
*   **Key Takeaway:** Activated carbon has 2-10 times the surface area of regular charcoal.
