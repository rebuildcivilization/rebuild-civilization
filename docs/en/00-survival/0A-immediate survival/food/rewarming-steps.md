# Rewarming Steps | Survival Wiki

## Objective
Active rewarming is the next critical step once hypothermia has been recognized and heat loss has been stopped.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Recognition of hypothermia.
- Stopping heat loss (shelter, dry clothing, ground insulation).

## Material
- Sleeping bags, blankets, driest insulating material.
- Ground insulation (pine boughs, foam, dry leaves, at least 10 cm (4 inches) thick).
- Watertight containers (water bottles, canteens, sealed jars, double-bagged plastic bags).
- Cloth layers (socks, shirt sleeves, bandannas) for wrapping heat sources.

## System
- **Priority Order for Rewarming:**
    1. Stop ongoing heat loss (shelter, dry clothing, ground insulation).
    2. Rewarm the core (trunk, armpits, groin).
    3. Provide warm fluids (mild cases only).
    4. Allow extremities to rewarm naturally last.

## Technology
- Skin-to-Skin Contact (most effective rewarming technique without medical equipment).
- Improvised Heat Sources (Warm Water Containers, Heated Stones, Fire-Warmed Blanket Rotation).

## Tools
- Sleeping bag, blankets, ground insulation materials.
- Watertight containers.
- Cloth layers.
- Fire (for blanket rotation).

## Process

### Skin-to-Skin Contact
1. Prepare the cocoon: Lay out sleeping bag/blankets, place ground insulation underneath.
2. Strip both people (torso contact). Keep underwear on for dignity if conscious.
3. Position correctly: Rescuer behind/beside, pressing chest/abdomen against the victim's back/torso, wrapping arms around the trunk.
4. Wrap everything tightly, tucking edges to eliminate air gaps. Cover heads, leaving only faces exposed for breathing.
5. Two rescuers create a heat sandwich, roughly doubling the rewarming rate.

### Warm Fluid Administration
- **When to Give Fluids:** Victim must be conscious, alert enough to hold a cup, and able to swallow without coughing.
- **What to Give:**
    - Warm sugar water (2-3 tablespoons sugar per cup of warm water).
    - Warm honey water (1-2 tablespoons honey per cup of warm water).
    - Warm broth (any meat or bone broth heated to drinking temp).
    - Herbal tea (mild herbs only; avoid stimulants).
- **Temperature:** Comfortably warm to drink, around 40-45°C (104-113°F).
- **Amount:** Small, frequent sips (a quarter cup (60 ml) every 10-15 minutes) because cold stomachs lose heat/fluids quickly.
- **Never Give:** Alcohol, coffee, or strong tea (can cause blood vessel dilation or cardiac arrhythmia), or cold water.

### Improvised Heat Packs
- **Warm Water Containers:** Heat water to 40-50°C (104-122°F). Wrap containers in cloth. Place at Neck (carotid arteries), Armpits (brachial arteries), Groin (femoral arteries), or against the chest.
- **Heated Stones:** Warm smooth, dry stones near (not in) a fire. Wrap in multiple cloth layers. Place against the trunk in priority locations.
- **Fire-Warmed Blanket Rotation:** Warm one blanket near the fire, wrap the victim, and swap with the next warmed blanket continuously.

### Monitoring Recovery
- **Signs of Recovery:**
    1. Shivering returns.
    2. Mental clarity improves.
    3. Coordination returns (can grip objects, walk unassisted).
    4. Skin color normalizes (pink replaces pale/gray/blue tones).

### When Rewarming Fails
- Continue rewarming.
- Ensure adequate ground insulation.
- Add more heat sources (heated stones, skin-to-skin, warm fluids simultaneously).

## Notes
- **Rescuer Safety:** Providing skin-to-skin contact with a severely hypothermic person will make the rescuer cold. Monitor rescuer for shivering; if shivering hard, switch out.
- **Burn Risk:** Hypothermic skin has reduced blood flow and cannot dissipate heat normally, making burns easy. Always use an insulating layer between heat sources and skin, and check regularly.
- **Recovery Duration:** Recovery takes hours, not minutes. The body’s thermoregulation system remains disrupted, and relapse is common if the person returns to cold exposure too soon. Commit to sustained rewarming and monitor for at least 12 hours after improvement.
- **Key Takeaway:** Skin-to-skin contact is the most reliable field rewarming method; warm the core first (trunk, armpits, groin), never the extremities to prevent afterdrop.
