# Smokehouse Build Guide

## Objective
To build a smokehouse by placing a small firebox at the bottom of a natural slope and connecting it to a food chamber at the top via a trench, cooling the smoke to 15–30°C before it reaches the food, enabling precise control over temperature and smoke density for preservation.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Natural slope (hillside) is ideal for natural draft.
- Well-drained soil is required; waterlogged ground is impractical.
- Proximity to water is needed for fire safety.
- Wind protection is needed to prevent draft problems; if exposed, the firebox should be on the windward side.

## Material
- Hardwood chunks or green hardwood for the firebox fire.
- Flat stones or packed clay for lining the firebox walls.
- Timber for the smoking chamber frame and rails.
- Green sticks for removable lattice racks (shelf racks).
- Clay or mud for sealing joints and mortar.
- Split planks, woven wattle (plastered with mud/clay daub), or bark panels for chamber walls.

## System
The smokehouse system consists of three main components:

| Component | Purpose | Key Requirement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Firebox | Burns wood, generates smoke | Separate from chamber, easy fuel access |
| Smoke channel | Carries smoke from firebox to chamber | 2–3 meters long, slopes upward, cools smoke |
| Smoking chamber | Holds food in smoke | Airtight sides, ventilation at top, hanging/rack system |

## Technology
- Natural Draft: The slope provides the draft, and the distance of the smoke channel cools the smoke before it reaches the food.
- Temperature Control: The system allows for cold smoking (15-30°C) or hot smoking (55-80°C) by adjusting the top vent.

## Tools
- Tools for digging the firebox pit (60 cm deep, 60 cm wide, 90 cm long).
- Tools for lining walls (flat stones, packed clay).
- Tools for construction of the chamber frame (setting posts, connecting rails).
- Tools for sealing gaps (clay, mud, moss).
- Tools for hanging systems (hardwood pegs or bent wire hooks).

## Process
### Building the Smokehouse
1. **Build the Firebox:** Dig a pit 60 cm deep, 60 cm wide, and 90 cm long at the low end of the slope.
2. **Line the Walls:** Line the walls with flat stones or packed clay to prevent cave-ins and reflect heat.
3. **Create Front Opening:** Create a front opening for loading fuel and airflow, using a flat stone as a door (slide it partially closed to reduce airflow).
4. **Connect the Channel:** Connect the firebox to the smoke channel at the back wall, with the channel opening at ground level or slightly above.
5. **Build the Chamber:** Construct the frame with minimum dimensions of 90 cm wide, 90 cm deep, and 150–180 cm tall.
6. **Wall the Frame:** Wall the frame with split planks, wattle, bark panels, or materials that block airflow.
7. **Create the Floor:** Ensure the chamber floor has an opening connecting to the smoke channel (bare earth or raised stone floor with a gap).
8. **Build the Channel:** Dig a trench from the firebox uphill to the chamber location. The minimum length is 2–3 meters; 4–5 meters is better for cold smoking.
9. **Cover and Seal the Channel:** Cover the trench with flat stones or split logs, pile 10–15 cm of earth on top, and seal joints with packed clay or mud.
10. **Install Hanging Systems:** Drive hardwood pegs or wire hooks into horizontal rails and hang food.
11. **Install Ventilation:** Cut or leave a gap at the top of the chamber (10–15 cm across) for the top vent.
12. **Install Adjustable Vent:** Install a sliding stone, wooden flap, or bark panel over the top vent to regulate airflow.

### Operating the Smokehouse
**Cold Smoking Procedure:**
1. Build a small fire in the firebox using hardwood and let it burn to coals.
2. Add green hardwood chunks or dampened chips to the coals.
3. Partially close the firebox door to restrict airflow.
4. Check chamber temperature; it should feel cool or barely warm (below body temperature).
5. Maintain smoke for 12–48 hours, adding green wood every 1–2 hours.
6. Food is done when it has a deep golden to mahogany color, a firm surface pellicle, and has lost significant weight.

**Hot Smoking Procedure:**
1. Build a larger fire in the firebox with more coals and heat.
2. Open the firebox door wider for more airflow.
3. Chamber temperature should be 55–80°C (uncomfortable to hold your hand inside for more than 1–2 seconds).
4. Smoke for 4–8 hours.
5. Food should be fully cooked, firm, and deeply colored.

### Maintenance
- Clean soot and creosote from chamber walls every 5–10 uses.
- Inspect the channel after rain or freeze-thaw cycles to check for leaks from shifting stones.
- Rebuild clay/mud seals as they crack and dry out.
- Replace wooden racks and pegs when they become charred or weakened.

## Notes
- The key design principle is the 2–3 meter (minimum) smoke channel between the firebox and chamber, sloping uphill to create natural draft and cool the smoke.
- The chamber minimum dimensions are 90 cm × 90 cm × 150 cm tall, handling 20–30 kg of food per batch.
- A fire the size of a dinner plate generates adequate smoke for the chamber load.
- If the channel is shorter than 2 meters, the smoke reaching the chamber will be too hot for cold smoking, though hot smoking with a short channel is possible.
- The top vent is mandatory for controlled ventilation to allow spent smoke and moisture to exit.
- The bottom airflow is controlled by the firebox door.
- Cold smoking (15–30°C, 12–48 hours) combined with salt curing produces the longest-lasting preserved food.
