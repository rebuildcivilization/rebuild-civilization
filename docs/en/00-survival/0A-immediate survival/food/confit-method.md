# Confit Method

## Objective
To preserve fully cooked meat for three to twelve months without refrigeration by sealing it under a continuous layer of solidified animal fat that cuts off oxygen and moisture.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Meat: Any animal — game, poultry, pork, fish (oily species). Bone-in pieces work best; cut into fist-sized portions.
- Animal fat: Rendered from the same or different animal.
- Salt (optional): Sea salt, rock salt, or plant-ash salt.
- Cooking vessel: Clay pot, metal pot, or stone-lined pit. Must be heat-resistant and deep enough to submerge meat.
- Storage vessel: Ceramic crock, clay pot with lid, or sealed jar. Must be non-porous or lined to prevent fat seepage.
- Heat source: Campfire with coals, or earth oven. Need sustained low heat for 3-6 hours.

## Material
- Meat: Bone-in pieces.
- Animal fat: Rendered liquid fat.
- Salt (optional).

## System
Spoilage requires bacteria, moisture at the surface, and oxygen. The confit method attacks all three by:
1. Slow-cooking at low heat killing surface bacteria and parasites.
2. Rendering fat driving moisture out of the outer layers.
3. Solidified fat creating an airtight seal that cuts off oxygen.

## Technology
- Fat Sealing: The solidified fat forms an airtight, antimicrobial barrier.
- Temperature Control: Slow cooking at roughly 90-100 degrees Celsius for 3-6 hours.

## Tools
- Cooking vessel
- Storage vessel
- Forked stick or two flat sticks (for transferring meat)

## Process
### Step 1: Render the Fat
1. Collect raw fat (belly fat, kidney fat, fat around organs, skin with fat attached).
2. Cut fat into small pieces (1-2 cm cubes).
3. Heat slowly over very low heat; melt the fat, ensuring it melts, not fries. Check for gentle bubbling, not sizzling or popping.
4. Strain the liquid fat through woven cloth or layered grass into the storage vessel. Discard cracklings.

### Step 2: Prepare the Meat
1. Cut meat into bone-in pieces the size of a fist.
2. Optional salt cure: Rub meat with coarse salt (15-20g per kg of meat) and let it rest for 12-24 hours.
3. Pat meat dry to remove surface moisture.

### Step 3: Slow-Cook in Fat
1. Place meat in the cooking vessel.
2. Pour rendered fat over the meat, ensuring at least 2-3 cm of fat covers the topmost piece.
3. Heat to a gentle simmer (90-100°C); occasional small bubbles are acceptable, but avoid a rolling boil.
4. Maintain temperature for 3-6 hours. Meat is done when it pulls easily from the bone and feels tender.
5. Do not stir the meat.

### Step 4: Pack and Seal
1. Transfer cooked meat to the storage vessel snugly.
2. Pour hot liquid fat over the meat, ensuring fat covers all meat by at least 3-4 cm.
3. Tap the vessel gently to release trapped air bubbles.
4. Let cool undisturbed (4-8 hours) for the fat to solidify into a wax-like seal.
5. Inspect the seal; melt additional fat and pour it over cracks to fill gaps.
6. Cover the vessel to keep out dust and insects.

## Storage Conditions and Shelf Life
| Storage Temperature | Expected Shelf Life |
| :--- | :--- | 
| Cool cellar / underground (10-15 C) | 3-6 months |
| Root cellar / cave (5-10 C) | 6-12 months |
| Warm room temperature (20-25 C) | 2-4 weeks only |
| Frozen ground / snow cache | 12+ months |

**Key storage rules:**
- Store in the coolest, darkest place available (Underground is ideal).
- Never store in direct sunlight or near a heat source.
- Check the fat seal weekly; re-seal immediately if cracks appear.
- Once the seal is broken, consume exposed meat within 2-3 days; re-seal remaining meat with fresh melted fat.

## Serving Confit Meat
1. Scrape off excess fat (save it for the next batch).
2. Reheat the meat near a fire or in a hot pan for better flavor and texture.

## Troubleshooting
| Problem | Cause | Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Meat smells sour or off when unsealed | Air pocket in fat seal, or storage too warm | Discard; improve seal and storage next time |
| Fat has gone rancid (sharp, bitter smell) | Exposure to light/heat, or fat overheated during rendering | Discard; render fat at lower temperature next time, store in dark |
| Meat is dry and stringy | Cooked at too high a temperature | Cook more gently next time; fat should barely simmer |
| Mold on fat surface | Moisture contamination or cracked seal | Scrape off mold and 2 cm of fat below it. Re-seal if meat beneath smells fine; discard if meat smells off |

## Key Takeaways
- Confit preserves meat for 3-12 months without salt, smoke, or dehydration.
- The fat seal must be continuous and airtight.
- Cook meat low and slow (90-100°C for 3-6 hours) fully submerged in rendered fat.
- Store in the coolest, darkest location available; underground storage extends shelf life dramatically.
- Save and reuse confit fat as it improves with each batch and is calorie-dense.

Notes
- The rendered fat is reusable for subsequent batches.
- The heat control discipline (90-100°C) is critical to prevent the fat from boiling or smoking.
- Poultry requires about 3 hours of cooking time; larger pieces need 4-6 hours.
