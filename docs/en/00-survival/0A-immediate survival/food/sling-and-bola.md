# Sling and Bola

## Objective
To create and utilize sling and bola as practical throwing weapons for survival situations, offering effective range and coverage for hunting.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Time Commitment:** Hours are required to build the weapons, and significant practice is needed to achieve consistent release timing.
*   **Skill Development:** Consistent release timing requires 200–500 throws for consistency and 1,000–2,000 throws for effective hunting accuracy.
*   **Environment:** Practice must occur in open areas with no one within 100 meters in any direction.

## Material
*   **Pouch:** Leather, woven fabric, or braided cordage.
*   **Cords:** Strong cordage such as braided plant fiber, sinew, or rawhide strips.
*   **Projectile Ammunition:** Smooth, dense river cobbles (50–100 g), round stones (40–80 g), clay bullets (30–60 g), or lead bullets (30–50 g).

## System
### Sling Anatomy
*   Retention cord: Attached to a finger loop; held during the throw.
*   Pouch: Cradles the projectile (typically woven or leather).
*   Release cord: Held between the thumb and forefinger; released at launch.
*   Projectile: Smooth, dense stones (or shaped clay/lead bullets).
*   Total Length: 60-90 cm (24-36 inches) for beginners.

### Bola System
*   The bola (boleadora) is a set of weights connected by cords that spread in flight to entangle the target.

## Technology
### Projectile Selection
| Projectile | Weight | Effectiveness | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| River cobbles (egg-shaped) | 50-100 g | Excellent | Naturally smoothed, consistent shape |
| Round stones | 40-80 g | Good | Any smooth, dense stone |
| Clay bullets (shaped, dried) | 30-60 g | Excellent | Can mass-produce uniform ammo |
| Lead bullets (cast) | 30-50 g | Superior | Requires metalworking (future tech tier) |

### Effective Range and Coverage
*   **Sling:** Effective range up to 40–50 meters once trained.
*   **Bola:** Effective range of 15–25 meters. The spreading cords create a capture zone of 1–2 meters.

## Tools
### Building Tools
*   Leather cutting and punching tools (for the pouch).
*   Aptitude for braiding or weaving.
*   Tools for carving grooves (if using the simpler method for securing stones).

### Throwing Tools
*   The ability to manage release timing (the entire skill).
*   The ability to execute specific techniques (Overhand, Underhand).

## Process
### Sling Construction Process
1.  **Pouch Preparation:** Cut an oval of leather (approx. 8 x 12 cm), punch holes, thread cords, and shape a slight cup by soaking the leather around a stone.
2.  **Cord Preparation:** Ensure cords are strong (braided plant fiber, sinew, rawhide) and set the retention cord (loop) and release cord (overhand knot).
3.  **Length Adjustment:** Start with shorter cords and lengthen them as skill improves to generate more velocity.

### Bola Construction Process (Three-Ball Example)
1.  **Stone Preparation:** Use 3 stones, each 150–300 g, as round as possible.
2.  **Wrapping:** Wrap each stone in a leather pouch (15 x 15 cm square), securing it with a cord, leaving a long tail.
3.  **Joining:** Join the three cord ends at a central point by tying an overhand knot.
4.  **Equalization:** Ensure all three cords are the same length from the central knot to the stones to prevent the bola from flying lopsided.

### Throwing Techniques
*   **Overhand Technique (Most Accurate for Beginners):**
    1.  Load pouch, hold retention loop on the middle finger.
    2.  Begin windup, swinging the sling backward and overhead in a vertical plane.
    3.  Release timing: Release the release cord when the pouch is moving forward and slightly upward (roughly when the hand passes the ear).
*   **Underhand Technique (For Longer Range/Lower Trajectories):**
    1.  Pouch starts hanging behind the thrower.
    2.  Swing forward and upward in one smooth motion.
    3.  Release when the pouch passes hip level on the upswing.
*   **Bola Throwing:**
    1.  Grip the central knot/handle section.
    2.  Hold one or two weights in the dominant hand.
    3.  Swing the free weight in a horizontal circle overhead to build centrifugal force and spin all weights.
    4.  Release when the bola is spinning at full speed, aiming below and slightly ahead of the target.

### Training Progression
| Stage | Target | Distance | Goal |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Large blanket hung vertically | 10 m | Hit the blanket 8/10 times |
| 2 | 1-meter circle on ground | 15 m | Land stones in the circle 6/10 times |
| 3 | Tree trunk (30 cm width) | 20 m | Hit the trunk 5/10 times |
| 4 | Stump or log (small target) | 25-30 m | Hit 3/10 times |
| 5 | Moving target (rolling stone, tossed object) | 15-20 m | Track and connect |

## Notes
*   **Sling Effectiveness:** The sling is the most effective primitive ranged weapon relative to construction effort, taking an hour to build and requiring a week of practice to become effective.
*   **Projectile Consistency:** Consistency in ammunition (using smooth, dense, uniform stones) is critical for accuracy.
*   **Safety:** Beginners must start with soft projectiles (clay balls, bundled grass) until release timing is consistent, as early misreleases are random.
*   **Bola Limitation:** The bola does not work well in dense brush or forest because the cords catch on branches.
*   **Bola Outcome:** The bola entangles the target but does not kill; a follow-up weapon (club or knife) is required.
*   **Maintenance:** Sling cords must be checked before every use; leather pouches should be rubbed with animal fat to keep them supple; cracked stones must be replaced.
*   **Key Advantage:** Both weapons utilize free, unlimited ammunition, which is their greatest advantage over bows and spears.
