# Animal Tracking: Following Animal Signs

## Objective
Tracking is the art of reading the landscape for evidence of animal presence and movement. The goal is to build a complete picture of what animals live in an area, where they go, when they move, and what they are doing.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Suitable substrate (mud, snow, soft soil, sand) is required for tracks to be visible.
- Requires time for surveying, specifically spending two days surveying a 1 km radius around the base camp before attempting to hunt or trap.

## Material
Animal signs fall into seven categories:

1. **Tracks (Footprints):** Tell species, size, direction of travel, speed, and recency. Require suitable substrate.
2. **Scat (Droppings):** Reveal species, diet, health, and recency. Types include pellets (Deer, rabbit, goat), tubular segments (Canids), large tubular ends (Bear), twisted rope-like forms (Cats), irregular splashes (Birds of prey), and small cylindrical in latrines (Raccoon, badger).
3. **Browse and Feeding Sign:** Indicates what plants are eaten. Deer leave ragged, torn edges; rabbits leave clean 45-degree cuts; Beavers leave cone-shaped stumps; Bears leave torn logs and claw marks; Rodents leave parallel tooth grooves; Porcupines leave bark stripping.
4. **Trails and Runs:** Visible paths through vegetation. Width indicates species (Deer 20-30 cm, Rabbit 10-15 cm, Bear 30-50 cm). Tunnel-like runs indicate small mammals.
5. **Bedding and Denning Sites:** Deer beds are oval depressions; Rabbit forms are shallow scrapes; Bear dens are hollow logs or caves; Predator lairs are caves or rock overhangs.
6. **Territorial and Communication Sign:** Includes rubs (Deer/Elk stripping bark), scrapes (Deer pawing and urinating), claw marks on trees (Bear size indication), and scent posts (Canids marking objects).
7. **Disturbance Sign:** Includes overturned leaves, broken spider webs, dew trails, compressed vegetation, and disturbed water.

**Reading Sign in Different Substrates:**
| Substrate | Best Sign | Limitations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mud | Detailed tracks, clear prints | Dries and cracks; prints degrade within hours in sun |
| Snow | Excellent tracks, visible trails | Melts, fills with new snow; age estimation difficult |
| Sand | Good tracks, shows gait patterns | Wind erases prints quickly (hours in open areas) |
| Forest floor (leaf litter) | Overturned leaves, compressed litter, scat | No detailed track impressions |
| Grass | Trails, compressed vegetation, dew trails | Recovers quickly; hard to see in tall grass |
| Rock | Almost none | Look for scuffed lichen, scat deposits, hair caught on rough surfaces |

## System
The system involves surveying an area to map crucial elements and predicting animal movements.

**Habitat Mapping Steps:**
1. Water sources (streams, ponds, seeps, puddles) and noting tracks/trails leading to them.
2. Feeding areas (berry patches, nut trees, browse areas, insect-rich rotting logs).
3. Travel corridors (game trails connecting water, food, and bedding areas).
4. Bedding areas (locations with deer beds, rabbit forms, or den sites).
5. Territorial markers (rubs, scrapes, scat latrines, claw marks).

## Technology
- Quartz (used for generation/metadata).

## Tools
- Scratch map (for mapping).
- Traps and snares (for interception).

## Process
Tracking inverts the traditional hunting gamble by following these steps:
1. **Survey:** An area for signs of animal activity.
2. **Identify:** Which species are present and how many.
3. **Map:** Travel routes, feeding areas, water access, and bedding sites.
4. **Predict:** Where specific animals will be at specific times.
5. **Intercept:** At a known location, or set traps along confirmed travel routes.

## Notes
- Tracks are only one of seven categories of animal sign; effective trackers must read scat, browse damage, trails, beds, territorial marks, and disturbance patterns.
- Scat identification and aging are crucial for confirming species and recency, especially when substrate limitations exist.
- Game trails connecting water sources to feeding areas are identified as the highest-value locations for traps and ambush positions.
