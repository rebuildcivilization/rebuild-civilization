# Track Identification: Print Anatomy and Gait Patterns

## Objective
To extract an extraordinary amount of information from a single footprint, which includes the species, body size, sex (sometimes), direction of travel, speed, and whether the animal was healthy or injured.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Extracting information requires understanding print anatomy (the structural features of different foot types) and gait patterns (how the sequence and spacing of prints reveals behavior).

## Material
A single footprint contains an extraordinary amount of information.

## System
Mammal tracks fall into four foot types: ungulate (hoofed), canid (oval, 4 toes, claws), felid (round, 4 toes, no claws), and plantigrade (5 toes, flat-footed).

## Technology
Field testing methods are used to classify tracks and determine behavioral intent.

## Tools
*   Print analysis skills
*   Measurements (Stride and Straddle)
*   X-test for Canids vs. Felids
*   Straightedge (for distinguishing bear claw reach)

## Process
**Field Practice Method:**
1.  **Week 1 — Substrate Hunting:** Find and visit best tracking surfaces daily (muddy stream banks, sandy washes, dusty trails, snow fields). Photograph or sketch every print.
2.  **Week 2 — Species Classification:** For each print, determine the foot type (ungulate, canid, felid, plantigrade). Measure length, width, and count toes. Note presence or absence of claws.
3.  **Week 3 — Gait Reading:** Find and follow a track sequence for at least 50 meters. Determine the gait (walk, trot, lope, gallop). Measure stride and straddle. Note direction changes and potential causes.
4.  **Week 4 — Predictive Tracking:** Choose one track sequence and follow it to its destination. Predict the animal's path based on the pattern.

## Technology
### Print Anatomy
**Type 1: Ungulates (Hoofed Animals)**
*   **Species:** Deer, elk, moose, wild boar, goats, sheep, cattle, horses.
*   **Print Structure:** Cloven-hoofed (two parallel halves), Single-hoofed (horses).
*   **Key Measurements for Common Cloven-Hoofed Species:**
    *   White-tailed deer: Print Length 5-8 cm, Print Width 3-5 cm, Distinguishing Feature: Narrow, pointed tips, heart-shaped.
    *   Mule deer: Print Length 6-9 cm, Print Width 4-6 cm, Distinguishing Feature: Slightly wider and rounder than whitetail.
    *   Elk: Print Length 9-13 cm, Print Width 7-10 cm, Distinguishing Feature: Large, rounded tips, wide spread.
    *   Moose: Print Length 13-18 cm, Print Width 10-14 cm, Distinguishing Feature: Very large, pointed, splayed apart.
    *   Wild boar: Print Length 5-8 cm, Print Width 4-7 cm, Distinguishing Feature: Rounder than deer, dewclaws usually register.
    *   Domestic goat: Print Length 4-6 cm, Print Width 3-5 cm, Distinguishing Feature: Squarish toes, blunt tips.
*   **Reading Ungulate Prints:** Spread of two halves indicates speed/substrate softness. Dewclaw registration indicates running/jumping/deep mud/snow. Drag marks in front indicate tiredness, injury, or deep snow.

**Type 2: Canids (Dog Family)**
*   **Species:** Wolves, coyotes, foxes, domestic dogs.
*   **Print Structure:** Four toes symmetrically arranged around a central pad; claws usually visible; overall shape oval, longer than wide; two middle toes extend further forward.
*   **Distinguishing Feature:** The X-test (imaginary X between toes and pad) passes through open space in canid prints.
*   **Distinguishing Wild Canid from Domestic Dog:** Wild canid prints are compact; Domestic dog prints are often splayed.

**Type 3: Felids (Cat Family)**
*   **Species:** Cougar/mountain lion, bobcat, lynx, domestic cat.
*   **Print Structure:** Four toes asymmetrically arranged around a large central pad; **No claw marks** (cats retract claws); overall shape round.
*   **Key Identifier:** The absence of claw marks is the primary distinction from canids. If the print is round, four-toed, and lacks claw marks, it is a cat.

**Type 4: Plantigrade (Flat-Footed) Animals**
*   **Species:** Bears, raccoons, opossums, skunks, porcupines, humans.
*   **Print Structure:** Five toes visible; full foot registers (heel to toe); claws usually visible.
*   **Bear Track Caution:** Grizzly bears (claws extend 5-10 cm and are relatively straight) can be distinguished from black bears (claws extend 2-3 cm and curve sharply) by placing a straightedge across the top of the toe pads.

### Gait Patterns
**The Four Basic Gaits**
1.  **Walk:** Slowest gait. All four feet move independently. Prints are evenly spaced, close together, in a relatively straight line. Many animals “direct register” (hind foot lands in front foot’s print).
2.  **Trot:** Faster than a walk. Diagonal legs move together. Prints are further apart than a walk, still in a relatively straight line.
3.  **Lope/Canter:** Asymmetric gait for sustained medium-speed travel. Print pattern shows a repeating group of four prints with uneven spacing (two closer, gap, two more).
4.  **Gallop/Bound:** Maximum speed. All four feet leave the ground between strides. Print pattern shows groups of four prints clustered with large gaps between groups. In a full gallop, hind feet often land AHEAD of the front feet.

**Reading Gait for Behavior**
*   **Walk, direct register, straight line:** Normal travel (moving between known points).
*   **Walk, meandering, frequent stops:** Foraging or hunting (searching).
*   **Trot, straight line:** Purposeful travel, moderate urgency.
*   **Trot, frequent direction changes:** Hunting behavior (canids), or evasion without full alarm.
*   **Gallop/bound, straight line:** Flight (running from something).
*   **Gallop, then sudden stop:** Detected something and froze (common in deer).
*   **Irregular gait, uneven print depth:** Injury (one limb favored, producing lighter or dragged prints).

**Straddle and Stride Measurements**
*   **Stride:** Distance from one print to the next print of the same foot. Longer stride = larger animal or faster gait.
*   **Straddle:** Width between left and right prints. Wider straddle = heavier or wider-bodied animal. Efficient travelers (deer, canids) have narrow straddle.
*   **Key Measurements Table:**
    *   White-tailed deer: Walking Stride 45-55 cm, Straddle 10-15 cm (very narrow).
    *   Wolf: Walking Stride 60-75 cm, Straddle 12-18 cm.
    *   Black bear: Walking Stride 40-55 cm, Straddle 20-30 cm (wide).

## Notes
*   **Key Takeaways:** All mammal tracks fall into four foot types. The X-test separates canids from felids.
*   **Practical Application:** Successful tracking requires structured practice on reliable substrates (mud, snow, sand) for at least one month before relying on skills for food acquisition.
*   **Predator Warning:** Cougar tracks indicate a large predator is active; travel in groups and avoid running.
