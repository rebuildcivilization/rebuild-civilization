# Mushroom Foraging Guide

## Objective
To teach the reader how to safely identify wild mushrooms, distinguishing between edible and deadly species, and to outline the rules necessary to minimize risk during food-gathering activities.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

*   Training in mushroom foraging is recommended for safe practice.
*   Knowledge of deadly species (death cap, destroying angel, deadly webcap) should be acquired first.
*   The Universal Edibility Test (UET) is explicitly stated as insufficient for mushrooms.

## Material

Wild mushrooms.

## System

*   Risk-to-reward ratio analysis (high risk for modest nutritional payoff).
*   The Universal Edibility Test (UET).
*   Identification Checklist (requiring confirmation across multiple features).

## Technology

*   Spore Print Identification technique (the single most useful identification technique).
*   Knowledge of mushroom features (cap shape, gill/pore/ridge type, spore print color, stem features, habitat, season, smell).

## Tools

*   Paper (half white, half dark) for spore prints.
*   A bowl or cup to cover the spore print.
*   Cooking implements (oil or fire) for thorough cooking.

## Process

### Foraging Rules
1. Only eat mushrooms you can identify with 100% certainty.
2. Learn the deadly species FIRST (death cap, destroying angel, deadly webcap).
3. Use ALL identification features (cap shape, color, gill type, spore print, stem features, ring/volva, habitat, season, smell, growth pattern).
4. Start with the “Foolproof Five” species.
5. Never eat raw wild mushrooms; always cook them thoroughly.

### Identification Process (Spore Print)
1. Cut the cap from a mature (but not rotting) mushroom and place it gill-side down on a flat surface.
2. Use a piece of paper (half white, half dark spore colors).
3. Cover with a bowl or cup to prevent air currents from dispersing spores.
4. Wait 4-12 hours (overnight is ideal).
5. Carefully lift the cap and note the spore color.

### Key Identification Steps (Checklist)
Confirm the following:
*   Cap shape, size, and color match known species
*   Gill/pore/ridge type matches
*   Spore print color matches
*   Stem features match (ring, volva, solid vs. hollow)
*   Habitat matches
*   Season matches
*   Smell matches (if distinctive)
*   NO features of deadly species present (white gills + ring + volva = potential Amanita)
*   Confidence in identification.

## Notes

*   **Universal Edibility Test (UET) Failure:** The UET does not work for mushrooms because deadly species (like the death cap) taste mild and cause no immediate reaction, with toxins absorbed before symptoms appear (6-24 hours delay).
*   **Deadly Species to Learn First:** Death cap (*Amanita phalloides*), Destroying Angel (*Amanita virosa / A. bisporigera*), and Deadly Webcap (*Cortinarius rubellus*).
*   **Foolproof Five Species:** Giant Puffball (*Calvatia gigantea*), Chicken of the Woods (*Laetiporus sulphureus*), Chanterelle (*Cantharellus cibarius*), Hen of the Woods / Maitake (*Grifola frondosa*), and Morel (*Morchella* species).
*   **Puffball Check:** Always slice puffballs open before eating; an immature death cap egg looks like a small puffball from the outside.
*   **Morel Distinction:** True morels are completely hollow; false morels are wrinkled/brain-like and contain cottony tissue (gyromitrin).
