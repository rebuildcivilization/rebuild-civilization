# Natural Indicators

## Objective
To provide directional clues when a compass is lost by utilizing landscape features through vegetation, wind-sculpted features, snow melt patterns, and built structures.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

*   **Convergence:** Gather at least three independent indicators and look for agreement within roughly thirty degrees.
*   **Celestial Methods:** Use sun or stars as a fallback method when natural indicators disagree.

## Material

### Vegetation Indicators
*   **Sun-Side Growth:** Denser foliage, fruits/flowers ripen first, drier/lighter bark (Northern Hemisphere South side); darker, more textured, mossy bark (Northern Hemisphere North side).
*   **Tree Lean and Growth Asymmetry:** Trees develop asymmetric crowns; branches on the windward side are shorter/stunted.
*   **Stump Rings:** Rings are wider on the side receiving more sunlight (south side in the Northern Hemisphere).
*   **Moss Reality:** Moss grows wherever moisture and shade exist. It is dangerously unreliable as a standalone indicator but is somewhat useful in dry climates with open woodlands, often appearing on the shadier (north) side.

### Wind Indicators
*   **Prevailing Wind Direction:** General patterns vary by latitude (Trade winds E to W in tropics; Westerlies W to E in mid-latitudes; Polar easterlies E to W in polar regions).
*   **Wind-Sculpted Snowfields:** Sastrugi (wind-carved ridges) align parallel to the prevailing wind direction; cornices (overhanging snow) form on the downwind side.

### Water and Moisture Indicators
*   **Spring Melt Patterns:** Snow melts first on sun-facing slopes (South-facing slopes in the Northern Hemisphere).
*   **Dew and Frost Patterns:** Dew persists longer on north-facing surfaces (Northern Hemisphere) as they receive less early sun; frost melts first on south-facing surfaces.
*   **Stream and River Flow:** Generally flow downhill, but major rivers often have known general flow directions.

### Terrain Indicators
*   **Satellite Dish Alignment (Post-Apocalyptic Clue):** In North America, dishes point roughly south; in Europe, roughly south-southeast; in the Southern Hemisphere, roughly north.
*   **Church and Mosque Orientation:** Churches are oriented with the altar at the east end (main entrance west); Mosques are oriented toward Mecca (qibla roughly east-northeast to east-southeast in North America/Europe).
*   **Cemetery Headstones:** Traditional Western cemeteries bury heads to the west and feet to the east, so headstones face east.

## System

### Technology
*   Natural indicators provide directional clues when a compass is absent.
*   Post-collapse terrain features (like satellite dishes) offer reliable directional data.

### Tools
*   Observation of vegetation, wind patterns, snowmelt, dew/frost, animal behavior, and built structures.
*   Celestial methods (sun, stars) serve as the ultimate cross-reference tool.

## Process

### Step 1: Observe at Least 3 Independent Indicators
Identify indicators such as: sun-side growth, wind-sculpted features, snow melt/dew/frost patterns, animal behavior, and man-made structures.

### Step 2: Rate Each Indicator
*   **High Confidence:** Isolated tree in open ground, strong wind-sculpting, clear melt patterns.
*   **Medium Confidence:** Edge-of-forest tree, moderate wind evidence, ambiguous melt.
*   **Low Confidence:** Dense forest, no clear wind pattern, humid conditions.

### Step 3: Look for Convergence
Determine if three or more indicators point in the same general direction (within 30 degrees of each other) to establish a usable bearing. If indicators disagree widely, trust celestial methods.

### Step 4: Cross-Reference
Always confirm natural indicators against the sun or stars when available; natural indicators are the backup method.

## Notes

*   **Golden Rule:** Never trust a single natural indicator because every technique has exceptions (e.g., moss can grow on any side of a tree).
*   **Moss Myth Clarification:** The rule that moss grows on the north side is unreliable because moss needs moisture and shade, which can occur on any side due to nearby water sources or canopy gaps.
*   **Reliability Context:** Vegetation indicators are most reliable on isolated trees in open ground; reliability decreases in dense forests.
*   **Seasonal Context:** The most reliable indicators shift seasonally (e.g., snow melt in Spring, frost patterns in Autumn, sastrugi in Winter).
