# Spearfishing

## Objective
Spearfishing is defined as active fish hunting using improvised spears, which is effective when traps are not yet built or when targeting large individual fish.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Clear water (for visibility and refraction understanding).
*   Improvised spears (hardwood, bone, or metal).
*   Sharp tools (knives or sharp rocks) for construction and carving.
*   Cordage or sinew for binding.
*   A torch for night spearfishing.

## Material
*   Hardwood shaft (2-2.5 meters long, 3-4 cm diameter).
*   Bone, antler, or scavenged metal (for tips).
*   Pine resin or birch bark (for sealing bindings).
*   Cordage or sinew.
*   Spear tips (single point or multi-prong).
*   A torch (bundle of resinous pine sticks or birch bark).

## System
Spearfishing serves to bridge the gap between passive methods (like trapping or weirs) by providing immediate food, though it is labor-intensive. It is most effective on the first day of a survival scenario.

## Technology
### Understanding Refraction
The core technological skill is understanding that light bends when passing from water to air, making fish appear higher and closer to the surface than they actually are. To strike accurately, the spear must be aimed lower than the fish appears to be.

**Aim Adjustment Rules:**
*   Looking almost straight down (steep angle): Aim slightly below the apparent position.
*   Looking at 45 degrees: Aim roughly 1/3 of the apparent depth below the fish.
*   Looking at a shallow angle: Aim at least 1/2 the apparent depth below the fish.

## Tools
*   Improvised Spears (Single-Point, Multi-Prong Gig, Bone or Metal-Tipped).
*   Sharp rock or knife (for carving and splitting shafts).
*   Torch (for night spearfishing).

## Process
### Spear Construction
*   **Single-Point Spear:** Select hardwood shaft, carve sharp point, harden tip over hot coals (not flames) for 2-3 minutes, add a barb (notch 2 cm back from tip).
*   **Multi-Prong Gig:** Select shaft, split working end into 3-4 prongs (15-20 cm deep), wedge sticks/pebbles between prongs, bind tightly, sharpen tips, carve inward-facing barbs (2 cm from tip).
*   **Bone or Metal-Tipped Spear:** Shape tip, split shaft (5-8 cm), insert tip, bind tightly with wet cordage/sinew, seal binding with pine resin if available.

### Technique
*   **Wading:** Enter slowly, stand still (2-3 minutes), hold spear at 45-60 degrees, keep the tip submerged, thrust downward fast/smooth, pin the fish against the bottom, slide the free hand down the shaft.
*   **Bank Spearing:** Position over water, stay back from the edge, wait for fish movement, thrust straight down.
*   **Night Spearing:** Build a torch, hold it over the water to stun fish, spear the motionless, illuminated fish.

### Processing Your Catch
Act fast. The speared fish must be killed immediately with a sharp blow to the head, then gutted within 30 minutes, rinsed, and cooked or preserved immediately due to increased bacterial introduction from the spear wound.

## Best Targets
| Target | Difficulty | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Carp | Easy | Large, slow, often in shallow water |
| Catfish | Easy | Bottom dwellers, slow to react, active at night |
| Suckers | Easy | Aggregate in shallows during spawning |
| Salmon (spawning) | Moderate | Large targets in shallow runs, but fast |
| Trout | Hard | Fast, spooky, prefer deep water |
| Pike/Pickerel | Moderate | Often motionless near weed edges, but explosive when spooked |

Focus on Carp and Catfish as the most calorie-efficient species. |

## Notes
*   A multi-prong gig with barbs is significantly more effective than a single-point spear.
*   Spearfishing requires full attention but yields immediate results.
*   Speared fish spoil faster than other catches, necessitating rapid processing.
*   Fire Safety near water: Torches must be held over the water, not dry grass, and have a means to extinguish them.
*   The key to success is correcting the aim based on refraction.
