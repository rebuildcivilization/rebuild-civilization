# Basket Traps

## Objective
The objective of using basket traps is to passively catch fish, providing a reliable daily protein supply through the construction and strategic deployment of woven funnel containers.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Rigid sticks (8-16) for the structural frame.
*   Flexible strands for weaving material.
*   Cordage (if available, thin twine works).
*   Waterways or suitable locations (lakes, rivers, estuaries, tidal pools).

## Material
### Frame Ribs
*   **Quantity**: 8-16 rigid sticks.
*   **Length**: 60-100 cm (24-40 inches) depending on target fish size.
*   **Diameter**: 8-15 mm (about finger thickness).
*   **Best species**: Willow, hazel, bamboo, dogwood, or any straight-grained green wood.
*   **Preparation**: Cut fresh (green wood bends without snapping), strip bark and side branches.

### Weaving Material
*   **Best options**: Split willow or hazel (split thick branches into 3-4 mm strips).
*   **Vines**: Grape vine, honeysuckle, clematis (any flexible climbing plant).
*   **Bark strips**: Inner bark of basswood, elm, or tulip poplar, soaked until pliable.
*   **Cordage**: Thin twine if available.
*   **Roots**: Spruce, pine, or cedar roots (dig, peel, and split).
*   **Preparation**: Soak all weaving material in water for at least 2 hours before use. Green, freshly cut material works immediately.

## System
### Basket Trap Mechanism
Basket traps are woven funnel containers that catch fish passively. The core design is a cylinder of interlocked ribs and weaving material featuring a narrow-throat funnel entrance. Fish can enter through the funnel but cannot navigate back out.

### Construction: Cylinder Trap
1.  **Build the Frame**: Lay out 10-12 ribs in a parallel bundle, tie them at the center with cordage, and fan the ribs outward evenly (approx. 30 degrees apart for 12 ribs).
2.  **Weave the Body**: Start 5 cm from the center tie, weave flexible material over and under alternating ribs in a spiral, maintaining consistent row spacing (gaps of 1-2 cm to allow water flow).
3.  **Close the Back End**: Gather the rib ends, bend them inward, and tie them together with cordage to form a closed dome or cone.
4.  **Build the Funnel Entrance**: Use 8 flexible sticks (30-40 cm long), tie their thick ends around the open end, bend them inward to form a cone, ensuring the tips nearly meet 15-20 cm inside, and tie the tips loosely (opening 3-5 cm across).
5.  **Add an Access Door (Optional)**: Options include leaving a section untied, cutting a 15 cm slit with a flap, or making the funnel entrance removable with quick-release knots.

### Alternative Designs
*   **Bottle Trap**: Shaped like a bottle with a narrow neck. Requires weaving a wide cylinder (30-35 cm diameter), tapering the entrance end over 20 cm, and inserting a funnel.
*   **Flat Trap**: For shallow water (under 30 cm deep). Requires a flat, rectangular frame (60 x 40 cm, 15 cm tall), funnel entrances on both short ends, and weighting with rocks.
*   **Multi-Chamber Trap**: A standard cylinder trap with a second internal funnel halfway through the body to maximize retention.

### Deployment and Anchoring
| Water Type | Position | Orientation | Anchor Method |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| River | Against the bank, in eddies | Funnel facing upstream | Tie to bankside tree or stake |
| Lake | Near structure (fallen trees, weed beds) | Funnel facing shore | Weight with rocks inside, tie to shore |
| Estuary | In tidal channels | Funnel facing incoming tide | Stake into mud, tie to mangroves |
| Stream | Below riffles, in pools | Funnel facing upstream | Wedge between rocks |

**Anchoring Rule**: Anchor Every Trap. Tie traps to a fixed point with strong cordage (e.g., a 2-meter cord from the trap to a tree/stake in rivers).

### Baiting
Bait should be placed inside the trap, past the funnel. Effective baits include:
*   Crushed mussels or snails (excellent scent, stays put).
*   Fish scraps wrapped in a grass bundle (slow-release scent).
*   Bread dough pinched around a stick inside the trap.
*   Cheese or animal fat (strong scent in warm water).

### Maintenance and Lifespan
*   **Checking**: Twice daily (dawn and dusk produce best catches).
*   **Rebaiting**: After every check, even if no fish were caught.
*   **Drying**: Dry traps for a full day every 3-4 days to kill algae and slow rot.
*   **Lifespan**: Green wood traps last 2-4 weeks; bark-stripped and dried wood traps can last 2-3 months with care.
*   **Replacement**: Replace weaving material as it loosens or breaks; the frame usually outlasts the weave.

### Scaling Up
*   **Production Rate**: A skilled builder can produce 2-3 traps per day.
*   **Spacing**: Space traps 20-30 meters apart along a riverbank.
*   **Strategy**: Rotate locations every few days; mark trap locations with a distinctive stick or flag.
*   **Inventory**: Keep a repair kit (spare weaving material and cordage).

## Technology
Passive fish capture using woven, funnel-entrance containers made from natural, flexible materials found near waterways.

## Tools
*   Cutting tools (for frame ribs).
*   Weaving implements.
*   Cordage (for tying and anchoring).

## Process
### Construction Process (Cylinder Trap)
1.  Build the Frame (Lay out ribs, tie center, fan out).
2.  Weave the Body (Start weaving, maintain gap consistency).
3.  Close the Back End (Gather ends, bend inward, tie closure).
4.  Build the Funnel Entrance (Tie flexible sticks to the open end, bend inward to form a cone).
5.  Add an Access Door (Implement Option A, B, or C).

### Deployment Process
1.  Select Placement based on Water Type.
2.  Orient the Funnel according to the water flow (upstream for rivers/streams, toward the shore for lakes).
3.  Apply the required Anchoring Method.
4.  Bait the trap with appropriate material.

### Maintenance Process
1.  Check traps twice daily.
2.  Rebait traps after each check.
3.  Dry traps every 3-4 days.
4.  Monitor material integrity and replace loose weaving.

Notes
- The key takeaway is that a cylinder trap with a woven funnel entrance is the most reliable and portable design.
- The critical factor for success in construction is the gap size: weaving gaps must be smaller than the target fish (under 2 cm for panfish and trout).
- Green, flexible wood is preferred for both ribs and weaving, while prepared (dried) wood offers a longer lifespan.
- The necessity of anchoring every trap is emphasized to prevent loss via currents or wildlife.
