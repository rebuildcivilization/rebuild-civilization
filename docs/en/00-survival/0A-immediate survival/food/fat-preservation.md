# Fat Preservation

## Objective
To preserve fat by rendering it first—melting it slowly over low heat to separate pure oil from the water and protein that cause spoilage. The ultimate goal is to achieve long-term storage for fat, which is one of the most calorie-dense substances available.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Low heat for rendering (critical to prevent scorching).
*   Airtight, opaque containers for storage.
*   Coolest and darkest location available for storage.
*   Salt (for salt-packed fat).
*   Water (for water-sealed storage and double rendering).
*   For Confit: Salted meat and rendered fat, and temperature control.

## Material
*   Raw fat
*   Beef suet (kidney fat)
*   Beef/mutton tallow
*   Pork lard (back fat)
*   Pork leaf lard
*   Bear fat
*   Goose/duck fat
*   Fish oil
*   Nut oils (walnut, acorn)
*   Salt
*   Lye (wood ash leachate)
*   Beeswax or pine pitch (for sealing)

## System
### Fat Degradation
Fat spoils through two main processes:
1.  **Oxidation:** Oxygen reacts with fat molecules, breaking them into foul-smelling compounds (aldehydes and ketones), leading to 'rancidity.' This is accelerated by heat, light, and air exposure. Unsaturated fats oxidize faster than saturated fats.
2.  **Microbial decomposition:** Bacteria and mold feed on proteins and water trapped in raw fat, producing putrid smells and dangerous toxins.

### Fat Shelf Life Comparison
| Fat Source | Saturation Level | Rendering Difficulty | Shelf Life (Rendered) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Beef suet (kidney fat) | Very high | Easy | 1-2 years |
| Beef/mutton tallow | High | Easy | 6-12 months |
| Pork lard (back fat) | Moderate-high | Easy | 6-12 months |
| Pork leaf lard | High | Very easy | 8-12 months |
| Bear fat | Moderate | Moderate | 4-8 months |
| Goose/duck fat | Moderate | Easy | 4-6 months |
| Fish oil | Very low (unsaturated) | Difficult | 1-3 months |
| Nut oils (walnut, acorn) | Low-moderate | Labor-intensive | 1-3 months |

### Confit Principle
The principle of confit is that cooked meat is completely submerged in fat, sealing it from oxygen and bacteria.

## Technology
### Rendering Methods
**Dry Rendering (Preferred for Large Batches):**
1. Cut fat into small pieces (1/2-inch cubes or smaller). Remove meat, blood, or organ tissue.
2. Place in a heavy pot over low heat. Add a splash of water (approx. 1/4 cup per pound of fat) to prevent scorching.
3. Heat slowly; maintain a gentle simmer, avoiding a boil.
4. Watch for stages: Liquid fat accumulates (1-2 hours); cracklings turn golden and sink (2-3 hours, signaling completion).
5. Strain immediately through cloth and squeeze cracklings to extract remaining fat.
6. Let the strained fat cool undisturbed.

**Wet Rendering (For Small Batches or Salvage Fat):**
1. Place chopped fat in a pot and cover completely with water.
2. Bring to a gentle boil, then reduce to a simmer for 2-3 hours, stirring occasionally.
3. Remove from heat and let cool completely.
4. Lift off the solid fat disc and scrape any protein residue from the bottom.
5. Re-melt and strain through cloth for purity.

**Double Rendering (Maximum Shelf Life):**
1. Render once using either dry or wet method.
2. Re-melt the rendered fat with an equal volume of water.
3. Simmer for 30 minutes.
4. Cool and separate the fat disc again to remove remaining protein and water.

### Storage Methods
1.  **Sealed Vessel Storage:** Pour hot fat into clean, dry containers, filling completely to the brim to minimize air contact. Cover tightly once solidified.
2.  **Water-Sealed Storage:** Pour rendered fat into a container, leave 2-3 inches of space, pour a layer of clean water on top, and replenish the water seal as it evaporates.
3.  **Salt-Packed Fat:** Render fat, stir in salt (1 tablespoon per pound of fat) while warm, pour into containers, and sprinkle additional salt on top once solidified.
4.  **Confit (Fat-Sealed Meat Storage):** Meat is cooked and submerged in fat. The fat seal must remain completely intact. Temperature dictates shelf life:
    *   Below 40°F (4°C): 6-12 months
    *   40-55°F (4-13°C): 2-4 months
    *   55-70°F (13-21°C): 2-4 weeks
    *   Above 70°F (21°C): 1-2 weeks
5.  **Smoking Fat:** Render, pour into wide, shallow containers (max 2 inches deep), cold-smoke for 6-12 hours, then re-melt gently to distribute smoke compounds, and pour into storage containers.

## Process
**Key Rendering Steps:**
1.  **Pre-treatment:** Cut fat into small pieces (Dry Rendering) or chop fat (Wet Rendering).
2.  **Heating:** Heat slowly over low heat to separate pure oil from water and protein.
3.  **Completion Check:** Dry rendering is complete when cracklings turn golden and sink.
4.  **Purification:** Strain thoroughly through multiple layers of cloth.
5.  **Double Rendering (Optional):** Re-melt with water and simmer to remove remaining impurities.

**Key Storage Steps:**
1.  **Preparation:** Ensure fat is pure (thorough straining).
2.  **Sealing:** Utilize sealed vessels or create a water barrier layer.
3.  **Enhancement (Salted Fat):** Stir in salt while warm, then sprinkle extra on top.
4.  **Confit Application:** Ensure the meat is fully submerged in the fat and the seal is maintained.
5.  **Post-Storage:** Store in the coolest, darkest location available.

**Usage Application:**
*   **Cooking:** Re-melt stored fat over moderate heat.
*   **Pemmican:** Use freshly re-melted tallow, warm but not smoking hot.
*   **Waterproofing:** Rub warm tallow into leather, cloth, or wood, optionally mixed with beeswax.

## Notes
*   **Rancidity Distinction:** Mildly rancid fat is not immediately dangerous in small amounts for survival uses (candles, soap), but putrid smells indicate bacterial contamination, not just oxidation.
*   **Critical Safety Check:** For confit, the fat seal must be completely intact. Check weekly for cracks, shrinkage, or mold. If the meat smells sour or putrid beneath a broken seal, discard it.
*   **Mixing Rule:** When mixing different fat types, the mixture spoils at the rate of the least stable component.
*   **Rendering Rule:** Low and slow heat is the rule to prevent scorching.
*   **Fat Density:** A pound of rendered fat contains over 4,000 calories.
