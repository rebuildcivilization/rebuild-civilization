# Catch Processing

## Objective
To process a freshly caught fish quickly and correctly to ensure it remains safe to eat by addressing the primary spoilage source, the digestive tract.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Warm weather rule: Gut fish within 30 minutes of catching in warm weather (above 20°C / 68°F).
- Cold weather rule: Gut fish within 2–3 hours.
- Availability of cooling methods (cold water, shade, wet cloth, or underground cache, or snow/ice).

## Material
Freshly caught fish.

## System
- Field processing station (flat rock or log as a cutting surface).
- Water source nearby.
- Waste pile downwind.

## Technology
- The clock: Time constraint dictates processing speed.
- Spoilage indicators (eyes, gills, flesh firmness, smell, skin condition).

## Tools
- Rock or stick or knife handle (for killing the fish).
- Knife or shell (for scaling).
- Thumbnail, spoon, or knife tip (for scraping the kidney and removing the gallbladder).
- Mesh bag or basket (for cold water).
- Damp cloth (for wrapping/evaporative cooling).

## Process
### Step 1: Kill Quickly
- Action: Strike the top of the head sharply between and slightly behind the eyes with a rock, stick, or knife handle. One decisive blow.
- Large Fish Action: Follow the stun with a cut through the gill arches on both sides to bleed the fish.

### Step 2: Scale (If Applicable)
- Method: Lay the fish on a flat surface, hold firmly by the tail. Use the back (dull side) of a knife, shell, or rough-edged rock to scrape from tail to head, against the direction the scales lie. Rinse or wipe to remove loose scales.

### Step 3: Gut the Fish
- Procedure:
    1. Lay the fish on its side. Insert the knife tip into the belly at the vent (near the tail, just forward of the anal fin).
    2. Cut forward along the belly toward the head, keeping the cut shallow (slicing through the belly wall only).
    3. Spread the belly open and pull out the entire gut cavity contents in one mass.
    4. Locate and scrape out the dark bloodline running along the spine inside the cavity (the kidney) using a thumbnail, spoon, or knife tip.
    5. Remove the gills by cutting or tearing them out from both sides.
    6. Rinse the cavity thoroughly with clean water, or wipe it with grass or clean cloth if water is scarce.
- Critical Check: Avoid puncturing the gallbladder, as bile staining must be immediately cut away if it ruptures.

### Step 4: Keep It Cool
- Methods:
    - Cold water: Submerge the fish in a stream or spring in a mesh bag or basket (moving cold water is best).
    - Shade and breeze: Hang the fish where air circulates for evaporative cooling.
    - Wet cloth wrap: Wrap the fish in damp cloth.
    - Underground cache: Dig a shallow hole in damp earth, line with leaves, and cover loosely.
    - Snow or ice: Pack the fish in snow or place it on ice (buys 24–48 hours).
- Never:
    - Leave gutted fish in direct sunlight.
    - Pile multiple fish touching each other in a warm container.
    - Store fish in a sealed container without cooling.

### Step 5: Inspect Before Eating
| Indicator | Fresh (Safe) | Spoiled (Discard) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Eyes | Clear, bright, slightly bulging | Cloudy, sunken, gray |
| Gills | Bright red or pink | Brown, gray, slimy |
| Flesh | Firm — springs back when pressed | Soft, mushy, leaves fingerprint |
| Smell | Mild, ocean-like or clean | Strong ammonia, sour, putrid |
| Skin | Shiny, tight, scales intact | Dull, loose, slimy |
| Belly cavity | Clean, no discoloration | Yellow-green stains, foul odor |

### Processing Multiple Fish
- Assembly line method:
    1. Kill all fish immediately upon landing.
    2. Set up a processing station (cutting surface, water source, waste pile downwind).
    3. Scale all fish first (batch processing).
    4. Gut all fish.
    5. Rinse all fish.
    6. Sort: fish to eat today go to the cooking pile; others go immediately into preservation.
- For catches exceeding 10 fish:
    - Recruit help (one person kills/scales, another guts/rinses).
    - Have smoking or drying setup ready before starting.
    - Limit warm-weather processing to no more than what can be preserved within 2–3 hours.

## Notes
- Fish spoilage reasons:
    - High moisture content (60–80% water).
    - Low acidity (near-neutral pH).
    - Digestive enzymes (will begin digesting the fish within hours if not removed).
    - Surface bacteria (skin and gills carry cold-adapted bacteria).
- Key takeaways:
    - Gut within 30 minutes in warm weather because the digestive tract is the primary spoilage source.
    - Kill quickly and bleed large fish through the gills.
    - Use every part: Head/bones for broth, guts for bait, roe for food, bladder for glue.
- Hygiene requirements:
    - Process fish downwind and at least 50 meters (160 feet) from camp.
    - Bury offal or dump it in moving water well downstream.
    - Wash hands and tools after processing.
    - Clean cutting surfaces with sand and water scrubbing.
- What to Save Table:
    - Fillets and flesh: Primary food (cook, smoke, dry, salt).
    - Head: Soup/broth stock.
    - Bones and spine: Soup stock, bone meal, fish glue.
    - Skin: Eat with the fish or dry for leather.
    - Guts and organs: Bait or fertilizer.
    - Liver: Edible (high in vitamins A and D), but discard if discolored or foul-smelling.
    - Roe (eggs): Edible (fry, smoke, salt).
    - Swim bladder: Fish glue (boil until adhesive).
    - Scales: Gelatin source (boil in water).
