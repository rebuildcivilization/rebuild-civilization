# Fish Weirs

## Objective
To redirect fish movement by reshaping the waterway itself rather than trying to catch individual fish. The goal is to create a semi-permanent barrier that forces fish into a holding area or toward a trap for bulk capture.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- The streambed must be firm enough to support the structure; pure sand shifts and undermines walls.
- Water safety: Work in pairs at minimum, and never build in water above waist height.

## Material
- **Rocks (preferred for durability):** Flat rocks 15-30 cm across to stack and resist current.
- **Sticks and stakes:** Cut stakes 5-8 cm (2-3 inches) in diameter, sharpened one end.
- **Flexible branches:** Willow, hazel, or any green wood, to weave horizontally between stakes.
- **Filler materials:** Smaller stones, gravel, mud, and clay to seal gaps.

## System
Fish Weirs are semi-permanent barriers built across a stream or tidal channel that redirects fish movement. They are among the oldest fishing technologies and offer an effective bulk-capture method using zero manufactured materials.

## Technology
The core technology involves constructing a V-shaped or straight barrier with defined gaps to funnel fish into a holding area or trap, leveraging the natural flow and the structure to manage the fish population.

## Tools
- Tall sticks (to mark the wall path).
- Sharpened stakes.
- Basket traps (combined with the weir for increased capture rate).
- Flat rock or log (to cover the gap).

## Process
### 1. Survey the Site
- Wade the stream and map the bottom to note bedrock, gravel, sand, and mud.

### 2. Mark Your Layout
- Use tall sticks to mark the wall path. For a V-weir, stand at the center and place markers angling back to each bank at 30-45 degrees.

### 3. Build from the Banks Inward
- Start by driving the first stakes firmly into the bank to anchor the wall.
- Work outward stake by stake, driving each 20-30 cm into the streambed.
- Immediately weave branches between stakes to create rigidity.
- Stack rocks on the upstream side as the current presses them against the stakes.

### 4. Build the Gap
- For a V-weir, the last 50 cm of each wall arm should be reinforced with extra stakes and heavy rocks.
- The gap should narrow slightly from top to bottom (funnel shape).
- Place a flat rock or log across the top of the gap to prevent fish from jumping over.
- Position a basket trap or build a small stone pen downstream at the gap.

### 5. Seal the Wall
- Walk the entire wall from downstream to find gaps where water flows through with force.
- Plug these gaps with small rocks wedged between large ones, handfuls of gravel pressed into cracks, or mud packed over the top.

## Dimensions Guide
| Stream Width | Weir Type | Wall Height Above Water | Gap Width | Build Time (2 people) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 2-3 m | V-weir | 15 cm | 25-35 cm | 2-3 hours |
| 4-6 m | V-weir | 20 cm | 35-50 cm | 4-6 hours |
| 7-10 m | Heart or straight | 20-25 cm | 40-60 cm | 6-10 hours |
| Tidal flat | Semicircle | 40-60 cm (above low tide) | None (harvest at low tide) | 4-8 hours |

## Maintenance
- **After every rain:** Check for washouts and repair immediately, as a single gap ruins the system.
- **Weekly:** Remove accumulated debris (leaves, branches) that can dam water or overtop the wall.
- **Monthly:** Reinforce the base of the wall, as current erodes the streambed underneath.
- **Seasonally:** Major rebuilds may be needed after spring floods.

## Notes
- The most effective design for streams under 6 meters wide is the V-weir.
- Combining the weir with a funnel trap at the gap dramatically increases the capture rate by preventing fish from swimming back out.
- Common mistakes include building too tall (10 cm above waterline is sufficient), ignoring the streambed foundation, making the gap too wide, and forgetting a rain backup wall at the gap.
- Fish find gaps, so sealing every opening is critical.
