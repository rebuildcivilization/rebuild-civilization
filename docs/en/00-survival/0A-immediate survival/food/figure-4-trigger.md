# Figure-4 Trigger

## Objective
To create a three-stick deadfall trigger requiring no cordage or manufactured parts: an upright (vertical), a diagonal (load-bearing), and a bait stick (horizontal trigger) interlock through carved notches to form a shape resembling the numeral “4,” holding a deadfall weight.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Dead, dry hardwood for sticks.
*   A cutting tool for carving.
*   A weight (book or brick) for practice and testing.
*   A long twig (50+ cm) for sensitivity testing.

## Material
*   **Sticks:** Cut from dead, dry hardwood. Excellent options include Oak, maple, ash, hickory, and beech.
*   **Avoid:** Pine, willow, and poplar (too soft for reliable notch engagement).

## System
The Figure-4 Trigger is a classic trigger mechanism that uses three sticks interlocked through carved notches to achieve a self-locking geometry. The three components are:

| Component | Also Called | Function |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Upright | Vertical stick, post | Stands vertically, holds the diagonal |
| Diagonal | Support stick, lever | Angles from upright to weight, bears the load |
| Bait stick | Horizontal, trigger | Extends horizontally under the weight, holds bait |

## Technology
The system relies on three sticks interlocked through carved notches to form the numeral “4.” The trigger notch (diagonal-to-bait stick engagement) is critical, as its depth controls sensitivity. The design is a self-locking geometry that holds significant weight but collapses instantly when the bait stick is disturbed.

## Tools
*   Cutting tool for carving notches.
*   Weight (book or brick) for loading and drop-testing.
*   Long twig for sensitivity testing.

## Process
### Carving the Notches
*   **Upright Stick:**
    1.  Cut a flat base at the bottom to stand vertically.
    2.  Carve a square notch near the top (2-3 cm from the end), facing the direction the diagonal stick will come from.
    3.  Carve the very top to a chisel point or flat shelf for the diagonal stick.
*   **Diagonal Stick:**
    1.  Carve a matching square notch at the upper end to interlock with the upright's notch (right angle).
    2.  Carve the tip to a blunt chisel point to press against the underside of the weight.
    3.  Carve a small notch or groove midway on the underside where the bait stick will engage.
*   **Bait Stick:**
    1.  Carve a flat chisel edge that engages with the notch on the underside of the diagonal.
    2.  Carve a small notch or point at the opposite end to hold the bait.
    3.  Carve a square notch near the bait end that matches the upright stick’s top, completing the “4” shape.

### Assembly
1.  **Dry Assembly (No Weight):** Stand the upright vertically. Place the bait stick horizontally resting its notch on the upright. Set the diagonal at roughly 45 degrees, interlocking its upper notch with the upright’s notch, and engaging its mid-point notch with the bait stick’s chisel end.
2.  **Load the Weight:** Tilt the weight up beside the assembly. Slide the tip of the diagonal stick under the weight and slowly lower it until the assembly bears the full load.
3.  **Sensitivity Test:** Use a long twig to gently push the bait. Adjust notch depth based on test results.

### Scaling the Trigger
| Target Weight Range | Stick Diameter | Stick Lengths | Notch Depth |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Under 1 kg | 5-8 mm | 8-15 cm | 2-3 mm |
| 1-5 kg | 8-12 mm | 12-20 cm | 3-5 mm |
| 5-15 kg | 12-18 mm | 18-25 cm | 5-8 mm |
| 15-30 kg | 18-25 mm | 22-30 cm | 6-10 mm |
| 30-50+ kg | 25-40 mm | 28-40 cm | 8-12 mm |

### Practice Protocol
1.  Build 5 triggers in succession and time the process.
2.  Test each trigger with a weight at least 3 times.
3.  Build triggers using different wood species to observe carving difficulty and grain direction.

## Notes
*   **Sensitivity Control:** The trigger notch depth must be the shallowest to allow light lateral force (approx. 50–100 grams of lateral pressure) to release the weight.
*   **Grain Direction:** Carve with the grain on all load-bearing notches; cross-grain cuts on the diagonal will crack under weight.
*   **Common Mistakes:**
    *   Using green or soft wood, leading to stiffness.
    *   Making all notches the same depth; the trigger notch must be the shallowest.
    *   Not testing sensitivity before deployment.
    *   Rushing the carving, as precise cuts are required for reliable triggering.
*   **Troubleshooting Highlights:**
    *   If the weight slides off: Carve a broader, flatter tip.
    *   If the diagonal rotates sideways: Deepen the interlocking notch on one side.
    *   If the trigger fires from wind: Deepen engagement notches by 1 mm increments.

*   **Key Takeaways:** The figure-4 uses three sticks with interlocking notches; use dead, dry hardwood; the trigger notch determines sensitivity; and always test the assembled trigger three times before leaving.
