# Fly Tying | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To tie functional fishing flies without a fly shop.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Everything needed exists in the post-collapse world, requiring only scavengeable materials.

## Material
| Material | Source | Use |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Feathers | Any bird (chicken, pigeon, crow, pheasant, duck) | Wings, hackle (collar), tails |
| Fur/hair | Rabbit, squirrel, deer, dog, cat | Dubbing (body material) |
| Thread | Unraveled clothing, sinew, thin plant fiber | Wrapping and binding |
| Hooks | Thorns, small bone hooks, bent wire, safety pins | The foundation |
| Wax | Rendered beeswax, pine pitch, animal fat | Thread coating for grip |
| Tinsel | Foil scraps, thin wire, shiny thread | Flash and segmentation |

## System
The system relies on scavengeable materials and the ability to observe the environment to create reusable lures.

## Technology
The primary technology is the creation of artificial flies that imitate natural insects to outperform random bait.

## Tools
Without a modern fly rod and reel, the methods involve:
*   A long rod (3-4 meters / 10-13 feet) for Dapping.
*   Heavier line and a float (sealed bottle, cork, bark bundle) for Bubble and fly.
*   The ability to secure a hook (split stick, vise, or thumb/finger pinch).

## Process
### Preparing Materials
**Preparing Feathers:**
1. Collect feathers from any available bird.
2. Strip the fluffy base material from the quill, focusing on sections with relatively uniform fiber length.
3. Store feathers dry to prevent mold and fiber breakdown.

**Making Thread:**
4. Unravel a single thread from cotton or polyester fabric, or split sinew into the thinnest strands.
5. Coat the thread with a thin layer of wax (pine pitch or beeswax) by drawing the thread across a lump to make it tacky.

### Tying the Woolly Bugger (Most Versatile Pattern)
1. **Secure the Hook:** Secure the hook in something steady (split stick, vise, or pinch).
2. **Thread Base:** Start the thread about 2 mm behind the hook eye and wrap it backward in tight, touching turns to the bend of the hook.
3. **Tail:** Select 4-6 long, soft feather fibers (e.g., marabou) and tie them in at the hook bend so they extend about one hook-length beyond the bend. Wrap thread over the butt ends to secure.
4. **Body (Dubbing):** Take a pinch of fur or hair, spread it thinly along a 10 cm (4 inch) length of waxed thread, and twist the thread between fingers to create 'dubbing'. Wind this fuzzy thread forward along the hook shank in touching turns to about 3 mm behind the eye.
5. **Hackle:** Take a feather with fibers about the length of the hook gap. Tie in the feather by its tip at the front of the body. Wind the feather in 3-4 spiral wraps backward through the body, spacing wraps evenly. Tie off the feather stem with 2-3 thread wraps.
6. **Head:** Build a small neat head with 5-6 wraps of thread behind the eye. Finish with two half-hitch knots (loop thread over eye, pass end through loop, pull tight).
7. **Seal:** Apply a drop of pine pitch on the head to seal everything.

### Essential Patterns
*   **Dry Fly (Floater):** Imitates an insect on the water surface. Use stiff feather fibers wound densely around the hook to keep it floating. Add a small tuft of light-colored fur or feather as a wing for visibility. Grease with animal fat to repel water.
*   **Nymph (Sinker):** Imitates larval insects underwater. Wrap the hook shank with thread and dubbing for a plump body. Add a few fibers at the tail. Wrap thin wire (or heavy thread) in open spirals over the body for segmentation and weight. No hackle needed.
*   **Streamer (Minnow Imitation):** Imitates a small fish. Use a longer hook. Tie a long feather wing (two feathers back-to-back) extending past the hook bend. Add tinsel or foil strips along the body for flash.

### Matching Strategy
1. **Observe Substrate:** Turn over rocks to see what crawls underneath (nymphs), noting size, color, and shape.
2. **Observe Surface:** Watch the water at dawn and dusk to note small flies floating or taking off, noting size and color.
3. **Match Materials:** Match the body color with the closest fur or feather, and select an appropriately sized hook based on the observed insect.

### Storage
Flies must be stored dry by hooking them into a strip of thick bark, a folded piece of leather, or dense foam to maintain shape and attractiveness.

## Notes
- A woolly bugger pattern covers 80% of fishing situations and should be learned first.
- Flies are reusable for dozens of catches, making them more sustainable than live bait.
- Waxing the thread before tying is crucial as it grips materials and holds knots.
- Improvised hooks made from thorns or bone are brittle; a hard strike will snap the hook point, requiring a gentle set.
- Match the fly to whatever insects are currently on or in the water for the most effective results.
