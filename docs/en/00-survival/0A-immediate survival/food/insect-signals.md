# Insect Signals

## Objective
To use insect behavior to predict weather changes, utilizing various insects as natural weather indicators.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Atmospheric variables: Temperature, humidity, pressure, and static charge must exist to affect the insects.
*   Specific insect behaviors must be observed.
*   For accurate temperature reading using crickets, a 14-second observation window is required.

## Material
*   **Insects:** Crickets, ants, bees, mosquitoes, flies, spiders, butterflies, and fireflies.
*   **Physical Traits:** Insects are cold-blooded, small-bodied, and exoskeleton-bound. Their small mass makes them highly sensitive to air pressure changes, and their exoskeletons absorb or release moisture.

## System
**Insect Weather Station/Forecasting System**
The system relies on combining multiple insect signals with other observations (clouds, wind, sky color) for the most accurate predictions.

## Technology
*   **Chemical Reaction:** Cricket chirping is a chemical reaction where muscle contraction speed is tied to temperature.
*   **Physics of Flight:** Small mass makes insects disproportionately affected by air pressure changes.
*   **Hygroscopy:** Spider silk absorbs water, allowing spiders to detect humidity changes.
*   **Magnetite Response:** Bees have internal magnetite that responds to electromagnetic changes before storms.

## Tools
*   **Cricket Thermometer:** Used to determine temperature based on chirp rate.
*   **Ant Barometer:** Used to detect falling barometric pressure via leg/antenna organs and rising humidity.
*   **Observation Tools:** Required for monitoring chirp rates, ant mounds, bee flight patterns, aggression levels, and spider web behavior.

## Process
### Building Your Insect Weather Station
1.  **Monitor cricket chirp rate** each evening at the same time to track temperature trends.
2.  **Check ant mounds daily** to look for raised entrances or frantic activity.
3.  **Watch bee flight patterns** in the morning to determine if they are close to the hive or ranging widely.
4.  **Note mosquito/fly aggression levels** to detect sudden increases indicating falling pressure.
5.  **Check spider webs at dawn:** Newly built webs with dew indicate fair weather; shortened or abandoned webs indicate severe weather approaching.

### Specific Indicator Mechanisms
*   **Crickets as Thermometers:** Temperature (Fahrenheit) = 50 + (chirps in 14 seconds). For Celsius: count chirps in 25 seconds, divide by 3, then add 4.
*   **Ants as Barometers:** Ants build up mound entrances before rain (indicating waterproofing). Increased speed/activity signals preparation for storms. Carrying eggs uphill indicates expected flooding.
*   **Bees as Storm Predictors:** Clustering near the hive means bad weather is incoming. Early, massive returns signal a detected pressure drop.
*   **Mosquitoes and Flies:** Increased biting aggression occurs in low pressure. Midges/gnats forming dense clouds indicate high humidity and falling pressure, appearing 2-6 hours before rain.
*   **Spider Web Behavior:** Shortening anchor lines means detecting pressure/humidity changes. Abandoning webs indicates severe weather.

## Notes
Insects typically provide 6-24 hours of advance warning, which is sufficient time to prepare. The species of cricket matters for accuracy, with snowy tree crickets being the most accurate.
