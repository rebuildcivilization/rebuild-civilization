# Reading Water | Survival Wiki

## Objective
> The surface of moving water tells you everything happening beneath it — depth, speed, obstacles, and where fish hold. Learning to read these signals turns any stretch of river into a map of opportunity.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- The ability to observe water movement and surface features.

## Material
- Moving water (rivers, lakes).
- Fish (as the primary targets).
- Surface features: currents, seams, eddies, structure (rocks, logs, depth changes).

## System
- The river system, which consists of repeating features: Riffles, Runs, and Pools.

## Technology
- Surface observation to determine subsurface conditions.
- Surface signs reveal subsurface structure: pillows mean shallow rocks, V-wakes mean just-submerged rocks, and color/texture indicate depth.

## Tools
- Eyes (for observation).
- Ability to identify current seams, eddies, structure, and depth changes.

## Process
1. Stand back and observe for 2-3 minutes before entering the water. Look for rising fish, bird activity, foam lines, and current seams.
2. Identify the riffle-run-pool sequence if it is a river.
3. Map the seams — where fast meets slow.
4. Note depth changes by reading surface color and texture.
5. Check banks for undercuts, overhanging vegetation, and root tangles.
6. Plan your approach from downstream, moving into the current so fish face away from you.
7. Work the edges first — the highest-percentage fish are along seams, structure edges, and depth transitions, not in open water.

## Notes
- Current seams are the most reliable fish-holding feature in moving water.
- Fish are energy economists: holding position in fast current costs calories, but fast current carries the most food.
- The most productive zones for spearfishing in a pool are the pool head (where the riffle enters) and the pool tail (where the water begins to shallow).
- Foam lines mark exactly where drifting food concentrates, pointing directly to feeding fish.
- Wind effects on still water create Windward shores (piling up food/bait) and Lee shores (calmer, clearer water).
- Structure reading identifies specific fish-holding zones, including submerged rocks (Pillow, V-wake, Boil) and undercut banks.
