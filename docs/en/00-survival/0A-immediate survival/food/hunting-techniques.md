# Hunting Techniques

## Objective
Part of Hunting and Trapping, the objective is to catch food efficiently by choosing the right method of pursuit, balancing the energy gained against the energy spent.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Energy:** The decision hinges on whether the energy gained from the hunt will exceed the energy spent (e.g., 2,000 calories chasing an animal that yields 800).
*   **Physical Condition:** Required for persistence hunting (must be well-hydrated, well-fed, physically fit).
*   **Terrain and Weather:** Determines the suitability of the hunting method.
*   **Weapons:** Necessary for active hunting methods.
*   **Group Size:** Required for Drive hunting (minimum 3 people, ideally 5 or more).

## Material
*   Target species (deer-sized or larger for persistence hunting).
*   Cover (trees, rocks, tall grass, terrain folds).
*   Water sources (streams, ponds, puddles).
*   Food sources (fruit trees, berry patches, nut-bearing trees).

## System
Survival scenario where active hunting burns calories, necessitating a decision between active pursuit and trapping.

## Technology
*   The ability to utilize weapons (bows, spears, throwing sticks, knives).
*   The understanding of environmental factors (wind, terrain).

## Tools
*   Bow, spear, throwing stick, clubs.
*   Knife.
*   Traps (for food caught while sleeping).

## Process
### Choosing Your Method
Active hunting burns calories. Every decision must pass the test: will the energy gained exceed the energy spent?

| Method | Best For | Energy Cost | Skill Requirement | Weapon Needed | 
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | 
| Stalking | Open woodland, plains | Moderate | High | Bow, spear, throwing stick | 
| Ambush | Water holes, trails, feeding areas | Low | Moderate | Any | 
| Drive hunting | Group hunts, dense cover | High | Low (per person) | Spears, clubs | 
| Persistence hunting | Open terrain, hot weather | Very high | Moderate | Spear (for the finish) | 
| Opportunistic | Anywhere, anytime | Very low | Low | Whatever you have | 

### Stalking Process
1. Identify the animal’s position and determine the wind direction. Toss a pinch of dust, light ash, or a tuft of plant fiber into the air. Approach from downwind — always.
2. Plan your route before you move. Identify cover points (trees, rocks, rises in terrain) you can move between. Never walk directly at the animal.
3. Move when the animal is feeding (head down) or looking away. Freeze when it raises its head or looks in your direction.
4. Close to within effective weapon range (10-20 meters for bows/sticks; 2 meters for spear thrusts).
5. Take your shot when the animal is broadside and unaware. Aim for the chest cavity behind the front shoulder.

### Ambush Hunting Process
1. Scout the location first. Look for fresh tracks, droppings, and browse signs. Visit at dawn and dusk to confirm animals are currently using the area.
2. Build or find a concealment position (natural feature or constructed blind).
3. Position yourself downwind of the expected approach direction. Relocate if the wind shifts.
4. Arrive at your position well before the animals are expected (30 minutes before dawn for dawn hunters).
5. Remain completely still and silent. Sit on something comfortable and wait for a clean shot.

### Drive Hunting Process
*   **Group Requirement:** Minimum 3 people, ideally 5 or more.
1. Identify a patch of cover where animals are hiding.
2. Position 1-3 standers at natural exit points where fleeing animals are likely to emerge.
3. Beaters enter the cover from the opposite side, moving slowly and making steady noise to push animals toward the standers.
4. Standers take shots as animals emerge. Standers must never shoot toward beaters. Establish a clear safe zone.

### Persistence Hunting Process
1. Identify a target animal that is slightly compromised (limping, young, old, or isolated).
2. Begin following at a steady jog, tracking by prints, broken vegetation, and disturbed ground.
3. The animal will sprint, then stop to rest and cool down. Appear again before it has fully recovered.
4. Repeat over 2-6 hours, as midday heat accelerates the process.
5. Finish with a spear thrust to the neck or chest.

### Opportunistic Hunting Process (Mindset)
*   Always carry a throwing stick or spear when moving.
*   Move quietly as a default habit.
*   Scan the ground ahead constantly.
*   Know which animals are safe to eat and which are dangerous.
*   React immediately.

### Timing Your Hunts
| Time of Day | Activity Level | Best Method |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Pre-dawn (30 min before sunrise) | Animals moving to feeding areas | Ambush at water/trails |
| Dawn to mid-morning | Peak feeding activity | Stalking, ambush |
| Midday | Most animals resting in shade | Rest (save your energy) |
| Late afternoon | Feeding resumes | Stalking, ambush |
| Dusk | Peak movement to water and bedding | Ambush at water sources |
| Night | Many predators active | Stay in camp (unless trapping) |

## Safety Considerations
*   **Group Hunting:** Never hunt alone if pursuing dangerous game (boar, moose, buffalo, large predators).
*   **Identification:** Identify the target before shooting or throwing, especially in low light.
*   **Gear Security:** Carry your knife securely; a lost knife is a serious setback.
*   **Navigation:** Mark your location when tracking to avoid disorientation.
*   **Post-Kill Care:** Field dress meat immediately to prevent spoilage.

## Key Takeaways
*   Ambush hunting is the best solo method, offering the highest probability of a clean shot at close range.
*   Stalking is a high-skill method requiring wind awareness and patience.
*   Drive hunting requires a group to successfully flush game.
*   Persistence hunting is a last resort due to extreme calorie cost.
*   Opportunistic hunting (always being ready) is crucial, and traps still outperform active hunting for daily calories.


Notes
- Traps catch food while the hunter sleeps, serving as a baseline for daily caloric intake.
- Persistence hunting carries a high risk, potentially killing the hunter if they are not physically fit enough for the expenditure.
- The core principle distinguishing hunting methods is the energy balance: energy gained vs. energy spent.
