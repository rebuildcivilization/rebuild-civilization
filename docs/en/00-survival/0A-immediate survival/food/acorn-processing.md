# Acorn Processing | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To transform a bitter, stomach-wrecking nut into a nutritious flour staple through proper tannin leaching.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Knowledge of acorn species (distinguishing between White oaks and Red oaks).
*   Access to water (for leaching).
*   Access to fire or sunlight (for drying).
*   The ability to perform manual tasks (shelling, grinding).

## Material
*   Acorns (from White oaks and Red oaks).
*   Water (cold water, running water, boiling water).
*   Flat rocks or hard surfaces (for shelling).
*   Containers (watertight vessels like clay pots, hollowed logs, plastic containers, woven baskets).
*   A mesh mat or screen (for sifting).

## System
*   **Tannin Leaching:** The core chemical process where water washes away water-soluble tannins, transforming the nutmeat into a starchy, mildly sweet product.
*   **Heat Management:** Comparison between Cold Water Leaching (preserves starch structure) and Hot Water Leaching (faster but breaks down starch).
*   **Drying:** The critical phase to prevent mold, resulting in a hard, brittle product.
*   **Grinding:** The mechanical process to convert dry meat into flour.

## Technology
*   **White Oak Acorns:** Characterized by rounded leaf lobes; have lower tannins and require less leaching time.
*   **Red Oak Acorns:** Characterized by pointed, bristle-tipped lobes; have higher tannins and require longer leaching time.
*   **Leaching Methods:**
    *   Cold Water Leaching: Preserves natural starch structure.
    *   Hot Water Leaching: Produces a grainier flour; requires careful temperature management (transferring hot meat to boiling water).
*   **Grinding Tools:** Two flat stones (mano and metate), or any flat stone with a round stone tool.

## Tools
*   Flat stones (for grinding).
*   Containers for soaking.
*   Watertight vessels (clay pot, hollowed log, plastic container, woven basket lined with hide).
*   Mesh bag or loosely woven basket (for running water).
*   A rolling boil (for hot leaching).

## Process
### Choosing the Right Acorns
1.  **Identify the oak group:** Look at the leaf lobes.
2.  **Collect:** Brown, firm acorns are ready; green ones are not yet ripe.
3.  **Inspect:** Discard acorns with small round holes, cracks, dark stains, or soft spots.
4.  **Float-test:** Discard floaters (empty, rotten, or infested); keep the sinkers.

### Shelling
1.  Place acorn seam-side up on a flat surface.
2.  Tap with another rock to crack the shell along the seam (split, don't smash).
3.  Peel away the shell and the thin papery skin (testa).
4.  Break the nutmeat into small chunks (pea-sized or smaller) to increase surface area.
5.  Process into water promptly after shelling to prevent oxidation.

### Leaching Methods
**Cold Water Leaching:**
1.  Place crushed acorn meat in a container and cover with cold water.
2.  Soak for 12-24 hours.
3.  Pour off the brown, tannin-laden water and replace with fresh cold water.
4.  Repeat the cycle. (White oak: 3-5 changes over 2-3 days; Red oak: 5-10 changes over 4-7 days).
5.  Taste-test; keep leaching if bitterness/astringency is present. Stop when bland.

**Running Water Method:**
1.  Place crushed meat in a mesh bag/basket anchored in flowing water.
2.  Weight the bag with a stone.
3.  Leaches tannins much faster (White oak: 1-2 days; Red oak: 2-4 days).

**Hot Water Leaching:**
1.  Bring water to a rolling boil.
2.  Add crushed meat and boil for 15-20 minutes.
3.  Pour off ALL brown water (critical step).
4.  Transfer meat to fresh boiling water and boil another 15-20 minutes.
5.  Repeat until water stays clear and meat tastes bland. (White oak: 3-5 cycles; Red oak: 5-8 cycles).

### Drying and Making Flour
1.  **Drying:** Spread meat in a thin single layer on a flat surface (sunlight or near a fire).
2.  Turn pieces periodically. Drying takes 1-2 days in sun, or 4-8 hours near a fire.
3.  **Completion:** Meat is dry when it is hard, brittle, and snaps (like a cracker).
4.  **Grinding:** Grind into flour using two flat stones, working in small batches.
5.  **Sifting:** Sift through a woven grass mat or screen and re-grind large pieces.

### Using Acorn Flour
*   **Flatbread:** Mix flour with water into a thick paste, cook on a hot stone.
*   **Porridge:** Stir coarse acorn flour into boiling water, cook for 10-15 minutes.
*   **Pancakes:** Use thinner batter.
*   **Thickener:** Stir fine flour into soups and stews.
*   **Blended Flour:** Mix acorn flour (50/50) with other flours (cattail root, grass seed) for improved texture.

## Notes
*   **Tannin Benefit:** Tannins are water-soluble and must be leached out to make acorns edible.
*   **Oxidation:** Nutmeat oxidizes quickly once exposed to air, which affects flavor but not safety if properly leached.
*   **Hot Leaching Warning:** The critical rule is to always transfer hot acorn meat into fresh boiling water; failure to do so results in permanent bitterness.
*   **Troubleshooting Summary:** Bitterness implies pieces are too large or the wrong oak variety; lack of binding in flatbread implies hot leaching was used; mold implies insufficient drying/ventilation.
*   **Weevil Larvae:** Larvae in shelled acorns are edible and protein-rich, though heavily infested nuts should be discarded.
*   **Storage Risk:** Acorn flour, like other products, can develop a sour or rancid smell (due to rancid fat) and should be discarded if off-smells develop. Acorn flour is gluten-free and will not rise.

| Medium | Flatbread, pancakes |
