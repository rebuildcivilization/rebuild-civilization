# Primitive Weapons

## Objective
Primitive weapons bridge the gap between bare hands and manufactured tools. Their objective is to facilitate hunting and trapping while providing means for active hunting, defense, and dispatching trapped animals.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Skill and Practice:** Every weapon requires practice to achieve usable accuracy and effectiveness.
*   **Materials:** Hardwoods (oak, hickory, maple), stone, bone, metal, leather, or woven cordage.
*   **Time:** Investment of time determines the effectiveness and range of the weapon built.

## Material
*   Hardwood (for sticks, spear shafts, atlatl handles).
*   Stone (for spear tips, club wedges, sling ammunition).
*   Bone (for tipped spear points).
*   Leather or woven cordage (for sling pouches and lashings).
*   Metal (for scavenged blades or nail tips).

## System
Primitive weapons are part of a survival system that includes hunting and trapping. Traps function while the user sleeps, but active hunting is necessary when traps are empty, game is spotted, or self-defense is required.

## Technology
The technology involves leveraging physics through mechanical advantage (like the atlatl) or kinetic energy transfer (like the sling and the club) to achieve lethality and range.

## Tools
*   **Construction Tools:** Knife, sharp rock, broken glass (for sharpening and splitting).
*   **Weapon Components:** Sticks (for throwing stick), hardwood branches, stone/bone/metal points, leather/cordage.

## Process
### Weapon Hierarchy
The priority for building weapons should follow the hierarchy, starting with the quickest builds and moving toward more capable weapons as time allows.

| Weapon | Build Time | Effective Range | Skill Required | Best Targets |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Throwing stick | 0 min (pick up) | 5-15 m | Low | Rabbits, ground birds |
| Club | 5 min | Melee only | Low | Trapped/stunned animals, defense |
| Simple spear | 30 min | 5-10 m (thrown), melee | Low-Medium | Fish, small-medium game |
| Tipped spear | 1-2 hours | 5-10 m (thrown), melee | Medium | Medium game, defense |
| Atlatl (spear thrower) | 2-3 hours | 20-40 m | Medium-High | Medium-large game |
| Sling | 1-2 hours | 20-50 m | High (lots of practice) | Small-medium game, defense |
| Bow and arrows | 1-3 days | 10-30 m | High | Small-large game |
| Bola | 1 hour | 10-20 m | Medium | Birds, small running game |

### Specific Weapon Processes

**The Throwing Stick:**
1.  Select a stick roughly arm-length, wrist-thick, and heavy, with angular or slightly curved shapes.
2.  Throw the sidearm at waist height, releasing when the stick points at the target.
3.  Practice at 10-15 meters on stationary targets.

**The Club:**
1.  Find a hardwood branch 50-70 cm long with a natural thickening or knot at one end.
2.  If necessary, split the end and wedge a heavy stone in, lashing tightly with cordage in a crisscross pattern.
3.  Smooth the handle by removing bark and sharp edges from the grip section.

**The Simple Spear:**
1.  Select a straight hardwood sapling or branch 1.5-2 meters long and about 3 cm thick.
2.  Sharpen one end to a long, tapering point, creating a gradual taper 10-15 cm long.
3.  Fire-harden the tip by holding it over hot coals, rotating slowly until the wood darkens to a deep brown.
4.  Let it cool completely and re-sharpen if necessary.

**The Fishing Spear (Gig):**
1.  Split the spear tip 15-20 cm deep into 3-4 prongs.
2.  Lash the shaft tightly just below the split.
3.  Sharpen each prong and fire-harden.
4.  Spread the prongs 5-8 cm apart, optionally carving backward-facing barbs.

**The Tipped Spear:**
1.  Acquire a point (knapped stone, scavenged metal, bone, or glass).
2.  Prepare the shaft by splitting the end 8-10 cm deep or carving a flat notch.
3.  Insert the point into the split/notch and lash it securely with cordage, potentially coating the lashing with pine resin or birch tar to waterproof it.
4.  For throwing spears, ensure the point of balance is about one-third of the way from the tip; shave wood from the back if the spear is butt-heavy.

**The Atlatl (Spear Thrower):**
1.  Cut a flat, straight piece of hardwood 45-60 cm long (3 cm wide, 1.5 cm thick).
2.  Carve or attach a small hook/peg at the spur end, perpendicular to the board.
3.  Make darts 1.5-2 meters long from lightweight shafts and fletch the back end.
4.  Carve a cup or dimple in the butt end to receive the atlatl spur.
5.  To throw, hold the handle with the dart along its length and release the spur overhand.

**The Sling:**
1.  Cut two cords 60-80 cm long and attach them to a small pouch (10x8 cm leather).
2.  Tie a finger loop on one cord (retention cord) and leave the other free (release cord).
3.  Use by holding both cords: loop the retention cord on the middle finger and pinch the release cord between thumb and forefinger.

**The Bola:**
1.  Find 2-3 heavy, round stones (golf ball to tennis ball size).
2.  Wrap each stone in leather or cloth and secure it.
3.  Attach cords 60-90 cm long to each stone and join them at a central knot.
4.  Swing the central knot overhead to build momentum, releasing toward the target.

### Maintenance and Storage
*   **Re-sharpen** spear points after every use.
*   **Re-lash** tipped spears regularly as cordage dries.
*   **Replace** atlatl darts and arrows that are cracked or warped.
*   **Keep weapons dry** to prevent cordage weakening, shaft swelling, and metal rusting.
*   **Store spears** vertically or hanging horizontally to prevent shaft warping.

## Notes
*   Primitive weapons are categorized by investment of time and skill, with the Atlatl being the best return on investment due to its simplicity and superior throwing force.
*   The Throwing Stick is the cheapest and most accessible option, serving as a supplement rather than a primary food source.
*   The Sling requires significant practice to achieve usable accuracy, demanding hundreds of throws.
*   A Club serves a dual role as both a tool and a defensive weapon.
*   The critical maintenance factor across all weapons is the need to keep them dry to maintain the integrity of the cordage and shafts.
