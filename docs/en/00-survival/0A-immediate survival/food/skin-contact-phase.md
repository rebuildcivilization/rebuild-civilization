# Skin Contact Phase | Survival Wiki

## Objective
The Skin Contact Phase is Phase 1 of the Universal Edibility Test. Its objective is to serve as the first physical defense against toxic plants, allowing for the detection of contact irritants and caustic compounds before the plant is consumed.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Clean water to wash the test area and hands.
*   Clean hands to prevent contamination.
*   Specific plant material to be tested (leaf, root, stem, fruit, or flower).
*   The ability to crush, break, or bruise the plant material to release internal compounds.

## Material
*   The plant being tested.
*   The test site on the skin (inner wrist or inner elbow preferred).
*   Clean water.
*   The test material after crushing/preparation.

## System
The process is part of the Universal Edibility Test, which systematically tests plants for edibility.

## Technology
The technology relies on observing immediate physical reactions (sensory and visual) resulting from contact with plant compounds on the skin.

## Tools
*   Timer or precise method to track the 15-minute contact duration.
*   Observation skills to monitor for specific sensations and visual changes.
*   A food safety log for recording results.

## Process
### Preparation
1. Wash the test area with clean water to remove dirt, sweat, or sunscreen.
2. Wash your hands.
3. Prepare the plant material by crushing, breaking, or bruising it to release juices and internal compounds.
4. Note the time.

### Application
1. Place the crushed plant material directly against the skin of the inner wrist or inner elbow.
2. Apply enough material to cover an area roughly the size of a coin (1-2 cm diameter).
3. Secure the material in place using one of these methods: wrap with a strip of clean cloth, use a broad leaf as a wrap tied with cordage, or hold it manually.
4. Leave in contact for exactly 15 minutes.
5. Stay still and pay attention to any sensation at the contact site during this period.

### What to Monitor During the 15-Minute Contact Period
| Sensation | What It Means | Action
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Nothing at all | No immediate irritant detected | Good — continue the test |
| Mild warmth | May be normal skin response to pressure | Monitor closely but continue |
| Tingling | Possible mild irritant | Note it — if it intensifies, remove immediately |
| Itching | Likely contact irritant | Remove the plant material, wash the area, and record a FAIL |
| Burning | Caustic or strongly irritant compound | Remove immediately, wash thoroughly with water, FAIL |
| Numbness | Plant may contain local anesthetic compounds (some alkaloids)
 | Remove immediately, wash, FAIL |

### Removal and Observation
1. After 15 minutes, remove the plant material.
2. Wash the test area gently with clean water — do not scrub.
3. Observe the site for immediate visual changes:
    *   Redness (erythema)
    *   Swelling (edema)
    *   Small bumps (papules)
    *   Blisters (vesicles)
    *   Any color change.
4. Wait 8 hours from the time of removal, checking the site every 1-2 hours.

### The 8-Hour Observation Window
| Time After Removal | What to Look For |
| :--- | :--- |
| Immediately | Redness, swelling, visible reaction |
| 30 minutes | Developing rash, spreading redness |
| 1 hour | Bumps, hives, increasing redness |
| 2 hours | Blisters forming, itching developing |
| 4 hours | Delayed reaction developing |
| 8 hours | Final check — any change from baseline |

### Interpreting Results
**PASS — Proceed to Lip Test**
*   Condition: The test site shows absolutely no change after 8 hours (No redness, swelling, rash, bumps, blisters, itching, burning, or numbness compared to surrounding skin).
*   Action: Proceed to Phase 2 (lip test) of the Universal Edibility Test.

**FAIL — Do Not Proceed**
*   Condition: Any visible or sensory reaction means the plant part fails the skin test.
*   Severity Levels:
    *   Mild: Slight redness that fades within 1 hour. (Record as FAIL. May retest in 48 hours if redness might be pressure-related).
    *   Moderate: Persistent redness, itching, small bumps. (Record as FAIL. Wash area, apply cool water compresses. Do not retest).
    *   Severe: Blisters, swelling, spreading rash, pain. (Record as FAIL. Wash thoroughly, keep area clean and dry. Monitor for infection. Never test this plant again).
    *   Emergency: Rapid swelling spreading beyond the test site, difficulty breathing, hives elsewhere on the body. (Possible systemic allergic reaction. Remove all plant material, wash, keep airway open, this is anaphylaxis territory).

**INCONCLUSIVE**
*   Condition: If unsure whether a faint mark is a reaction or pressure redness.
*   Action: Wait 24 hours. Repeat the test on a fresh area of skin. If the same ambiguous reaction appears, treat it as a FAIL.

## Notes
*   **Key Takeaways:**
    *   The skin test is the lowest-risk first filter, catching contact irritants before they reach the mouth, but it cannot detect all toxins.
    *   Use thin-skinned areas only: inner wrist or inner elbow; never palms, face, or near eyes.
    *   Crush the plant material to release internal compounds; surface contact alone is insufficient.
    *   Wait the full 8 hours, as delayed reactions are common.
    *   Any reaction is a FAIL; the cost of a false negative is higher than a false positive.
    *   Watch for photosensitizers, which cause severe burns when the skin is exposed to sunlight.
    *   One plant, one part, one test at a time to avoid cross-testing.
    *   Passing the skin test is necessary but not sufficient; proceed to lip, tongue, chew, and swallow tests.
*   **Special Situations:**
    *   Testing Roots: Test the outer surface first; if it passes, scrape away the skin and test the inner flesh separately.
    *   Testing Sap or Latex: Test the sap specifically, as many latex-producing plants have highly irritating sap.
    *   Wet vs. Dry Conditions: Test both the raw plant material and the cooled cooking water if boiling is planned, as cooking water (e.g., acorn tannins) can be more irritating.
    *   Children and Sensitive Individuals: If testing for others, test on yourself first. A negative result means the plant passed for your skin; the other person should still do an abbreviated test (skin contact only, 2-hour observation) before proceeding.
*   **Recording Format Example:**
    ```
    Plant: [description, drawing, location]
    Part tested: [leaf / root / stem / fruit / flower / sap]
    Preparation: [raw crushed / boiled / dried]
    Test site: [inner wrist / inner elbow / other]
    Contact duration: 15 minutes
    Observations during contact: [none / warmth / tingling / etc.]
    8-hour check: [no reaction / redness / rash / blisters]
    RESULT: PASS / FAIL
    Tester: [name]
    Date: [date]
    ```
