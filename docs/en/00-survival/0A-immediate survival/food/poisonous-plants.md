# Poisonous Plants | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To inform the reader that in a post-collapse world without hospitals, eating the wrong plant is a death sentence, emphasizing that avoiding deadly species is more valuable than knowing what to eat.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

*   Survival context (post-collapse world without hospitals).
*   The ability to cross-reference multiple identification markers.
*   Knowledge of the Universal Edibility Test.

## Material

### General Warning Signs

| Warning Sign | What It Means | Exceptions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Milky or discolored sap | Often contains irritant or toxic compounds | Dandelion has milky sap but is edible |
| Bitter or burning taste | Plant may contain alkaloids or oxalates | Some bitter greens (chicory) are safe |
| Umbrella-shaped flower clusters (umbels) | Family includes both edible and deadly species | Wild carrot is edible; hemlock is not |
| Shiny leaves in groups of three | Possible contact irritant (poison ivy/oak) | Strawberries also have three leaflets |
| Seeds inside pods | Some legume-family plants are toxic raw | Many beans are edible after cooking |
| Strong or unpleasant smell when crushed | May indicate volatile toxic compounds | Garlic mustard smells strong but is edible |

### The Most Dangerous Plant Families

**Apiaceae (Carrot Family)**
*   **Signature Feature:** Umbrella-shaped flower clusters (umbels).
*   **Key Danger Species:**
    *   **Poison hemlock (*Conium maculatum*):** Purple-blotched hollow stems, musty smell, finely divided leaves. Contains coniine, causing ascending paralysis and respiratory failure.
    *   **Water hemlock (*Cicuta* species):** Most toxic in North America. Roots have chambers with yellowish oily liquid, causing violent seizures within 15-30 minutes.
    *   **Fool’s parsley (*Aethusa cynapium*):** Distinguished by long, downward-hanging bracts beneath the flower clusters.
*   **Identification Strategy:** Cross-reference smell (edible carrot smells like carrot vs. musty/mousy hemlock), stem check (hemlock has purple blotches and is smooth; wild carrot stems are hairy), and root structure (wild carrot has a single taproot; water hemlock roots are chambered).

**Solanaceae (Nightshade Family)**
*   **Key Danger Species:**
    *   **Deadly nightshade (*Atropa belladonna*):** Shiny black berries, bell-shaped purple flowers. Contains atropine and scopolamine. Five berries can kill a child; ten to twenty an adult.
    *   **Bittersweet nightshade (*Solanum dulcamara*):** Red berries in clusters, purple flowers with yellow centers.
    *   **Jimsonweed (*Datura stramonium*):** Spiny seed pods, large white trumpet flowers, foul-smelling leaves. Causes hallucinations, hyperthermia, and death.

**Ericaceae (Heath Family)**
*   **Key Danger Species:** Mountain laurel and rhododendron.
*   **Toxicity:** Contain grayanotoxins. Honey made from these flowers ('mad honey') can cause poisoning.
*   **Symptoms:** Drooling, vomiting, progressive muscle weakness, cardiac arrest in severe cases.

### Poisoning Symptoms and Timeline

| Time After Ingestion | Symptoms | Likely Toxin Type |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0-30 minutes | Burning mouth, excessive salivation | Oxalates, caustic compounds |
| 30 min - 2 hours | Nausea, vomiting, abdominal cramps | Glycosides, alkaloids |
| 2-6 hours | Diarrhea, confusion, blurred vision | Alkaloids, solanine |
| 6-24 hours | Organ damage signs (jaundice, blood in urine) | Amatoxins (mushrooms), ricin |
| 1-3 days | Liver/kidney failure | Amatoxins, pyrrolizidine alkaloids |

### Emergency Response Without Modern Medicine

1.  **Identify what was eaten:** Save a sample of the plant (leaves, stem, root) if possible.
2.  **Induce vomiting only if:** The person is conscious, alert, and ingested the plant within the last 30 minutes. Have them drink warm salt water (2 tablespoons salt per glass). Skip vomiting if the person is drowsy, seizing, or if the plant causes chemical burns.
3.  **Administer activated charcoal (if available):** Crush hardwood charcoal to fine powder, mix with water, and have the person drink it. Ratio: roughly 10:1 charcoal to estimated poison weight.
4.  **Keep the person hydrated:** Small sips of clean water.
5.  **Monitor breathing:** Be prepared to give rescue breaths as many plant toxins cause respiratory depression.
6.  **Keep them warm and lying on their side:** Recovery position prevents choking on vomit.

### Building a Local Poison Plant Reference

1.  **Survey your area** within a 2-mile radius.
2.  **Collect samples** of every plant that cannot be positively identified.
3.  **Press and dry specimens** between flat surfaces with weight on top.
4.  **Label each** with location, date, physical description, and smell.
5.  **Test cautiously** using the universal edibility test only after ruling out obvious danger signs.
6.  **Build a “known safe” list** and a “confirmed dangerous” list.

### Seasonal Considerations

*   **Spring shoots:** Many toxic plants look edible when young (e.g., Pokeweed shoots resemble asparagus; Hemlock shoots resemble wild celery).
*   **Summer flowers:** Easier to identify plants in bloom, but some toxic compounds peak during flowering.
*   **Fall berries:** The most common source of accidental poisoning due to bright colors attracting attention when food is scarce.
*   **Winter roots:** Digging roots when the above-ground plant is unseen is extremely dangerous; only dig roots of previously identified and marked plants.

### Teaching Others

In a survival group, ensure everyone knows: the three most dangerous plants locally, the warning signs, where the reference collection is kept, and the emergency protocol.

## System

The system relies on systematic identification, cross-referencing, and localized reference building.

## Technology

*   Universal Edibility Test.
*   Activated Charcoal (for emergency treatment).

## Tools

*   Flat surfaces and weights (for pressing and drying specimens).
*   Labels and reference collection storage.

## Process

*   **Foraging Phase:** Surveying, collecting samples, pressing, drying, and labeling.
*   **Identification Phase:** Cautious testing using the universal edibility test, followed by cross-referencing multiple features.
*   **Emergency Phase:** Immediate identification, controlled vomiting (if appropriate), charcoal administration, hydration, and respiratory monitoring.

Notes
- The core philosophy of survival here is that avoidance is the primary strategy: knowing what NOT to eat is more valuable than knowing what to eat.
- The greatest danger in the Carrot Family (Apiaceae) is that it contains both staple foods and the deadliest plants.
- Poisoning symptoms from toxins like amatoxins (mushrooms) can be delayed, potentially showing no liver damage for 24 to 72 hours.
- Milk should not be given as it can increase the absorption of some toxins.
- Children are noted as especially vulnerable due to attraction to bright berries.
