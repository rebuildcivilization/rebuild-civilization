# Arrow Crafting

## Objective
To craft functional arrows that are precision instruments with three critical components: shaft, fletching, and point. The ultimate goal is to ensure the arrow flies true and hits with killing force.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

*   A bow (to match arrow spine to draw weight).
*   Raw materials (wood for shafts, feathers for fletching, stone/bone for points).

## Material

### Arrow Shaft Materials
| Material | Pros | Cons | Best For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Shoot/sucker growth | Naturally straight, consistent diameter | Must find the right species | All-around best |
| Split wood | Can make many from one piece | Requires careful splitting and rounding | When shoots unavailable |
| Reed/cane | Lightweight, straight, hollow | Fragile, can shatter on impact | Small game, birds |
| Cattail stalks | Very straight, easy to find | Weak, only for lightest points | Practice arrows only |
| Dogwood | Dense, tough, straight | - | Considered the best native North American arrow wood |
| Viburnum (arrow-wood) | Produces long, straight shoots | - | Produces long, straight shoots |
| Rose | Produces remarkably straight, hard shoots | Must remove thorns carefully | - |
| Willow | Widely available, reasonably straight | Lightweight | Needs heavier points to fly well |
| Hazel | Excellent European arrow wood | - | Straight coppice shoots are ideal |
| River cane (Arundinaria) | Strong, light, naturally straight and hollow | - | Used extensively by southeastern Native Americans |
| Privet | Produces very straight, dense shoots | Invasive in many areas | - |

### Fletching Materials
*   **Feather Sources:** Turkey (Excellent), Goose (Excellent), Duck (Good), Crow/Raven (Good), Hawk/Eagle (Excellent), Pigeon (Fair), Owl (Poor).
*   **Wing Consistency:** All feathers on one arrow must come from the same wing (left or right). Mixing wings causes competing air currents and erratic flight.

### Point Materials
*   **Point Types:** Sharpened shaft, Field point (stone), Broadhead (stone), Blunt (hardwood knob), Bone point.
*   **Stone Point Construction:** Start with a thin flake (flint, chert, obsidian, or similar), pressure-flake the edges using an antler tine or copper tool. Target dimensions: 3-5 cm long, 2-3 cm wide at base, 3-4 mm thick.

## System

### Arrow Precision Components
*   Shaft: Must be straight and matched to the bow's draw weight (spine matching).
*   Fletching: Must stabilize the arrow by creating drag at the rear.
*   Point: Determines the kill mechanism (trauma vs. penetration).

### Spine Matching System
*   **Test Protocol:** Support the shaft 66 cm (26 inches) apart and hang a 0.9 kg (2 lb) weight from the center to measure deflection.


## Technology

*   **Heat Straightening:** Essential because almost no shaft dries perfectly straight. Heat the crooked section over coals or flame, bend against the curve while hot, and hold for 30–60 seconds.
*   **Oil Trick:** Rubbing a thin layer of animal fat on the shaft before heat-straightening helps the wood hold the new shape more permanently and conditions it against moisture.
*   **Aerodynamics (Fletching):** Feathers must be equally spaced (120 degrees apart), with the cock feather perpendicular to the nock slot. A slight helical twist (2-3 degrees) can create spin, improving accuracy.

## Tools

*   Sharp knife or copper tool (for pressure-flaking stone points).
*   Heat source (coals or small flame) for straightening shafts.
*   Sinew thread and pitch adhesive for fletching attachment.
*   Measuring tools (implied for testing/cutting).

## Process

### Shaft Selection and Preparation
1.  **Selection Criteria:** Must be straight, 75–85 cm long, 8–10 mm in diameter, with no side branches in the working section.
2.  **Harvesting:** Cut shoots in late fall or winter when sap is down.
3.  **Bundling:** Bundle loosely with cord (10–15 shoots per bundle).
4.  **Drying:** Hang vertically in a dry, shaded location. Drying time is 2–4 weeks for thin shoots, up to 3 months for thicker shafts.
5.  **Straightening:** Heat the crooked section, bend against the curve while hot, hold for 30–60 seconds, and repeat for all curves.
6.  **Oil Trick Application:** Rub a thin layer of animal fat on the shaft before heat-straightening.

### Fletching Preparation and Attachment
1.  **Selection:** Select 3 matching feathers from the same wing.
2.  **Preparation:** Split each feather down the center of the quill. Trim to length (10–15 cm).
3.  **Shaping:** Choose a profile (Shield cut, Parabolic, or Straight).
4.  **Attachment (Sinew-and-glue method - Most Durable):** Mark three equally spaced lines along the last 15 cm of the shaft (120 degrees apart). Apply pitch adhesive. Press the first feather in (quill-side down, curved side out). Wrap fine sinew thread around the shaft at the front and back of the feather section, binding the quills tightly. Apply more pitch to seal.
5.  **Attachment (Quick Field Method):** Split the quill, slide it over a shallow groove scraped in the shaft, and tie at the front and back with thread or fine cordage.
6.  **Orientation:** The cock feather sits perpendicular to the bow string nock. The other two feathers sit on either side, angled toward the bow. Experiment with a slight helical twist (2–3 degrees) for spin.

### Point Hafting
1.  **Notch Cutting:** Cut a notch in the shaft tip, 1–2 cm deep, slightly narrower than the point’s base.
2.  **Insertion:** Insert the point base into the notch.
3.  **Reinforcement:** Wrap with wet sinew, starting below the notch, wrapping up over the point’s base, and back down, using a figure-eight pattern crossing over the notch.
4.  **Sealing:** Apply pine pitch over the sinew wrap.
5.  **Curing:** Let dry 24 hours minimum before use.

### Nock Cutting
1.  **Cutting Direction:** Cut perpendicular to the grain to prevent splitting when the string hits.
2.  **Dimensions:** Depth 6–8 mm (to hold the string but not weaken the shaft); Width just wider than the bowstring (must clip on with a gentle snap).

### Assembly and Testing

*   **Pre-flight Checklist:**
    *   Shaft is straight (sight down from nock end).
    *   Fletching is secure (tug each feather — no movement).
    *   Point is tight (twist — no rotation in the shaft).
    *   Nock fits bowstring snugly but releases cleanly.
    *   All feathers from the same wing.
    *   Cock feather oriented correctly (perpendicular to nock slot).
    *   Arrow length matches your draw length plus 2–3 cm.

*   **Test Shooting Protocol:**
    *   Start at 5 meters into a soft target.
    *   Watch arrow flight for indicators: fishtail (wrong spine), porpoise (nock point wrong), spiral (fletching issue).
    *   Inspect after each shot for cracks, loose points, or damaged fletching.
    *   Increase distance to 10 m, 15 m, 20 m as confidence grows.
    *   Group arrows to assess consistency.

## Notes

*   Arrows deserve as much attention as the bow; consistency in the shaft, spine, fletching, and point is key.
*   A working minimum of at least 6–12 arrows is required for hunting because arrows will be lost or broken.
*   The Oil Trick significantly improves the durability and shape retention of the heat-straightened shafts.
*   The broadhead’s flat plane must be perpendicular to the nock slot to ensure a clean pass.
