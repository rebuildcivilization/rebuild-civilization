# Meat-Fat Ratio

## Objective
To determine the single most important variable in pemmican making: the ratio of dried meat to rendered fat, and to guide users in achieving optimal proportions for food preservation and longevity.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Dried meat powder
*   Rendered fat (tallow)
*   Consideration of fat source, climate, intended use, and required shelf life.

## Material
*   Dried meat powder
*   Rendered tallow
*   Dried berries (blueberries, cranberries, saskatoon berries) for optional additions.

## System
The system relies on the proper coating and saturation of meat particles with fat to ensure structural integrity and prevent spoilage.

## Technology
*   **Density and Coating:** The fat must fully coat every meat particle to maximize caloric density and maintain structural integrity.
*   **Thermal Effects:** Temperature fluctuations determine fat state (solid vs. liquid), affecting separation and shelf life.
*   **Absorption Rate:** Berries absorb fat, requiring compensatory fat addition.

## Tools
*   Precise scale (for standard ratio calculation).
*   Hand Squeeze Test (most reliable field method).
*   Press Test (to check for structural integrity).
*   Temperature Check (to assess final mixture consistency).

## Process
### Standard Ratio
*   The traditional ratio is 1 part dried meat powder to 1 part rendered tallow by weight.
*   This yields approximately 2 pounds (900g) of finished pemmican from 1 pound (450g) meat and 1 pound (450g) tallow.

### Field Measurement Methods
1.  **The Volume Method:** Use approximately 1.5 parts meat powder to 1 part liquid tallow by volume to account for the lower density of meat powder.
2.  **The Hand Squeeze Test:** Mix tallow into meat powder gradually. The correct mixture holds its shape firmly when squeezed, feeling slightly waxy but without dripping.
3.  **The Press Test:** Press the mixture about 1/2 inch thick. A correct mixture presses flat cleanly with a matte surface; too dry cracks, and too wet oozes.
4.  **The Temperature Test:** After cooling to room temperature (70°F / 21°C), check for consistency: hard/crumbly means too little fat; soft/deforms means too much fat.

### Adjustments and Variations
*   **Ratio Adjustments:**
    *   Too Little Fat (< 1:0.7): Meat particles exposed, crumbly, short shelf life.
    *   Too Much Fat (> 1:1.3): Excess fat separates, greasy, weak structure.
    *   **Sweet Spot:** 1:1 ratio for general long-term storage.
*   **Climate Adjustments:**
    *   **Hot Climate (> 80°F / 27°C):** Use a leaner ratio (1:0.8 to 1:0.9).
    *   **Cold Climate (< 40°F / 4°C):** Use a richer ratio (1:1.1 to 1:1.2) as fat remains solid.
*   **Fat Type Adjustments:** Adjust ratios based on melting point (e.g., use leaner ratios for lower melting point fats like deer tallow).
*   **Berry Content Adjustment:** Increase fat by approximately 10% for every portion of dried berries added.

### Troubleshooting
*   **Pemmican Too Dry After Setting:** Re-warm gently, crumble, add warm tallow, mix, and reform.
*   **Pemmican Too Greasy After Setting:** Re-warm until soft, add more dried meat powder, mix, and reform (or press in cloth to let excess fat drip out).
*   **Fat Separating During Storage:** Re-melt the batch gently, stir vigorously to re-emulsify, and let cool slowly.

### Historical Context
The 1:1 ratio was refined through practical use by Plains Indigenous peoples and documented by explorers and Arctic expeditions (like Peary and Stefansson) as the most reliable.

## Notes
*   Tallow must be liquid but not hot (130-150°F / 55-65°C) to prevent cooking the meat powder.
*   The standard ratio is 1:1 weight, which is equivalent to roughly 1.5:1 volume (meat:fat).
*   For hot climates, mixing in beeswax (5-10% of the fat portion) can raise the melting point.
*   The most reliable field test is the Hand Squeeze Test, verifying that the mixture holds shape without crumbling or dripping.
*   When in doubt, err slightly toward more fat, as a slightly rich pemmican stores better than a dry, crumbly one with exposed meat surfaces.
