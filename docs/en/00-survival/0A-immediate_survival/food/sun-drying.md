# Sun Drying

## Objective
Sun drying preserves food by reducing moisture below 10–15%, which is low enough to stop bacterial, mold, and yeast growth.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Consistent sun (at least 6 hours of direct light per day).
*   Low to moderate humidity (below 60% is ideal; reliable below 60%).
*   Airflow.

## Material
*   Fresh meat (starts at 60–75% water).
*   Fresh fruit (starts at 80–90% water).
*   Root vegetables.
*   Herbs and leaves.

## System
**Drying Rack Setup**

### Basic Frame Design
| Component | Material | Dimensions |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Legs | 4 sturdy branches or poles | 90-120 cm (3-4 ft) tall |
| Frame rails | Straight branches | 120-180 cm (4-6 ft) long |
| Cross supports | Thinner branches | 60-90 cm (2-3 ft) wide |
| Drying surface | Woven reeds, split bamboo, or thin sticks | Spaced 1-2 cm apart |

**Construction Steps:**
1. Select four straight poles of roughly equal length for legs. Strip bark to reduce insect harboring.
2. Lash two long rails to the tops of the legs using cordage, vine, or stripped bark. Keep the frame level.
3. Add cross supports every 20-30 cm across the width.
4. Lay thin sticks, reeds, or woven grass across the cross supports to create the drying surface. Leave gaps of 1-2 cm between pieces for airflow underneath.
5. Angle the rack slightly (5-10 degrees) so any remaining moisture or rain runoff drains rather than pooling.

### Placement
*   Face the rack’s long axis east-west to maximize sun exposure throughout the day.
*   Position in an open area away from trees, buildings, or anything that casts shade.
*   Elevate at least 90 cm off the ground to discourage animals and improve air circulation.
*   If insects are a problem, drape a loose mesh of woven grass or thin cloth over the rack, keeping it 10-15 cm above the food so air still flows.

## Technology
Sun drying achieves moisture reduction below 10-15% through direct solar heat and air circulation, evaporating water from thin slices over several days.

## Tools
*   Sharp blade (for slicing).
*   Cordage, vine, or stripped bark (for lashings).
*   Salt (for pre-treatment).
*   Acidulated water (for fruit pre-treatment).
*   Cover or mesh (for insect protection).

## Process
1. **Pre-treat if possible.** A light salt rub on meat (roughly 2-3% of the meat’s weight in salt) draws out moisture faster. For fruit, brief dipping in acidulated water (crushed wild berries or citrus juice mixed with water) prevents browning.
2. **Arrange on the rack.** Place slices in a single layer with no overlapping. Leave 1-2 cm of space between pieces for air circulation.
3. **Turn every few hours.** Flip pieces 2-3 times per day to ensure even drying on both sides.
4. **Bring inside at night.** Nighttime dew reverses drying progress. Move the rack under cover or bring food inside at dusk. Resume drying at sunrise.
5. **Protect from rain.** Have a cover or indoor space ready, as rain introduces moisture and bacteria.

## Notes
*   **Slicing Technique:** Thin, uniform slices are critical. Meat should be 3-6 mm thick, cut against the grain. Fruit and vegetables require uniform thickness. Always remove all visible fat from meat before slicing, as fat goes rancid.
*   **Moisture Testing (How to Tell When Food is Done):**
    *   **Meat:** Stiff, dark, and brittle; should crack or snap when bent, not flex. No moist or soft spots.
    *   **Fruit:** Leathery and pliable but not sticky; should not leave moisture on fingers when squeezed.
    *   **Vegetables:** Brittle and crisp; should shatter or crumble, not bend.
*   **Food Safety Consideration:** Sun drying meat in humid climates (above 60% humidity) is risky, and fire drying should be used instead for faster, more controlled dehydration in humid conditions.
*   **Storage:** Dried food must be kept dry, stored in the driest, coolest, darkest space, wrapped tightly in clean cloth, bark containers, or sealed pottery. Proper storage yields 2-6 months depending on climate and food type.
*   **Troubleshooting Summary:**
    *   Mold: Fix by moving to a drier location, slicing thinner, and turning more often.
    *   Meat still flexible: Continue drying 1-2 more days or slice thinner next time.
    *   Insects: Fix by adding a mesh cover or raising the rack height.
    *   Off taste after storage: Fix by trimming fat before drying or improving storage sealing.
