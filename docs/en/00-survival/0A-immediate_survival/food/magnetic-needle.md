# Magnetizing and Floating Steel

## Objective
To make an improvised compass.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   A steel sewing needle or razor blade.
*   A salvaged magnet (hard drive, speaker, or motor magnets are strongest).
*   Still water.
*   Float platform (dry leaf, cork slice, or bark chip).
*   For magnetization: Iron-based metals (carbon steel, cast iron, wrought iron).

## Material
*   **Magnetic Metals (Hold charge):** Carbon steel, cast iron, wrought iron, nickel, stainless steel (400 series).
*   **Non-Magnetic Metals:** Aluminum, copper, brass, 300-series stainless steel, lead.
*   **Float Platforms:** Dry leaf, cork slice, bark chip, closed-cell foam, grass blade (folded), paper.
*   **Friction Materials:** Silk, hair, wool, dry animal fur.
*   **Electromagnetic Materials:** Insulated wire, battery.

## System
### Magnetism
Every piece of iron or steel contains microscopic regions called magnetic domains. In unmagnetized metal, these domains point randomly, causing fields to cancel out. Magnetization occurs when domains are forced to line up, allowing the metal to produce a magnetic field that interacts with the Earth’s field, causing the needle to align north-south.

### Surface Tension
Water molecules create an elastic surface layer that can support lightweight objects, provided they are placed gently and do not break through. Surface contamination (grease, oil, soap) reduces surface tension.

## Technology
*   **Magnetic Fields:** The interaction between the magnetized steel and the Earth's magnetic field determines the compass direction.
*   **Triboelectric Effects:** Friction (silk/hair) slightly reorganizes magnetic domains.
*   **Electromagnetism:** Using a battery and wire to create a temporary, strong magnetic field.

## Tools
*   **Strong Magnets (Permanent):** Hard drive magnets, speaker magnets, motor magnets, refrigerator magnets, magnetic cabinet latches.
*   **Electromagnetic Tools:** Battery (any type), insulated wire.
*   **Friction Tools:** Silk or hair.
*   **Induction Tools (Emergency):** Rock or hammer.

## Process
### Magnetizing Methods
**Method 1: Permanent Magnet Stroking (Best)
*   **Effectiveness:** Excellent. **Duration:** Days to weeks.
*   **Procedure:** Stroke the needle 30–50 times in one direction with a salvaged magnet. The end of the stroke becomes a pole. Consistency (stroking from the same end to the same end) is key.
*   **Best Sources:** Hard drive magnets, speaker magnets, motor magnets.

**Method 2: Electromagnetic Coil (Strong)
*   **Effectiveness:** Excellent. **Duration:** Days to weeks.
*   **Procedure:** Wrap 20-30 turns of wire around the needle. Connect one bare wire end to the positive terminal and the other to the negative terminal of a battery for 30-60 seconds.

**Method 3: Friction with Silk or Hair (Weakest)
*   **Effectiveness:** Marginal. **Duration:** Hours at best.
*   **Procedure:** Stroke rapidly along fabric or hair for a minimum of 80-100 strokes in the same direction.

**Method 4: Induction from Earth’s Field (Emergency Only)
*   **Effectiveness:** Very weak. **Duration:** Minutes.
*   **Procedure:** Align the steel rod vertically with the Earth’s magnetic field lines. Strike the top end sharply 20-30 times.

### Floating the Magnetized Needle
**Float Options Ranked**
| Float Type | Reliability | Availability | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cork slice | Excellent | Wine bottles, bulletin boards | Push needle through; very stable |
| Closed-cell foam | Excellent | Life jackets, packaging | Cut a small square; needle sits on top or pushed through |
| Dry leaf | Good | Everywhere in temperate climates | Replace when waterlogged (every 10-15 minutes) |
| Bark chip | Good | Any tree | Thin, flat pieces work best |
| Paper | Fair | Books, packaging | Saturates quickly; good for one reading |
| Direct surface tension | Variable | Nothing needed | Only works with very clean, dry, thin needles or razor blades |

**Direct Float Technique (No Platform)**
1. Fill the container to the brim so the water surface is slightly domed.
2. Place tissue paper on the water.
3. Place the needle on the tissue.
4. Wait for the tissue to absorb water and sink, leaving the needle floating on surface tension.

### Testing Your Magnetized Needle
1. **Repeatability test:** Spin the float gently and release. Confirm it returns within 10-15 degrees of the same heading three times.
2. **Reversal test:** Remove the needle, flip it end-for-end on the float, and re-launch. It should point the opposite way.
3. **Strength test:** Bring a small piece of steel near the needle tip to check for deflection.

### Troubleshooting
| Symptom | Likely Cause | Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Needle does not rotate at all | Not magnetized, or too much friction | Re-magnetize with more strokes; use a better float |
| Needle points a different direction each time | Very weak magnetization | Use a stronger source; re-magnetize |
| Needle sticks to container wall | Metal container or wall has residual magnetism | Switch to a non-metallic container |
| Float sinks within seconds | Leaf waterlogged or needle too heavy | Use cork or foam instead; select a lighter needle |
| Needle oscillates without settling | Wind or vibration | Shield the container; place on stable ground |
| Reading seems wrong | Nearby iron or steel distorting field | Move at least 3 meters from any metal |

### Magnetization Duration
| Method | Expected Duration | Re-magnetize Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Neodymium magnet stroke | 1-4 weeks | Weekly or when readings weaken |
| Ferrite magnet stroke | 3-7 days | Every few days |
| Electromagnetic coil | 1-4 weeks | Weekly |
| Silk/hair friction | 2-6 hours | Before every use |
| Earth-field induction | Minutes | Not practical for repeated use |

Hard, high-carbon steel retains magnetism far longer than soft, low-carbon steel.

## Notes
*   **Metal Selection:** Only iron-based metals (carbon steel, cast iron, wrought iron) hold a magnetic charge reliably.
*   **Container Choice:** The container must be wide and shallow, level, and non-metallic (ceramic, glass, wood, plastic, stone) to prevent distortion or sliding.
*   **Water Quality:** Use the cleanest water available (rainwater and spring water are best) to maximize surface tension effectiveness.
*   **Critical Verification:** The repeatability and reversal tests are mandatory before trusting the compass reading.
