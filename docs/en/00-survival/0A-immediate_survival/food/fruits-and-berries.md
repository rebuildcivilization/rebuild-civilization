# Fruits and Berries | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To teach readers how to tell the difference between wild berries and fruits, understand their survival value, identify safe and dangerous species, and learn methods for harvesting and preserving them.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Specific identification knowledge is required to determine berry safety, as relying on color, taste, or animal behavior is insufficient. The ability to distinguish between similar species (e.g., elderberry vs. pokeweed) is critical.

## Material
Wild berries and fruits provide essential vitamins, sugars, and morale in a survival situation. They serve three critical functions:

1. Immediate energy (natural sugars for quick calories).
2. Vitamins (wild berries are often high in vitamin C, crucial to prevent scurvy).
3. Morale (psychological value to reset mental state).

## System
### Color Rules and Limits
These rules serve as a starting framework, but are not guarantees.

| Berry Color | General Safety |
| :--- | :--- | 
| Blue/Black | ~90% edible |
| Red | ~50% safe |
| White/Yellow | ~90% dangerous |
| Green (unripe) | Almost always unsafe |

### Aggregate Berry Rule
Aggregate berries (made of many small fleshy segments clustered together, like a raspberry or blackberry) are almost always safe to eat.

### Species Identification
**Reliably Safe Wild Berries (Temperate Regions):**
*   **Blackberries and Raspberries (*Rubus*):** Identified by thorny/bristly canes, compound leaves with 3-5 serrated leaflets, white or pink flowers with five petals, and fruit ripening from green to red to deep purple/black (blackberry) or red (raspberry).
*   **Blueberries and Huckleberries (*Vaccinium*):** Identified by small shrubs, small oval leaves, bell-shaped flowers, and round blue-to-black berries with a distinctive five-pointed crown on the bottom.
*   **Elderberries (*Sambucus nigra*):** Identified by shrub/small tree form, compound leaves with 5-7 serrated leaflets, flat-topped clusters of tiny white flowers, and clusters of small dark purple-black berries.
*   **Rose Hips (*Rosa*):** Identified by small red-orange oval fruits remaining on rose bushes after flowers fade.

**Dangerous Berries to Know on Sight:**
*   **Deadly Nightshade (*Atropa belladonna*):** Shiny black berries, bell-shaped purple-brown flowers, sweet-tasting, and the risk that 2-5 berries can kill a child or 10-20 an adult.
*   **Baneberry / Doll’s Eyes (*Actaea*):** White berries with a distinctive black dot on each (looks like a doll’s eye).
*   **Pokeweed (*Phytolacca americana*):** Dark purple-black berries in drooping clusters, thick red-purple stems, and large smooth leaves.
*   **Yew Berries (*Taxus*):** Bright red fleshy cups (arils) surrounding a single hard seed; the seed is lethal.

## Technology
The system relies on precise visual identification combined with structural recognition (aggregate berries) to manage the risk inherent in foraged food.

## Tools
*   Fork or fingers (for harvesting/stripping).
*   Rigid container (for fresh harvesting).
*   Woven mesh (to remove seeds from berry leather).
*   Flat rock, large leaf, or bark slab (for drying).
*   Direct sun and protection from rain/dew (for drying).

## Process
### Fresh Harvesting
1. Pick berries gently to avoid crushing.
2. Collect into a rigid container if possible to prevent crushing by weight.
3. Process within 24-48 hours in warm weather, as wild berries spoil rapidly.

### Drying
1. Spread berries in a single layer on a flat surface in direct sun.
2. Turn twice daily and protect from rain and dew overnight.
3. Allow 3-7 days for berries to become wrinkled, leathery, and stop moisture loss.
4. Store dried berries in a sealed, dry container.

### Berry Leather
1. Mash berries into a smooth paste.
2. Remove seeds by pressing through a woven mesh if desired.
3. Spread the paste thinly (3-5mm) on a flat surface.
4. Dry in sun for 1-2 days until leathery and peelable.
5. Roll up and store.

## Notes
*   Elderberries MUST be cooked before eating because raw berries, stems, leaves, bark, and roots contain cyanogenic glycosides that cause severe nausea, vomiting, and diarrhea.
*   The aggregate berry structure is the single most useful berry identification shortcut.
*   White and yellow berries are almost always toxic and should not be eaten without positive species identification.
*   The risk/reward ratio for Yew Berries is poor because the seed, though potentially passable, releases taxine alkaloids upon chewing.
*   Never rely on taste or animal behavior to judge berry safety.

### Seasonal Availability (Temperate Climate)
*   **Late spring:** Wild strawberries, mulberries.
*   **Early summer:** Raspberries, serviceberries, currants.
*   **Mid-summer:** Blackberries, blueberries, huckleberries.
*   **Late summer:** Elderberries (cook!), wild grapes, chokeberries.
*   **Autumn:** Rose hips, hawthorn berries, crabapples.
*   **Winter:** Rose hips (still on bushes), hawthorn berries, dried stores.
