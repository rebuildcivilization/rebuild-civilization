# Brining | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To brine meat for preservation.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Clean water (boil if water safety is in doubt).
*   Non-reactive containers (ceramic, glass, food-grade plastic, wooden barrel, or stoneware crock). Bare metal containers must be avoided as salt corrodes them.

## Material
*   Meat
*   Salt
*   Warm water
*   Aromatics (Sugar, Bay leaves, Peppercorns, Garlic, Juniper berries, Cinnamon, Allspice, Cloves)
*   Weights (plate, wooden disc, or sealed water-filled jar)

## System
Brining (Wet salt preservation).

## Technology
Brining works by using dissolved salt in water as the delivery vehicle, allowing for more even salt penetration and thermal buffering compared to dry salting.

## Tools
*   Measuring tools
*   Injector (sharpened hollow reed, cleaned syringe, or improvised injector) for injecting thick cuts.
*   Weighted plate or weight to keep meat submerged.
*   Container for brine.
*   Knife (to puncture air pockets).

## Process
### Making Brine
1.  Measure water. Boil water if safety is in doubt.
2.  Heat the water to 140-160°F (60-70°C).
3.  Add salt gradually, stirring continuously until fully dissolved, ensuring no crystals settle at the bottom.
4.  Cool the brine to room temperature before submerging meat.
5.  Verify concentration using the egg float test before use.

### Flavored Brine Additions (added while water is hot)
*   Sugar: 1/2 cup per gallon of standard brine.
*   Bay leaves: 3-4 per gallon.
*   Peppercorns: 1 tablespoon whole, per gallon.
*   Garlic: 4-6 crushed cloves per gallon.
*   Juniper berries: 1 tablespoon crushed, per gallon.
*   Cinnamon, Allspice, Cloves: 1 teaspoon each per gallon.

### Brining Meat Step-by-Step
1.  Preparation: Trim excess fat; inject thick cuts with brine to reach the center, especially around bones.
2.  Select a non-reactive container.
3.  Submerging: Place meat in the container. Pour cooled brine over the meat until everything is submerged by at least 1 inch (2.5 cm) of liquid.
4.  Weight it down: Place a plate, disc, or stone on top, then add weight. Every surface must remain below the brine line.
5.  Curing: Store in the coolest location available (below 50°F/10°C ideal, below 40°F/4°C perfect).
6.  Agitate the brine every 1-2 days to prevent salt stratification.
7.  Replace the brine every 5-7 days for long cures.

### Brining Vegetables
1.  Wash and cut vegetables, removing bruised portions.
2.  Pack tightly into a clean container.
3.  Pour 3-5% brine (about 2 tablespoons per quart of water) over vegetables until fully submerged.
4.  Weight down with a plate and stone.
5.  Cover with cloth to keep out insects; do not seal airtight.
6.  After 3-7 days, lacto-fermentation begins, and the brine turns cloudy and sour.

### Post-Brining
1.  Remove meat when cure time is complete.
2.  Rinse briefly under clean water to remove surface salt.
3.  Soak to desalt if heavily brined: 6-12 hours in fresh water, changing water every 3-4 hours.
4.  Dry the surface: Hang in a cool, airy location for 12-24 hours until a dry pellicle forms.
5.  Store, smoke, or cook.

### Long-Term Brine Barrel Maintenance
1.  Start with saturated brine (26.3% concentration) confirmed by the egg float test.
2.  Add salt-rubbed meat, adding 1/4 cup of dry salt per pound of new meat added.
3.  Skim scum weekly (normal is white foam; pink, green, or foul-smelling scum means the brine has weakened; add salt and skim thoroughly).
4.  Replace the brine entirely every 4-6 weeks, or when it becomes dark and thick with dissolved proteins.

## Notes
*   **Minimum Safe Concentration:** Never use brine below 10% concentration for the preservation of raw meat and fish; below this threshold, the brine acts as a warm, moist, nutrient-rich bacterial growth medium.
*   **Brine Line Rule:** Any meat surface above the brine line will spoil, as it lacks the full salt concentration needed to kill salt-tolerant bacteria.
*   **Physics of Brining:** The liquid medium offers even coverage, faster penetration (dissolved salt diffuses faster than solid crystals), and temperature control.
*   **Curing Duration:** Brining takes approximately 50% longer than dry salting for equivalent cuts.
*   **Vegetable Brine:** Vegetable brine (3-5% salt) is lighter than meat brine and is intended to allow lactobacillus bacteria to thrive through fermentation.
