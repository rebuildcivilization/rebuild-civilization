# Pemmican Making

## Objective
To create the most calorie-dense, longest-lasting portable food possible without modern technology. Properly made pemmican should remain edible for over a decade, providing enough energy for a full day of hard physical labor.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Meat:** Lean red meat (Bison/buffalo, venison, lean beef, goat/sheep, horse, rabbit/hare). 
*   **Fat:** Rendered tallow (beef or bison fat is standard and best). 
*   **Optional Additions:** Dried berries (Saskatoon, blueberries, cranberries, chokecherries), dried herbs (wild sage, juniper berries, dried mint), or honey.
*   **Prerequisite for Success:** All meat must be rendered completely dry; fat must be rendered cleanly; berries must be thoroughly dried.

## Material
### The Meat
*   **Preference Order:** Bison/buffalo (Excellent), Venison (Excellent), Beef (lean cuts - Good), Goat/sheep (lean - Good), Horse (Good), Rabbit/hare (Fair), Fish (Poor), Pork (Poor).
*   **Critical Material Rule:** Remove ALL visible fat from the meat before drying. Fat left on the meat will spoil the batch.

### The Fat
*   **Standard Choice:** Tallow (rendered beef or bison fat) due to high saturation and resistance to rancidity.
*   **Acceptable Alternatives:** Rendered mutton/sheep fat, rendered bear fat, rendered deer/elk fat.
*   **Do NOT Use:** Lard (pork fat), any liquid oil (fish, nut, plant oil), or butter (due to milk proteins and water).

### Optional Additions
*   Dried berries (for flavor, Vitamin C, carbohydrates).
*   Dried herbs (for flavor).
*   Honey (a small amount for energy and flavor).

## System
### Core Principle
Pemmican succeeds by eliminating all three spoilage vectors simultaneously:
1.  **No moisture:** Meat is dried to bone-dry crispness.
2.  **No oxygen exposure:** Rendered fat saturates and coats every particle of dried meat, sealing out air.
3.  **No protein contamination in the fat:** Properly rendered tallow is pure fat with no bacterial food source.

### Nutritional Profile (4-ounce serving, 1:1 meat-to-fat ratio)
| Nutrient | Amount | % Daily Need |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Calories | 550-600 | 25-30% |
| Protein | 20-25g | 40-50% |
| Fat | 50-55g | 70-80% |
| Carbohydrates | 0-2g (without berries) | Negligible |
| Iron | High | Significant |
| B vitamins | Moderate | Partial |

### Storage Performance
| Storage Conditions | Expected Shelf Life |
| :--- | :--- | 
| Cool and dry (below 60°F / 15°C, low humidity) | 3-10+ years |
| Room temperature, moderate humidity | 1-3 years |
| Warm climate (above 80°F / 27°C) | 3-6 months |
| Carried on person (body heat, jostling) | 1-3 months |
| Exposed to direct sunlight or rain | Weeks at most |

## Technology
*   **Modern Technology:** Modern technology is excluded from the pemmican-making process.
*   **Key Factor:** The effectiveness relies on eliminating moisture and oxygen exposure, which requires careful drying and thorough grinding.

## Tools
*   **Drying Methods:** Sun drying on racks, fire drying over a low smoky fire, smoking.
*   **Grinding Tools:** Two flat rocks used as mortar and pestle, a heavy, smooth stone on a flat rock surface, wooden mortar carved from a log section with a heavy wooden pestle.
*   **Forming Tools:** Bark trays or containers, animal hide pouches (rawhide is ideal), hollowed wood sections, or shaping by hand.

## Process
### Phase 1: Making the Jerky
1.  **Slice lean meat thin:** 3-5 mm (1/8 to 1/4 inch) strips, cut with the grain.
2.  **Remove:** Every trace of fat, sinew, and connective tissue.
3.  **Dry completely:** Methods include sun drying (1-3 days), fire drying (6-12 hours), or smoking.
4.  **Test for doneness:** Pemmican-grade jerky must **snap cleanly in half** like a dry twig.
5.  **Final sun cure:** Spread strips in direct sun for an additional 2-4 hours to remove last traces of moisture.

### Phase 2: Grinding to Powder
1.  **Break jerky into small pieces** by hand (snap into 1-inch fragments).
2.  **Grind to a fine, fluffy powder.** Methods involve pounding with rocks or using a wooden mortar and pestle.
3.  **Sift out large pieces:** Pass through a loosely woven basket or cloth. Re-grind material that does not pass through.

### Phase 3: Rendering Tallow
1.  **Cut hard fat** (suet is best) into 1/2-inch cubes.
2.  **Melt slowly** over low heat, stirring occasionally.
3.  **Strain:** When cracklings sink and turn golden (2-3 hours), strain through cloth to collect the clear liquid fat (tallow).
4.  **Double-render:** For maximum purity and shelf life.

### Phase 4: Mixing
1.  **Warm the tallow** until fully liquid but not smoking (Target temperature: 130-150°F / 55-65°C).
2.  **Add additions:** If using dried berries, mix them in now, ensuring they comprise no more than 10-15% of the total meat powder volume.
3.  **Pour warm tallow slowly over the meat powder** while stirring constantly, adding tallow in stages.
4.  **Target Consistency:** The mixture should hold together when squeezed but not drip liquid fat. Add more meat powder if too dry; mix in more meat powder if fat pools on the surface.
5.  **Mix thoroughly:** Every particle of meat must be coated with fat.

### Phase 5: Forming and Storage
1.  **Forming:** While warm and pliable, press the mixture firmly into molds (bark trays, hide pouches, hollowed wood) to eliminate air pockets. Use a flat rock or smooth wood to compress.
2.  **Cooling:** Let the pemmican cool completely until it is a firm, solid mass.
3.  **Storage:** Wrap using rawhide parfleche or multiple layers of cloth/bark containers.
4.  **Location:** Store in the coolest, driest, darkest location available, off the ground, away from direct sunlight and animals.

## Notes
*   **Common Mistakes:** The number one failure is meat not being dry enough (flexibility means moisture and guaranteed spoilage). Failure to render fat cleanly (protein bits in tallow) invites bacterial growth. Using unsaturated fats (pork, fish, liquid oils) significantly shortens shelf life.
*   **Key Takeaways:**
    *   Pemmican combines bone-dry meat powder with pure rendered tallow at roughly a 1:1 ratio by weight.
    *   Meat must snap cleanly to ensure proper drying.
    *   Use only rendered ruminant tallow (beef, bison, deer, sheep); avoid pork fat, fish oil, or liquid plant oils.
    *   Grind meat to a fine powder and saturate every particle with fat to eliminate air exposure.
    *   Press firmly when forming to remove air pockets, as oxygen is the primary enemy of storage stability.
*   **Eating Methods:** Can be eaten Raw (for fuel), Fried (most palatable), or Stew (Rubaboo) with other ingredients.
*   **Foraging Caution:** Only use berries positively identified as edible; avoid high moisture content berries (like strawberries).
*   **Warm Climate Strategy:** In warm climates, the primary failure mode is fat melting. Use the highest-melting fat available (beef tallow melts around 104°F / 40°C).
*   **Batch Sizing Reference:** A 10 lb raw meat input yields approximately 5 lbs of pemmican, providing 10-14 person-days of food.
