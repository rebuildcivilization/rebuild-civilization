# Drying Methods

## Objective
To preserve food by removing moisture below 15%, at which point most bacteria stop growing, and below 10% even mold cannot survive.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- **Sun Drying:** Dry, warm, breezy conditions. Works poorly in humid climates (above 60% relative humidity), during rainy seasons, or with persistent cloud cover.
- **Wind Drying:** Arid, windy climates (deserts, open plains, high-altitude areas).
- **Fire Drying:** Works in any weather, works at night, and works in humid climates where sun drying fails.
- **Wind Drying Specifics:** Relative humidity below 30%; consistent wind; no rain for 2–3 consecutive days; low insect pressure.

## Material
- Green wood (fresh-cut branches that won’t snap)
- Cordage
- Salt (optional for meat, used to accelerate drying and inhibit bacteria)
- Citrus juice (optional for fruit, to prevent browning)
- Hot peppers (optional rub on meat surfaces to repel insects)
- Fine mesh, cheesecloth, or loosely woven fabric (to protect the rack from insects)
- Flat rocks (to line the lower drying rack to absorb sunlight and radiate heat)

## System
### Sun Drying
The simplest method, requiring only sunlight, moving air, and a rack.

### Fire Drying
Uses radiant heat from a fire to drive off moisture faster than sun alone. It works at night, in cloudy weather, and in humid climates.

### Wind Drying
Utilizes wind alone in arid environments to dry food effectively, producing high-quality meat with no smoke flavor or cooked texture.

## Technology
Bacteria require moisture to survive and reproduce. Drying works by reducing moisture content below critical thresholds to inhibit microbial growth.

## Tools
- Drying Rack
- Fire (coals or dry hardwood)
- Small enclosure made from flat stones
- Measuring tools (implied for thickness and weight tests)

## Process
### Preparation
1. **Slice thin:** Cut food into uniform strips no thicker than 5 mm (1/4 inch) for meat and 3–5 mm for fruits and vegetables. Remove all visible fat.
2. **Pre-treat (optional but recommended):** Meat rubbed with salt; fruit dipped in salt water or squeezed with citrus juice; vegetables blanched (dip in boiling water for 30–60 seconds, then cold water).

### Method 1: Sun Drying
1. **Build Rack:** Cut two Y-shaped forked branches (1 m / 3 ft long), drive them 1–2 m (3–6 ft) apart, and rest a horizontal crossbar in the forks. Lay thin sticks or cordage across the crossbar.
2. **Rack Placement:** The rack should be at least 60 cm (2 ft) above the ground to allow air circulation underneath.
3. **Arrangement:** Space pieces so they don’t touch to ensure air exposure.
4. **Protection:** Keep a small smoky fire upwind; drape mesh or fabric over the rack, or rub crushed hot peppers on meat.
5. **Turning:** Turn food every 4–6 hours.
6. **Nighttime:** Bring food inside or cover the rack with bark, a tarp, or large leaves to prevent nighttime dew.

### Method 2: Fire Drying
1. **Setup (Option A):** Build a drying rack positioned 60–90 cm (2–3 ft) above a low fire, using gentle, steady heat from coals or dry hardwood.
2. **Setup (Option B):** Build a rack at an angle (about 45 degrees) facing the fire, 60–90 cm away, utilizing radiant heat.
3. **Setup (Option C):** Build a small enclosure from flat stones, burn down the fire to coals, place food on a rack of green sticks, and reload with fresh coals.
4. **Process:** Place food on the rack and maintain a steady low heat. Target temperature at the food level: 40–60°C (100–140°F).
5. **Turning:** Turn food every 1–2 hours.
6. **Duration:** Typically takes 6–12 hours depending on food type and thickness.

### Method 3: Wind Drying
1. **Setup:** Hang thin strips from a horizontal line or branch in a location with a constant breeze. No fire or rack needed.
2. **Best For:** Lean meat in dry climates.

### Moisture Testing
- **Squeeze Test:** Press a piece firmly for 10 seconds. If visible moisture or a dark/wet fingerprint impression exists, keep drying.
- **Bend Test (Meat/Fish):** The food should crack or fracture on the outer surface but not snap completely in half. If it bends without cracking, it’s too wet.
- **Weight Test:** Properly dried meat weighs roughly 25–30% of its original weight.

### Storage of Dried Food
1. **Location:** Store in the driest location available, preferably in a ventilated container (woven basket, cloth bag, bark box) in a cool, dry spot or underground if the soil is dry.
2. **Sealing:** Never seal in an airtight container while still warm; let food cool completely first.
3. **Inspection:** Check every 3–5 days for white fuzzy mold. Re-dry affected pieces immediately.
4. **Protection:** Keep containers away from humidity sources (water supply, cooking area) and protect from insects/rodents by hanging containers high or using wood ash on the outside of bags.

### Rehydrating Dried Food
- **Jerky and Dried Fish:** Eat as-is or soak in warm water for 30–60 minutes to add to soups and stews.
- **Dried Fruit:** Eat as-is or soak in water for 15–30 minutes.
- **Dried Vegetables:** Add directly to boiling water and simmer for 15–30 minutes.
- **Dried Herbs:** Crush and add directly to cooking (no rehydration needed).

## Notes
- **Science:** Fresh meat is 60–80% water; fresh fruit is 80–90% water.
- **Thickness and Fat:** The two biggest causes of drying failure are incorrect thickness and the presence of fat, which must be trimmed before drying.
- **Drying Time Comparison (Meat Jerky):** Sun Drying (1–3 days); Fire Drying (6–12 hours).
- **Shelf Life:** Properly dried meat jerky has a shelf life of 1–3 months; dried fish 1–2 months; dried fruit 3–6 months; dried vegetables/herbs 6–12 months (in dry, cool storage).
