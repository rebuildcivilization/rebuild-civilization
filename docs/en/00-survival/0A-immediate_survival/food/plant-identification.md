# Plant Identification Guide

## Objective
To correctly identify wild plants before eating them by following a fixed sequence of systematic observation, aiming to distinguish edible species from dangerous look-alikes.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

*   **Knowledge Base:** Familiarity with basic plant families is key for informed decision-making.
*   **Observation Skills:** Ability to systematically examine plants based on specific characteristics.
*   **Universal Edibility Test (UET):** Familiarity with the UET as a last resort, understanding its limitations.

## Material

### Plant Families to Recognize
*   **Mint Family:** Indicated by square stems or tubular, lipped flowers.
*   **Carrot Family (Apiaceae):** Indicated by round, hollow stems with umbrella-shaped flower clusters (umbels).
*   **Rose Family:** Indicated by 5 radially symmetric petals (often associated with edible fruits like strawberry, apple, cherry, raspberry).
*   **Legume Family:** Indicated by irregular/bilateral (pea-shaped) flowers.
*   **Daisy/Aster Family:** Indicated by composite flower heads (e.g., dandelion, chicory, sunflower).

### Dangerous Look-Alikes (Critical Pairs)
| Edible Plant | Deadly Look-Alike | Key Difference |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Wild carrot (Queen Anne’s lace) | Poison hemlock | Wild carrot has hairy stems and a single dark floret at the center; Poison hemlock has smooth, purple-spotted stems and no dark floret |
| Wild onion / garlic | Death camas | Wild onion/garlic ALWAYS smells like onion when crushed; Death camas has no onion smell |
| Elderberry (cooked) | Water hemlock | Elderberry has opposite compound leaves and flat-topped berry clusters; Water hemlock has alternate compound leaves and no berries |
| Wild grape | Moonseed | Grape has forked tendrils, multiple seeds per fruit; Moonseed has no tendrils, single crescent-shaped seed |
| Chicken of the woods (mushroom) | Jack-o-lantern (mushroom) | Chicken of the woods is a shelf fungus on trees; Jack-o-lantern has gills and grows in clusters on the ground |

## System

The identification process follows a fixed sequence to narrow down possibilities effectively.

## Technology

*   **Visual Inspection:** The primary tool for identification, relying on detailed observation of physical characteristics.
*   **Universal Edibility Test (UET):** A multi-step process of skin contact, lip contact, tongue contact, and small ingestion, used as a last resort.

## Tools

*   **Systematic Observation:** The core method involving examining plants in a specific order.
*   **Field Knowledge:** Local mapping and tracking of seasonal availability.

## Process

The identification sequence is:

### Step 1: Habitat and Location

| Habitat | Common Edibles | Common Dangers |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Open meadow / grassland | Dandelion, clover, plantain, wild onion | Hemlock (often near water edges) |
| Forest floor (shade) | Wood sorrel, ramps, fiddleheads, chickweed | Death camas, false hellebore |
| Wetland / stream edges | Cattail, watercress, wild rice | Water hemlock (the most toxic plant in North America) |
| Disturbed ground / roadsides | Lamb’s quarters, amaranth, purslane | Nightshades, jimsonweed |
| Rocky / dry slopes | Wild thyme, juniper berries, prickly pear | Larkspur, locoweed |

### Step 2: Growth Form

*   **Trees and shrubs:** Nut trees (oak, walnut, hazel), fruit trees (crabapple, wild plum, mulberry), berry bushes (elderberry, blueberry, serviceberry).
*   **Herbaceous plants:** Most wild greens, root vegetables, and herbs.
*   **Vines:** Wild grape, passionflower, some squash relatives. Potential dangers include moonseed and poison ivy.
*   **Ground cover / rosettes:** Dandelion, plantain, wild strawberry.

### Step 3: Leaf Examination

Key observations include arrangement (alternate, opposite, whorled), shape (simple or compound), edge (smooth, toothed, or lobed), surface (hairy, waxy, rough, or smooth), and smell when crushed (aromatic or acrid/bitter).

### Step 4: Stem Characteristics

| Stem Feature | Suggestion |
| :--- | :--- |
| Square cross-section | Mint family (Lamiaceae) — most are safe, many edible |
| Round, hollow | Carrot family (Apiaceae) — CAUTION: includes hemlock and poison hemlock |
| Round, solid | Many families — not diagnostic alone |
| Milky white sap | Often toxic (euphorbias, some lettuce relatives). Exception: dandelion sap is safe |
| Clear watery sap | Generally safer, but not conclusive |
| Red or orange sap | Usually toxic (bloodroot, greater celandine) |

> **The Hollow-Stem Danger:** A round, hollow stem with umbrella-shaped flower clusters (umbels) is the signature of the carrot family (Apiaceae). This family contains both excellent edibles (wild carrot, fennel, parsley) and some of the most lethal plants on Earth (poison hemlock, water hemlock, fool’s parsley). Never eat any umbel-bearing plant unless absolutely certain.

### Step 5: Flower Examination

*   **5 petals, radially symmetric:** Rose family (often edible fruits).
*   **Irregular/bilateral (pea-shaped):** Legume family (beans, clover, vetch).
*   **Tiny flowers in flat-topped umbels:** Carrot family (proceed with extreme caution).
*   **Tubular, lipped flowers on square stems:** Mint family (generally safe).
*   **Composite flower heads:** Daisy/aster family (dandelion, chicory, sunflower) — mostly safe.

### Step 6: Fruit and Seed

*   **Aggregate fruits (raspberry/blackberry type):** Almost always safe worldwide.
*   **Blue/black berries:** More often edible than toxic, but exceptions exist (pokeweed berries, nightshade berries).
*   **White or yellow berries:** More often toxic than edible; treat with suspicion.
*   **Red berries:** Mixed—some edible (wintergreen, hawthorn), some deadly (bittersweet, yew). Requires positive identification.

### Step 7: Smell and Taste (Final Confirmation Only)

1. **Crush a leaf and smell it:** Look for distinctive, pleasant aromas (onion/garlic, minty, anise). Bitter or acrid smells are warning signs.
2. **Touch to lip:** Wait 5 minutes for tingling, burning, or numbness. If none, proceed.
3. **Touch to tongue:** Intensely bitter, soapy, or burning taste means stop immediately and spit.

### Rules That Save Lives

1. **Never eat anything you cannot positively identify.** "Probably safe" is insufficient.
2. **Never eat plants with milky or colored sap** unless the specific species is confirmed safe (e.g., dandelion).
3. **Never eat plants with an almond or bitter smell** in the leaves or bark (potential cyanogenic glycosides).
4. **Never eat white or yellow berries** without confirmed identification.
5. **Avoid all mushrooms** unless trained, as identification requires spore prints and gill examination.
6. **When in doubt, walk away.** Starvation takes weeks; poisoning can kill in hours.

### Building Your Local Knowledge

*   Map edible plants when found.
*   Track seasonal availability (phenology).
*   Start with easy, widespread plants (Dandelion, clover, cattail, plantain, wood sorrel).
*   Learn one new plant across its full lifecycle (seedling, mature, flowering, fruiting, dormant stages).

## Notes

*   The Universal Edibility Test (UET) takes 24+ hours per plant and tests only one part at a time, making visual identification faster and safer.
*   The key to successful identification is recognizing plant families rather than memorizing every species.
*   The definitive sequence is: habitat, growth form, leaves, stems, flowers, fruit, and *then* smell and taste.
*   The most critical survival takeaway is the rule: 'When in doubt, walk away,' due to the high speed of poisoning effects.

## Key Takeaways

*   Follow the identification sequence to catch errors.
*   Learn plant families to manage the knowledge load.
*   Memorize the critical look-alike pairs.
*   Adhere strictly to the 'Rules That Save Lives'.
