# Brine Strength

## Objective
To determine if a brine is strong enough to reliably kill or suppress all dangerous bacteria to preserve food without using a scale or measuring cups.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   A raw egg in its shell (or a suitable substitute like a peeled raw potato).
*   Salt and water to create the brine.
*   A raw egg to perform the definitive test.
*   For calibration without an egg: A raw potato (small, whole) is the best substitute.

## Material
*   Brine (salt dissolved in water).
*   Raw egg.
*   Raw potato (as a substitute).

## System
The system is based on the principle of specific gravity (density). The density of a raw egg (approx. 1.03-1.09) compared to fresh water (density of 1.00) indicates the concentration of salt in the solution.

## Technology
*   Egg Float Test: The definitive low-tech method for measuring brine concentration.
*   Salinometer (Brine Hydrometer): A glass float with a weighted bottom and graduated scale that provides a precise numerical reading.

## Tools
*   Raw egg.
*   Raw potato (substitute).
*   Salinometer (if available).

## Process
### The Egg Float Test
1. Mix your brine — dissolve salt into water until the concentration is believed to be adequate.
2. Drop a raw egg (uncracked, in its shell) into the brine.
3. Read the result to determine strength.

### Egg Float Test Results
| Salt Concentration | Density | Egg Behavior |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0% (fresh water) | 1.000 | Sinks to bottom, lies flat |
| 3-5% | 1.02-1.04 | Sinks slowly, may tilt |
| 8-10% | 1.06-1.07 | Hovers mid-water, barely rises |
| 10-12% | 1.07-1.09 | Floats with just the tip breaking the surface |
| 15% | 1.10 | Floats with a quarter-sized patch of shell above the surface |
| 20% | 1.15 | Floats high — a silver-dollar-sized area exposed |
| 26% (saturated) | 1.20 | Bobs on the surface like a cork |

### Calibration Without an Egg
If eggs are unavailable, substitutes provide approximate readings:
*   Raw potato (small, whole): 10-12%.
*   Apple (small, unpeeled): 5-8% (too buoyant for high concentrations).
*   Raw beet (peeled): 10-12%.
*   Dry hardwood chip (oak, maple): Floats in fresh water (useless).
*   Small stone or pebble: Never floats (useless).

### Taste Testing
Experienced salt curers can gauge strength by taste, which supplements the float test:
*   3-5%: Pleasantly salty (Too weak).
*   8-10%: Noticeably salty (Borderline).
*   15-18%: Aggressively salty (Good preservation range).
*   20%+ : Painfully salty (Heavy-duty brine).

### Adjusting Brine Strength
**Strengthening Weak Brine (Egg sinks or barely hovers):**
1. Add salt gradually, stirring after each addition until fully dissolved.
2. Re-test with the egg after each addition.
3. Warming the brine helps dissolve salt faster.

**Weakening Overly Strong Brine (Egg floats too high):**
1. Add water gradually, stirring between additions.
2. Re-test with the egg.
3. Save the displaced brine rather than discarding it.

### Monitoring During Curing
*   Brine weakens over time as meat releases moisture.
*   For cures lasting more than 5-7 days, re-test with the egg weekly.
*   If the egg drops lower than the initial test, add salt until the target float level is returned.
*   For long cures (2+ weeks), replace the brine entirely with fresh solution at the original concentration.

### Quick Reference: Brine Strength by Application
| Application | Target % | Egg Float Description | Salt per Gallon |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Vegetable fermentation | 3-5% | Egg sinks | 1/2 cup (4 oz) |
| Flavor brine (poultry) | 5-8% | Egg barely lifts off bottom | 3/4 cup (6 oz) |
| Minimum preservation | 10% | Egg hovers mid-water | 1.5 cups (12 oz) |
| Standard preservation | 15% | Egg floats, coin-sized exposure | 2.5 cups (20 oz) |
| Heavy preservation | 20% | Egg floats high | 3 cups (24 oz) |
| Saturated (barrel method) | 26% | Egg bobs like a cork | 3.5 cups (28 oz) |

## Key Takeaways
*   The egg float test is the most reliable low-tech method; a raw egg floating with a coin-sized patch exposed signifies approximately 15%, the standard preservation target.
*   Brine below 10% accelerates bacterial growth.
*   Re-test brine weekly during long cures because meat moisture dilutes the solution.
*   Taste-testing serves as a supplement, not a replacement.
*   A peeled raw potato approximates the egg's float concentration.

## Notes
- The egg float test is noted as a method used for centuries before laboratory instruments existed.
- The goal concentration for most preservation is explicitly stated as 10-20% salt by weight.
- When testing with an egg, use the freshest egg available to avoid falsely optimistic readings from aged eggs.
