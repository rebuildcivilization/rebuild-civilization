# Ambush Hunting

## Objective
Ambush hunting means choosing your position before the animal arrives and waiting for it to come to you — the lowest-energy active hunting method, with far higher success rates than stalking.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Scouting Time:** Scout your site 2–3 days in advance to confirm recent animal activity.
*   **Blind Construction Time:** Build your blind at least 24–48 hours before hunting so construction scent fades and animals accept the structure.
*   **Wind Knowledge:** Determine the prevailing wind direction and position the blind downwind of the expected animal position.
*   **Waiting Discipline:** The ability to remain still and minimize all movement during the wait.

## Material
*   Water sources (most reliable attractant).
*   Game Trail Funnels (landscape features forcing animals into narrow corridors).
*   Feeding Areas (fruiting trees, berry patches, nut-bearing trees, crop remnants).
*   Salt and Mineral Licks (natural mineral deposits attracting herbivores).
*   Terrain and Vegetation (for natural blinds and constructed blinds).

## System
The system relies on the predictability of animal behavior: every animal sleeps, eats, drinks, and travels between predictable locations on predictable routes. Success is achieved by inverting stalking—choosing the position, managing wind, setting up concealment, and becoming comfortable before the animal arrives, leading to a relaxed and unaware shot.

## Technology
*   **Hunting Method:** Ambush Hunting (lowest-energy active hunting).
*   **Wind Management:** Utilizing prevailing wind directions.
*   **Positioning:** Utilizing elevated blinds (tree stands) to disperse scent and gain better sightlines.

## Tools
*   **Weapon:** Spear or arrow (for broadside shots).
*   **Blinds:**
    *   Natural Blinds (terrain and vegetation).
    *   Constructed Blinds (cut branches, weaving material).
    *   Elevated Blinds (Tree Stands).
*   **Tracking Markers:** Broken branches or placed stones for blood trails.

## Process
### Preparation (Scouting & Positioning)
1.  **Scout:** Confirm recent animal activity and note which species visit, from which direction, and at what time, focusing on water sources.
2.  **Wind Check:** Determine the prevailing wind direction and prepare two blind positions if the wind is unpredictable.
3.  **Site Selection:** Identify sites with water sources, game trail funnels, feeding areas, or mineral licks.
4.  **Blind Construction:** Build the blind 24–48 hours early, ensuring it is positioned 10–25 meters from the kill zone, downwind of the expected approach.

### The Wait (Waiting Discipline)
*   Arrive at least 30 minutes before expected activity.
*   Do not eat strong-smelling food (no smoking, no cooking).
*   Minimize all movement (no fidgeting, scratching, or adjusting clothing).
*   Sit on something comfortable.
*   Keep the weapon ready, strung, and nocked.
*   Stay alert, listening for footsteps, twigs, or bird alarms.

### Taking the Shot
1.  **Wait for Settling:** Allow the animal to lower its head to drink or eat, indicating it is relaxed.
2.  **Position Weapon:** Move the weapon into shooting position during a moment when the animal’s head is down or turned away (slow, smooth movements).
3.  **Aiming:** Aim for the chest cavity, just behind the front shoulder (largest vital area). If quartering toward, aim for the near shoulder to angle the projectile into the chest cavity. If quartering away, aim behind the last rib.
4.  **Post-Shot Action:** Remain in the blind. Wait 15–30 minutes before blood-trailing.

### Blood Trailing After the Shot
| Blood Sign | Meaning | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Bright red, frothy blood | Lung hit — fatal | Wait 15 min, then follow |
| Dark red blood | Heart or liver hit — fatal | Wait 20 min, then follow |
| Greenish-tinged or foul-smelling | Gut hit — slow death | Wait 2-4 hours before following |
| Small drops, widely spaced | Superficial wound | May not be fatal; track carefully |

Follow blood on leaves, grass, and the ground. Mark each spot so the trail can be backtracked if it goes cold.

## Notes
*   **Core Philosophy:** Ambush hunting is 90% waiting, emphasizing patience over impulsive action.
*   **Building Timing:** The timing of blind construction is critical to prevent spooking animals.
*   **Energy Cost:** The energy cost of ambush hunting is negligible compared to stalking or persistence hunting.
*   **Common Mistakes Highlighted:** Choosing a site without scouting, building the blind on hunting day, facing the wrong direction, and pursuing a gut-shot animal immediately.
*   **Key Takeaway:** Water is the best ambush location because it concentrates game into small, predictable areas.
