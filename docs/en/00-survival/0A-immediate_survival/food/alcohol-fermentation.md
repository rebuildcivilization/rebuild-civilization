# Alcohol Fermentation | Survival Wiki

## Objective
Alcohol fermentation converts sugars into ethanol and carbon dioxide using wild yeast. The resulting alcohol acts as a preservative, a disinfectant, a solvent for herbal medicines, a trade commodity, and a morale booster.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Wild yeast: Present on the skins of fruits and grain husks; does the work.
- Sugars: Required input for the yeast to consume.
- Water: Necessary for the fermentation process.
- Grain/Fruit: Required for beer or fruit wine, respectively.

## Material
- Honey (for Mead).
- Fruit (grapes, berries, apples, pears, plums) (for Fruit Wine).
- Grain (barley, wheat, or any cereal grain) (for Beer).
- Water.
- Vessels: Clay pots, wooden barrels/buckets, gourds, glass jars.
- Optional Additives: Honey or sugar (to increase alcohol content) for Fruit Wine and Mead.
- Optional Additives: Hops, yarrow, mugwort, or other bitter herbs (for Beer).

## System
**The Science**
Yeast (a single-celled fungus) eats sugar and produces two waste products: ethanol (alcohol) and carbon dioxide (gas).

**Fermentation Stops When:**
1. All sugar is consumed (dry finish, higher alcohol).
2. Alcohol concentration reaches 14-18% and kills the yeast (sweet finish, maximum natural alcohol).

**Key Biological Fact:** Wild yeast exists on the skins of all fruits, on grain husks, and floating in the air. You do not need to add yeast.

**Beer Specific System (Mashing):**
1. Malting: Sprouting grain to activate enzymes (amylase) that convert starch to sugar.
2. Kilning: Drying the sprouted grain (malt) without overheating to halt sprouting and dry the grain.
3. Grinding: Crushing the dried malt coarsely.
4. Mashing: Mixing crushed malt with hot water (roughly 3 parts water to 1 part malt by volume) and holding at a target temperature (63-68C / 145-155F) for 60-90 minutes to convert starch to fermentable sugar.

## Technology
- **Yeast:** Wild yeast, naturally present.
- **Enzymes:** Amylase, activated during malting, responsible for starch-to-sugar conversion.
- **Temperature Control:** Critical for enzyme activity (Mashing: 63-68C/145-155F; Fermentation: 18-25C / 65-77F).
- **Airlock/Sealed Vessel:** Technology used to keep oxygen out while allowing CO2 to escape.

## Tools
- Crushing implements (flat rocks, log mortar).
- Heating source (for malting and mashing).
- Measuring tools (for volume/parts).

## Process
### Mead (Honey Wine) — Easiest Starting Point
1. Dissolve honey in warm (not boiling) water and stir thoroughly.
2. Pour into a non-reactive vessel, leaving 20% headspace.
3. Air-lock creation (for narrow necks): plug the opening with cloth/cork, insert a hollow reed or tube to allow CO2 out.
4. Place in a warm location (18-25C / 65-77F). Active fermentation begins within 24-72 hours.
5. Swirl or stir gently once daily for the first week to keep yeast in suspension.
6. After bubbling slows (1-3 weeks), stop stirring. Allow yeast and sediment to settle.
7. Racking: Carefully pour or siphon the clear liquid off the sediment into a clean container.
8. Age for at least 2 more months for dramatic improvement.

### Fruit Wine — Any Sugar Source
1. Crush fruit thoroughly (grapes, berries, apples, pears, plums). The skins are important as wild yeast lives there.
2. Place the crushed fruit (must) into a fermentation vessel.
3. Add water if the fruit is low in juice (no water needed for grapes).
4. Optionally add honey or sugar to increase alcohol content.
5. Cover and ferment at 18-25C (65-77F). Stir daily, pushing the fruit solids (cap) back down to prevent mold and extract flavor.
6. Strain out the fruit solids after 5-10 days of active fermentation.
7. Transfer the strained liquid, cover or airlock, and allow to ferment another 2-6 weeks.
8. Rack off the sediment and age at least 1 month.

### Beer — Grain-Based Alcohol
1. Malting: Soak grain in water (12-24 hours), drain, and keep moist for 3-5 days to sprout and activate enzymes.
2. Kilning: Dry the sprouted grain (malt) without overheating (temperatures above 70C / 160F destroy enzymes).
3. Grinding: Crush the dried malt coarsely.
4. Mashing: Mix crushed malt with hot water (3 parts water to 1 part malt by volume). Hold at 63-68C (145-155F) for 60-90 minutes to convert starch to fermentable sugar.
5. Lautering: Separate the sweet liquid (wort) from the grain husks, rinsing grains with warm water to extract remaining sugars (sparging).
6. Boiling: Boil the wort for 30-60 minutes to sterilize and concentrate sugars. Add hops/herbs during the boil for bitterness and antimicrobial properties.
7. Cooling: Cool the wort as quickly as possible to fermentation temperature (18-25C / 65-77F).
8. Fermentation: Transfer to a vessel, wait 1-2 weeks, then rack off sediment.
9. Beer is drinkable almost immediately after fermentation completes and is best consumed within 2-4 weeks.

## Troubleshooting
| Problem | Cause | Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| No fermentation after 3 days | Too cold, no viable yeast, too much sulfur on fruit | Move to warmer spot; add a handful of unwashed grape skins or raisins (yeast source) |
| Vinegar taste | Oxygen exposure allowed acetobacter to convert alcohol to acetic acid | Seal vessel better; use airlock. |
| Rotten egg smell | Hydrogen sulfide from stressed yeast | Stir vigorously to outgas; usually dissipates. Caused by nutrient deficiency or high temperatures |
| Fermentation stuck halfway | Temperature fluctuation or low nutrients | Warm gently; add a pinch of crushed grain or fruit for nutrients |
| Cloudy final product | Yeast still in suspension | Rack again; allow more settling time; cold temperatures help yeast drop |
| Mold on surface | Oxygen exposure, contamination | Skim off; transfer liquid to clean vessel; improve airlock |

## Storage and Shelf Life
| Beverage | Shelf Life (sealed) | Shelf Life (opened) | Improves with Age? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mead | 1-5+ years | Weeks | Yes, dramatically |
| Fruit wine | 6 months-3 years | 1-2 weeks | Yes |
| Cider | 3-12 months | 1 week | Slightly |
| Beer | 2-8 weeks | Days | No — drink fresh |

Store all fermented beverages in the coolest available location, sealed from air. Use corks, wax, or tight-fitting stoppers.

## Key Takeaways
- Wild yeast on fruit skins and grain husks does the work.
- Mead (honey + water) is the simplest starting beverage and produces the strongest natural alcohol.
- Beer requires malting grain first to convert starch to sugar — the most complex process.
- Keep oxygen out after initial fermentation starts; use an airlock or sealed vessel with gas release.
- Normal fermentation does not produce dangerous methanol — that risk only arises during distillation.

## Notes
- Methanol (wood alcohol) is toxic and can cause blindness or death, but it is NOT produced in dangerous quantities during normal fermentation of fruit, honey, or grain; the risk arises only during distillation.
- Beer does not improve much with age and is best consumed within 2-4 weeks.
- Vessels should be non-reactive (clay, wood, glass). Metals, especially copper and iron, should be avoided in prolonged contact as they can create off-flavors or toxic compounds.
