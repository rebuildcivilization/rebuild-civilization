# Fire Drying | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To dehydrate food.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   A sustained, low-heat fire.
*   Hardwood preferred (oak, hickory, maple, beech, fruitwoods) to prevent tar deposition.
*   Rack position must be between 60–90 cm above the fire.
*   Fire safety measures: Keep water or loose dirt nearby to control flare-ups; build the fire on cleared ground, away from dry grass or overhanging branches.

## Material
*   Food items: Meat, fish, vegetables, and fruit.
*   Fuel: Hardwood (pine, spruce, cedar produce bitter tar).
*   Optional: Light salt rub (2-3% by weight) for meat and fish to accelerate drying and add antimicrobial protection.

## System
Fire Drying utilizes radiant heat and warm airflow from a fire to dehydrate food faster than sun drying, making it the go-to method in humid climates, rainy seasons, or when speed is critical. The smoke adds antimicrobial compounds that further resist spoilage.

## Technology
*   Radiant heat and warm airflow from a fire.
*   Smoke adds antimicrobial compounds.

## Tools
*   Smoke Rack: Used to suspend food above a low fire.
    *   Simple Tripod Rack: Requires 3 poles (150-180 cm long), thinner cross-sticks (60-90 cm long), and cordage/vine.
    *   Parallel Rail Rack: Requires four forked sticks (forming a rectangle roughly 120 cm by 60 cm), two long rails, and cross-sticks.

## Process
1.  **Prepare the food:** Slice meat 3-6 mm thick against the grain. Remove all fat. Butterfly or fillet fish. Cut vegetables and fruit to uniform thin pieces.
2.  **Salt (Optional):** Apply a light salt rub.
3.  **Arrange on the rack:** Place food in a single layer, with 1-2 cm gaps between pieces, ensuring no overlapping.
4.  **Start the fire:** Build the fire directly beneath the rack, letting it burn down to a low, steady heat.
5.  **Turn regularly:** Flip the pieces every 1-2 hours so the side facing the fire dries faster.
6.  **Monitor:** Allow drying for 4-12 hours. Meat/fish often finishes in 4-6 hours; thicker pieces or whole small fish take 8-12 hours. Fruit/vegetables take 4-8 hours.
7.  **Test for doneness:** Meat should snap when bent; fruit should be leathery; vegetables should be brittle.

## Notes
*   **Fire Management:** The fire must be small and steady. If the fire is too hot (hand test is less than 2 seconds), lower the flame or raise the rack.
*   **Hardwood Rule:** Avoid resinous softwoods (pine, spruce, cedar) as they deposit bitter tar and contaminants.
*   **Smoke Addition:** To add smoke (for extra preservation benefits), place green hardwood chips or damp sawdust on the coal bed. Replenish every 30-60 minutes.
*   **Overnight Drying:** If the batch requires more than 8 hours, either maintain the fire through the night (banking the fire) or pause, wrap the food, and resume drying at first light. 
*   **Partial Drying Risk:** Partially dried food is in a danger zone; it must be finished drying or consumed within 12 hours of being partially dried.
*   **Common Mistakes:**
    *   Fire too hot: Fix by lowering the flame and raising the rack.
    *   Fire too smoky (softwood): Fix by switching to hardwood only.
    *   Slices too thick: Fix by slicing to 3-6 mm maximum.
    *   Pieces touching or overlapping: Fix by spacing pieces 1-2 cm apart.
    *   Leaving fat on meat: Fix by trimming all visible fat before drying.
*   **Time Comparison:** Fire drying achieves dehydration in 4–12 hours, compared to 2–5 days for sun drying.
*   **Fuel Estimation:** 4 hours requires one armload of split hardwood; 8 hours requires two armloads; 12 hours requires three armloads (one armload approx. 10-15 kg).
