# Field Dressing

## Objective
To remove the internal organs and open the body cavity to prevent bacterial contamination and facilitate cooling.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- A kill.

## Material
Game animals (medium game, small game, birds).

## System
The system involves managing the heat generated by the carcass and controlling bacterial multiplication through rapid processing and air circulation.

## Technology
- **Bacteria:** Begin multiplying the moment an animal dies.
- **Temperature Control:** Spoilage begins within 30 minutes in warm weather (above 15C / 60F), and within an hour in hot weather (above 30C / 85F).
- **Air Circulation:** Opening the body cavity allows the carcass to cool.

## Tools
| Tool | Purpose | Substitute |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sharp knife | Primary cutting tool | Sharp stone flake, broken glass, razor blade |
| Second blade or smaller knife | Detail work around organs | Sharpened bone splinter |
| Cordage (1-2 meters) | Tying off intestines, hanging carcass | Strips of bark, vine |
| Clean cloth or dry grass | Wiping body cavity | Moss, large leaves |
| Sturdy stick (30 cm) | Propping cavity open | Any rigid material |

## Process
### Medium Game (Deer-Sized)
1. **Confirm the Kill:** Approach carefully. Watch for breathing (rib movement) and eye response. Touch the eye with a stick to confirm life. Finish with a quick, deep cut across the throat (both carotid arteries) or a spear thrust behind the ear into the brain stem.
2. **Positioning:** Position the animal on its back on a slope, head uphill, to help drain blood and pull organs away from the chest cavity.
3. **Open the Body Cavity:** Make a small starting cut through the skin just below the sternum. Insert two fingers under the skin, place the knife blade between the fingers (edge up), and cut toward the pelvis in a single smooth stroke.
4. **Access Heart/Lungs:** Cut through the sternum if needed; cut or crack the breastbone to access the heart and lungs.
5. **Remove Organs:** Cut around the anus, freeing the rectum and tying it off with cordage. Reach into the chest cavity to cut through the windpipe (trachea) and esophagus. Cut the diaphragm along its attachment to the ribs. Pull the windpipe and esophagus downward and outward to remove the heart, lungs, and digestive tract.
6. **Kidneys/Liver:** Remove kidneys if desired. The liver should come out with the organ mass, but cut it free if necessary.
7. **Bladder:** Remove the bladder last, pinching the urethra closed and cutting below the fingers.
8. **Inspect and Clean:** Inspect the liver for health.

### Small Game (Rabbits, Squirrels)
1. **Skinning (Case Skinning):** Pinch the skin on the back (midsection) and make a small cut through the skin only. Insert fingers and pull the skin apart toward the head and tail.
2. **Joints:** Cut off the head and feet at the joints.
3. **Organ Removal:** Make a small slit in the belly, tip the animal head-down, and the organs will fall out with minimal effort.
4. **Cleaning:** Rinse with clean water if available; otherwise, wipe clean.

### Birds
1. **Feathers:** Pluck feathers while the bird is still warm, pulling in the direction of growth to avoid tearing the skin. Dip waterfowl in hot water (not boiling) for 30 seconds before plucking.
2. **Organ Removal:** Cut a small slit below the breastbone. Insert two fingers and scoop out internal organs.
3. **Gizzard:** The gizzard (muscular stomach) must be cut open, cleaned of grit and food contents, and its inner lining peeled off before eating.
4. **Crop:** Remove the crop (food storage pouch in the throat area) and discard its contents.

### After Field Dressing
**Cooling the Carcass:**
- Hang the carcass in shade with good airflow, off the ground (a tree branch 2 meters up is ideal).
- Elevate off the ground if hanging is not possible.
- In hot weather, place it in flowing cold water if processing is imminent.
- In cold weather (below 5C / 40F), the carcass can hang for 1-3 days for cold aging.

**Protecting from Insects and Predators:**
- Cover the body cavity opening with a cloth or grass mat to keep flies out.
- Hang the carcass high enough to prevent access by dogs, foxes, coyotes, and other scavengers.

**Disposal of Gut Pile:**
- Move discarded organs at least 100 meters away from the hunting position and camp. In bear country, increase distance to 300 meters minimum. Bury if practical.

### Inspect and Clean (Liver)
| Liver Appearance | Meaning | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Smooth, dark red, firm | Healthy | Safe to eat |
| White spots or cysts | Parasites (liver flukes, hydatid cysts) | Discard liver; cook all meat thoroughly |
| Pale, yellowish | Possible disease or malnutrition | Discard liver; cook meat thoroughly |
| Abscesses (pus-filled lumps) | Bacterial infection | Discard liver; cook meat thoroughly |
| Green-stained areas | Bile contamination (gall bladder ruptured) | Trim green areas; remainder is safe |

**What To Save Besides Meat:**
- Heart, liver, kidneys (Immediate high-nutrition food).
- Stomach/intestines (cleaned) for sausage casings, water containers.
- Bladder for water container (must be cleaned).
- Sinew for cordage, bowstrings, thread.
- Bones for tools, needles, fishhooks, soup stock.
- Fat (around kidneys, intestines) for cooking, lamp fuel, hide tanning.
- Hide for clothing, shelter, containers, cordage.
- Blood for food (blood sausage, thickener for stews).
- Brain for hide tanning.

## Key Takeaways
- **Start immediately after the kill.** Delay increases spoilage risk.
- **Finger-guide technique:** Two fingers under the abdominal wall, blade between them, edge up, prevents gut punctures.
- **Save the organs:** Heart, liver, and kidneys are the most nutrient-dense parts.
- **Cool the carcass as fast as possible** by hanging in shade with airflow and propping the cavity open.
- **Waste nothing.** Use every part of the animal for survival purposes.

Notes
- Knife Sharpness Is Critical: A dull blade forces harder pressure, increasing the risk of puncturing intestines or bladder. Sharpen the blade before starting.
- Water Usage: Do not wash the body cavity with water unless you can immediately hang and air-dry the carcass, as water accelerates bacterial growth.
- Speed vs. Technique: The entire process for a rabbit should take under 5 minutes with practice.
- Disposal Buffer: In bear country, the required distance for gut pile disposal is a minimum of 300 meters.
