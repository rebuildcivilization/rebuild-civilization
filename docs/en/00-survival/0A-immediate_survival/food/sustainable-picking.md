# Sustainable Picking

## Objective

The objective of sustainable picking is to ensure the long-term viability of food sources by practicing sustainable foraging principles, recognizing that these principles are a survival strategy, not an environmental luxury.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

*   Knowledge of the one-third rule and sustainable foraging principles.
*   Ability to map and divide foraging territory.
*   Group cooperation to prevent uninformed gathering by others.
*   Record-keeping skills to track productivity and phenology.

## Material

The material consists of edible plants gathered through sustainable picking. Species are categorized based on their resilience to harvest:

**High Resilience (Can Tolerate Up to 50% Harvest):**
*   Dandelion (Deep taproot survives, regrows from fragments).
*   Stinging nettle (Vigorous rhizome network, cutting stimulates growth).
*   Cattail (Massive rhizome systems).
*   Blackberry/Raspberry (Aggressive spreaders).
*   Plantain (Tough basal rosette that regrows rapidly).

**Moderate Resilience (Stick to One-Third):**
*   Wild garlic/ramps (Slow-growing from bulbs).
*   Fiddlehead ferns (Each fiddlehead is a developing frond).
*   Burdock (Biennial; digging the root in year one prevents seed production).
*   Wild carrot (Biennial; requires digging from dense patches).

**Low Resilience (Take Less Than One-Third):**
*   Mushrooms (The fruiting body does not harm the mycelium, but picking prevents spore dispersal).
*   Tree bark/cambium (Limit harvest to small patches on branches, not the trunk circumference).
*   Slow-growing perennial roots (Take only the largest specimens).
*   Rare or isolated populations (Take nothing until the population grows).

## System

The system is sustainable foraging and land management, focused on balancing immediate needs with long-term ecological health and food security in a post-collapse world where supply chains are absent.

## Technology

*   Mental or physical mapping of foraging territory.
*   Record-keeping (tracking productivity, species trends, phenology, and weather effects).

## Tools

*   The One-Third Rule (Foundational principle).
*   Area Rotation Schedule.
*   Species-Specific Guidelines.
*   Record Keeping methods (mental tracking or bark/stone scratches).

## Process

### The One-Third Rule
*   **Foundational Principle:** Never harvest more than one-third of any plant population or any individual plant’s harvestable parts.
*   **Practical Application:**
    *   From a patch of 30 dandelion plants, take from no more than 10.
    *   From a single large dandelion, take no more than one-third of its leaves.
    *   From a blackberry thicket, pick no more than one-third of the ripe berries.
    *   From a grove of oaks, collect no more than one-third of the acorns on the ground.

*   **Why One-Third?:** The fraction accounts for three parts:
    1.  One-third for you (your food supply).
    2.  One-third for wildlife (animals dependent on the same food).
    3.  One-third for the plant (reproduction, seed dispersal, and future growth).

*   **Harvest Rate Effects:**
    *   33% or less: Sustainable (Population stable or growing).
    *   34-50%: Still manageable (Slow decline over seasons).
    *   51-75%: Noticeable thinning (Significant decline within 1-2 years).
    *   76-100%: Maximum immediate yield (Population collapse, possible local extinction).

### Rotating Harvest Areas
*   **Step 1:** Map the territory and identify distinct productive plant patches.
*   **Step 2:** Divide each productive area into at least three sections.
*   **Step 3:** Harvest from only one section at a time, moving to the next section on the subsequent foraging trip.
*   **Step 4:** Allow the harvested section time to recover before returning to it.

### Protecting the Seed Bank
*   **Step 1 (Greens):** Leave some plants in every patch to flower and go to seed (mother plants).
*   **Step 2 (Seeds/Nuts):** Leave at least half of seeds on the ground or on the plant to become next year’s plants and feed wildlife.
*   **Step 3 (Spreading):** Actively spread seeds when eating berries or processing acorns to initiate deliberate planting.
*   **Step 4 (Biennials):** Identify first-year plants and leave them; only harvest second-year plants that have already set seed.

### Avoiding Damage Beyond the Harvest
*   **Soil Protection:**
    *   Stay on natural paths to prevent soil compression and erosion.
    *   Fill in holes when harvesting roots; do not dig more than needed.
    *   Avoid harvesting after heavy rain to prevent root networks from being torn up.
*   **Habitat Preservation:**
    *   Leave dead wood in place to support insects, fungi, and small animals.
    *   Do not clear vegetation around target plants (for shade, wind protection, and soil stability).
    *   Maintain water sources to ensure consistent water needs for plants like cattails and watercress.

### When to Break the Rules
*   **Immediate Starvation Risk:** Take what is needed NOW to survive the day.
*   **Evacuation:** Harvest fully if it ensures survival during travel.
*   **Invasive Species:** Harvest aggressively (e.g., garlic mustard) as taking 100% of them helps native food plants.
*   **Superabundance (Mast Years):** Safely take more than one-third when total production exceeds ecosystem needs; store the surplus.
*   **Rule of Thumb for Breaking Rules:** When breaking the rule, consciously plan to let the overharvested zone recover for at least one full growing season.

### Teaching Others
*   Teach the one-third rule and its rationale to the group.
*   Assign foraging zones to prevent overlap in harvesting.
*   Designate off-limits reserves to serve as seed sources and emergency food banks.

## Notes

*   The core philosophy is that sustainable picking is rooted in cold math and ecological observation, not environmental idealism.
*   The necessity of record-keeping is emphasized to transform the forager into a land manager with a reliable seasonal food calendar.
*   The critical distinction is made between immediate survival needs and long-term sustainability, with the one-third rule serving as the default sustainable baseline.
