# Pit Storage

## Objective
To preserve root vegetables for 3–6 months using the earth clamping method, utilizing the ground's stable temperature to slow bacterial growth without freezing the food.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- High ground with good natural drainage.
- Well-drained soil, ideally sandy loam.
- Shade is preferred.
- Accessibility in winter (ability to reach the pit through snow and mud).

## Material
- Food (root vegetables, cabbage, apples, etc.)
- Earth (for insulation and covering)
- Dry straw, bracken, or dried leaves (for insulation and bedding)
- Gravel (for lining the bottom in wet climates)
- Bark slabs, woven wattle, or flat stones (for lining the sides in wet climates)
- Flat stone or bark cap (for covering the straw chimney in rain)

## System
Pit Storage (Earth clamping) — burying food in an insulated pit and allowing the ground's stable temperature to maintain a consistent, cool environment.

## Technology
The principle relies on the ground temperature remaining stable year-round below roughly 18 inches (45 cm) of soil, typically 45-55°F (7-13°C) in temperate climates, which slows bacterial growth and enzymatic breakdown.

## Tools
- Digging stick.

## Process
### Basic Pit Clamp Method
1. **Choose Your Site:** Select high, well-drained ground and test drainage (dig a 12-inch hole, fill with water, wait 4 hours).
2. **Prepare the Ground:** Clear a rectangular area, dig out topsoil (6-8 inches deep).
3. **Prepare Insulation:** Lay down a 3-4 inch bed of dry straw, bracken, or dried leaves.
4. **Stack the Food:** Sort rigorously; cure produce for 2-3 days before clamping. Build a conical or ridge-shaped pile (base layer: hardiest items; top layer: smallest items).
5. **Insulate with Straw:** Cover the entire pile with a thick layer (at least 6-8 inches) of dry straw, tucking it firmly around the base.
6. **Cover with Earth:** Shovel excavated soil over the straw layer, applying at least 6-8 inches of soil. Pat the earth smooth.
7. **Final Touches:** Shape the soil surface to shed water, place a flat stone or bark cap over the straw chimney, and consider placing thorny branches around the base if rodents are a concern.

### Variations
- **Multiple Small Clamps vs. One Large Clamp:** Always build several small clamps (4-6 recommended), each holding 2-3 weeks' worth of food, to prevent the remaining stores from being exposed when one is opened.
- **In-Ground Pit Variation (Severe Winters):** Dig a pit 2-3 feet deep, line the bottom and sides with straw/leaves, stack produce, cover with a thick straw lid, and add a second straw layer on top in extremely cold climates.
- **Lined Pit for Wet Climates:** Line the bottom with 4-6 inches of coarse gravel for drainage and the sides with bark slabs or stones.

## Technology
- The earth provides insulation, creating a natural refrigerator that maintains consistent temperature and humidity (85-95%).

## Tools
- Digging stick.

## Process
### Accessing Stored Food
1. Open the clamp from one end only.
2. Dig away earth and pull back straw from one end.
3. Remove only what is needed for the next 5-7 days.
4. Repack straw tightly against the exposed food.
5. Replace earth and pack it down.

## Monitoring and Maintenance
**Weekly Checks:**
- Look for signs of animal digging.
- Check that the straw chimney is still open (frost can seal it).
- Look for sunken spots indicating collapse or rot.
- After heavy rain, verify drainage ditches are clear.

**Warning Signs of Failure:**
- Sweet, fermenting smell near the clamp.
- Visible mold on the straw chimney (indicates excessive moisture).
- Earth surface sinking or developing soft spots.
- Animal tracks or dig marks.

## Temperature Management by Climate
| Climate | Earth Cover Depth | Straw Thickness | Additional Measures |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Mild (rarely below 25°F / -4°C) | 6 inches | 6 inches | Basic clamp sufficient |
| Moderate (lows to 10°F / -12°C) | 8-10 inches | 8 inches | Double straw layer on windward side |
| Cold (lows to -10°F / -23°C) | 12 inches | 10 inches | In-ground pit with heavy mounding |
| Severe (below -20°F / -29°C) | 12+ inches | 12 inches | Deep pit, double insulation, snow cover as bonus insulation |

In severe climates, snow acts as additional insulation and should not be cleared from the clamps.

## Common Mistakes
- **Storing damaged produce:** One cut or bruise can destroy an entire clamp.
- **Insufficient drainage:** Water is the number one killer; must choose high ground and dig drainage channels.
- **One big clamp:** Exposes all stores when opened.
- **Skipping the curing step:** Fresh produce with skin wounds rots faster; cure for 2-3 days in shade.
- **Packing too tight:** Airflow between items is necessary.
- **Forgetting to mark it:** Use stakes or maps under snow.

## Key Takeaways
- Pit storage uses the ground’s stable temperature to preserve root vegetables for 3-6 months with zero technology.
- Choose high, well-drained ground and test drainage.
- Build several small clamps rather than one large one.
- The layering sequence is: straw base, stacked produce, straw insulation, packed earth, drainage ditch.
- A straw chimney must be left at the top for ventilation and checked weekly.
- Only store unblemished, cured produce.

--- 

## Notes
- Food that requires temperatures below 40°F (4°C) to be safe (meat, fish, dairy, cooked foods) is rarely preserved effectively by pit storage.
- Soft fruits (berries, stone fruits) should be avoided as they rot within days in pit conditions.
- A flooded pit is a total loss; all submerged food must be removed immediately.
- The specific lifespan for potatoes is 4-6 months, while carrots are 3-5 months.
