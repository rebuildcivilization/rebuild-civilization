# Container Selection: Choosing Safe Vessels

## Objective
To determine which vessels are safe for long-term water storage, understanding material safety hierarchies, identifying scavenged containers, crafting containers from natural materials, and ensuring containers are cleaned and prepared before use.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   A post-collapse scenario where containers must be scavenged or crafted from raw materials.
*   Knowledge of material safety and plastic recycling numbers.
*   Access to fire for processing or sanitization (for boiling water, pit-firing, or heat treatment).

## Material
### Material Safety Hierarchy
| Material | Safety Rating | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Glass | Excellent | Completely inert. Fragile. Heavy. Best for long-term storage. |
| Stainless steel | Excellent | Inert, durable, tolerates heat. Cannot see water level. |
| Fired ceramic/stoneware | Excellent | Inert if properly fired. Lead-glazed pottery is DANGEROUS. |
| Food-grade HDPE plastic (recycling #2) | Good | Safe at room temperature. Degrades in UV. Do not heat. |
| Food-grade PET plastic (#1) | Good | Standard water bottles. Single-use design; degrades with reuse over months. |
| Unglazed clay/earthenware | Good | Porous — provides evaporative cooling but allows slow seepage. |
| Wood (oak, cedar, birch) | Acceptable | Imparts flavor. Can harbor bacteria in grain. Better for short-term. |
| Copper | Acceptable | Antimicrobial properties. Acidic water leaches excess copper — limit storage to 8 hours. |
| Aluminum | Caution | Acidic water causes leaching. Lined aluminum (coated interior) is safer. |
| Galvanized steel (zinc-coated) | Caution | Zinc leaches into acidic water. Short-term transport only. |
| PVC plastic (#3) | Dangerous | Contains phthalates and can leach vinyl chloride. Never use for drinking water. |
| Polystyrene (#6) | Dangerous | Leaches styrene. Never use. |
| Unknown plastics | Dangerous | If the plastic type cannot be identified, do not use it for water storage. |
| Lead-glazed pottery | Lethal | Lead poisoning is cumulative and irreversible. |

### Plastic Identification
| Number | Plastic Type | Water Safe? | Common Items |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | PET/PETE | Yes | Water bottles, soda bottles |
| 2 | HDPE | Yes | Milk jugs, detergent bottles (if food-grade) |
| 3 | PVC | No | Pipes, some food wrap — never for water storage |
| 4 | LDPE | Yes (short-term) | Squeeze bottles, plastic bags |
| 5 | PP (Polypropylene) | Yes | Yogurt containers, bottle caps, some jugs |
| 6 | PS (Polystyrene) | No | Styrofoam cups, takeout containers |
| 7 | Other/Mixed | Unknown | Could contain BPA. Avoid unless labeled “BPA-free” |

### Chemical Container Rule
If a container ever held fuel, pesticides, solvents, paint, or industrial chemicals, it is permanently unsuitable for water because substances permeate plastic and bond to metal surfaces, and washing does not remove them.

### Ceramic Assessment
*   **Risk:** Lead glazes are the highest risk, especially bright orange, red, or yellow glazes.
*   **Safety:** White, brown, or unglazed interiors are generally safer.
*   **Test:** The Scratch Test: Gently scratch the interior glaze with a metal knife. Lead-based glazes are softer and scratch more easily than modern food-safe glazes.

### Natural Material Suitability
*   **Birch Bark Vessel:** Naturally waterproof, lightweight, and antimicrobial. Capacity: 2-5 liters. Duration: Weeks to months if kept moist.
*   **Hollowed Wood:** Sections of softwood (pine, poplar, basswood) are used. Limitation: Heavy, slow to make, and prone to cracking.
*   **Clay Pot (Unfired or Pit-Fired):** Excellent long-term solution. Unglazed pottery is porous (good for cooling, bad for sealed storage). For sealed storage, coat the interior with beeswax.

### Container Shape and Design Principles
| Feature | Good Design | Bad Design | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Opening | Narrow mouth | Wide mouth | Narrow openings limit contamination entry. |
| Body | Smooth interior | Rough, textured interior | Rough surfaces harbor biofilm. |
| Bottom | Flat, stable | Rounded | Flat containers do not tip and spill. |
| Material thickness | Uniform | Thin spots, cracks | Thin areas fail first and leak. |
| Seal | Tight-fitting lid or cap | Loose cover | Tight seals prevent insect and dust entry. |
| Opacity | Opaque or dark | Clear or translucent | Opaque containers block light and inhibit algae. |

## System
*   **Water Purification:** The container selection is a crucial part of water purification.
*   **Material Safety Hierarchy:** A system to assess material risk based on inertness and chemical leaching.
*   **Plastic Identification System:** Uses recycling numbers as the primary safety indicator.

## Technology
*   **Chemical Leaching:** The phenomenon where acidic water leaches compounds from materials like copper and galvanized steel.
*   **Biofilm Formation:** The process where biofilm forms on container interiors, requiring thorough cleaning.

## Tools
*   Metal knife (for the Scratch Test).
*   Bone awl or thorn (for punching holes during vessel crafting).
*   Stone or shell tool (for scraping charred material).
*   Sand, wood ash, or crushed charcoal (abrasives for cleaning).
*   Boiling water (best sanitizer).
*   Beeswax (for coating clay pots).

## Process
### Identifying Scavenged Containers
1.  **Plastic Identification:** Flip the container to look for the recycling triangle and check the number.
2.  **Smell the Interior:** Check for chemical odors, sweetness, fuel smell, or pungency.
3.  **Check Original Contents:** Determine if the original contents pose a risk (e.g., milk vs. bleach).
4.  **Inspect Plastic:** Check for cracks, discoloration, or cloudiness. Discard if the plastic crumbles or flakes.
5.  **Ceramic Assessment:** Check origin, glaze color (red/yellow/orange are highest risk), and perform the Scratch Test if uncertain.

### Crafting Containers from Natural Materials
*   **Birch Bark Vessel:** Harvest bark, fold into a box/cone, secure corners with root fibers/sinew, seal seams with pine pitch or spruce resin.
*   **Hollowed Wood:** Select softwood section, burn and scrape the interior to create a cavity (5-8 cm deep), and seal the interior by charring or coating with rendered fat/pitch.
*   **Clay Pot (Pit-Fired):** Harvest, knead, build using the coil method (1-1.5 cm wall thickness), dry slowly, and fire for 4-6 hours.

### Cleaning and Preparing Containers
1.  **Rinse:** Fill with clean water, shake vigorously, empty, and repeat three times.
2.  **Scrub:** Use sand, wood ash, or crushed charcoal to physically remove biofilm and residue.
3.  **Sanitize:** Fill with boiling water for 5 minutes, or use water with wood ash (mild disinfectant) for 30 minutes if heat is not tolerable.
4.  **Final Rinse:** Rinse with clean purified water.
5.  **Dry Inverted:** Turn the container upside down and allow it to air-dry completely before storage.

## Notes
*   The fundamental rule is that the container's history (what it held) often matters more than the material itself.
*   Narrow-mouth containers dramatically reduce contamination risk compared to wide-mouth buckets.
*   The Chemical Container Rule is absolute: containers holding chemicals are permanently unsuitable for water.
*   Opaque containers stored in shade last longer than clear containers in sunlight.
