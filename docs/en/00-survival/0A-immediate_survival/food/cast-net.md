# Cast Net

## Objective
To build and use a cast net.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Skill to throw.
- Ability to see fish or know their exact location.

## Material
- Cordage for mesh: 150–250 meters (490–820 feet) of thin, strong line.
- Perimeter weight: 2–4 kg (4.5–9 lbs) total of weights evenly distributed (e.g., stone chips, metal scraps, clay balls, or lead).
- Hand line: 5–8 meters of strong rope, at least 5 mm (3/16 inch) diameter.

## System
Cast nets are a circular weighted mesh system constructed with a 1.5–2.5 meter radius.

## Technology
- The net opens in the air, sinks over a school of fish, and traps them as the weights close the bottom.
- Even weight distribution around the perimeter is critical for the net to open fully.

## Tools
- Shuttle.
- Mesh gauge (for determining mesh size).

## Process
### Construction: Step by Step

**Phase 1: The Net Body**
1. Start at the center (horn). Tie a small metal ring, bone ring, or tightly wound cordage loop, about 3 cm (1.2 inches) in diameter.
2. Tie 8–12 starter lines to the ring, each about 15 cm (6 inches) long (radial foundation lines).
3. Begin netting in a circular pattern, tying sheet bend knots to connect starter lines to neighbors, working outward in concentric rings.
4. As each ring grows larger, add new meshes (increases): Add one new mesh for every 4–6 existing meshes in each ring to keep the net lying flat.
5. Continue weaving outward until the net radius reaches the target size (1.5–2.5 meters).

**Phase 2: The Lead Line**
6. Thread a strong line through the outermost row of mesh around the entire perimeter to serve as the lead line.
7. Attach weights evenly around the lead line based on the weight type:
    - Small stones: 30–50 g each, Every 8–12 cm (3–5 in).
    - Metal scraps: 20–40 g each, Every 8–12 cm.
    - Clay balls: 40–60 g each, Every 10–15 cm (4–6 in).
    - Lead strips: 15–25 g each, Every 5–8 cm (2–3 in).
    *Total perimeter weight should be 2–4 kg (4.5–9 lbs).*

**Phase 3: Brail Lines (Purse Closure)**
8. Cut 4–8 lengths of strong line, each as long as the net radius plus 30 cm.
9. Tie one end of each brail line to the lead line, evenly spaced around the perimeter. Run each brail up through the net body to the horn ring, passing it through the ring.
10. Gather all brail lines and tie them together just above the horn to close the net into a purse.

**Phase 4: Hand Line**
11. Tie the hand line to the gathered brail lines above the horn with a secure knot. The other end gets a wrist loop large enough to slide over the hand, allowing the user to release the net while keeping the hand line attached.

### How to Throw a Cast Net

**The Basic Two-Hand Throw**
1. Coil the hand line in the throwing hand.
2. Hold the net at the horn with the throwing hand, letting it hang straight down.
3. With the free hand, grab the lead line at the point nearest to you and hold it up to the throwing hand, creating a fold.
4. With the free hand, grab the lead line at about one-third of the way around the perimeter and hold this point at waist height.
5. Place the nearest section of lead line in the teeth (or over the throwing-side shoulder).
6. Rotate the upper body toward the target, loading the throw. Release the net from hands and teeth simultaneously.
7. Wait 2–3 seconds for the net to sink fully, then pull the hand line steadily.

**Common Throwing Mistakes**
- Net doesn’t open fully (practice simultaneous release).
- Net tangles in the air (untangle and re-fold before each throw).
- Net lands sideways or folded (throw with a smoother, flatter arc).
- Net hits the water too hard (throw softer with good rotation).

## Notes
- The critical factor for successful operation is the even weight distribution around the perimeter; uneven weights prevent the net from opening fully.
- Maintenance requires rinsing after saltwater use, drying fully before storing, checking weights regularly, and patching mesh tears with sheet bend knots.
- Cast nets are the fastest active fishing method, capable of catching 5–20 fish per throw in good conditions.
- Best used in water depths of 0.5–3 meters (1.5–10 feet) deep.
