# Roots and Tubers

## Objective
To find calorie-dense wild root vegetables for survival, as roots provide the concentrated starch and energy needed to sustain physical activity, unlike leafy greens.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   A digging stick (fire-hardened sharpened branch) is an essential foraging tool.
*   Positive identification of wild carrot using all distinguishing features is critical to avoid lethal look-alikes.

## Material
*   **Cattail (*Typha latifolia* and *T. angustifolia*):** Rhizomes are the primary calorie source, available year-round.
*   **Burdock (*Arctium lappa* and *A. minus*):** First-year roots are the edible part; second-year roots are woody and inedible.
*   **Wild Carrot / Queen Anne’s Lace (*Daucus carota*):** Edible but requires strict identification to distinguish from poison hemlock.

## System
Roots and tubers provide the calories that greens cannot, serving as the primary energy source for survival scenarios. The system relies on the fact that stored underground energy transfers directly to the consumer.

## Technology
*   **Energy Storage:** Plants store energy as concentrated starch in the underground roots.
*   **Survival Advantage:** Root systems remain alive and intact below the frost line, extending the foraging calendar through winter.

## Tools
| Tool | How to Make It | Best For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Digging stick** | Hardwood branch, 60-90 cm, one end sharpened and fire-hardened | General root digging in most soils |
| **Antler or bone pick** | Deer antler tine or large animal leg bone | Breaking up hard or rocky soil |
| **Flat rock** | Find a flat stone with a thin edge | Prying roots from banks and wet ground |
| **Knife** | Metal blade if available | Cutting through root systems, precision work |

## Process
### Cattail Processing (Rhizomes)
1.  Locate a cattail stand in shallow water or saturated mud.
2.  Grasp a cattail stalk firmly at the base and pull it out of the mud; the rhizome comes up attached.
3.  Follow the rhizome through the mud by hand, pulling up connected segments.
4.  Wash the mud off thoroughly. Peel the outer brown skin with a knife or thumbnail.
5.  Break or pound clean peeled rhizomes into small pieces.
6.  Place the crushed rhizome material in clean water, knead and squeeze to wash out starch.
7.  Remove the fibrous material.
8.  Let the water sit undisturbed for 2-4 hours; starch settles to the bottom.
9.  Carefully pour off the clear water and scrape up the wet starch for immediate use or drying to make flour.

### Burdock Processing
1.  Identify first-year plants (rosette of leaves, no flower stalk) and harvest in late autumn through early spring.
2.  Dig around the plant with a digging stick, loosening soil 15 cm out from the base.
3.  Work in a circle around the plant, loosening soil progressively deeper, exposing the root on all sides.
4.  Grasp the base firmly and pull straight up with steady pressure.
5.  Scrub the root clean and peel the outer brown skin.
6.  Preparation methods include boiling, roasting, or drying.

### Wild Carrot Identification (Crucial Step)
1.  Confirm identification using ALL features:
    *   Hairy, solid stem.
    *   No purple blotches.
    *   Root smells like carrot when broken.
    *   White, slender taproot.
    *   White umbel flower cluster with a dark purple/red flower in the center.

## Notes
*   **Cattail Warning:** Young cattail shoots can be confused with toxic wild iris (*Iris* spp.). Always wait for the flower head to appear before harvesting.
*   **Burdock Timing:** Only harvest first-year roots; second-year roots are woody and inedible.
*   **Wild Carrot Risk:** The distinction between wild carrot and poison hemlock is a critical survival skill; failure to confirm all features risks death.
*   **Storage Strategy:** The ground is the best natural refrigerator. Roots should be harvested as needed through winter rather than stockpiled above ground where they spoil faster.
*   **Nutritional Note:** Burdock root contains inulin (a prebiotic fiber) rather than primary starch, indicating that while caloric absorption may be lower, the gut health benefits are significant.
