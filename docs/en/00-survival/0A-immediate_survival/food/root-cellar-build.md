# Root Cellar Build

## Objective
To build an underground or partially underground structure that utilizes the earth’s stable temperature and natural humidity to store food for months without electricity. A well-constructed cellar can store enough food to sustain a family through an entire winter.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Site Selection:** The single most important decision; requires a north-facing hillside with good drainage and a low water table.
*   **Drainage Testing:** Dig a test hole 1 meter deep and fill with water; if it takes longer than 4 hours to drain, choose a different site.
*   **Wall Reinforcement:** Essential because unreinforced earth walls will eventually collapse.
*   **Roof Reinforcement:** Must support the weight of the earth covering (1,500–2,000 kg per cubic meter of soil).
*   **Ventilation Planning:** Necessary to prevent CO2 buildup, ethylene gas acceleration, and excessive moisture.

## Material
*   **Walls:** Dry-stacked stone, log cribbing, stacked sod blocks, wattle and daub, brick, or concrete blocks.
*   **Roof Structure:** Hardwood beams (15-20 cm / 6-8 inch diameter minimum), smaller poles or planks, bark slabs, or plastic sheeting.
*   **Floor/Drainage Layer:** Coarse gravel or crushed rock.
*   **Door:** Heavy timber for the frame; double-layer planks sandwiched with straw or dried leaves for insulation.
*   **Humidity Management:** Damp sand, wet burlap, water containers, dry charcoal, wood ash, or rock salt.
*   **Interior Layout:** Rot-resistant wood for shelving.

## System
### Temperature Stability
Earth insulation keeps the interior cool in summer and above freezing in winter. Target temperatures are 0-5°C (32-41F) in cold climates, and 10-15°C (50-59F) in temperate climates.

### Humidity Control
Target humidity for root vegetables is 85-95%. Too high causes mold and rot; too low causes vegetables to shrivel.

### Ventilation
**Two-Pipe System (Natural Convection Loop):**
1.  **Intake Pipe (Cold Air In):** Positioned low on a wall, near the floor.
2.  **Exhaust Pipe (Warm Air Out):** Positioned high, at or near the ceiling, on the opposite side of the cellar from the intake.

### Roof Load Calculation
One cubic meter of soil weighs approximately 1,500–2,000 kg (3,300–4,400 lbs). A cellar 2.5 m wide with 1 m of earth on top puts 4,000–5,000 kg per running meter on the roof structure.

## Technology
*   **Earth Insulation:** The earth provides stable temperature regulation.
*   **Natural Convection:** The ventilation system creates a loop where cold air enters low, warms up, rises, and exits high.
*   **Moisture Management:** Utilizing dry materials (charcoal, salt) to absorb moisture and controlling airflow to manage humidity.

## Tools
*   Marking out dimensions.
*   Digging.
*   Filling holes with water for drainage testing.
*   Building heavy timber frames.
*   Measuring.

## Process
### Site Selection
1.  Identify a north-facing hillside with good drainage.
2.  Confirm a low water table (more than 2 m / 6.5 ft below floor is ideal).
3.  Test drainage by filling a 1-meter hole with water and checking the drainage time.

### Excavation
1.  Mark cellar dimensions on the hillside.
2.  Excavate, keeping walls as vertical as possible. Remove all loose soil.
3.  Dig the floor 15-20 cm (6-8 inches) deeper than the finished floor level to allow for a gravel drainage layer.

### Wall Reinforcement
1.  Choose a reinforcement method based on materials (stone, logs, sod blocks, wattle and daub, brick/concrete).
2.  For stone or log walls, leave no gaps larger than 2 cm and pack them with smaller stones, clay, or moss.

### Floor Construction
1.  Lay 15 cm (6 inches) of coarse gravel or crushed rock as the drainage layer.
2.  Lay the walking surface on top (flat stones, planks, or compacted earth).
3.  Do not seal the floor; a dirt or gravel floor helps maintain humidity.

### Roof Structure
1.  **Option A (Log Beam Roof):** Lay heavy beams (15-20 cm diameter minimum) spaced 40-60 cm (16-24 inches) apart. Cover with a waterproof membrane, then top with 60-100 cm (2-3 feet) of packed earth and sod.
2.  **Option B (Stone Arch/Corbeled Roof):** Build walls that step inward or construct a true arch.

### Door Construction
1.  Build a door frame from heavy timber, fitted tightly.
2.  Construct a thick door, at minimum 10 cm (4 inches) thick, using double-layer planks sandwiched with straw or dried leaves for insulation.
3.  Consider a double-door entry (airlock) if severe winters are expected.

### Ventilation System Setup
1.  Install intake pipe low near the floor.
2.  Install exhaust pipe high at the ceiling on the opposite wall.
3.  Ensure the exhaust pipe extends above the roof line.

### Seasonal Management
*   **Summer:** Open both pipes fully for maximum airflow.
*   **Winter:** Partially close the intake pipe (using a cloth plug) or close almost entirely during extreme cold snaps to prevent freezing.
*   **Spring/Fall:** Moderate opening; adjust airflow based on temperature (increase if above 10°C/50°F, restrict if below 0°C/32°F).

### Interior Layout
1.  Install shelving along both side walls, keeping the lowest shelf 10 cm (4 inches) off the floor for air circulation.
2.  Maintain a central aisle wide enough for comfortable movement (60-80 cm / 24-32 inches).
3.  Store items by temperature preference:
    *   **Floor level (coldest):** Root vegetables in sand-filled crates, potatoes in darkness.
    *   **Middle shelves:** Canned goods, fermented vegetables, cured meats.
    *   **Upper shelves (warmest):** Squash, onions, garlic, dried goods.
4.  Separate ethylene-producing fruits (apples, pears, tomatoes) from ethylene-sensitive vegetables (potatoes, carrots, cabbage).
5.  Store root vegetables in containers of damp sand or sawdust to maintain humidity and prevent contact rot.

### Maintenance Schedule
*   **Weekly:** Check for spoiled items, monitor temperature extremes, check humidity (condensation/shriveling).
*   **Monthly:** Adjust ventilation for the season, inspect walls for water seepage.
*   **Seasonally:** Clean empty shelves, sweep the floor.
*   **Annually:** Check the roof for settling or leaks.
*   **As Needed:** Replace rotting wood shelves or beams.

## Notes
*   The door is identified as the weakest thermal point in the design.
*   The key takeaway emphasizes that site selection dictates success, followed closely by the necessity of wall reinforcement and roof overbuilding.
*   The maintenance schedule requires weekly inspection of perishable items and humidity levels.
*   The requirement to separate ethylene producers from sensitive vegetables is crucial for spoilage prevention.
