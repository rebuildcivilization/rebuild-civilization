# Hanging Systems

## Objective
To hang meat in a smoking chamber rather than laying it flat, ensuring maximum surface area is exposed to airflow and smoke simultaneously while preventing moisture from pooling.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Framework:** A crossbar structure must be installed inside the chamber before hanging.
*   **Load Testing:** Before hanging food, each crossbar must be tested to ensure it supports the equivalent weight without sagging (test: hang a sack of rocks weighing 20 lbs from the center of each bar; if it bends more than 1/2 inch, reinforce or replace).

## Material
*   **Crossbars:** Metal rods, rebar, or thick metal pipe (at least 1/2-inch diameter), or hardwood poles (oak, hickory, or maple at least 1.5 inches thick).
*   **Hooks/Tethers:** Fence wire, coat hangers, heavy gauge wire, rebar, bent rebar, forged metal, wire loops, natural fiber twine (cotton, hemp, jute), rawhide strips, green bark strips (willow, basswood, or elm inner bark).
*   **Meat Treatment:** Connective tissue or tendon for hooking; meat itself for the load.

## System
### The Smoking Chamber
*   The chamber must allow for airflow and smoke penetration.
*   **Spacing Rules:**
    *   Between pieces: 4-6 inches minimum (more for large cuts).
    *   From walls: 3-4 inches minimum.
    *   From ceiling: 6-8 inches minimum (exhaust zone).

### Support Structure
*   **Crossbars:** Must span the full width of the chamber, resting in notches or brackets on opposite walls, spaced 10–12 inches apart vertically.

### Hook Types
| Hook Style | Material | Best For | Capacity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| S-hook | Fence wire, coat hangers | Whole cuts, sausages | 5-10 lbs per hook |
| Meat hook (J-hook) | Heavy gauge wire, rebar | Large joints, whole legs | 15-25 lbs |
| Gambrel hook | Bent rebar, forged metal | Whole small animals, ribs | 20-40 lbs |
| Wire loop | Any wire | Fish, small strips | 1-3 lbs |

### Hanging Techniques
*   **Whole Muscle Cuts (Legs, Shoulders, Loins):** Pierce through the thickest end with an S-hook or meat hook; hook through connective tissue or tendon, not muscle; hang with the thickest part up so gravity pulls moisture downward; maintain 4-6 inches of clearance on all sides.
*   **Strips and Jerky:** Cut strips 1/4 to 3/8 inch thick, 1-2 inches wide, up to 12 inches long; drape directly over crossbars, centered; space strips 1-2 inches apart.
*   **Sausages and Casings:** Tie sausage links in pairs with twine, 12-16 inches of twine between pairs; drape twine over the crossbar so one link hangs on each side; leave 3 inches minimum between sausages; prick casings with a needle in 3-4 spots.
*   **Fish:** Small fish (under 2 lbs) threaded vertically through gill openings; large fish butterfly (split along the backbone), spread with wooden skewers, hung from the tail end.
*   **Ribs and Flat Cuts:** Thread wire or hooks through between the bones at one end; hang vertically so smoke flows across flat surfaces; for boneless cuts, use two hooks through the top edge, spaced 4-6 inches apart.

### Rope and Twine Methods
*   **Natural Fiber Twine:** Works at hot-smoking temperatures but may char over 200°F.
*   **Rawhide Strips:** Soak in water, tie around meat, hang from the crossbar; tightens as it dries.
*   **Green Bark Strips:** Used as lashing; flexible when fresh, tightens as it dries.
*   **Synthetic Rope Warning:** Never use nylon, polyester, or polypropylene rope as they melt at smoking temperatures, releasing toxic fumes.

### Rotation and Monitoring
*   **Rotation:** Meat must be rotated every 6-8 hours to move pieces from edges to center and vice versa.
*   **Position Swapping:** Swap vertical positions halfway through the smoking session.
*   **Monitoring:** Check pieces for wet spots, discoloration, or off smells during rotation, repositioning pieces to better airflow if necessary.

## Technology
*   **Physics of Smoking:** Utilizing airflow and smoke to expose all surfaces simultaneously to achieve even curing or drying.
*   **Gravity Assistance:** Using gravity to assist in fat and moisture dripping away from the meat.

## Tools
*   S-hooks, Meat hooks (J-hooks), Gambrel hooks, Wire loops.
*   Measuring tools for ensuring spacing (4-6 inches, 3-4 inches, 6-8 inches).
*   Sack of rocks for load testing.

## Process
1.  **Build Framework:** Install crossbars spanning the full width of the chamber.
2.  **Test Load:** Verify that each crossbar can support the required weight (15-20 lbs). Use hardwood or metal for crossbars.
3.  **Prepare Meat:** Select appropriate cutting techniques based on the meat type (Whole Cuts, Strips, Sausages, Fish, Ribs).
4.  **Hang Meat:** Use the appropriate hook type to secure the meat, ensuring the thickest part hangs up, and maintaining required spacing.
5.  **Monitor & Rotate:** Rotate pieces every 6-8 hours and check for moisture or discoloration.

## Notes
*   The primary distinction between hanging and laying meat flat is the exposure of all surfaces to airflow and smoke, contrasting with flat surfaces that trap moisture.
*   Hardwood or metal is preferred for crossbars, as softwood (pine, spruce, cedar, fir) risks warping or releasing resin.
*   The decision to use natural fibers over synthetic rope is critical to avoid toxic fumes.
*   The goal is maximum surface area exposed to smoke and minimum contact with other elements.
