# Bird Behavior | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To use birds as weather indicators by observing flight altitude, roosting patterns, and species-specific signals to predict or gauge atmospheric conditions.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Learning local species.
- Establishing baseline behavior (normal flight altitude, singing time, roosting time).

## Material
- Birds (specifically insectivores like swallows, flycatchers, warblers).
- Atmospheric conditions (air pressure, humidity, wind shifts, temperature changes).
- Specific bird behaviors (flight patterns, roosting times, noise levels).

## System
Birds serve as living barometers due to their survival depending on flight, which is directly affected by atmospheric conditions. They possess hollow bones and air sac respiratory systems that act as living barometers.

## Technology
- Flight Mechanics:
    - Low-flying birds indicate falling pressure and deteriorating weather.
    - High-flying birds (raptors, swifts) indicate stable or rising pressure (thermals) and fair weather.
- Species-Specific Signals:
    - Swallows/Swifts:
        - Skimming water/grass tops indicates storm approaching (6-18 hours).
        - Screaming at rooftop level indicates fast-changing conditions.
    - Seabirds:
        - Heading inland signals approaching storm.
        - Gulls facing wind indicates high winds approaching.
        - Petrels/shearwaters near shore signal severe storm within 24-48 hours.
    - Roosters/Poultry:
        - Crowing at unusual hours is noted as a storm predictor.
        - Chickens refusing to leave the coop can indicate pressure changes.

## Tools
- Practical Observation System:
    1. Learn local species (insectivores are key pressure gauges).
    2. Establish baseline behavior.
    3. Watch for convergence (multiple species changing behavior simultaneously).
    4. Note the timeline (behavior changes typically precede weather by 6-24 hours).
    5. Record and verify observations.

## Process
1. Learn local species and establish baseline behavior.
2. Observe deviations from the baseline (e.g., flight altitude, singing time, roosting time).
3. Watch for convergence of multiple bird species changing behavior simultaneously.
4. Note the timeline of behavior changes.
5. Record observations and verify against actual weather.

## Notes
- The reliability of swallow/swift indicators is high enough that the folk saying “swallows fly high, weather is dry; swallows fly low, rain will show” has genuine meteorological backing.
- Geese flying in V-formation indicates stable conditions.
- Geese grounded when they should be flying signals a weather system they wish to avoid, potentially severe weather 24-48 hours out.
- Roosters crowing at unusual hours is correlated with storm prediction, though the mechanism is debated.
- Ducks and geese becoming unusually noisy and active often precedes rain, though this has moderate reliability.
- The core principle linking bird behavior to weather is that air pressure, humidity, wind shifts, and temperature changes alter how birds fly, feed, and behave.
