# Notch Carving

## Objective
To carve reliable trigger notches that must both hold firm under load and release easily when disturbed.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Material:** Dry hardwood (ash, oak, maple, or hickory).
*   **Tool Requirement:** A sharp cutting edge is the only absolute requirement.
*   **Testing:** Always test the trigger under actual deadfall weight, not just unloaded.

## Material
**Best choice for durability:** Ash, oak, maple, or hickory.

**Wood Species Behavior Summary:**
*   **Oak:** Straight, hard; excellent durability, notches stay crisp for weeks.
*   **Ash:** Straight, medium; clean cuts, moderate effort, very good durability.
*   **Maple:** Tight, hard; requires sharp blade, clean surfaces, excellent durability.
*   **Hickory:** Stringy, tough; excellent once cut, outstanding durability.
*   **Beech:** Fine, even; easy to carve precisely, good durability.
*   **Birch:** Fine, medium; carves well, moderate durability (requires recarving after heavy rain).
*   **Willow:** Soft, straight; poor durability (notches deform within days).
*   **Pine:** Soft, resinous; poor durability (compresses and weeps sap).
*   **Poplar:** Soft, even; poor durability (too soft for sustained load).

## System
The system involves carving precise engagement surfaces (notches) for deadfall triggers, which are a system of interlocking cuts that must satisfy two contradictory requirements: holding firm under weight and releasing on command.

## Technology
**Wood Grain Principles:**
1.  **Load-bearing surfaces:** Must be cut across the grain to create flat, stable shelves that don't slide. Cross-grain fibers act like columns.
2.  **Trigger-release surfaces:** Must be cut at an angle to the grain to create a surface that can slide free when pushed from the correct direction (like a ramp).
3.  **Splitting Prevention:** Never leave long-grain splits on load-bearing notches; back-cut across the grain to terminate splits.

**Notch Depth Guideline:**
*   Load-bearing notches need half the host stick’s diameter depth for stability.
*   Trigger notches need 2-5 mm depth for sensitivity.

**Chisel Angle Sensitivity:**
*   90 degrees (flat, square end): Low sensitivity (requires strong pull).
*   60 degrees: Medium sensitivity (standard trapping).
*   45 degrees: High sensitivity (light touch releases).
*   30 degrees (very acute): Very high sensitivity (minimal contact).

## Tools
**Minimum Requirement:
*   A sharp cutting edge.

**Recommended Tools and Quality:
| Tool | Quality for Notch Work | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Fixed-blade knife | Excellent | Best control for precision cuts |
| Folding knife | Good | Lock-back preferred to prevent closure on fingers |
| Sharp stone flake | Adequate | Obsidian and flint produce razor edges; less control |
| Broken glass | Adequate | Wrap the grip end in cloth; very sharp but fragile |
| Metal file or rasp | Specialized | Excellent for fine-tuning notch depth after initial cut |
| Axe or hatchet | Poor | Too coarse for trigger notches; use only for rough shaping |

**Blade Safety Rules:**
1.  Always cut away from your body and hands.
2.  Brace the workpiece against a solid surface (log, rock, thigh with leather protection).
3.  Never hold a small stick in one hand while cutting toward it with the other.
4.  Keep the blade sharp.

**Measurement Reference (Body References):
*   Thumbnail thickness: 1.5-2 mm
*   Matchstick diameter: 2-3 mm
*   Pencil lead: 0.5-0.7 mm
*   Penny / small coin thickness: 1.5 mm
*   Credit card thickness: 0.8 mm

**Typical Notch Depth Range:** 2-5 mm.

## Process
**Carving Steps:
1.  **Type 1: Square Notch (Load-Bearing):**
    *   Mark position (2-3 cm from the top for the upright).
    *   Make two parallel cuts across the grain, spaced equal to the diameter of the stick that will rest in the notch.
    *   Remove wood between cuts by slicing horizontally.
    *   Clean the bottom flat.
    *   *Target Dimensions:* Width equal to mating stick diameter; Depth equal to half the host stick diameter.
2.  **Type 2: Chisel Point (Engagement Surface):**
    *   Carve the end of the bait stick to a wedge shape (flat-head screwdriver tip).
    *   Determine chisel angle based on desired sensitivity (90° low, 60° medium, 45° high, 30° very high).
    *   For the diagonal’s tip (weight support), carve a broader chisel with a roughened surface and score light cross-hatches.
3.  **Type 3: V-Notch (Toggle Engagement):**
    *   Cut a single V-shaped groove (3-5 mm deep, 5-8 mm wide at the opening).
    *   Angle determines release direction (Symmetrical V releases equally; Asymmetrical V releases preferentially from the shallow side).
4.  **Type 4: Shoulder Notch (Step Cut):**
    *   Cut a single cross-grain cut (one-third of the diameter deep).
    *   Carve inward from the end to remove a wedge, creating an L-shaped step or shoulder.
5.  **Fitting and Adjustment:**
    *   **Test Fit Without Load:** Check for rotation or seating issues. 
        *   *Stick rotates in notch:* Notch too wide. Shim or carve new notch.
        *   *Stick won’t seat:* Notch too narrow. Widen by 0.5 mm increments.
        *   *Joint holds but feels loose:* Acceptable for trigger notches; tighten for load-bearing notches.
    *   **Adjust Sensitivity Under Load:**
        *   *Too stiff (weight doesn’t fall):* Notch engagement is too deep. Shave the chisel point by 0.5-1 mm.
        *   *Too loose (falls without contact):* Notch engagement is too shallow. Deepen by 0.5 mm or cut a new chisel at a steeper angle.
        *   *Inconsistent:* Smooth the engagement surface until contact is uniform.
6.  **Preserving Notch Integrity:**
    *   **Weather Protection:** Carry sticks under shelter; coat engagement surfaces lightly with animal fat, pine resin, or wax to slow moisture absorption.
    *   **Spare Triggers:** Carve spare sets during downtime and keep them dry (carry 3-5 pre-carved figure-4 trigger sets).

## Notes
*   **Common Mistakes:**
    *   Cutting too deep on the first pass; always start shallow (1 mm adjustment is critical).
    *   Ignoring grain direction (using grain on a load-bearing surface causes slippage).
    *   Using dull blades (leading to rough, unpredictable engagement surfaces).
    *   Making all notches the same depth (load-bearing vs. trigger notches require different depths).
    *   Not testing under actual load.
*   **Key Takeaways Summary:**
    *   Notch depth dictates function (load-bearing needs half-diameter depth; trigger notches need 2-5 mm).
    *   Use dry, dead hardwood (oak, ash, maple, hickory).
    *   Cut load-bearing surfaces across the grain; cut trigger surfaces at an angle.
    *   Start shallow and adjust in 0.5 mm increments for sensitivity.
    *   Carry pre-carved spares for efficiency.
