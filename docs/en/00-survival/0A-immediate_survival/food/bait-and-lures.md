# Bait and Lures

## Objective
To explain the principles of effective fishing bait and lure techniques for survival fishing.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Knowledge of what fish eat and how to present the bait to the fish.

## Material
### Live Bait
*   Worms and Grubs (most universally effective freshwater bait).
*   Insects (Grasshoppers, Crickets, Mayfly/caddisfly larvae, Ants, Beetles, Caterpillars).
*   Minnows and Small Fish (best bait for large fish).
*   Other Live Bait (Crayfish, Frogs, Leeches, Snails/slugs).
*   Fish flesh (for Cut Bait).
*   Organ Meat (Liver, heart, intestines, for catfish bait).

### Prepared Bait
*   Cut Bait (fish flesh strips).
*   Dough Bait (mixture of flour/grain, scent attractant, formed balls).
*   Organ Meat (cut into small cubes).

### Artificial Lures (Improvised)
*   Feather Jig (using a feather to mimic minnows or insects).
*   Spinner (cut metal shape to create flash and vibration).
*   Fly (Dry or Wet) (using hair, fur, or fiber with a wing shape).
*   Bone or Shell Spoon (carved shape for wobble and reflection).

## System
The fundamental principle is that the most effective bait is something that already lives in or near the water being fished, as fish eat what they are accustomed to.

## Technology
The system relies on two primary triggers for a strike: scent (for fish that feed by scent) and movement/flash (for predatory species that strike at movement).

## Tools
*   Hooks (the basic tool).
*   Sticks (used for creating vibrations to surface worms).
*   Containers (for keeping insects alive).
*   Cloth/Netting (for cloth traps and hand seining).
*   Metal (for spinner material).
*   Bone, Shell, or Wood (for spoon material).

## Process
### Live Bait Process
1.  **Finding Bait:** Turn over rocks in shallows, inspect overhanging vegetation, and watch what falls onto the water surface.
2.  **Worm Collection:** Dig in moist soil or use stick vibration to encourage worms to surface.
3.  **Insect Collection:** Gather insects during peak activity or mating flights.
4.  **Minnow Catching:** Employ cloth traps, bottle traps, or hand seining.
5.  **Hooking Technique:** Hook minnows through the back (still water) or through the lips (current fishing).

### Prepared Bait Process
1.  **Cut Bait:** Cut strips (1 inch wide, 2-3 inches long) from fish belly or side.
2.  **Dough Bait:** Mix flour/grain with water and scent attractants, form into pea-sized balls.
3.  **Organ Meat Prep:** Cut liver, heart, and intestines into small cubes (1/2 inch).

### Improvised Lure Process
1.  **Feather Jig:** Lash a feather to the hook shank, wrap with thread, secure with resin, and work it with short twitches.
2.  **Spinner:** Cut metal shape, drill a hole, thread line, and attach to the hook.
3.  **Fly Presentation:** Wrap fiber/hair, add a wing shape, and present on the surface (dry fly) or just below (wet fly).
4.  **Spoon Presentation:** Carve a spoon shape, drill a hole, attach the hook, and pull steadily to create wobble.

## Notes
*   **Universal Rule:** Live bait from the same water always outperforms other bait.
*   **Movement is Key:** Keep live bait alive and moving; dead bait catches significantly less.
*   **Bait Change Frequency:** Change bait every 20-30 minutes, especially for live bait, to maintain scent and movement.
*   **Scent Carrier:** When using scent-based bait, position the angler downstream of where the scent will travel, as the current carries the attractant.
*   **Safety:** Handle all bait with clean hands to mitigate infection risk from contaminated cuts.
