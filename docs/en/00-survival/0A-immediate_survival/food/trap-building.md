# Trap Building

## Objective
To construct traps that catch food, utilizing a system approach rather than relying on individual devices. The objective is to achieve a guaranteed catch per day through strategic placement and trap density.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Time and Patience:** Trapping requires patience, as traps are force multipliers that hunt 24 hours a day while the trapper works.
*   **Skill:** Understanding that trapping is a system, not an individual device, and the ability to match trap type to terrain.
*   **Materials:** Availability of natural materials or salvaged materials.

## Material
### Natural Materials
| Material | Use | Where to Find | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Green saplings (thumb-thick) | Spring poles, trigger sticks | Forest edges, stream banks | Must be alive and flexible |
| Dead hardwood sticks | Trigger assemblies, stakes | Forest floor, standing dead trees | Must be dry and rigid, not rotten |
| Flat stones (5-30 kg) | Deadfall weights | Stream beds, rocky outcrops | Flat bottom essential for clean kills |
| Natural cordage fiber | Snare loops, lashings | Bark (basswood, cedar), roots, sinew | |
| Thorny branches | Funneling, barriers | Hawthorn, blackberry, rose thickets | Handle with thick gloves or wrapped hands |

### Salvaged Materials
*   **Wire (any gauge):** Superior to cordage for snares; does not stretch and holds loop shape.
*   **Nylon line or fishing line:** Strong, weather-resistant, nearly invisible; excellent for trigger lines and snares on small game.
*   **Metal cans:** Cut and bent into trigger plates for deadfalls.
*   **Springs:** Add tension to spring snares (from pens, appliances, vehicles).
*   **Cable ties:** Fast temporary lashings for trigger assemblies.

## System
Trapping is a system where the combination of material selection, construction quality, placement, camouflage, baiting, and maintenance determines success. A perfectly built trap in the wrong location catches nothing.

### Core Trap Categories
1.  **Snares:** Use a loop that tightens around an animal’s neck or body. Highest priority due to speed and effectiveness.
2.  **Deadfalls:** Use gravity, where a heavy weight held up by a trigger mechanism collapses when disturbed.
3.  **Spring Snares:** Combine a snare with a bent sapling that snaps upright when triggered, lifting the catch off the ground.
4.  **Pit Traps:** Require significant labor for larger game, digging a pit 1-1.5 meters deep.

### Trap Line Management
*   **Circuit Establishment:** Establish a walking loop that connects all traps and returns to camp. A good circuit should cover 1-3 km, include 15-25 traps of mixed types, and pass through varied habitats.
*   **Maintenance Checks:** Check the line twice daily (dawn and dusk) to prevent predators from stealing catches, suffering, spoilage, and scavenging.

### Catch Rate Expectations
| Experience Level | Expected Daily Catch Rate (per trap) | Traps Needed for 1 Catch/Day |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Beginner (first week) | 2-5% | 20-50 |
| Intermediate (after 2-4 weeks) | 5-10% | 10-20 |
| Experienced | 10-20% | 5-10 |

### Seasonal Adjustments
*   **Spring:** Bait less effective; focus on trail snares.
*   **Summer:** Check traps 3x daily; process catch immediately.
*   **Autumn:** Best season; bait with nuts, berries, or late fruit.
*   **Winter:** Snow tracks make placement easy; bait highly effective.

## Process
### Trap Construction
1.  **Snares:** Cut wire/cordage (60-90 cm for rabbit). Form a fixed loop. Pass the free end through to create a running noose.
2.  **Deadfalls:** Determine if a heavy weight and trigger mechanism are appropriate.
3.  **Spring Snares:** Find a flexible sapling. Attach the snare line. Build a trigger mechanism (simple peg trigger or notched trigger). Test by pulling gently.
4.  **Pit Traps:** Dig pit 1-1.5 meters deep and slightly wider than the target, optionally add sharpened stakes.

### Construction Quality Standards (Mandatory Checks)
1.  **Testing:** Apply expected force, verify the trigger releases cleanly, confirm the killing mechanism works.
2.  **Scent Elimination:** Rub components with local soil, crushed leaves, or mud. Wear gloves. Approach trap sites from downwind.
3.  **Camouflage:** Bury stakes/anchor points. Cover triggers with leaf litter. Break up straight lines and right angles.

### Trap Line Setup
1.  Establish the circuit (1-3 km). Ensure 15-25 traps of mixed types are included.
2.  Verify the line is set across varied habitats.

## Notes
*   **Force Multiplier:** Traps are force multipliers, hunting 24 hours a day.
*   **Critical Failure Point:** The number one failure is building too few traps; twenty traps is a strategy.
*   **Location Over Construction:** Perfect construction in the wrong location is worse than a mediocre trap in the right spot.
*   **Contamination:** Food odor on hands transfers to every component; build and set traps before eating.
*   **Critical Pit Trap Warning:** Pit traps with stakes are lethal to any animal or person that falls in. Mark locations clearly.
*   **Key Takeaway:** If nothing is caught after 3 days, the problem is location; move traps to better sign areas.
