# Trapping Methods

## Objective
Passive fish capture techniques are designed to work while the survivor sleeps, freeing time for other survival priorities.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Site Selection:** Choosing the right location is more important than trap construction quality. Ideal features include narrow points in streams, confluences, shaded banks, downstream locations of obstacles, and tidal channels.
*   **Core Principle:** Exploiting fish behaviors (following current, seeking shelter, chasing food) by making it easy for fish to enter and difficult for them to leave.
*   **Maintenance:** Frequent checking (at least twice daily) to prevent spoilage, as trapped fish die quickly in warm water if overcrowded.
*   **Long-Term Goal (Legal/Ethical):** Establishing rotation schedules, mesh size minimums, and seasonal closures to ensure the fishery sustains the group long-term.

## Material
*   Rocks
*   Sticks
*   Mud
*   Flexible wood
*   Cordage
*   Branches
*   Logs
*   Bait (Crushed shellfish, fish guts and heads, bread or grain dough, rotting meat scraps, crushed insects)

## System
*   **The Funnel Principle:** The foundation of effective traps, where a wide opening narrows to a point, allowing fish to swim in but making the exit nearly impossible to find, as fish swim along barriers.
*   **Layered Approach:** The most productive method involves combining techniques, such as building a fish weir, placing a basket trap at the gap, and adding a brush pile upstream.

## Technology
Passive fish capture techniques that operate while the survivor is asleep.

## Tools
*   Fish Weirs
*   Basket Traps
*   Brush Piles

## Process
### Categories of Passive Capture
| Method | Best Water Type | Materials Needed | Build Time | Yield Potential |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Fish Weirs | Shallow streams, tidal flats | Rocks, sticks, mud | 2-6 hours | High (bulk catch) |
| Basket Traps | Rivers, lakes, estuaries | Flexible wood, cordage | 1-3 hours per trap | Medium (selective) |
| Tidal traps | Coastal areas | Rocks, stakes | 3-8 hours | Very high |
| Brush piles | Lakes, slow rivers | Branches, logs | 30 minutes | Low-medium |

### Key Steps and Considerations
1.  **Construction:** Design traps using the funnel principle (Entrance width: 30-60 cm; Throat width: 3-8 cm; Throat depth: at least 15 cm).
2.  **Placement:** Select sites with narrow points, confluences, shaded banks, or downstream obstacles.
3.  **Baiting:** Place bait inside the holding chamber, past the funnel throat, to ensure fish must enter fully to reach it.
4.  **Maintenance:** Check traps at least twice daily (dawn and dusk).
5.  **Seasonal Adjustments:**
    *   **Spring:** Place traps facing downstream to intercept spawning fish.
    *   **Summer:** Place traps near overhanging banks and deep pools.
    *   **Autumn:** Bait traps aggressively and check more frequently.
    *   **Winter:** Focus on deep, slow pools as yields drop.

## Notes
*   **Efficiency:** Passive traps multiply fishing effort, allowing one person to maintain many traps simultaneously.
*   **Site Over Construction:** Site selection (natural bottlenecks) is more critical than construction quality.
*   **Spoilage Risk:** Dead fish rot quickly in warm water, necessitating daily maintenance.
*   **Sustainability:** For stable communities, regulation (rotation, mesh size, closures) is necessary to prevent local fish population collapse.
