# Chamber Design | Survival Wiki

## Objective
The primary objective of chamber design is to create a smoking chamber that preserves food by utilizing heat, antimicrobial compounds in smoke, and dehydration, ensuring smoke circulates around all surfaces to prevent spoilage.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Undersizing the chamber forces batch processing.
- Raw meat spoils within hours in warm weather if the chamber is poorly designed.
- Proper sizing based on the ratio of chamber volume to meat load is critical.

## Material
- **Barrel smoker**: 55-gallon drum or large barrel.
- **Box smoker**: Scrap wood, sheet metal.
- **Brick/stone chamber**: Salvaged bricks, clay mortar.
- **Wood walls**: Hardwood planks (at least 1 inch thick); softwood walls must be lined with clay or mud plaster for fire resistance.
- **Insulation**: Clay, mud plaster, or fiberglass (for metal walls).
- **Flooring**: Metal grate floor raised 2-3 inches with a drip pan (for barrel/box smokers).

## System
### Chamber Design and Sizing
- **Sizing Rule**: 1 cubic foot of interior space per 3–4 pounds of meat.
- **Spacing Requirement**: Leave 4–6 inches between hanging pieces so smoke can circulate.
- **Capacity Examples (for 60-80 lbs of meat)**:
    - Minimum chamber interior: 3 feet wide x 3 feet deep x 4 feet tall (36 cubic feet).
    - Comfortable size: 4 feet wide x 3 feet deep x 5 feet tall (60 cubic feet).

### Airflow System
- **Principle**: Cool air enters low, heats and rises, exits high.
- **Intake Vents**: Positioned at the bottom of the chamber, 6–8 inches above ground level, with minimum two on opposite sides, each 3–4 inches in diameter.
- **Exhaust Vents**: Positioned at the top of the chamber, ideally at the highest point.
- **Exhaust Area Requirement**: Total exhaust area should be about 20% larger than the total intake area to maintain steady upward draw.

### Temperature Zones
| Zone | Location | Temperature Range | Purpose |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Hot zone | Bottom 12 inches | 200–300°F (93–149°C) | Fire/smoke generation |
| Smoking zone | Middle section | 125–175°F (52–79°C) | Where meat hangs |
| Cool zone | Top 6–8 inches | 100–140°F (38–60°C) | Lighter items, fish |

### Basic Chamber Types
| Type | Materials | Capacity | Best For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Barrel smoker | 55-gallon drum or large barrel | 20–30 lbs meat | Small group, quick build |
| Box smoker | Scrap wood, sheet metal | 40–60 lbs meat | Medium group, semi-permanent |
| Brick/stone chamber | Salvaged bricks, clay mortar | 80–150 lbs meat | Large group, permanent installation |
| Earth pit smoker | Dug hole with cover | 15–25 lbs meat | Immediate need, no materials |

## Technology
- **Smoking Mechanism**: Preservation through heat, antimicrobial compounds in smoke, and dehydration.
- **Draft Principle**: The system relies on controlled airflow (cool air in low, hot air out high) to ensure continuous smoke flow.

## Tools
- Adjustable dampers (to control intake/exhaust).
- Thermometer probe hole (to monitor temperature at meat-hanging height).
- Basic tools for building (for barrel smokers).

## Process
### Airflow Testing (The Draft Test)
1. Light a small fire.
2. Confirm smoke enters low.
3. Confirm smoke rises steadily through the chamber space.
4. Confirm smoke exits cleanly through the top vents.
5. Verify no visible pooling, swirling back down, or dead spots.

### Construction Steps (Example: Quick Barrel Smoker)
1. Cut a 12x12-inch door in the side, 8 inches from the bottom (firebox access).
2. Drill or punch 6–8 holes (1-inch diameter) around the bottom rim for intake air.
3. Drill 4 holes (2-inch diameter) in the lid or top for exhaust.
4. Install 3 levels of metal grate or rebar crossbars inside at 10-inch intervals.
5. Place a fire pan on the bottom.
6. Hang or lay meat on the upper two levels.

## Notes
- **Common Mistakes**: Lack of damper control results in inability to regulate temperature. Chambers that are too short cause meat to cook on the outside while staying raw inside.
- **Wind Management**: Orientation of the chamber must place prevailing wind on the intake side; crosswinds on the exhaust side can reverse draft.
- **Temperature Monitoring**: Monitor temperature at the meat level, not at the fire or ceiling.
- **Cold Smoking Setup**: For cold smoking (below 90°F), the fire must be outside the chamber with smoke piped in through a flue.
- **Key Takeaway**: Build bigger than the minimum requirement.
