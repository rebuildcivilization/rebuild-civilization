# Universal Edibility Test

## Objective
To test an unknown plant for safety in stages — each step limiting exposure before committing to ingestion. The Universal Edibility Test (UET) is a last-resort protocol adapted from military survival manuals, working by progressively increasing exposure to an unknown plant part over approximately 24 hours, watching for adverse reactions at each stage.

**What the Test Can Detect:**
- Contact irritants (rashes, burning, swelling)
- Immediately acting toxins (burning mouth, nausea, cramping)
- Short-acting poisons that cause symptoms within 8 hours

**What the Test Cannot Detect:**
- Cumulative toxins that only cause harm after repeated exposure
- Amatoxins (mushroom toxins) — symptoms delayed 6-12 hours, beyond the test window
- Carcinogens and chronic toxins
- Toxins that require large doses to produce symptoms

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Before testing any plant, eliminate obvious dangers:
1. **Rule out known toxic families:** If the plant belongs to a family known to contain deadly species (Apiaceae with purple-blotched stems, Solanaceae with black berries), identification trumps testing.
2. **Separate the plant into parts:** Test roots, stems, leaves, flowers, fruits, and seeds individually.
3. **Fasting:** Eat nothing else for 8 hours before beginning the test to ensure any reaction is from the test plant.
4. **Hydration:** Have water available to rinse the mouth and stay hydrated.
5. **Companion:** Have a companion monitor for subtle symptoms like confusion, slurred speech, or unsteady gait.

## Material
Plant parts, including roots, stems, leaves, flowers, fruits, and seeds, which may possess different toxicities.

## System
The UET is a systematic protocol designed to progressively increase exposure to an unknown plant part over approximately 24 hours, monitoring for adverse reactions at each stage.

## Technology
Progressive exposure testing across six distinct phases, timed to observe reactions within specific windows (e.g., 8 hours for skin contact, 24 hours for the larger portion).

## Tools
- Water (for rinsing and hydration)
- A companion (to monitor for symptoms)
- A written log (for record-keeping)

## Process
### Phase 0: Smell Test (5 minutes)
- **Action:** Crush a small piece of the plant part and hold it near the nose to inhale.
- **Stop if:** The smell is strongly unpleasant, acrid, or produces immediate irritation of the nose or eyes (indicates volatile toxins).
- **Pass criteria:** Mild, neutral, or pleasant smell; no irritation.

### Phase 1: Skin Contact Test (8 hours)
- **Action:** Apply moist plant material to the inside of the wrist or elbow for 15 minutes, then observe the contact site for 8 hours.
- **Stop if:** Redness, rash, blisters, burning, itching, swelling, or numbness develop at the contact site.
- **Pass criteria:** No skin reaction after 8 hours.

### Phase 2: Lip Test (15 minutes)
- **Action:** Press a small piece of plant part against the outer lip for 3 minutes, then wait 15 minutes.
- **Stop if:** Burning, tingling, numbness, or swelling of the lip.
- **Pass criteria:** No reaction after 15 minutes.

### Phase 3: Tongue Test (15 minutes)
- **Action:** Place a small piece on the tongue and hold it for 15 minutes (do not chew or swallow).
- **Stop if:** Burning, extreme bitterness, numbing, soapy taste, or unpleasant tingling.
- **Pass criteria:** Mild or neutral taste, no adverse sensation.

### Phase 4: Chew Test (15 minutes)
- **Action:** Chew a small piece thoroughly, hold it in the mouth for 15 minutes (do not swallow), and wait 15 minutes after spitting.
- **Stop if:** Any adverse sensation develops (burning, numbness, nausea, unusual salivation, or throat tightness).
- **Pass criteria:** No reaction after 15 minutes of holding, plus 15 minutes of waiting.

### Phase 5: Swallow Test (8 hours)
- **Action:** Chew and swallow a small amount (approx. 1 tablespoon / 15ml). If required, cook the portion first.
- **Action:** Eat nothing else for 8 hours and monitor for symptoms.
- **Stop if:** Nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, cramping, dizziness, drowsiness, difficulty breathing, or other abnormal symptoms. (If symptoms appear, induce vomiting with warm salt water and drink water.)
- **Pass criteria:** No adverse symptoms after 8 hours.

### Phase 6: Larger Portion Test (24 hours)
- **Action:** Eat a larger portion (approx. 1/4 cup / 60ml).
- **Action:** Wait 24 hours, eating other known-safe foods normally, and monitor for delayed reactions.
- **Pass criteria:** No adverse symptoms after 24 hours. The plant part is provisionally safe.

### Important Rules
- **Test One Plant Part at a Time:** Never test multiple plants simultaneously.
- **Cooking Changes Chemistry:** Test the preparation method (raw vs. cooked). If raw is toxic, cooked might be safe (Example: Kidney beans).
- **Different Parts May Have Different Toxicity:** Test each part separately, as different parts (e.g., rhubarb stalk vs. leaf) can have different toxic components (e.g., oxalic acid vs. solanine).

### Reactions That Demand Immediate Action
| Symptom | Severity | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Skin rash or burning | Low | Wash thoroughly, discontinue test, record plant as skin irritant |
| Lip or tongue numbness | Moderate | Rinse mouth thoroughly, spit repeatedly, do not proceed |
| Nausea or cramping after swallowing | High | Induce vomiting with salt water, drink water, rest |
| Difficulty breathing or throat swelling | EMERGENCY | Keep airway open, rest in sitting position (Possible anaphylaxis)|
| Dizziness, confusion, visual disturbance | EMERGENCY | Induce vomiting, have companion monitor breathing (Possible systemic poisoning)|

### Record-Keeping
A written log must be maintained for every test, documenting date, plant description, location, part tested, preparation method, results for all phases, and the final RESULT (SAFE / UNSAFE / INCONCLUSIVE).

## Notes
- **Limitations to Accept:** Even after passing the full UET, risks remain for cumulative toxins (e.g., comfrey causing long-term liver damage), potential allergic reactions in others, and unknown nutritional value.
- **Key Takeaways:** The UET is a last resort. The full test takes about 49 hours; shortcuts are not acceptable. The primary rule is to stop immediately if any phase yields a reaction.
- **Time Investment:** The full test requires approximately two full days.
