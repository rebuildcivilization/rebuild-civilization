# Bone and Joint | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To detail the assessment, treatment, and recovery of bone and joint injuries in a survival scenario, emphasizing the critical need for proper handling to prevent permanent disability.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Basic understanding of anatomy.

## Material
### Anatomy
- **Long bones** (femur, tibia, humerus, radius, ulna): The structural columns of the limbs; fractures here cause the most dramatic deformity and highest risk of complications.
- **Joints**: Where bones meet (shoulder, elbow, wrist, hip, knee, ankle). Dislocations occur when bone ends are forced out of normal alignment.
- **Ligaments**: Connect bone to bone at joints; sprains are torn or stretched ligaments.
- **Tendons**: Connect muscle to bone; they cannot be splinted and need rest to heal.
- **Blood vessels and nerves**: Run alongside bones; broken bone ends can sever these, causing bleeding and loss of sensation or movement below the injury.

### Injury Differentiation Table
| Feature | Fracture | Sprain | Dislocation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Deformity | Often visible (wrong angle, shortened, or rotated) | Swelling but normal shape | Joint looks “wrong” (bone end visibly out of place) |
| Pain Location | At a specific point on the bone shaft | Around a joint | At the joint, with bone end palpable in wrong position |
| Crepitus (grinding) | Often present when bone ends move against each other | Absent | Absent |
| Movement | Abnormal motion at fracture site; motion at the joint above/below may be intact | Painful but possible at the joint | Joint is locked — cannot move through normal range |
| Weight Bearing | Usually impossible on lower limb fractures | Painful but sometimes possible | Impossible |
| Swelling | Develops rapidly (minutes to hours) | Develops over hours | Immediate, with visible deformity |

### Fracture Types
- **Closed Fracture**: The bone is broken but the skin is intact. More common and less dangerous; infection risk is low. Treatment: Splint, immobilize, monitor.
- **Open (Compound) Fracture**: The bone has punctured through the skin or the wound communicates with the fracture. A surgical emergency.

### Dislocation Types
- **Common Survival Dislocations**: Shoulder (anterior), finger, and patella (kneecap).

### Recovery and Healing Times
| Bone | Approximate Healing Time | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Finger | 3-4 weeks | Buddy tape; start gentle movement after 3 weeks |
| Wrist (radius) | 6-8 weeks | Splint forearm wrist to elbow |
| Forearm | 8-12 weeks | Must immobilize both radius and ulna |
| Humerus (upper arm) | 6-10 weeks | Sling and body binding |
| Collarbone | 6-8 weeks | Sling; heals well without surgery |
| Rib | 4-6 weeks | No splinting possible; rest and pain management |
| Femur (thigh) | 12-16 weeks | Traction splint required |
| Tibia (shin) | 10-16 weeks | Long leg splint |

### Key Takeaways
- Always check circulation (pulse, sensation, movement) below any bone or joint injury; compromised blood flow is a limb-threatening emergency.
- Open fractures carry a 30-50% infection rate without surgical debridement; clean aggressively and monitor obsessively.
- Dislocations should be reduced as quickly as possible; muscle spasm makes reduction progressively harder after 30 minutes.
- Only attempt fracture realignment when circulation below the break is absent; otherwise, splint in the position found.

## System
The system involves contrasting modern orthopedic solutions with centuries of field medicine knowledge, noting that without modern tools, practitioners must rely on physical examination.

## Technology
- **Modern Tools (Solved Problem)**: Orthopedic surgeons and X-ray machines.
- **Field Knowledge (The Baseline)**: Centuries of field medicine knowledge, which is necessary when modern tools are absent.

## Tools
- Splinting (for closed fractures and immobilization).
- Traction splinting (required for femur fractures).
- Buddy-taping (for finger dislocations).
- Gentle traction/pulling (for dislocation reduction).
- Circulation check assessment.

## Process
### Circulation Check (Non-negotiable)
Every bone and joint injury requires checking three things distal (below) the injury site:
1. **Pulse**: Feel for a pulse at the wrist (radial artery) or ankle (posterior tibial artery).
2. **Sensation**: Check if the patient can feel touch on fingers or toes.
3. **Movement**: Check if the patient can wiggle fingers or toes.

### Fracture Management
- **Closed Fracture**: Splint, immobilize, monitor.
- **Open Fracture**: Control bleeding with direct pressure around the bone end; do NOT push the bone back under the skin; cover with a clean, moist dressing; splint in the position found; monitor aggressively for infection.

### Dislocation Management
- **General Principles**: Time matters (attempt reduction within the first 30 minutes); never force reduction (use slow, steady traction); check circulation before and after.
- **Shoulder Dislocation (Anterior) Reduction (Stimson Method)**:
    1. Patient lies face-down on a raised surface with the arm hanging straight down.
    2. Attach a 3-5 kg weight to the wrist or apply gentle downward traction.
    3. Wait 15-30 minutes for spasm release.
    4. If needed, gently rotate the forearm externally while maintaining traction.
    5. Post-reduction: Immobilize the arm in a sling with the elbow bent to 90 degrees and the arm bound to the torso for 2-3 weeks.
- **Finger Dislocation Reduction**:
    1. Grip the finger firmly.
    2. Apply steady traction along the axis of the finger while gently guiding the displaced bone end back into alignment.
    3. Buddy-tape the reduced finger to an adjacent finger for 2-3 weeks.
- **Patella (Kneecap) Dislocation Reduction**:
    1. Slowly straighten the knee.
    2. Gently push the patella medially (toward the midline) back into its groove.
    3. Splint the knee in full extension for 3-4 weeks.

### Fracture Realignment Process
- **The Exception**: Realignment of a fracture should only occur if circulation is absent below the fracture (no pulse, blue/white extremities, no sensation).
- **Procedure**: Stabilize the limb above the fracture, apply slow, steady traction along the long axis below the fracture, gently realign while maintaining traction, and immediately check circulation.

## Notes
- Open fractures without surgical debridement have an infection rate of 30-50% and require aggressive wound care (clean dressing, honey antiseptic, check for gas gangrene).
- Realignment of a fracture carries risks of severing blood vessels or damaging nerves; it is a last resort when limb loss from ischemia is imminent.
- Adequate protein intake accelerates bone healing, with bone broth providing calcium, phosphorus, and collagen.
