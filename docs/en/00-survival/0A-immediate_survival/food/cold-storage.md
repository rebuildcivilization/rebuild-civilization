# Cold Storage Fundamentals

## Objective
To preserve food in a state close to its original condition by utilizing natural cold sources and physical insulation to slow or stop bacterial growth.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- **Ground Temperature:** Digging at least 1.5 meters (5 feet) deep is required for near-constant ground temperature in temperate climates (10–15°C / 50–59°F).
- **Spring House:** Requires access to a cold spring or year-round stream with water temperature below 10°C (50°F).
- **Evaporative Cooling:** Requires hot, arid regions with low relative humidity (below 50% RH).
- **Ice Harvesting:** Requires reliable winter temperatures below freezing to harvest ice.

## Material
- Dry sand or straw
- Leaves
- Clay pots
- Wooden planks or bark slabs
- Logs, stone, or brick (for lining/walls)
- Saw or axe (for harvesting ice)
- Sawdust or straw (for insulation)

## System
### Earth Pit Storage
- A simple, primitive method involving digging a pit, lining it, layering food with insulation, and covering it.

### Spring House
- An enclosure built around a cold water source to channel cold water through for continuous cooling.

### Ice House
- A heavily insulated pit used to store harvested ice, maintaining temperatures near 0–4°C.

### Evaporative Cooling
- The Zeer pot method, using wet sand between two pots to utilize evaporative cooling in hot/dry environments.

### Combined Systems
- Effective systems combine multiple approaches, such as Root cellar + earth insulation, or Ice house + root cellar.

## Technology
- **Core Principle:** Below roughly 4°C (40°F), bacterial growth slows dramatically, and below freezing, growth nearly stops.
- **Thermal Regulation:** Exploiting the earth's relatively constant temperature below 1.5 meters for natural cooling.
- **Evaporative Cooling:** Utilizing the energy required for water evaporation to draw heat from the stored contents.

## Tools
- Saw or axe (for ice harvesting)
- Containers (for food storage)
- Measuring tools (for depth and dimensions)

## Process
### Method 1: Earth Pit Storage
1. **Location Selection:** Choose a shaded, well-drained, and accessible location away from aggressive roots.
2. **Digging:** Dig a pit at least 1 meter (3 feet) deep and 0.5–1 meter (2–3 feet) wide.
3. **Lining:** Line the bottom with a 10 cm (4 inch) layer of dry straw, leaves, or sand.
4. **Layering:** Store root vegetables in layers, separated by straw or dry sand, ensuring vegetables are surrounded by insulating material.
5. **Covering:** Cover the top layer with a thick cushion of straw (at least 30 cm / 12 inches).
6. **Lid:** Create a lid from wooden planks, bark slabs, or a flat stone, covered with dirt or sod to insulate and shed rain.

### Method 2: Spring House
1. **Locate Source:** Identify a spring or stream with water temperature below 10°C (50°F).
2. **Enclosure:** Build or dig a small enclosure (1.5 x 1.5 meters / 5 x 5 feet) around the source, lining it with rocks or logs.
3. **Channeling:** Channel cold water to flow through the enclosure, entering on one side and exiting on the other to maintain continuous flow.
4. **Storage:** Place food in watertight containers directly in the flowing cold water.
5. **Roof:** Build a roof for shade and protection from rain and animals.

### Method 3: Evaporative Cooling (Zeer Pot)
1. **Preparation:** Obtain two unglazed clay pots, one fitting inside the other with a gap of 2–5 cm (1–2 inches).
2. **Filling:** Fill the gap between the pots with wet sand and pack it firmly.
3. **Storage:** Place food inside the inner pot.
4. **Maintenance:** Cover the top with a wet cloth and re-wet the sand 2–3 times per day.

### Method 4: Ice Harvesting and Ice Houses
1. **Harvesting:** Wait for ice to reach at least 15 cm (6 inches) thickness, score the surface, cut the blocks free, and transport them immediately.
2. **Ice House Construction:** Dig a pit at least 2 meters (6.5 feet) deep on a north-facing slope with good drainage. Line walls with logs, stone, or brick.
3. **Insulation & Drainage:** Create a drainage channel at the bottom. Insulate the floor with 30 cm (12 inches) of sawdust, straw, or dry leaves.
4. **Stacking:** Stack ice blocks tightly, packing sawdust between and around blocks (10 cm / 4 inches on all sides).
5. **Covering:** Cover the top with 60 cm (2 feet) of sawdust or straw and build a heavily insulated roof.

## Material
### Ground Temperature Stability
| Depth Below Surface | Temperature Stability | Typical Range (Temperate Climate) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Surface | Follows air temperature | -20°C to 40°C (-4°F to 104°F) |
| 0.5 m (1.5 ft) | Moderate swing, delayed | 5°C to 25°C (41°F to 77°F) |
| 1.0 m (3 ft) | Mild swing, 6-week delay | 8°C to 18°C (46°F to 64°F) |
| 1.5 m (5 ft) | Minimal swing | 10°C to 15°C (50°F to 59°F) |
| 3.0 m (10 ft) | Nearly constant | 10°C to 13°C (50°F to 55°F) |
| 5.0+ m (16+ ft) | Constant | ~12°C (54°F) regional average |

### Food Storage Requirements
| Food Category | Ideal Temperature | Ideal Humidity | Storage Method |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Root vegetables | 0–5°C (32–41°F) | 90–95% (high) | Root cellar, earth pit, in damp sand |
| Apples, pears | 0–4°C (32–39°F) | 85–90% | Root cellar (separate from vegetables) |
| Cabbage, squash | 5–10°C (41–50°F) | 70–80% | Cool cellar, hanging |
| Fresh meat, fish | 0–4°C (32–39°F) | 70–80% | Ice house, spring house only |
| Dairy | 2–7°C (36–45°F) | 80–90% | Spring house |
| Eggs (unwashed) | 10–15°C (50–59°F) | 70–80% | Root cellar (last 3–6 months unwashed) |
| Cured/smoked meat | 10–15°C (50–59°F) | 60–70% (dry) | Root cellar, hanging |
| Fermented vegetables | 5–15°C (41–59°F) | Any | Root cellar (slows continued fermentation) |
| Grain, dried beans | 10–20°C (50–68°F) | Below 60% (dry) | Any cool, dry storage |

## Notes
- **Ethylene Gas:** Apples, pears, and some fruits release ethylene gas, which accelerates ripening and spoilage in nearby vegetables (especially potatoes and carrots). Fruits and vegetables must be stored separately.
- **Monitoring and Maintenance:**
    - Check weekly for spoilage and remove items immediately.
    - Maintain humidity in root cellars by placing open water or wet burlap on the floor.
    - Monitor drainage in root cellars or ice houses; standing water leads to rapid deterioration.
    - Rotate stock, using the oldest items first.
    - Ventilate all cold storage areas to prevent mold.
- **Ground Temperature Insight:** The key takeaway is that digging at least 1.5 meters (5 feet) deep provides a natural refrigerator effect year-round in temperate climates.
