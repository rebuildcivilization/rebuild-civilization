# Plant Growth Patterns | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To use plant growth patterns as a navigational toolkit to encode directional information, allowing for navigation day and night in any weather without equipment.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Open terrain with isolated trees or forest edges (Deep forest is too ambiguous).
- At least 5 trees for a reliable assessment.
- Multiple indicators must be checked (e.g., crown asymmetry, bark differences, moss distribution).
- Observation across different seasons (Spring and late autumn are best).

## Material
- Plants (trees, moss, lichen, fungi).
- Tree stumps or fallen trees with clean cross-sections (for growth ring analysis).
- Ground cover (for slope aspect analysis).

## System
Plants are slow-motion compasses whose growth responds to light, moisture, wind, and gravity over months and years, creating patterns that encode directional information in their shape, bark, foliage, and ring structure. This is driven by the constant competition for sunlight.

## Technology
Plant growth patterns serve as a slow-motion compass.

## Tools
- Compass or the sun/shadow stick method.
- Observation skills to compare multiple indicators across several trees.

## Process
### Field Assessment Protocol
1. Find open terrain with isolated trees or forest edges.
2. Assess at least 5 trees for:
    - Crown asymmetry (which side is denser?).
    - Bark color and texture differences.
    - Moss/lichen distribution.
    - Heaviest branch direction.
3. Check the ground for:
    - Slope greening patterns.
    - Snow/frost melt patterns.
    - Wildflower bloom advancement.
4. Record observations on a tally, looking for consensus across 5+ trees.

### Specific Indicator Reading Methods
**Moss Myth Debunking:** Moss requires moisture, shade, and a surface; it does not sense magnetic fields.

**Tree Crown Asymmetry Check:**
- Northern Hemisphere: Denser, wider-spreading branches on the south side (sun-facing).
- Look for the single largest branch; if multiple trees point in the same direction, that direction is likely south (Northern Hemisphere) or north (Southern Hemisphere).

**Bark Difference Comparison:**
- Sun-Facing Side: Lighter, bleached, drier, cracked.
- Shaded Side: Darker, greener (algae/lichen), moister, smoother.

**Growth Ring Analysis (Stump):**
- Wider rings are on the sun-facing side.
- The pith (center) is offset toward the shaded side.
- The line drawn from the wide-ring side through the pith approximates north-south.

**Ground Cover Analysis:**
- South-facing slopes green up first (more sun, warmer soil).
- North-facing slopes remain brown or snow-covered longer.

## Notes
- **Key Takeaways:** Moss is the least reliable indicator. Crown asymmetry is the most reliable. Local factors override sun direction (water sources, wind, canopy gaps, soil variation).
- **Birch and Beech Exception:** White-barked trees reflect sunlight; focus on lichen/algae presence and moisture differences instead of color.
- **Limitations:** Ring readings are best for conifers in open terrain. Ring width is influenced by water availability and competition, not just light.
- **Environmental Override:** Always identify local causes (stream, wind funnel, canopy gap) before concluding a pattern is due to the sun.

### Comparative Indicators Table
| Indicator | Sun-Facing Side | Shaded Side | Reliable Level |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | 
| **Bark Color** | Lighter, bleached | Darker, greener (algae/lichen) | Moderate |
| **Texture** | Drier, cracked, flaky | Moister, smoother | Moderate |
| **Growth Rings** | Wider spacing | Narrower spacing | High (for conifers) |
| **Crown Asymmetry** | Denser, wider branches | Shorter, sparser branches | High |
| **Moss Distribution** | Less moss | More moss | Low (Least Reliable) |
| **Ground Cover** | Greens up first | Remains brown/snow-covered | High (for slopes) |
