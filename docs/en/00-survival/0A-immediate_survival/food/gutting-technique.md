# Gutting Technique

## Objective
> The gutting step of field dressing is where most beginners make mistakes. This guide covers precise organ removal, identification, damage assessment, and salvage of every usable part.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Sharp knife
- Clean hands (wash or rub with clean sand/ash if no water available)
- A clean surface to place salvaged organs (large leaves, bark slab, clean cloth)
- Cordage for tying off the esophagus and rectum

## Material
### Organ Mass (From the Body Cavity)
| Organ | Edible? | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Heart | Yes — excellent | Dense muscle, best-tasting organ. |
| Lungs | Edible but low nutrition | Pink-gray, spongy, lightweight. |
| Trachea (windpipe) | No — discard | White, ribbed tube. |
| Esophagus | No — discard | Smooth, muscular tube. |
| Liver | Yes — most nutrient-dense organ | Large, dark red-brown, smooth, dense. |
| Gall bladder | No — contains bile (bitter, will taint meat) | Small green sac attached to the liver underside. |
| Stomach/Rumen | Tripe (cleaned stomach lining) is edible | Large bulge, gray-green, filled with partially digested food. |
| Spleen | Edible but low value | Dark purple, elongated, flat. |
| Kidneys (pair) | Yes — excellent | Bean-shaped, dark red, surrounded by white fat. |
| Intestines (small + large) | Cleaned: sausage casings | Tubes, pale pink to gray. |
| Bladder | Container (cleaned) | Thin-walled sac, yellowish, filled with urine. |

### Salvaged Non-Organ Parts
- Sinew: Long tendons running along the spine (back straps) and large tendons behind the lower leg joints.
- Fat:
    - Perirenal fat (around kidneys, best quality, hard and white).
    - Omental fat (lacy fat sheet draped over the intestines).
    - Subcutaneous fat (beneath the skin).
- Blood: Highly nutritious if collected at the kill time.

## System
The body cavity is divided by the diaphragm, which is a thin muscular sheet that attaches to the ribs in a dome shape. The goal is to cut through the diaphragm to access the chest organs.

## Technology
- Knife (for precision cuts)
- Cordage (for tying off the esophagus and rectum)
- Containers (for collecting blood and rendering fat)

## Tools
- Sharp knife
- Cordage
- Container for blood
- Containers/surface for salvaged organs
- Stick (to prop the cavity open)

## Process
### Phase 1: Secure the Endpoints
1. Tie off the esophagus by reaching up into the chest cavity and pinching the soft tube closed.
2. Tie off the rectum by cutting a circle around the anus, freeing it, pulling through a few centimeters of lower intestine, and tying it off.

### Phase 2: Remove the Organ Mass
3. Cut the diaphragm by slicing along the inside edge where it attaches to the ribs, working in both directions.
4. Return to the esophagus and trachea at the top, grip them firmly, and pull downward to peel away the heart and lungs.
5. Cut connective tissue resisting the pull with short knife strokes, keeping the blade against the rib wall.
6. Continue pulling downward. The entire organ mass should come out as one unit.
7. Decide on kidneys: cut around each kidney and its surrounding fat to save them cleanly, or leave them in place.
8. Complete extraction by pulling the intestines and rectum (which are tied off) through the pelvic opening.

### Phase 3: Separate and Inspect
9. **Heart:** Cut free from surrounding vessels; squeeze to expel blood. Set aside.
10. **Liver:** Remove the gall bladder first. Pinch the bile duct, cut below the fingers, peel the gall bladder away, and trim away stained tissue if bile contacts the liver.
11. **Kidneys:** If still in the cavity, cut them free. Remove the thin membrane covering them to save the surrounding white fat.
12. **Stomach:** Cut free from the esophagus and small intestine. Open, dump contents (downwind), wash thoroughly, scrape the inner lining, and optionally boil the cleaned wall (tripe).
13. **Intestines:** Separate small and large intestines. Squeeze out contents between pinched fingers, turn inside out, wash, scrape, and soak if salt is available for sausage casings.
14. **Bladder:** If intact, drain the urine, wash the interior repeatedly, and inflate by blowing into the urethra before letting it dry.

### Salvaging Non-Organ Parts
- Sinew: Harvested from back straps and leg tendons; dried sinew becomes incredibly strong.
- Fat Processing: Render fat by cutting into small pieces, heating slowly, straining the liquid fat through cloth to create rendered fat (tallow/lard).
- Blood Utilization: Can be used for blood sausage or as a thickener for stews.

## Notes
- **Contamination Risk:** Gut bacteria are the enemy; work slowly and keep blades shallow to prevent contamination of muscle tissue.
- **Priority Order for Eating:** Liver first (spoils fastest, most nutritious), then kidneys, then heart.
- **Common Mistakes:
    - Rushing the gall bladder removal, which ruins the liver.
    - Forgetting to tie off the rectum, leading to fecal contamination.
    - Discarding fat, which is essential for calories and non-food uses.
    - Not inspecting the liver for disease (parasitic cysts, abscesses).
    - Leaving organs in the sun, which causes spoilage 2-3x faster than muscle.
- **Hygiene Protocol:** Hands and knife must be cleaned thoroughly with water and ash (or sand/grass) after gutting to prevent severe illness.
- **Preservation Times (Fresh Storage):
    - Heart: 4-6 hours (above 5C) / 2-3 days (below 5C).
    - Liver: 2-4 hours (above 5C) / 1-2 days (below 5C).
    - Kidneys: 4-6 hours (above 5C) / 2-3 days (below 5C).
    - Intestines: 4-6 hours (above 5C) / 2-3 days (below 5C).
    - Rendered fat: Weeks (sealed container) / Months.
