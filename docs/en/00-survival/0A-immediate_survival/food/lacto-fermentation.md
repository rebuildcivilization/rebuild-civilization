# Lacto-Fermentation

## Objective
Lacto-fermentation is a simple and oldest preservation method that aims to preserve vegetables for long periods without refrigeration, canning equipment, or fuel. It also aims to increase the nutritional value of vegetables by producing B vitamins and making minerals like iron and zinc more bioavailable, and to create probiotics that support gut health.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Lacto-fermentation requires only salt, vegetables, a container, and time. No fire, no cooking, and no special equipment are needed because the necessary Lactobacillus bacteria are already on the vegetables’ surface.

## Material
*   **Fresh vegetables:** The food to preserve (any raw, non-rotten plant material).
*   **Salt:** Creates the selective environment for lactobacillus bacteria.
*   **Container:** Holds the ferment (clay pot, hollowed log, animal stomach, bark vessel, glass jar, any non-reactive watertight vessel).
*   **Weight:** Keeps vegetables submerged.
*   **Cover:** Keeps insects out while allowing gas to escape (cloth, leather, leaves secured with cordage).

## System
The core system involves the action of Lactobacillus bacteria consuming sugars in the vegetables and producing lactic acid. This lactic acid lowers the pH below 4.6, killing spoilage organisms and dangerous pathogens. The acidic, anaerobic (oxygen-free) environment preserves the food indefinitely as long as it stays submerged.

## Technology
The technology relies on the action of Lactobacillus bacteria, which thrive when salt draws water out of plant cells through osmosis, creating a brine. This process is a form of microbial preservation.

## Tools
*   Sharp knife or sharpened shell (for shredding cabbage).
*   Hands (for massaging and squeezing vegetables).
*   Weight (to keep vegetables submerged).
*   Cloth or leather/cordage (for the cover).

## Process
### Basic Sauerkraut Steps
1.  **Select and Prep:** Select cabbage, remove outer leaves (set aside as a cover), quarter the head, and cut out the core.
2.  **Shred:** Shred or chop the cabbage into thin strips, roughly 3-5 mm (1/8 to 1/4 inch) wide.
3.  **Salt:** Apply salt at 2-3% of the vegetable weight (e.g., 10-15 g for 500 g of cabbage).
4.  **Massage:** Combine cabbage and salt in a bowl, massage and squeeze vigorously for 5-10 minutes until a pool of brine forms.
5.  **Pack:** Pack the salted cabbage tightly into the container, pressing down hard to push out air pockets, ensuring the liquid rises above the solid cabbage.
6.  **Brine Check:** If natural brine is insufficient, make a brine solution (1 teaspoon of salt in 1 cup of water) and add just enough to submerge everything.
7.  **Weight:** Place the weight on top of the cabbage. The reserved outer leaf can act as a barrier between the weight and the cabbage.
8.  **Covering:** Cover the container with cloth or leather to keep out insects, ensuring the container is not sealed airtight to allow CO2 to escape.
9.  **Fermentation:** Place in a cool, shaded location (Ideal temperature: 15-22°C / 60-72°F). Fermentation is active within 24–72 hours (indicated by CO2 bubbles).
10. **Monitoring:** Check daily to skim off any white film (kahm yeast) or mold. Discard pink, black, or fuzzy mold if it appears throughout the batch.
11. **Taste Test:** Taste after 3-5 days. It should be pleasantly sour.

### Kimchi Process
1.  **Prep:** Quarter napa cabbage, salt heavily (use 5% salt), and let wilt for 2-4 hours. Rinse off excess salt.
2.  **Aromatics:** Mix with available aromatics: crushed garlic, ginger root, wild onion, hot peppers, fish scraps (or fish sauce).
3.  **Pack & Ferment:** Pack tightly into a vessel, submerge in brine, and ferment for 3-7 days at room temperature, then move to cold storage.

### Adapting for Other Vegetables
| Vegetable | Preparation | Salt % | Fermentation Time |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cabbage | Shred thin | 2-3% | 5-14 days |
| Carrots | Grate or julienne | 2-3% | 5-10 days |
| Cucumbers (pickles) | Leave whole, pack in brine | 3.5-5% brine | 3-7 days |
| Green beans | Leave whole or snap in half | 3-5% brine | 5-10 days |
| Radishes | Slice thin | 2-3% | 3-7 days |
| Beets | Peel and shred | 2-3% | 7-14 days |
| Wild greens | Chop roughly | 2-3% | 3-7 days |
| Hot peppers | Chop or leave whole | 3-5% brine | 7-21 days (becomes hot sauce) |

### Dry-Salt Method
Salt the vegetables directly and massage to release their own liquid. Best for vegetables with high water content.

## Notes
*   **Salt Purity:** Avoid salt with anti-caking agents or iodine additives if possible. Pure evaporated sea salt or mined rock salt gives the most reliable results. If salt smells of chemicals, dissolve it in water, let it settle, and use the clear brine.
*   **Temperature Effects:** Warmer temperatures (above 22°C / 72°F) speed up fermentation but increase the risk of off-flavors. Cooler temperatures slow it but produce a crisper, cleaner result.
*   **Storage:** Properly fermented vegetables stored in a cool location (10-15°C / 50-60°F) last 3-12 months. Sauerkraut has been documented lasting over a year in a root cellar.
*   **Key Takeaways:** The fundamental requirements are salt, vegetables, a container, and time, executed without fire, cooking, or special equipment.
*   **Troubleshooting Highlights:** Pink or slimy texture throughout the batch indicates harmful bacteria dominated; discard the batch. White film on the surface is harmless Kahm yeast.
*   **CO2 Management:** The container must not be sealed airtight, as CO2 must escape to prevent pressure buildup and bursting.
