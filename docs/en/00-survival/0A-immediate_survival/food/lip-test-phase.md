# Lip Test Phase | Survival Wiki

## Objective
The Lip Test Phase is the second contact stage of the Universal Edibility Test (UET), designed to use the high sensitivity of lip tissue to detect plant toxins before they enter the body.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Completed the **8-hour fast** (empty stomach for clear symptom detection).
- Passed the **skin contact phase** (15 minutes on inner wrist, no redness, rash, burning, or swelling).
- Prepared the plant part in the **exact form** intended for consumption (raw, boiled, roasted, etc.).
- Isolated a **single plant part** for testing (one of: root, stem, leaf, flower, or fruit; never multiple parts at once).

**Warning:** Do NOT skip the skin contact phase and jump straight to the lip test, as the wrist test catches the most aggressive contact toxins.

## Material
- Plant part (root, stem, leaf, flower, or fruit).
- Clean water for rinsing.

## System
The Lip Test is one step in a multi-stage process (Universal Edibility Test) that exposes the subject to increasing levels of the plant’s chemistry while keeping the dose small enough that a toxic compound is unlikely to cause serious harm. 

## Technology
- High sensitivity of lip tissue to detect plant toxins.
- Nerve endings in lip tissue are roughly 100 times more sensitive per square centimeter than fingertips.

## Tools
- A method to prepare the plant part (crushing or breaking).
- A means to hold the plant piece in place.
- A reflective surface for inspection (water surface or shiny object).

## Process
### Step-by-Step Procedure
1. **Prepare:** Prepare a small piece of the plant part. Crush or break it slightly to release any juices. Test the cooked version if planning to eat it, as toxicity can change with heat.
2. **First Contact:** Touch the prepared plant piece to the **outer corner of your lower lip**.
3. **Hold:** Hold it in place for **3 minutes** and monitor for sensations:
    - Nothing at all: Promising.
    - Mild warmth: Could be normal or an early warning.
    - Tingling or prickling: Possible irritant or toxin (Remove immediately, discard plant).
    - Burning sensation: Active toxin present (Remove immediately, rinse lips with water, discard plant).
    - Numbness: Nerve-affecting compound (potentially deadly) (Remove immediately, rinse thoroughly, discard plant).
    - Swelling or tightness: Allergic or toxic reaction (Remove, rinse, monitor for worsening).
4. **Second Contact (If Step 3 was clear):** Move the plant piece to the **inner surface of your lower lip** (where it is wetter and more absorbent). Hold for another **12 minutes**.
5. **Inspection:** Remove the plant piece and inspect your lip in a reflection for:
    - Redness or discoloration.
    - Swelling, even slight puffiness.
    - Any visible rash or blistering.
6. **Wait:** Wait a full **15 minutes total** from first contact. If lips feel completely normal and show no visible changes, the plant has passed the lip test.

### Context of the Test (Universal Edibility Test Progression)
| Stage | Body Part | Duration | What It Catches |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1. Skin contact | Inner wrist | 15 min | Severe contact toxins, dermatitis |
| **2. Lip test** | **Lip corner, then inner lip** | **15 min** | **Mucous membrane irritants, alkaloids** |
| 3. Tongue test | Tip of tongue | 15 min | Taste-based toxins (extreme bitterness, soapiness) |
| 4. Chew and hold | Full mouth | 15 min | Compounds released by chewing |
| 5. Swallow small dose | Stomach | 8 hours | Systemic toxins, GI irritants |
| 6. Larger dose | Stomach | 8 hours | Dose-dependent toxins |

### Reaction Interpretation
**Obvious reactions**:
- Sharp burning (like touching a hot surface).
- Rapid swelling (lip visibly puffs up within minutes).
- Blistering (small fluid-filled bumps form).

**Subtle reactions (Dangerous):**
- A faint “buzzing” or “electric” sensation (indicates neurotoxic alkaloids).
- Slight dryness or tightness that wasn’t there before (possible astringent toxins).
- A persistent metallic taste (can indicate heavy metal accumulation).
- Excessive saliva production (body’s attempt to flush an irritant).

**Most Dangerous Reaction:** Numbness, which means the plant contains compounds that affect nerve function and can cause respiratory paralysis when ingested (e.g., Poison hemlock).

### If You Get a Reaction
1. **Remove:** Remove the plant material from your lip immediately.
2. **Rinse:** Rinse your lips and the surrounding skin with clean water. Use spit repeatedly or a clean cloth if water is scarce.
3. **Stop:** Do NOT proceed to the tongue test or any further step of the UET with this plant part.
4. **Monitor:** Monitor yourself for 1 hour. If swelling spreads beyond the lip or difficulty breathing occurs, treat as a medical emergency.
5. **Re-test:** You may test a **different part** of the same plant (e.g., root instead of leaf), but start the entire UET from the beginning, including the skin contact phase.

### Environmental Considerations
- **Cold weather:** Makes lips less sensitive due to decreased blood flow and slower nerve response. Warm hands and press them against lips for 30 seconds before testing to restore sensitivity.
- **After spicy or acidic foods:** Wait at least 2 hours for baseline sensitivity to return.
- **Cracked or bleeding lips:** Provide a direct route for toxins into the bloodstream. Heal lip damage before testing unknown plants.

## Notes
- The lip test exploits the extreme nerve density of lip tissue to detect toxins at very low doses.
- Always test at the lip corner first to minimize the impact of a bad reaction.
- The full 15-minute hold time is critical because some reactions are delayed.
- Any abnormal sensation, even mild tingling, is considered a failure in a survival context.
- Testing cooked and raw plant forms as equivalent is a common mistake; the plant must be tested in the form intended for consumption.
