# Hook and Line | Survival Wiki

## Objective
The objective is to employ the simplest and most energy-efficient method of catching fish, using a hook, a line, and patience, which will feed the survivor when nets and traps are not yet built.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- A hook
- A line
- Patience

## Material
### Line Materials
| Material | Breaking Strength | Durability | Availability |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Braided plant fiber (nettle, dogbane) | 10-30 lb | Moderate — degrades when wet | Seasonal |
| Sinew (deer, elk tendon) | 15-40 lb | Excellent | Requires animal |
| Paracord inner strands | 35-50 lb per strand | Excellent | Salvage only |
| Unraveled fabric thread (braided) | 5-15 lb | Poor — rots quickly | Common salvage |
| Horsehair (braided) | 8-20 lb | Good | Requires horses |
| Thin wire (electrical) | 20-60 lb | Excellent | Salvage |

For most freshwater fish under 5 pounds, a line with 10-15 lb breaking strength is sufficient. For larger fish, braid multiple strands together (three strands twisted into a cord) to roughly triple the single-strand strength.

### Hook Types
1. **Gorge hook:** A straight piece of bone, thorn, or wood sharpened at both ends, tied at the center. Effective for larger fish.
2. **Curved hook:** The familiar J-shape, carved from bone, thorns, or bent wire. More reliable hooksets, works on all fish sizes.
3. **Compound hook:** A wooden shank with a separate thorn or bone point lashed at an angle. Faster to produce than carving a curved hook from a single piece.

### Rod Materials
A natural rod is a flexible sapling or branch, 6-10 feet long, with the line tied to the tip. Green willow, hazel, or bamboo work well.

## System
Hook and Line is the basic setup, with rods, reels, floats, and sinkers improving efficiency but not being essential.

## Technology
The system relies on a sharp object to catch the fish in the mouth and a strong line to pull the fish out of the water.

## Process
### Line Making (Plant Fiber Example)
1. Harvest long fibers (nettle stalks, dogbane, inner bark of basswood or cedar).
2. Dry partially, then separate individual fibers.
3. Take two bundles of fibers and twist each clockwise.
4. Wrap the two bundles around each other counter-clockwise (reverse-twist lock).
5. Splice in new fibers by overlapping 3-4 inches before the old bundle runs out.

### Rigging Methods
- **Basic Bottom Rig (For catfish, suckers, carp):**
    1. Tie hook to the end of the line.
    2. Attach a weight 6-12 inches above the hook (small stone or lead sinker).
    3. Cast or lower into water near structure.
- **Basic Float Rig (For trout, panfish, bass):**
    1. Tie hook to the end of the line.
    2. Attach a small float 2-4 feet above the hook (dry wood, cork, or sealed plastic container cap).
    3. Add a small weight just above the hook to keep the bait hanging below the float.
- **Trotline (Passive Fishing):**
    1. Stretch a main line across a stream or along a bank (20-50 feet).
    2. Tie short dropper lines (12-18 inches) every 3-4 feet along the main line.
    3. Attach a baited hook to each dropper.
    4. Anchor both ends securely.
    5. Check every few hours and rebait empty hooks.

### Environmental Strategy
- **Where to Fish (High-probability locations):**
    - Current seams (where fast water meets slow water).
    - Undercut banks (fish shelter beneath overhanging earth).
    - Fallen trees and debris (structure attracts insects, which attract fish).
    - Pool tailouts (where a deep pool shallows).
    - Inlet streams (where a tributary enters a larger body).
    - Shade lines (where fish avoid direct sunlight).
- **When to Fish (Best times):**
    - Dawn and dusk (feeding peaks during low-light transitions).
    - Overcast days (fish feed throughout the day when light is diffused).
    - After rain (rising water dislodges insects and worms).
    - Night (catfish and many large species feed most actively).
- **Rod Usage:** A rod improves reach and detection; without one, wrap extra line around the hand or tie it to a stake to feel bites.

## Notes
- Safety requires careful handling of trotlines due to tangling hazards.
- A hook through a finger in a survival situation presents a serious infection risk.
- Always handle hooks with the point facing away from you.
- A gorge hook and twisted plant fiber line can be assembled in under an hour from natural materials and catches fish immediately.
- Bottom rigs are recommended for survival as most edible freshwater species feed near the bottom.
- Trotlines can yield a high catch rate, potentially catching 3-6 fish overnight with a 10-hook setup in productive water.
- The worst time to fish is midday bright sun, as most fish retreat to deep cover.
