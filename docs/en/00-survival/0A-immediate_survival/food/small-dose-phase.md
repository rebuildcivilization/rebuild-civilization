# Small Dose Phase | Survival Wiki

## Objective
The small dose phase is the critical ingestion stage of the Universal Edibility Test — the point where a plant enters your digestive system for the first time. This is where systemic toxins reveal themselves.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
You must have completed ALL prior UET stages with no reactions:
1. 8-hour fast — stomach empty for clear symptom baseline
2. Skin contact — 15 minutes on inner wrist, no reaction
3. Lip test — 15 minutes on lips, no reaction
4. Tongue test — 15 minutes on tongue tip, no reaction
5. Chew and hold — chewed a small piece, held in mouth 15 minutes without swallowing, no reaction

Warning: If any preceding stage is skipped, the participant is gambling with their life.

## Material
A piece of the plant part (approximately 2-3 grams for the first dose, 30-50 grams for the second dose).

## System
The Universal Edibility Test (UET).

## Technology
Every test before ingestion (skin contact, lip test, tongue test, chew-and-hold) is about surface contact to catch irritants, burning compounds, and substances affecting mucous membranes. The ingestion phase catches toxins that pass surface tests but become lethal only when they reach the stomach, liver, or bloodstream. The first dose is deliberately tiny because most plant toxins have a dose-response relationship, making it unlikely to deliver a lethal dose.

## Tools
Water (permitted and encouraged for hydration and to dilute toxins).

## Process
### First Ingestion (Minimal Dose)
**Step 1:** Prepare a piece of the plant part approximately the size of your thumbnail (roughly 2-3 grams). Prepare it in the exact same way (raw, boiled, roasted) as the chew-and-hold phase.
**Step 2:** Chew the piece thoroughly and swallow it. Note the exact time.
**Step 3:** Consume nothing else for the next 8 hours. Water is permitted.
**Step 4:** Monitor continuously for symptoms:

| Timeframe | Symptoms to Watch For | Likely Cause |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0-30 min | Nausea, burning in throat or stomach | Direct GI irritants (saponins, oxalates) |
| 30 min - 2 hr | Cramping, diarrhea, excessive salivation | Gastrointestinal toxins |
| 2-4 hr | Dizziness, headache, blurred vision | Systemic absorption of alkaloids |
| 4-6 hr | Muscle weakness, tremors, confusion | Neurotoxic compounds |
| 6-8 hr | Delayed nausea, abdominal pain | Hepatotoxins (liver-damaging compounds like those in death caps) |

**Step 5:** If any symptom occurs during the 8 hours:
- Induce vomiting immediately (stick two fingers down throat or drink warm saltwater).
- Drink large amounts of clean water to dilute remaining toxins.
- The plant has **failed** the test — discard it permanently.
- Do not attempt to retest this plant part in any form.

**Step 6:** If 8 hours pass with absolutely no symptoms, proceed to the second ingestion.

### Second Ingestion (Larger Dose)
**Step 7:** Eat a small handful of the same plant part, prepared the same way (approximately 30-50 grams — roughly 10 to 15 times the first dose).
**Step 8:** Eat nothing else for 8 hours. Continue monitoring for all symptoms.
**Step 9:** If no reaction occurs after this second 8-hour window, this specific part of this specific plant, prepared in this specific way, is considered **conditionally safe** for regular consumption.

### Understanding “Conditionally Safe”
Passing the UET means:
- This specific **plant part** (leaf, root, stem, fruit, flower) did not cause acute toxicity at moderate doses.
- Prepared in this specific **way** (raw, cooked, etc.).
- In **your** body (individual reactions vary).

It does NOT mean:
- Other parts of the same plant are safe (rhubarb stalks are edible; rhubarb leaves can kill you).
- The plant is safe in large quantities or over long periods (some toxins accumulate).
- The plant is nutritious — it may simply be non-toxic but also non-nutritive.

### What Vomiting Tells You
- **Immediate nausea (within 30 minutes):** The plant contains direct GI irritants — saponins, raphides (calcium oxalate crystals), or similar compounds. These are the “least bad” outcome.
- **Delayed nausea (2-6 hours):** The toxin required absorption into the bloodstream before symptoms appeared. This is more concerning because some of the compound has already entered the system.
- **Very delayed symptoms (6-12+ hours):** This pattern is characteristic of the most dangerous toxins — hepatotoxins like amatoxin from death cap mushrooms, where significant liver damage may already be occurring.

### Managing the 8-Hour Wait
Strategies include:
- Staying busy with non-strenuous tasks (gathering firewood, repairing shelter, weaving cordage).
- Staying near water (for hydration and potential vomiting).
- Staying near your camp (to be near shelter if a reaction hits).
- Not sleeping during the first 4 hours to detect early symptoms.
- Mentally logging symptoms to note exactly when any sensation occurs.

### After a Successful Test
1. Memorize every identifying feature of the plant (leaf shape, stem characteristics, flower color, growth habitat, smell, season).
2. Keep a sample (pressed leaf, dried flower) as a reference.
3. Teach others in your group to identify it.
4. Start with moderate quantities in your diet, introducing it gradually as one component of a meal for the first week.
5. Monitor for long-term effects, as some compounds cause cumulative toxicity.

## Key Takeaways
- The small dose phase catches systemic toxins that surface tests cannot detect.
- The first dose is thumbnail-sized, small enough to produce warning symptoms without lethal toxicity in most cases.
- Monitor for a full 8 hours after each ingestion, consuming nothing else.
- Symptom timing reveals the type of toxin: immediate = GI irritant, delayed = systemic, very delayed = hepatotoxin (most dangerous).
- Induce vomiting at the first sign of any abnormal symptom — do not “wait and see.”
- A passed test means “conditionally safe for this part, prepared this way, for you.”
- The UET is a last resort when starvation is the alternative, never a casual experiment.

## Notes
- **Dose Comparison:** The first dose is designed to be 10-20x below the lethal dose for key toxins like Ricin (10-20x below lethal) and Amatoxin (14-23x below lethal).
- **Vulnerable Populations:** Children, the elderly, pregnant women, and the severely malnourished have much smaller safety margins, making the thumbnail dose potentially dangerous for them.
- **Toxin Types:** Immediate nausea indicates direct GI irritants. Very delayed symptoms indicate hepatotoxins.
- **Post-Test Action:** The crucial final step is teaching others and introducing the plant gradually, as cumulative toxicity from long-term consumption is a risk.
