# Nuts and Seeds | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To identify, harvest, process, and store nuts and seeds, which are the most calorie-dense wild foods available, to solve the calorie problem in a survival situation.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Knowledge of identification to distinguish safe nuts from toxic look-alikes.
*   Tools for processing (rocks, heavy tools, pointed sticks, thin blades).
*   Time and effort for labor-intensive processing (e.g., pine nuts).
*   Proper storage conditions (cool, dry, ventilated location).

## Material
*   **Nuts and Seeds:** Acorns, Black walnuts, Butternuts, Hazelnuts (Filberts), Pine nuts, Sunflower seeds, Cattail seeds and pollen, Grass seeds.
*   **Wild Plants:** Oak species, Hazel bushes, Pine species, Wild sunflowers, Cattails, Wild grasses.

## System
Nuts and seeds provide essential fats, proteins, and carbohydrates necessary for sustained energy, contrasting with leafy greens which provide vitamins.

## Technology
*   **Chemical/Physical Processes:** Tannin leaching (for acorns).
*   **Thermal Processing:** Using fire or sun to open pine cones.
*   **Storage Principle:** Utilizing shells as natural packaging to prevent oxidation and insect damage.

## Tools
*   Rock or heavy tool (for cracking walnuts).
*   Pointed stick or thin blade (for picking nutmeat).
*   Teeth (for cracking sunflower seeds).
*   Containers (for collecting, threshing, and winnowing).

## Process
### Acorns (All Oak Species)
1.  **Collect:** Gather only brown, firm acorns that have fallen from the tree (green ones are unripe).
2.  **Float-test:** Test in water; discard any that float (hollow, rotten, or infested).
3.  **Dry:** Dry in the sun or near a fire before storage.

### Walnuts and Butternuts
1.  **Husk Removal:** Remove the green/black outer husk by stomping, rolling underfoot, or cutting away.
2.  **Wash:** Wash the nuts thoroughly.
3.  **Dry:** Dry for 2-3 weeks in a ventilated area before cracking.
4.  **Crack & Extract:** Crack with a rock on a flat stone anvil; pick out nutmeat with a pointed stick or thin blade.

### Pine Nuts
1.  **Collect:** Gather closed or slightly open cones in late autumn.
2.  **Heat:** Place cones near a fire or in direct sun to cause scales to open and release seeds.
3.  **Extract:** Shake or tap the cone to extract seeds.
4.  **Crack:** Crack the thin outer shell with teeth or between two flat stones; eat the soft inner kernel.

### Grass Seeds
1.  **Strip:** Strip seed heads from tall grasses by running the stalk through a hand over a container.
2.  **Thresh:** Thresh by rubbing between hands or stomping in a container.
3.  **Winnow:** Winnow by pouring from one container to another in a breeze (chaff blows away).
4.  **Grind/Cook:** Grind between two flat stones and cook as porridge or flatbread.

### Storage and Preservation
1.  **Dry Completely:** Spread in a single layer in sun or near a fire. Nuts should feel light and rattle inside the shell when shaken.
2.  **Shell:** Store in shells whenever possible to prevent oxidation and insect damage.
3.  **Location:** Keep in a cool, dry, ventilated location, preferably on a raised platform in the shade. Avoid ground contact.
4.  **Check:** Check stored nuts regularly and discard any showing mold, dark spots, or off smells.

## Notes
*   **Nutritional Superiority:** Nuts and seeds provide caloric density that rivals or exceeds cultivated crops. A daily intake of 200-300g of mixed nuts provides 1,200-1,900 calories.
*   **Acorn Warning:** All acorns contain tannins that must be leached before eating.
*   **Toxic Look-Alikes:** Horse chestnut (*Aesculus*) must be distinguished from sweet chestnut; horse chestnut has a smooth husk with few thick spines, while sweet chestnut has a spiny husk with many fine needles.
*   **Pine Nut Density:** Pine nuts are among the most energy-dense wild foods (673 calories per 100g) but require the most labor-intensive processing.
*   **Processing Tip:** For walnuts, fresh husked walnuts are difficult to extract cleanly.
*   **Shelf Life Estimates (in Shell, cool, dry):**
    *   Acorns (leached, dried): 3-6 months
    *   Walnuts: 6-12 months
    *   Hazelnuts: 6-12 months
    *   Pine nuts: 1-3 months (Days when high oil content is factored in)
