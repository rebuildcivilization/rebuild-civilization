# Mushroom Dangers | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To detail the unique dangers of mushroom poisoning, explain the identification requirements for deadly species, outline the amatoxin poisoning timeline, and establish a practical safety protocol for foraging.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Absolute Certainty:** The burden of proof for safety is on the forager, not the mushroom.
*   **Identification Skills:** Proficiency in confirming all mandatory identification features before consumption.

## Material

### The Deadly Families
*   **Death Caps and Destroying Angels:** The most lethal species. They taste pleasant and show no warning symptoms for six to twelve hours before causing organ failure.
*   **False Morels (*Gyromitra* species):** Contain gyromitrin, which converts to monomethylhydrazine (rocket fuel).
*   **Autumn Skullcap (*Galerina marginata*):** Small, brown mushrooms growing on rotting wood containing amatoxins.

### Identification of the Amanita Death Cap (*Amanita phalloides*)
**Physical Features:**
*   **Cap:** 3-6 inches wide, smooth, olive-green to yellowish-brown to pale white.
*   **Gills:** White, free (not attached to the stem), closely spaced.
*   **Stem:** White, 3-6 inches tall, often with a faint greenish tinge.
*   **Ring (annulus):** Thin, skirt-like membrane partway up the stem.
*   **Volva (cup):** Sack-like cup at the very base; the single most important diagnostic feature.
*   **Spore print:** White.

**Critical Collection Rule:** Always dig up the entire mushroom, including everything below the soil line, to check the volva.

### Mimics of the Death Cap
| Safe Species | How Death Cap Mimics It | Key Differentiator |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Paddy straw mushroom (*Volvariella volvacea*) | Similar size, shape, and color when young | Paddy straw has pink spore print; death cap has white |
| Common field mushroom (*Agaricus campestris*) | Similar cap shape and size | Field mushroom has pink-to-brown gills (never white) and no volva |
| Puffball (young stage) | Resembles a small puffball when enclosed in its universal veil | Puffball is solid white throughout when cut in half; developing death cap shows mushroom structure inside |

### Comparison: True Morel vs. False Morel
| Feature | True Morel (Safe) | False Morel (Toxic) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cap texture | Honeycomb pattern — regular, defined pits and ridges | Brain-like, irregular, wrinkled, folded, lobed |
| Cap attachment | Cap is attached at the bottom of the cap directly to the stem | Cap hangs free from the stem (attached only at the top) |
| Interior (CUT IN HALF) | Completely hollow from top of cap to bottom of stem — one continuous empty chamber | Cottony, chambered, or solid interior — NOT a single hollow space |
| Cap shape | Conical or elongated, symmetrical | Irregularly shaped, often saddle-shaped or lumpy |
| Color | Tan, yellow-brown, gray, or black | Reddish-brown, chestnut, dark brown |

### Amatoxin Poisoning Timeline
*   **Phase 1 — Latent period (6-12 hours after ingestion):** No symptoms. The victim feels normal.
*   **Phase 2 — Gastrointestinal assault (12-24 hours):** Sudden onset of severe nausea, vomiting, watery diarrhea, and abdominal cramps. Toxin circulating through the liver.
*   **Phase 3 — False recovery (24-72 hours):** Symptoms seem to improve, but amatoxins are destroying liver cells.
*   **Phase 4 — Organ failure (3-6 days):** Jaundice, severe abdominal pain, bleeding, confusion, kidney failure. Death follows in 6-16 days.
*   **Lethal Dose:** Half a single mushroom cap can kill an adult. Approximately 0.1 mg/kg body weight of amatoxin is lethal.

## System

### Process and Protocols
**Practical Mushroom Safety Protocol:**
1.  **Step 1: Default Position:** Assume every mushroom is toxic until proven otherwise.
2.  **Step 2: Mandatory Identification Features (Must Confirm All):**
    *   Spore print color.
    *   Presence or absence of a ring (annulus) on the stem.
    *   Presence or absence of a volva (cup) at the base (dig up entire mushroom).
    *   Gill attachment.
    *   Habitat.
    *   Cut the mushroom in half to note interior color, texture, and hollowness.
3.  **Step 3: Apply the Kill Rules (If ANY of these are true, DO NOT EAT):**
    *   White gills + ring + volva = likely *Amanita*. **Do not eat.**
    *   Small brown mushroom + wood + ring = likely *Galerina*. **Do not eat.**
    *   Brain-like cap or non-hollow interior on a “morel” = false morel. **Do not eat.**
    *   Cannot identify the spore print color. **Do not eat.**
    *   Cannot see the base of the stem (it broke off). **Do not eat.**
    *   Any doubt exists. **Do not eat.**
4.  **Step 4: Start with Small Amounts:** Even for safe species, eat a small amount (tablespoon) first and wait 24 hours due to potential individual sensitivities.

**If Someone Eats a Suspected Toxic Mushroom:**
1.  Save a sample (cap, stem, base, remnants).
2.  Note the exact time of consumption.
3.  If within 1 hour: Induce vomiting (warm salt water).
4.  Administer crushed charcoal mixed with water if available.
5.  Monitor the clock: If symptoms appear 6+ hours after eating, suspect amatoxin poisoning.
6.  Hydrate aggressively, as the GI phase causes severe dehydration.
7.  Do not be reassured by improvement, as the false recovery phase is dangerous.

## Technology
*   **Toxin Mechanism:** Amatoxins cause organ failure (primarily liver damage).
*   **Key Differentiators:** The physical structure (volva, hollowness) and spore print color are the most critical identifiers.

## Tools
*   **Identification Tools:** Ability to perform spore print tests, inspect for rings/volva, and physically cut mushrooms.
*   **Safety Tools:** Ability to correctly apply the Kill Rules and manage immediate post-ingestion symptoms (vomiting, charcoal, hydration).

## Technology
*   **Technology:** In a world without liver transplants, prevention through correct identification is the only option.

## Notes
*   **Field Treatment:** There is no field remedy for amatoxin poisoning; modern treatments (IV silibinin, N-acetylcysteine, liver transplant) are unavailable post-collapse.
*   **Critical Takeaway:** The delay between ingestion and symptoms (6-12 hours) and the subsequent 'false recovery' phase are central dangers.
*   **Beginner Focus:** Beginners should start with species that have few dangerous look-alikes, such as Giant puffball, Chicken of the woods, and Hen of the woods.
*   **Default Rule:** The fundamental rule is to assume toxicity until absolute certainty is achieved.
