# Trap Placement Guide

## Objective
To determine the location, method (funneling), bait, and scent management for trapping success, recognizing that placement determines 80% of trapping success.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Spend 30-60 minutes scouting the area before setting traps.
- Active management of traps is required (relocate unproductive sets after 3 days).

## Material
- Traps (snare, deadfalls, cage traps).
- Bait (fresh greens, nuts, seeds, meat, fish, corn, peanut butter).
- Guide fences/sticks for creating artificial funnels.
- Gloves or hands coated in mud for scent management.
- Local vegetation (leaves, bark, grass) for scent masking.

## System
The system relies on identifying predictable animal paths driven by the three needs: food, water, and shelter. Success hinges on putting the trap exactly where the animal has no choice but to walk through it.

## Technology
- The effectiveness of the trap placement strategy.
- Understanding animal behavior patterns (food, water, shelter).

## Tools
- Surveying/Scouting skills.
- Measuring tools (implied by dimensions for funnels).

## Process
### Reading the Landscape
1. Walk the area in expanding circles from the camp to look for trails (compressed vegetation, worn earth, tunnels).
2. Follow each trail in both directions to determine its purpose (water, bedding, feeding area).
3. Look for pinch points along the trail—natural narrowings are prime trap sites.
4. Check for freshness: look for sharp-edged/eroded tracks, moist/dry scat, and green/dried browse marks.

### Creating Artificial Funnels
1. Gather sticks, brush, and debris from the immediate area; avoid foreign objects.
2. Build low guide fences on either side of the trail, angling inward to the trap.
    - Height: Just above the target animal’s eye level (15-20 cm for rabbits).
    - Length: 1-2 meters on each side, forming a V-shape.
    - Appearance: Random and natural (lean sticks, loose brush).
3. Leave the gap exactly wide enough for the animal and the snare (e.g., 10-15 cm for rabbits).
4. Place the snare in the gap.

### Bait Strategy
- **Primary Bait Placement:** Place bait inside or behind the trap mechanism so the animal must engage the trigger to reach it. For deadfalls, bait goes on the bait stick. For cage traps, bait goes at the far end past the trigger plate.
- **Bait Trail Creation:** Create small pieces of bait leading from the animal’s trail to the trap, spaced 15-30 cm apart to draw the animal off its normal path.
- **Freshness:** Use the freshest, most aromatic bait. Replace bait every 24-48 hours in warm weather.

### Scent Management
- **Reducing Human Scent:**
    1. Handle components with gloves or hands coated in mud.
    2. Rub finished traps with local vegetation to mask residual human scent.
    3. Boil wire snares in water with bark/leaves for 10-15 minutes, or bury them overnight.
    4. Approach the trap from the side or behind, perpendicular to the trail, and ideally from downwind.
    5. Minimize time at the site; set quickly and leave.
    6. Avoid stepping on the animal trail.
- **Using Attractant Scents (Optional):**
    - Urine from the target species (to attract territorial animals).
    - Fish oil (to draw foxes, raccoons, mink).
    - Anise oil (general wildlife attractant).
    - Blood from a previous catch (to attract other animals).

### Placement Patterns
- **The Trail Line:** Set 10-20 snares along a single animal trail, spaced 20-50 meters apart, best for clear trails.
- **The Water Ring:** Set traps in a ring around a water source, placing snares on every trail leading to the water's edge.
- **The Bedding Approach:** Set traps on the 2-3 trails leading in and out of bedding areas; check at dawn and dusk.
- **The Bait Station:** Create a central bait site with scattered food, setting 3-5 traps in a ring around it on approach trails.

### Seasonal Adjustments
| Season | Key Changes | 
| :--- | :--- | 
| Spring | Animals dispersing; trails shift. Scout fresh. Young animals are naive and easier to catch. | 
| Summer | Water sources concentrate animals. Set around water. Early morning activity peaks. Bait spoils fast—replace daily. | 
| Autumn | Animals fattening for winter, following food sources (nuts, berries, grain). Best trapping season.
| Winter | Trails concentrate in snow (easy to read). Set in sheltered areas. Animals less active; check less often. Frozen ground requires drag logs or anchoring.

## Notes
- Placement is paramount; focus on location before trap design.
- A common mistake is over-building funnels; funnels should look like natural debris accumulation, not construction projects.
- Traps must be spread across diverse habitats (forest edge, water, brush, clearings).
- Setting traps too close to camp (within 50 meters) drastically reduces catch rates; place the nearest trap at least 100 meters away.
- Key Takeaway: Relocate unproductive traps after 3 days.
