# Stalking Method

## Objective
Stalking is the art of getting close enough to a wild animal to kill it before it knows you are there.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Stalking demands mastery of wind, movement, and camouflage — three disciplines that must all work simultaneously. The skill is in persistence and learning from each failure.

## Material
### Wind Management
Wind is the single most important factor. If an animal smells you, the stalk is over instantly.

### Movement
Movement betrays the hunter. Animals are hardwired to detect motion.

### Camouflage
Camouflage is the third pillar. It is less critical than wind and movement, but proper concealment dramatically increases the margin of error.

### Scent Management
Beyond wind positioning, reduce the scent carried. Do not cook near hunting clothes, rub clothing/body with local vegetation, avoid crossing wet ground, and store clothes outside the shelter.

## System
### The Approach Plan
Before moving, plan the entire stalk mentally:
1. Identify the animal’s exact position and likely movement direction.
2. Check the wind and determine the approach angle (always downwind or crosswind).
3. Identify three or four intermediate cover points (waypoints).
4. Plan the route between waypoints, choosing the path with the softest ground.
5. Estimate the required closing distance (e.g., 15 meters or less for a survival bow).
6. Identify the escape route.

### When to Abort
Abort the stalk if:
- Wind shifts and stays unfavorable for more than 2 minutes.
- The animal becomes alert and begins looking in your direction repeatedly.
- You are spotted by a different animal that alarms.
- You realize you cannot get within weapon range without crossing open ground.
- You feel physically unsafe.

## Technology
Animals possess sharpened senses to detect predators, such as hearing a heartbeat at 20 meters or smelling a wild pig from 400 meters downwind.

## Tools
### Wind Reading Methods
- **Dust test:** Pick up fine dust or dry soil and watch where it drifts.
- **Ash test:** Pinch fire ash and release it to reveal slight air currents.
- **Fiber test:** Pull a tuft of plant down, milkweed silk, or animal fur and release it at arm’s length (most sensitive).
- **Wet finger:** Lick the index finger to sense the cool side facing the wind.

### Movement Techniques
- **The fox walk:** Place the ball of the foot down first, roll the weight onto it, feel the ground, and lift the foot if a twig is felt.
- **Body mechanics:** Keep knees slightly bent; take short steps (no more than half the normal stride); move the whole body as a single unit.

### Camouflage Techniques
- **Breaking Outline:** Tuck branches into clothing; smear exposed skin with mud/charcoal; avoid shiny items.
- **Posture:** Stay low (crouch, crawl, or belly-crawl).
- **Positioning:** When pausing, position yourself against a backdrop (tree trunk, rock).

## Process
### Wind Behavior by Terrain
| Terrain | Wind Pattern | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Open plains | Steady, predictable | Easiest to read; hardest to find cover |
| Forest | Swirling, unpredictable | Trees create eddies; check wind constantly |
| River valleys | Follows the valley axis | Morning: downhill (cool air sinks); Evening: uphill (warm air rises) |
| Hillsides | Thermals rise during day, sink at night | Stalk uphill in morning, downhill in evening |
| Ridgelines | Turbulent, switches rapidly | Avoid stalking along ridgelines if possible |

### Thermal Currents
- **Morning (cool):** Air sinks downhill. Position yourself above your target.
- **Midday (warm):** Air rises uphill. Position yourself below your target.
- **Evening transition:** Thermals reverse; chaotic and unpredictable for 30-60 minutes around sunset.

### Movement Speed Guide
| Distance to Animal | Movement Speed | Step Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 100+ meters | Normal cautious walk | Continuous, slow |
| 50-100 meters | Fox walk, frequent pauses | Move 3 sec, pause 10 sec |
| 20-50 meters | Ultra-slow fox walk | Move 2 sec, pause 20-30 sec |
| Under 20 meters | Inch-by-inch, or hold position | Move only when animal feeds |

### The Stop-and-Go Method
1. **Move — Freeze — Observe — Move.** (Each movement phase 2-5 seconds; each freeze 10-30 seconds minimum.)
2. **Movement Trigger:** Move only when the animal’s head is down (feeding/drinking). Freeze when the head comes up.
3. **Timing:** Time steps to coincide with natural sounds (wind gusts, bird calls).

## Notes
- Scent management supplements wind awareness; it never replaces it.
- A botched stalk that spooks the herd may push animals out of the territory entirely, making an early abort beneficial.
- The human silhouette is recognizable; the primary camouflage goal is to break this outline.
- Posture and profile rely on using backdrops to ensure a dark shape against a dark background disappears.
