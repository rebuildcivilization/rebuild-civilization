# Moon and Sun Halos

## Objective
Part of Weather Forecasting, using ice crystal rings, coronas, sun dogs, and lunar brightness as weather predictors.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Hexagonal ice crystals in high cirrostratus clouds are required for halos and sun dogs.

## Material
*   Ice crystal rings
*   Coronas (small, close rings with colored bands)
*   Sun dogs (bright spots of light)
*   Lunar brightness
*   Water droplets (in thin clouds)

## System
**Practical Observation System**
1.  **Nightly moon check:** Sharp and clear equals fair weather continuing. Halo means rain within 24-48 hours. Fuzzy means increasing moisture.
2.  **Daytime sun observation:** Use peripheral vision or shade the sun to look for halos. Sun dogs are visible without looking directly at the sun.
3.  **Track halo timing:** Note when a halo first appears and when it disappears to gauge the approach speed of the front.
4.  **Combine with cloud progression:** Observe cloud stages (cirrus to cirrostratus to altostratus to nimbostratus) to confirm and narrow the timing of rain.
5.  **Count the stars:** If star count drops on subsequent nights without moonlight change, atmospheric moisture is increasing.

## Technology
*   **Refraction:** Used for halos, caused by hexagonal ice crystals bending sunlight or moonlight at a minimum angle of 22 degrees.
*   **Diffraction:** Used for coronas, caused by diffraction through water droplets in thin clouds.

## Tools
*   Fist measurement (to distinguish corona from halo: within one fist-width is a corona; two or more fist-widths out is a halo).
*   Timing mechanism for tracking halo progression.
*   Peripheral vision and shading for sun observation.

## Process
### The 22-Degree Halo
*   **Formation:** Hexagonal ice crystals in high cirrostratus clouds refract sunlight or moonlight at a minimum angle of 22 degrees.
*   **Meaning:** Cirrostratus clouds at 20,000-30,000 feet are the leading edge of an approaching warm front. The halo forms 12-48 hours before lower clouds and rain arrive.
*   **Prediction:** A halo around the sun or moon means rain or snow within 24-48 hours. The prediction verifies roughly 65-75% of the time.

### Corona vs. Halo
*   **Corona:** Small, close ring (1-5 degrees from the sun/moon) with colored bands (bluish-white inner ring with reddish outer edge). Caused by diffraction through water droplets in thin clouds (altocumulus or altostratus).
    *   **Weather Implication:** Short-term fair or stable. A bright, sharply defined corona indicates a stable cloud layer.
*   **Halo:** Large ring (22 degrees) caused by refraction through ice crystals in cirrostratus.
    *   **Weather Implication:** Change coming; predicts frontal passage within 24-48 hours.

### Moon Brightness and Clarity
*   **Sharp, bright moon with clear edges:** Dry, clean air with high-pressure system; fair weather continuing.
*   **Fuzzy, haloed moon:** Moisture at high altitude; likely change within 24-48 hours.
*   **Moon visible but dim, with a diffuse glow:** Low-level moisture or thin fog developing; ground fog likely by morning.
*   **Moon not visible despite being above the horizon:** Thick cloud layer; if the moon's general location is visible as a brighter area behind clouds, the layer is thin; if the sky is uniformly dark, rain may be imminent or occurring.
*   **Moon appears unusually large near the horizon:** A moon illusion with no weather significance.
*   **Color changes in moonlight (Yellow or Orange):** Indicates particles in the lower atmosphere (dust, smoke, or pollution). Orange moon at high altitude suggests increasing lower-atmosphere moisture.

### Sun Dogs (Parhelia)
*   **Formation:** Hexagonal plate-shaped ice crystals floating horizontally in cirrus or cirrostratus clouds refract sunlight, concentrating light at the two horizontal points of the 22-degree halo.
*   **Prediction:** Indicates ice crystals in high clouds; the same 24-48 hour rain prediction applies.
*   **Vivid Colors (Red closest to sun, blue/green farther away):** Indicates well-formed ice crystals in a uniform cloud layer, suggesting a stronger indication of organized cirrostratus and an approaching front.
*   **Reliability:** Sun dogs without a full halo are less reliable, but persistence or intensification through the day suggests an expanding cirrus layer and a more likely frontal approach.

### Light Pillars and Arcs
*   **Light Pillars:** Vertical columns of light above or below the sun, caused by flat, plate-shaped ice crystals. Not a strong weather predictor by themselves.
*   **Upper Tangent Arcs:** Curved arcs touching the top of a 22-degree halo, caused by column-shaped ice crystals. Indicates a well-developed cirrostratus layer, strengthening the halo rain prediction.
*   **Circumzenithal Arc:** A bright, rainbow-like arc high in the sky directly above the observer (near the zenith), requiring well-formed ice crystals and indicating high-altitude moisture that may precede a front.

## Notes
- The folk saying “ring around the moon, rain by noon” has solid meteorological backing.
- The practical system emphasizes combining the halo observation with the progression of cloud types for the most accurate rain timing.
- The moon illusion (unusually large moon near the horizon) has no weather significance and should be ignored.
- Color changes in moonlight persisting for days often point to lower-atmosphere particles (dust, smoke, pollution).
