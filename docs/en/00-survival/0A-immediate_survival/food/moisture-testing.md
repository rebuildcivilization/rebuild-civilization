# Moisture Testing

## Objective
Knowing when food is dry enough to store safely is the difference between months of preserved nutrition and a moldy, wasted batch.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Hands and observation for field tests.
*   Food must be allowed to cool completely to ambient temperature (at least 30 minutes) before performing final moisture tests, as heat masks residual moisture.

## Material
*   Dried meat
*   Jerky
*   Fish
*   Dried fruit
*   Vegetables
*   Herbs
*   Leaves

## System
The system involves assessing moisture content to determine storage readiness, relying on physical tests rather than laboratory moisture meters.

## Technology
*   Physical tests (Snap Test, Squeeze Test, Tear Test, Crumble Test, Sound Test, Weight Test).
*   Observation of color, texture, and sound.

## Tools
*   Hands (for snap, squeeze, crumble tests).
*   A hard surface (flat stone, wooden board, ceramic) for the Sound Test.
*   A balance scale (simple balance scale) for the Weight Test.
*   A sealed container (pottery with a lid, tightly wrapped cloth bundle) for the 24-hour test.

## Process
### Moisture Content Thresholds
| Moisture Content | What Grows | Safe for Storage? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Above 30% | Bacteria, mold, yeast — rapid spoilage | No |
| 15-30% | Mold and some yeast — slower spoilage | No |
| 10-15% | Most organisms inhibited | Borderline |
| Below 10% | Almost no microbial growth | Yes |

### Testing Procedures
**The Snap Test (Meat and Fish):**
1. Hold a piece of dried meat between thumb and forefinger at both ends.
2. Bend it sharply.
*   Result: Snaps cleanly in two (Below 10% - Ready for storage).
*   Result: Cracks but does not break fully (10-15% - Needs more drying).
*   Result: Bends without cracking (Above 15% - Not ready).

**The Squeeze Test (Fruits):**
1. Squeeze a piece of dried fruit firmly for 5 seconds.
*   Result: Leathery, springs back, fingers dry (Below 15% - Ready for storage).
*   Result: Slightly tacky, slow spring-back (15-20% - Needs 2-4 more hours).
*   Result: Sticky, moisture visible on fingers (Above 20% - Continue drying).

**The Tear Test (Fruit Supplement):**
1. Tear thick fruit pieces in half.
*   Ready: Uniform color throughout, no visible moisture, slightly fibrous texture.
*   Not ready: Darker, wetter center compared to the edges.

**The Crumble Test (Vegetables, Herbs, Leaves):**
1. Press a dried piece between fingers.
*   Ready: Shatters or crumbles to powder (Below 10% - Ready for storage).
*   Close: Breaks into pieces but doesn’t crumble (10-15% - Close - 1-2 more hours).
*   Not ready: Bends or folds without breaking (Above 15% - Continue drying).

**The Sound Test:** Drop the dried piece onto a hard surface and listen for a hard click or clatter (Dry enough).

**The Weight Test (Objective Field Test):**
*   Meat/Fish Target Weight Loss: 65-75% (e.g., 100g starts at 25-35g finished).
*   Fruit Target Weight Loss: 75-85% (e.g., 100g starts at 15-25g finished).
*   Vegetable Target Weight Loss: 80-90%.

**The 24-hour Test (For Borderline Batches):**
1. Place questionable food in a sealed container for 24 hours.
2. Check if condensation appears or if the food has softened.

### Key Takeaways
*   Dried meat must snap cleanly when bent.
*   Test multiple pieces from different rack positions.
*   Always test food after it has cooled.
*   When unsure, dry longer. The cost of under-drying is greater than the cost of over-drying.

## Notes
*   Commercial jerky often uses chemical additives (sodium nitrite, potassium sorbate) to keep flexible jerky preserved, whereas survival dried meat must snap.
*   The Cooling Trap highlights that heat masks residual moisture, making tests done while food is warm potentially misleading.
*   The weakest link in a batch determines storage safety.
*   The 24-hour sealed container test is the most reliable field method for borderline batches.
