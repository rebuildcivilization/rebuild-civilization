# Hook Making | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To create effective fish hooks by utilizing materials that possess the key properties: a point sharp enough to penetrate mouth tissue, a bend or angle that prevents the fish from ejecting it, and enough structural strength to hold under the pull of the fish and your line.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Materials (rigid, sharp, strong enough to resist a fish’s bite).
- Time during downtime to produce extra hooks.

## Material
| Material | Sharpness | Strength | Ease of Shaping | Best For |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Bone | Excellent | Good | Moderate — needs carving | Curved hooks, gorges |
| Thorns | Excellent | Fair | Easy — natural shape | Compound hooks, small fish |
| Hardwood | Fair | Good | Easy — whittling | Gorge hooks |
| Shell | Good | Fair | Difficult — brittle | Curved hooks (coastal) |
| Wire/nails | Excellent | Excellent | Easy — bend with pliers | All hook types |
| Safety pins | Good | Good | Very easy | Immediate use |
| Soda can tabs | Fair | Fair | Moderate | Emergency hooks |

## System
The system involves utilizing natural materials or repurposed metal to create hooks, focusing on achieving penetration, retention, and durability.

## Technology
### Hook Mechanics
- **Gorge Hook Principle:** A straight piece of material sharpened at both ends, with the line tied at the center, which lodges crosswise in the fish’s throat or stomach when the line is pulled taut.
- **Barb Function:** A barb prevents the hook from backing out of the fish’s mouth during the fight. Barbless hooks penetrate more easily but require steady line tension.
- **Metal Hook Tempering:** Heating metal hooks to cherry red, plunging them into water to harden, and then reheating until straw-yellow (approx. 400°F / 200°C) and allowing them to air cool to achieve a balance of hardness and flexibility.

### Hook Sizing
| Target Fish | Hook Length | Material Thickness |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Minnows, small panfish | 1/2 inch (12 mm) | Fine — toothpick thickness |
| Trout, perch, crappie | 3/4 inch (20 mm) | Medium — matchstick thickness |
| Bass, walleye | 1-1.5 inches (25-38 mm) | Sturdy — pencil thickness |
| Catfish, pike, carp | 1.5-2 inches (38-50 mm) | Heavy — thick bone or wire |

## Tools
- Sharpening implements (to fine points).
- Pliers (to bend wire/nails).
- Flame/Heat source (for hardening and tempering).
- Knife (for carving and cutting).
- Sinew, fine cord, or thread (for lashing).

## Process
### The Gorge Hook
1. Select a piece of bone, hardwood, or thorn (1 to 2 inches long).
2. Sharpen both ends to fine points.
3. Carve a shallow groove around the center for the line.
4. Tie the line to the center groove with a clove hitch or constrictor knot.
5. Bury the gorge lengthwise inside the bait.
- **Important Tip:** Let the fish run with the bait for 5-10 seconds before pulling the line taut.

### Thorn Hooks
1. Cut the thorn with a section of the branch attached to provide a natural shank.
2. Trim the branch section to about 1 inch for the shank.
3. Carve a notch or groove in the shank end for line attachment.
4. Harden the thorn tip by briefly holding it near (not in) a flame (10-15 seconds).
5. Tie the line to the shank with a snell knot.

### Compound Hooks
1. Cut a straight piece of hardwood or bone (1.5-2 inches long) for the shank.
2. Flatten or notch the bottom end of the shank.
3. Select a sharp point (thorn, bone splinter, or sharpened nail).
4. Lash the point to the bottom of the shank at a 30-45 degree angle using sinew, fine cord, or thread.
5. Apply pine resin or birch tar over the lashing to waterproof and strengthen the joint.
6. Carve a groove at the top of the shank for line attachment.
- **Angle Rule:** Aim for 30-45 degrees from the shank; too shallow (<20 degrees) and the hook won't set; too steep (>60 degrees) and the fish can lever it free.

### Metal Salvage Hooks
- **Safety pins:** Open the pin, bend the non-pointed end into a small eye for line attachment.
- **Wire and nails:** Heat the nail or wire until red, then bend to a J-shape with pliers or by hammering around a cylindrical form, then file or grind the point sharp.
- **Soda can tabs:** Snap off the tab, cut one side open with a knife to create a hook shape, and sharpen the cut end.
- **Paper clips:** Unbend and reform into a hook shape.

### Tempering Metal Hooks
- Heat the nail or wire until red.
- Bend to a J-shape.
- Plunge into water to harden.
- Gently reheat until the metal turns straw-yellow (approx. 400°F / 200°C) and let it air cool.

## Carrying and Storage
- Primitive hooks should be produced in extras during downtime.
- Store bone and thorn hooks dry to prevent moisture from weakening the materials.
- Store hooks by sticking them into bark or a folded piece of leather.

## Key Takeaways
- Gorge hooks are the fastest to make and most effective for fish that swallow bait.
- Hawthorn and honey locust thorns provide ready-made hook points; harden tips briefly near flame.
- Compound hooks (wood shank + thorn point at 30-45 degrees) offer the best balance of speed and effectiveness.
- Salvaged safety pins, wire, and nails can be bent into serviceable metal hooks.
- Always make spare hooks during downtime.
