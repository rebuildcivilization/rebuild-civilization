# Storage Vessels: Water Storage Containers

## Objective
Collecting clean water solves only half the problem; the storage vessel determines whether the water stays safe to drink. The objective is to cover every viable storage option, ranging from salvaged modern containers to vessels built from scratch.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
To ensure safe water storage, a good vessel must meet five criteria:
1. **Watertight**
2. **Opaque or covered** (to prevent algae growth)
3. **Sealable** (to prevent contamination and mosquito breeding)
4. **Non-toxic** (to prevent leaching chemicals)
5. **Appropriately sized** (to match storage needs and mobility)

## Material
*   **For Fired Clay Vessels:** Clay (with low organic content), temper material (sand, crusite shell, or grog), water, heat source, beeswax, pine pitch, birch tar, lime, and sand.
*   **For Wooden Barrels/Casks:** Large-diameter hardwood (oak, chestnut), fire for charring, pine pitch, beeswax, or clay.
*   **For Stone Cisterns:** Flat stones, lime-and-sand mortar or puddled clay (15-20 cm thick), slaked lime, and fine sand.

## System
The system involves selecting the correct container based on water quality needs, ensuring adequate redundancy through multiple vessels, and positioning the storage system strategically.

## Technology
*   **Fired Clay Vessel Technology:** The coil method is the most reliable technique for forming large water storage vessels.
*   **Hollowing Technique:** Charring the interior of wooden logs before scraping provides an activated charcoal effect, improving water quality.
*   **Gravity-Fed Flow:** Elevating storage vessels on a platform allows for gravity-fed dispensing, eliminating the need for lifting or pumping.
*   **Natural Filtration:** The evaporative cooling effect achieved by porous fired clay vessels keeps water cool.

## Tools
*   **Forming Tools:** Flat stone or paddle (for smoothing clay), adze or chisel (for hollowing wood).
*   **Heat Sources:** Open fire or kiln (for firing clay).
*   **Measuring/Cleaning Tools:** Brush, cloth on a stick, handful of gravel and water (for scrubbing), ladle, dedicated dipper, drill or bore tool (for tap installation).
*   **Sealing Tools:** Beeswax, pine pitch, or silicone (if available).

## Process
### Cleaning Salvaged Containers
1. **Rinse:** Fill with the hottest water and let sit for 30 minutes. Drain and repeat.
2. **Scrub:** Use a brush, cloth on a stick, or gravel/water to physically scrub interior surfaces.
3. **Sanitize:** Rinse with a mild bleach solution (1 tablespoon per gallon) for 10 minutes, or fill with boiling water.
4. **Dry:** Invert and air-dry completely before filling with storage water.

### Building Storage Vessels From Scratch
**Fired Clay Vessels (Coil Method):**
1. **Prepare Clay:** Dig, remove debris, and mix with 15-25% temper material.
2. **Form Vessel:** Roll clay into ropes, coil them upward, smooth the interior and exterior, and form a narrow neck.
3. **Dry Slowly:** Let dry in shade for 3-7 days, turning periodically until completely dry.
4. **Fire:** Build a fire around and over the vessel, maintaining high heat for 2-4 hours. Cool slowly in the dying fire.

**Hollowed Log Method:**
1. **Select Log:** Minimum 40 cm diameter, straight-grained hardwood.
2. **Hollow Interior:** Char the interior with fire, then scrape out the charred wood until walls are 3-5 cm thick.
3. **Seal Bottom:** Cut a flat disk to fit the bottom and seal it with pine pitch, beeswax, or clay, or leave it on a flat stone base sealed with clay.
4. **Cover:** Cut or carve a lid to keep out insects and debris.

**Stone Cistern Construction:**
1. **Excavate:** Dig to desired capacity (e.g., 2 m x 2 m x 1.5 m for 6,000 liters).
2. **Line:** Stack flat stones against walls, mortared with lime-and-sand mortar or packed with puddled clay (15-20 cm thick).
3. **Plaster:** Apply 2-3 coats of thick lime plaster to the interior walls and floor, allowing each coat to dry.
4. **Cover:** Build a solid cover (stone slab, timber frame, or thatch) with an access opening.
5. **Install Overflow:** Build a spillway or pipe near the top to carry excess water away.

### Emergency and Portable Storage
*   **Carried Jugs:** 5-20 L, short range (< 500 m).
*   **Shoulder Yoke:** 20-30 L, medium range (< 1 km).
*   **Animal-drawn Cart:** 100-500 L, long range (km+).
*   **Bamboo Canteen:** Natural sealed containers (1-3 L) made by cutting a section of bamboo between two nodes, sealed with a stopper, and using a small hole near the top for filling.

## Notes
- The container matters as much as the water source; a contaminated vessel ruins clean water within hours.
- For salvaged containers, HDPE drums and IBC totes marked as food-grade are the most reliable post-collapse option. Industrial chemical drums must be avoided.
- The dedicated dipper rule is critical: never use unwashed hands or shared cups, requiring a dedicated ladle or a tap/spigot for contamination prevention.
- Redundancy is key: multiple medium containers provide better resilience than relying on a single large vessel.
- Positioning storage should prioritize elevation, shade, and separation from contamination sources (latrines, waste), ideally uphill from them.
