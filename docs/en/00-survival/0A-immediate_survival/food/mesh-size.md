# Mesh Size Selection

## Objective
To explain the principles, methods, and rules for selecting the appropriate mesh size when making a gill net to ensure a successful catch.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Before making a net, the following steps are prerequisites:
1. Observe the water (sit quietly by the water at dawn or dusk for 30 minutes).
2. Test fish with a hook and line to determine species and size.
3. Ask locally other survivors about what lives in the waterway.
4. Examine evidence along the bank (fish bones, dead fish, scales stuck to rocks).

## Material
*   Gill net (the primary tool).
*   Mesh (the opening in the net).
*   Cordage (used to tie the net).

## System
The gill net catches fish by their gill covers. The mesh must be large enough for the fish’s head to push through but small enough that its body cannot follow. When the fish tries to reverse, its gill covers flare out and lock in the mesh.

## Technology
### Mesh Size Calculation
Mesh size is the stretched distance between two opposite knots in one diamond-shaped opening. The mesh gauge (the flat piece of wood wrapped around the line) is half the mesh size. (Example: A 2.5 cm (1 inch) gauge produces 5 cm (2 inch) mesh.)

### The 30% Rule
A practical rule of thumb is that mesh size should be approximately 30% of the target fish’s body length. The actual relationship is between mesh size and the fish’s maximum head circumference.

### Conversion Reference (Gauge Width Estimation)
| Body Part | Approximate Width |
| :--- | :--- |
| Adult fingertip | 1.5-2 cm |
| Adult thumb width | 2-2.5 cm |
| Two fingers together | 3-4 cm |
| Three fingers together | 5-6 cm |
| Four fingers (palm width) | 7-9 cm |
| Full hand span (thumb to pinky, spread) | 18-22 cm |

### Target Species Sizing
| Target Fish | Body Length | Recommended Mesh Size | Gauge Width |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Minnows, shiners, small baitfish | 5-10 cm (2-4 in) | 2-2.5 cm (3/4-1 in) | 1-1.25 cm |
| Bluegill, perch, small panfish | 12-18 cm (5-7 in) | 3-4 cm (1.25-1.5 in) | 1.5-2 cm |
| Trout (small to medium) | 20-30 cm (8-12 in) | 4-5 cm (1.5-2 in) | 2-2.5 cm |
| Bass, walleye, large trout | 30-45 cm (12-18 in) | 6-8 cm (2.5-3 in) | 3-4 cm |
| Carp, catfish, pike | 40-70 cm (16-28 in) | 8-10 cm (3-4 in) | 4-5 cm |
| Large salmon, large catfish | 60-90 cm (24-36 in) | 10-13 cm (4-5 in) | 5-6.5 cm |
| Mullet, herring (coastal) | 20-35 cm (8-14 in) | 5-6 cm (2-2.5 in) | 2.5-3 cm |
| Shrimp, prawns | 8-15 cm (3-6 in) | 1.5-2 cm (1/2-3/4 in) | 0.75-1 cm |

### Multi-Species Strategy
*   **Option 1: Target the Most Common Size:** Size the mesh for the most common fish size in the water.
*   **Option 2: Build Multiple Nets:** Build two nets with different mesh sizes (one smaller, one larger) and set them in different locations or depths.
*   **Option 3: Graduated Mesh:** Build a single net where the mesh size increases from one end to the other (e.g., 3 cm at one end to 8 cm at the other).

## Tools
*   Measuring tools (for precise measurement).
*   A rigid gauge (flat piece of wood).

## Process
### How Mesh Is Measured
The mesh size is the stretched distance between two opposite knots in one diamond-shaped opening.

### How to Determine What’s In Your Water
1. **Observe:** Sit quietly by the water at dawn or dusk for 30 minutes, watching for surface splashes, fish jumping, shadows in shallow water, and fish holding in current seams or near structure.
2. **Test Fish:** Catch a few fish by hand line, measure them to determine species and size.
3. **Ask Locally:** Consult other survivors who have been fishing the same water.
4. **Examine Evidence:** Look along the bank for fish bones, dead fish washed up, or scales stuck to rocks.

### Testing Your Mesh Size
1. **Catch Test:** Hold the net mesh against a fish. The head should push through the opening while the body does not. The gill covers should catch on the mesh when pulling the fish backward.
2. **Deployment Test:** Set a short section of net (1-2 meters) in a likely spot for 12 hours and check what was caught and what was missed.
3. **Correction:** If nothing is caught after 24 hours in an area where fish exist, the mesh is wrong. If signs of fish escaping are present, go to a smaller mesh size.

## Notes
*   **Common Mistakes:**
    *   **Too large mesh:** Beginners overestimate fish size, leading to zero catch.
    *   **Inconsistent mesh:** Variation in mesh openings due to a slipping gauge or changing tension.
    *   **Not accounting for material stretch:** Natural fiber nets stretch 10-15% when wet; mesh should be tied slightly smaller than the target.
    *   **Setting the net in the wrong depth:** Matching the net depth and position to the behavior of the target species is crucial.

*   **Key Takeaways:**
    *   Mesh size is the single most important variable in gill net fishing.
    *   Use the 30% rule as a reliable approximation.
    *   When in doubt, go slightly smaller.
    *   Always observe the water before building a net.
