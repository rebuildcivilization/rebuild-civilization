# Finding Fish | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To determine where fish concentrate by understanding the overlap of the four requirements fish need: Food, Oxygen, Temperature, and Shelter.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
The prerequisite for finding fish is observing the intersection of the four fundamental needs of the fish, as they are not distributed randomly but follow predictable patterns based on food, oxygen, temperature, and shelter.

## Material
### The Four Things Fish Need
Fish require four elements to thrive, and concentration occurs where these overlap.

1. **Food**
Fish eat insects, smaller fish, crustaceans, worms, algae, and organic debris. Food concentrates at:
    * Stream inflows (where tributaries or runoff enter a larger body, carrying insects and nutrients)
    * Overhanging vegetation (trees and bushes dropping insects)
    * Weed beds (harboring insect larvae, snails, and small crustaceans)
    * Rocky bottoms (algae growing on rocks, attracting grazers, which attract predators)
    * Downstream of obstacles (rocks, logs, and bridge pilings trapping drifting food in eddies)

2. **Oxygen**
Fish breathe dissolved oxygen. Oxygen levels are highest where:
    * Water moves (rapids, riffles, waterfalls, and wave-washed shores)
    * Plants photosynthesize (weed beds during daylight hours, noting that plants consume oxygen at night)
    * Wind blows across the surface (the windward shore of a lake has higher oxygen)
    * Water is cool (cold water holds more dissolved oxygen)

*Low-Oxygen Zones: Stagnant water in hot weather can become oxygen-depleted. Fish will concentrate near any inflow or surface disturbance that adds oxygen.*

3. **Temperature**
Fish metabolism and location shift with water temperature. Temperature influences where fish go across seasons:

| Season | Where Fish Go | Why |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Early spring | Shallow, sun-warmed areas | Warming water activates metabolism |
| Late spring | Near spawning areas (gravel beds, weed margins) | Reproduction |
| Summer | Deeper water, shaded areas, near inflows | Seeking cooler temperatures and oxygen |
| Autumn | Shallow again, feeding aggressively | Building reserves before winter |
| Winter | Deep pools, slow water | Conserving energy in cold water |

*Thermal Layers in Lakes: In summer, lakes develop layers. Warm water sits on top (epilimnion), cold water sits on the bottom (hypolimnion), and a transition zone (thermocline) sits between them at roughly 3-6 meters depth in most temperate lakes. Most fish concentrate at or just above the thermocline where temperature and oxygen balance.*

4. **Shelter**
Fish need places to hide from predators, rest out of current, and ambush prey. Prime shelter locations include:
    * Submerged logs and fallen trees
    * Undercut banks (where current has eroded beneath the bank)
    * Deep pools below rapids or waterfalls
    * Boulder fields and rock piles
    * Weed bed edges (the transition zone, not the middle)
    * Bridge pilings, docks, and other man-made structures
    * Overhanging banks and root tangles

### Reading Different Water Bodies
**Rivers and Streams**
Fish in moving water seek spots where they can rest in slow water while food drifts past in fast water nearby.

| Priority Spots | Location | Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Pool tailouts | Where a deep pool shallows out before the next riffle. |
| 2 | Behind large rocks | The cushion of slow water immediately downstream of boulders. |
| 3 | Undercut banks on outside bends | Current carves deep holes on the outside of river bends. |
| 4 | Confluence points | Where two streams meet, food from both channels converges. |
| 5 | Below waterfalls and dams | Plunge pools hold fish that cannot or will not pass the barrier. |

**Lakes and Ponds**
Without current, look for structure and depth transitions.

| Priority Areas | Location | Reason |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 | Points and peninsulas | Land that juts into the lake creates depth changes and current deflection. |
| 2 | Weed bed edges | The line where weeds end and open water begins, acting as a highway for predatory fish. |
| 3 | Inflows and outflows | Creates a zone of higher oxygen, cooler temperature, and concentrated food. |
| 4 | Drop-offs | Where shallow water suddenly becomes deep (visible as a color change). |
| 5 | Windward shores | Wind pushes surface water toward one shore; baitfish follow. |

**Tidal and Coastal Waters**
* Incoming tide: Fish move into estuaries, tidal creeks, and shallows to feed.
* Outgoing tide: Fish retreat to deeper channels; target narrow outlets where water funnels out.
* Rock pools: At low tide, pools trapped between rocks hold small fish, crabs, and shellfish.
* Estuary mouths: Extremely productive zones where fresh and salt water mix.

### Time of Day Patterns
Fish activity follows predictable daily cycles:

| Time | Activity Level | Best Strategy |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Dawn (first light to 1 hour after sunrise) | High — peak feeding | Shallow water, near cover edges |
| Mid-morning | Moderate, declining | Transition zones, slightly deeper |
| Midday | Low in summer, moderate in cool weather | Deep shade, cool inflows |
| Late afternoon | Increasing | Fish moving toward shallows |
| Dusk (1 hour before sunset to dark) | High — second feeding peak | Same as dawn, but often more active |
| Night | Variable — some species very active | Shallows, torch spearing effective |

### Signs That Fish Are Present
Indicators of fish presence include:
* Surface rings: Concentric circles from a fish rising to take an insect.
* Jumping or splashing: Fish chasing prey or fleeing predators at the surface.
* Birds working the water: Herons standing still, kingfishers diving, cormorants swimming.
* Baitfish schools: Shimmering clouds of small fish near the surface indicating nearby predators.
* Bubbles: Rising bubble trails in still water indicating bottom-feeding fish disturbing sediment.
* Shadows on the bottom: Fish shadows on the substrate in clear water with sun overhead.
* Disturbed sediment: Muddy plumes in clear water suggesting fish rooting on the bottom.

### Seasonal Migration Patterns
* **Spring spawning runs**: Fish move upstream into tributaries, showing incredible concentrations in small streams for 2-4 weeks.
* **Summer dispersal**: Fish spread out, moving to wherever temperature and oxygen suit them.
* **Autumn feeding runs**: Fish feed aggressively before winter, found in food-rich shallows.
* **Winter concentration**: Fish gather in the deepest, most stable pools, where a single deep hole might hold dozens of fish.

## System
The system relies on identifying the intersection of four critical environmental factors to locate fish.

## Technology
The knowledge base is presented through a structured wiki format, utilizing images (SVG and photographic) to illustrate concepts.

## Tools
The primary tool is observation, supplemented by the structured knowledge of the four needs and specific water body characteristics.

## Process
The process involves:
1. Identifying the desired water body type (River/Stream, Lake/Pond, Tidal/Coastal).
2. Assessing the four needs (Food, Oxygen, Temperature, Shelter) within that environment.
3. Applying the time-of-day patterns to determine peak activity.
4. Reading the specific location priority lists for that water body type.
5. Looking for explicit signs of presence.
