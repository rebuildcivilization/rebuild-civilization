# Bone Hooks

## Objective
To provide the most durable primitive alternative to metal hooks when metal hooks are unavailable.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Bone Quality:** Dense cortical (outer) bone at least 3 mm thick, a flat cross-section that can be scored and shaped, and minimal porosity.
*   **Bone Condition:** Fresh or recently cleaned bone works best. Old sun-bleached bone becomes brittle and cracks during shaping. If old bone is used, soak it in water for 24-48 hours to restore some flexibility.

## Material
*   **Primary Material:** Dense leg bones from medium to large mammals (deer, elk, cattle, horse).
*   **Secondary Materials:** Rib bones (deer, elk, cattle), fish jawbones, and opercular bones (gill covers) from medium to large fish.
*   **Tertiary Material:** Antler.

**Bone Source Quality Summary:**
| Bone Source                          | Quality   | Notes                                   |
| :----------------------------------- | :-------- | :-------------------------------------- |
| Deer/elk leg bones (metapodials)    | Excellent | Dense, flat surfaces, ideal thickness   |
| Cattle/horse leg bones               | Excellent | Large — multiple hooks per bone         |
| Large bird leg bones (turkey, goose) | Good      | Thin-walled but very hard                |
| Rib bones (any large mammal)         | Good      | Naturally curved, easy to shape           |
| Antler                               | Fair      | Very hard to work but extremely durable  |
| Fish jaw bones                       | Good      | Already the right size for small hooks  |
| Pig bones                            | Fair      | More porous than deer, still usable     |

**Bone Sources to Avoid:** Skull fragments (too thin and curved unpredictably), vertebrae (too porous), and any bones that are dry-rotted or crumbling.

## System
*   **Core Concept:** Peak of primitive fish hook technology—durable, sharp, and capable of landing fish that would straighten a thorn or snap a wooden hook.

## Technology
*   **Durability:** Bone is harder than wood and holds a point better than a thorn.
*   **Shape Capacity:** Bone can be shaped into true curved hooks that perform on par with modern metal designs.
*   **Archaeological Proof:** Bone hooks have been in continuous use from 23,000 years ago through the modern era.

## Tools
*   **Abrasives:** Sandstone, granite, concrete.
*   **Scoring/Shaping Tools:** Sharp flint flake, obsidian edge, metal blade.
*   **Cutting/Drilling Tools:** Hand drill, heated wire.
*   **Finishing Tools:** Fine-grained stone or sand.
*   **Treatment Agents:** Animal fat or oil (for optional soaking).

## Process
### Gorge Hook (Bone Version)
*   **Total Production Time:** 15-30 minutes.
*   **Steps:**
    1. Select a splinter of dense bone (1-2 inches long, 25-50 mm).
    2. Sharpen both ends to fine points using an abrasive stone.
    3. Grind a shallow groove around the center for line attachment.
    4. Smooth the surface to remove any rough edges that could fray the line.
*   **Sharpening Technique:** Hold the bone at a 15-20 degree angle against a flat abrasive stone. Draw the tip across the stone in one direction (away from you), rotating slightly with each stroke to create a conical point.

### Curved Hook
*   **Production Time:** 1-3 hours depending on tools and experience.
*   **Step 1: Score the Outline:**
    *   **Material Required:** Flat piece of bone at least 1.5 inches (38 mm) across and 3-4 mm thick (best source: flat face of a deer or cattle leg bone, split lengthwise).
    *   **Goal Dimensions:** Shank about 1 inch (25 mm), bend radius about 1/4 inch (6 mm), point extending back about 1/2 inch (12 mm).
    *   **Technique:** Score deeply to cut through at least half the bone thickness; score both sides if the bone is thick enough.
*   **Step 2: Remove Excess Material:**
    *   **Method A (Snap and grind):** Score additional lines, snap away excess bone by flexing along score lines, grind remaining rough edges.
    *   **Method B (Abrasion):** Grind the entire piece on a flat sandstone surface, use a thin stone edge or wire to open the inside curve.
    *   **Method C (Drill and snap):** Drill a small hole at the inside top of the hook curve, then snap out the inner curve material.
*   **Step 3: Refine the Shape:**
    1. Grind the entire hook to even thickness (about 2-3 mm throughout).
    2. Sharpen the point using the gorge technique (15-20 degree angle on stone).
    3. **Carve a barb:** Score a small line 3-4 mm below the point on the inside of the curve, then carefully lift a small flap of bone outward (skip if the bone is too brittle).
    4. **Shape the eye:** Grind a groove around the top of the shank, or drill a small hole through it for line attachment.
*   **Step 4: Final Treatment:**
    1. Polish the entire hook on fine-grained stone or sand.
    2. Optionally soak in animal fat or oil overnight to add water resistance.
    3. **Test:** Hook it over a stick and pull with fishing line at full expected force.

### Rib Bone Hooks
*   **Advantage:** Utilizes the natural curve of the rib bone.
*   **Construction:**
    1. Cut a 2-3 inch section of rib that includes a natural curve.
    2. Split the rib lengthwise to reduce thickness if needed.
    3. Sharpen the bottom end to a point.
    4. Grind a line groove at the top.
*   **Total Time:** 30-60 minutes.

### Fish Bone Hooks
*   **Material:** Jawbones and opercular bones of medium to large fish.
*   **Process:** Save jawbones from caught fish; trim and sharpen as needed. These bones often contain a natural hook-shaped curve.

### Production Strategy
*   **Batch Production:** Do not make hooks one at a time as needed.
    *   Make 5-10 gorge hooks in an hour (consumables).
    *   Make 2-3 curved hooks in an afternoon (primary tools).
*   **Storage:** Store hooks in a leather pouch or stuck into a piece of bark.
*   **Inventory:** Keep one spare curved hook ready at all times.

## Notes
*   **Common Failures and Fixes:**
    *   **Hook snaps at the bend:** Cause is bone too thin or too dry. Fix: Use thicker bone; soak dry bone before carving.
    *   **Point dulls quickly:** Cause is porous bone or wrong grinding angle. Fix: Select denser bone; sharpen at 15-20 degrees.
    *   **Barb breaks off:** Cause is carving too aggressively. Fix: Make the barb smaller, or use a barbless design with steady line tension.
    *   **Line slips off shank:** Cause is groove too shallow. Fix: Deepen groove; add a notch or drill a hole.
    *   **Hook straightens under load:** Cause is bone too thin at the bend. Fix: Maintain at least 3 mm thickness at the bend.
*   **Safety:** Bone shards are razor-sharp. Wrap bone when splitting or snapping. Wear eye protection. Bone dust should be managed (work outdoors or with a face covering in enclosed spaces).
*   **Key Takeaways:**
    *   Deer and cattle leg bones provide the densest, most workable material.
    *   Gorge hooks are the fastest and most reliable for immediate use (15-30 minutes).
    *   Curved bone hooks are the most effective primitive design but require significant time (1-3 hours).
    *   Rib bones offer a shortcut for large fish hooks.
    *   Batch-producing spares is essential because losing a hook means losing fishing capability.
