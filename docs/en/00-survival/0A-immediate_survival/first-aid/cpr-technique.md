# CPR Technique | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To perform CPR when someone’s heart stops, which occurs when there are about 4-6 minutes before brain damage begins.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Scene safety must be ensured before starting CPR.
- Confirmation of cardiac arrest must occur within 10 seconds.
- Training and willingness to give rescue breaths are prerequisites for effective rescue breathing.

## Material
- **Cardiac Arrest Definition:** The heart has stopped pumping entirely, resulting in the person collapsing, stopping breathing, and having no pulse.
- **Common Causes of Cardiac Arrest in a Post-collapse World:**
    - Drowning (river crossings, fishing accidents)
    - Electrocution (improvised electrical systems)
    - Severe allergic reactions (insect stings, unknown plant ingestion)
    - Choking
    - Hypothermia (cold water immersion, exposure)
    - Trauma and blood loss leading to heart failure
- **Success Rates:** CPR without a defibrillator has a low long-term success rate (roughly 5-10% in the best modern studies), but success rates are significantly higher for drowning and hypothermia victims.

## System
- **Cardiac Arrest vs. Heart Attack:** A heart attack means blood flow to the heart muscle is blocked, and the person is usually conscious and in pain. Cardiac arrest means the heart has stopped pumping entirely.
- **Fatigue Management:** Effective CPR is physically exhausting, and quality compressions degrade significantly after about 2 minutes.

## Technology
- **Defibrillator:** Mentioned as a tool that improves success rates (though not required for basic CPR).

## Tools
- Hands (for compressions and airway management).
- Clock/Timer (to track the 10-second assessment).

## Process
### Assessment: The 10-Second Check
1. **Ensure scene safety:** Check for electrical hazards, unstable structures, or ongoing threats.
2. **Tap and Shout:** Tap the person’s shoulders firmly and shout their name or “Are you okay?” (No response means unconscious).
3. **Check the Chest (10 seconds):** Watch for rise and fall. Occasional gasping (agonal breathing) is NOT normal breathing—treat it as no breathing.
4. **Optional Pulse Check:** Place two fingers on the side of the neck in the groove between the windpipe and the large neck muscle. If no pulse within 10 seconds, begin CPR.

### Hand Placement
1. **Placement:** Place the heel of the dominant hand on the center of the chest, on the lower half of the breastbone (sternum), aligning hands between the nipples.
2. **Compression:** Place the other hand directly on top. Interlace fingers and pull upward so only the heel of the bottom hand contacts the chest.
3. **Body Mechanics:** Position shoulders directly over hands and lock elbows. Compress using body weight, not arm strength.

### Compression Technique
- **Depth:** Push the chest down at least 5 cm (2 inches) but no more than 6 cm (2.4 inches).
- **Rate:** 100-120 compressions per minute (count out loud: “one-and-two-and-three-and-four…”).
- **Recoil:** Allow the chest to come fully back up between compressions. Do not lean on the chest.
- **Interruptions:** Keep pauses under 10 seconds.

| Common Error | Why It Fails | Correction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Too shallow (<5 cm) | Blood is not adequately circulated | Push harder — use body weight |
| Too fast (>120/min) | Chest cannot fully recoil between compressions | Count out loud, maintain rhythm |
| Leaning on chest | Heart cannot refill, reducing output by 25-50% | Lift hands slightly between compressions |
| Hands too high | Compresses upper sternum, not the heart | Recheck nipple-line landmark |
| Bent elbows | Arms fatigue in 1-2 minutes | Lock elbows, compress from shoulders |

### Rescue Breathing
- **Cycle:** 30 compressions, then 2 breaths. Repeat.
- **Airway Management:**
    1. Tilt head back, lift chin with two fingers to open the airway.
    2. Pinch the nose shut.
    3. Seal mouth completely over theirs and deliver one breath over 1 second, watching for chest rise.
    4. Give a second breath (1 second), then resume compressions.
- **Airway Check:** If the chest does not rise, suspect a blocked airway; perform 30 compressions, check the mouth for visible objects, then attempt breaths again.
- **Hands-Only CPR:** Effective when breaths are not given, especially in the first 5-10 minutes.

### Special Situations
- **Drowning Victims:** Always give rescue breaths. Give 5 initial rescue breaths before starting the 30:2 cycle.
- **Hypothermia:** CPR may need to continue for over an hour while rewarming occurs (since cold slows brain metabolism).
- **Children (ages 1-8):** Use one hand for compressions; compress approximately one-third of the chest depth (about 5 cm); give 2 initial rescue breaths before starting compressions, then use a 30:2 ratio.
- **Infants (under 1 year):** Use two fingers (index and middle) placed just below the nipple line; compress one-third of chest depth (about 4 cm); cover both the nose and mouth with your mouth for breaths; use a 30:2 ratio.

### Managing Fatigue
- **Relief:** If a second rescuer is available, switch every 2 minutes (approximately 5 cycles of 30:2), aiming for under 5 seconds of interruption.
- **Body Use:** Use the core and body weight, not the arms.
- **When Alone:** Compress as long as physically possible, as even degraded compressions are better than none.

### When to Stop
Stop CPR when:
1. The person starts breathing normally and has a pulse (move them to the Recovery Position).
2. You are physically unable to continue and no one can relieve you.
3. The scene becomes unsafe.
4. It has been 30 minutes or more with no response (no breathing, no pulse, no signs of life at any point, unless hypothermia is present).

## Key Takeaways
- Start compressions within 10 seconds of confirming no normal breathing (speed matters more than perfection).
- Push hard (5 cm), push fast (100-120/min), let the chest fully recoil, and minimize interruptions.
- Broken ribs are expected and acceptable.
- Add rescue breaths (30:2 ratio) when possible, especially for drowning and child victims.
- If the person recovers, immediately place them in the Recovery Position and monitor continuously.
