# Severity Assessment

## Objective
Before you can treat a burn, you must accurately assess how deep it goes and how much of the body it covers. These two factors determine whether the injury will heal on its own or become life-threatening.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Accurate assessment of burn depth and body coverage is required before treatment can begin.

## Material
### Burn Depth Classification
Burns are classified by how deeply they penetrate the skin layers. Assessment relies on the appearance, sensation, and blister characteristics.

**First-Degree (Superficial)**
*   **What is damaged:** Only the outermost layer of skin (epidermis).
*   **What you see:** Red skin similar to a sunburn, no blisters, skin blanches (turns white) when pressed, then returns to red.
*   **What the patient feels:** Pain and tenderness to touch, mild to moderate burning sensation.
*   **Healing time:** 3-7 days without scarring.
*   **Example injuries:** Brief contact with a hot pot, mild sunburn, quick splash of hot (not boiling) water.

**Second-Degree (Partial Thickness)**
*   **What is damaged:** The epidermis and part of the dermis (the thicker layer beneath containing nerves, blood vessels, and hair follicles).
*   **What you see:** Bright red or mottled red/white appearance, blisters (small scattered to large fluid-filled sheets), wet, weeping surface when blisters break, skin may blanch when pressed.
*   **What the patient feels:** Intense pain (most painful burn depth) because nerve endings in the dermis are exposed but not destroyed, extreme sensitivity to air movement and temperature.
*   **Healing time:** 2-4 weeks for superficial second-degree. Deep second-degree burns may take 4-8 weeks and will likely scar.
*   **Example injuries:** Scalding with boiling water, contact with hot metal for 1-3 seconds, close proximity to open flame.
*   **Tricky Distinction:** Deep second-degree and third-degree burns can look similar initially. Reassess after 48 hours if the burn appears white or pale with reduced pain sensation, as a second-degree burn may convert to third-degree.

**Third-Degree (Full Thickness)**
*   **What is damaged:** The entire epidermis and dermis are destroyed, possibly extending into fat, muscle, or bone.
*   **What you see:** White, waxy, leathery, or charred appearance, no blisters (skin structure too damaged to form them), skin does not blanch when pressed, visible blood vessels may be seen through the destroyed skin (thrombosed, appearing as dark lines), hair pulls out easily.
*   **What the patient feels:** Paradoxically, little or no pain in the burn center because the nerve endings are destroyed, severe pain at the edges where the burn transitions to second-degree.
*   **Healing time:** Cannot heal on its own except from the edges inward, which takes months and results in severe scarring and contracture. Requires skin grafts in modern medicine.
*   **Example injuries:** Prolonged contact with flame, immersion in boiling liquid, contact with molten metal, electrical burns.

### Quick Reference Comparison
| Feature | First-Degree | Second-Degree | Third-Degree |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Color | Red | Red, mottled, pink | White, waxy, charred |
| Blisters | None | Yes | None |
| Moisture | Dry | Wet, weeping | Dry, leathery |
| Pain | Moderate | Severe | Minimal in center |
| Blanching | Yes | Yes | No |
| Texture | Normal | Soft, swollen | Firm, leathery |
| Healing | 3-7 days | 2-8 weeks | Months, with scarring |

### Estimating Burn Area: The Rule of Nines
**Adult Body Surface Percentages (Rule of Nines)**
*   Head and neck: 9%
*   Each arm (entire): 9%
*   Chest (front): 9%
*   Abdomen (front): 9%
*   Upper back: 9%
*   Lower back: 9%
*   Each upper leg (front and back): 9%
*   Each lower leg (front and back): 9%
*   Groin/perineum: 1%
*   **Total:** 100%

**The Palm Method (For Irregular Burns)**
For scattered or irregular burns, one palm equals approximately **1% of their body surface area**.

**Children Are Different**
Children have proportionally larger heads and smaller legs:
*   Child Under 5 Head: 18% (Adult is 9%)
*   Child Under 5 Each Leg: 14% (Adult is 18%)
*   A child’s head burn represents twice the body surface area of an adult’s.

### Severity Classification by Area
*   **Minor burn:** First-degree burn of any size; Second-degree burn less than 10% TBSA in adults, less than 5% in children; No third-degree involvement.
*   **Moderate burn:** Second-degree burn 10-20% TBSA; Third-degree burn less than 5% TBSA; Burns on face, hands, feet, or joints (regardless of size).
*   **Critical burn (Life-threatening):** Second-degree burn greater than 20% TBSA; Third-degree burn greater than 5% TBSA; Burns involving airway; Circumferential burns; Burns combined with other major trauma.

### Survival Statistics Without Modern Medicine
| Burn Severity | Survival Without Modern Care |
| :--- | :--- | 
| Less than 10% TBSA, second-degree | Greater than 90% with good wound care |
| 10-20% TBSA, second-degree | 70-80% with aggressive fluid and wound management |
| 20-40% TBSA, mixed depth | 30-50% --- infection is the primary killer |
| Greater than 40% TBSA | Less than 10% --- focus on comfort care |

### The 48-Hour Reassessment
Burns evolve; reassessment is critical:
*   **6 hours:** Initial swelling peaks, true extent becomes clearer.
*   **24 hours:** Blisters have fully formed, depth is more apparent.
*   **48 hours:** Final depth classification is usually possible.
*   **During reassessment, look for:** Areas that have lost sensation (deepening to third-degree), blisters that have collapsed and revealed white or pale wound beds, increasing firmness or leathery texture, and loss of blanching response.

### Circumferential Burns
A burn that wraps entirely around a limb, finger, or the chest is especially dangerous. As the burned tissue swells, the inelastic damaged skin acts like a tourniquet, cutting off blood flow. Hourly circulation checks are required to feel for pulse, check for numbness, and observe color. If circulation is compromised and no other help is available, the constricting burned tissue may need to be cut (escharotomy).

## System
The system classifies burns based on two primary factors: Burn Depth (First, Second, Third Degree) and Burn Area (Severity: Minor, Moderate, Critical).

## Technology
The technology used for assessment is the empirical observation of physical characteristics (color, texture, sensation, blisters, blanching) rather than requiring a medical degree.

## Tools
*   The Rule of Nines (for adult body surface area estimation).
*   The Palm Method (for scattered/irregular burn area estimation).
*   The 48-Hour Reassessment schedule.

## Process
1.  **Assess Depth:** Determine the burn depth by examining color, blisters, moisture, pain level, and blanching response to classify it as First, Second, or Third Degree.
2.  **Estimate Area:** Determine the body surface area percentage using the Rule of Nines (for adults) or the Palm Method (for irregular burns).
3.  **Classify Severity:** Combine depth and area to classify the burn as Minor, Moderate, or Critical.
4.  **Reassess:** Periodically check the burn status at 6, 24, and 48 hours to monitor for changes in depth.

Notes
- Burn depth is determined by color, blisters, moisture, pain level, and blanching, not the cause of the burn.
- Second-degree burns are the most painful; third-degree burns are paradoxically painless in the center.
- Burns can deepen as damaged tissue dies, necessitating reassessment.
- Circumferential burns around a limb are an emergency requiring hourly circulation checks and potential escharotomy.
