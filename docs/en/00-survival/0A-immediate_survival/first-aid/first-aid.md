# First Aid

## Objective
To keep people alive in post-medical-infrastructure settings by managing wounds, fractures, CPR, burns, and shock to prevent recoverable injuries from becoming fatal ones.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
First aid starts with the imperative: clean hands, clean water, and clean materials.

## Material
### Basic first aid supplies (scavenge or improvise):
*   Clean water (boiled and cooled)
*   Clean cloth strips (torn shirts, sheets — boil if possible)
*   Straight, rigid sticks (for splints)
*   Cordage or fabric strips (for securing splints)
*   Honey (natural antibacterial for wound dressing, if available)
*   Alcohol or strong spirits (wound cleaning, NOT for drinking during an emergency)
*   A sharp, clean knife or blade

### Improvised supplies:
*   Sphagnum moss (naturally antibacterial, absorbs 20x its weight in fluid)
*   Spider webs (contain antimicrobial compounds)
*   Clean plastic bags (for burn coverage, waterproof wound protection)
*   Charcoal paste (activated charcoal for potential poisoning)
*   Sticks, bark, rolled magazine/newspaper, or trekking poles (for splint material)
*   Large triangle of cloth or buttoned shirt (for a sling)
*   Belt, strip of cloth (4 cm+ wide), or rubber tubing (for tourniquet)
*   Fishing line with a curved needle (for emergency suture)
*   Butterfly closures from tape (for emergency suture)

## System
### Core Principle: Clean Hands, Clean Water, Clean Materials
*   Infection is the number one killer in post-medical-infrastructure scenarios. Every first aid intervention must start by making it as clean as possible.
*   Wash hands before touching any wound, even if only rinsing with clean water.
*   Use clean water for wound irrigation.
*   Use the cleanest available cloth for bandages (boiled fabric is best).
*   When in doubt, leaving a wound open and clean is better than wrapping it in dirty material.

### Bleeding Control
**Minor to Moderate Bleeding
1.  **Expose the wound:** Cut or remove clothing to see the injury. Do not pull out embedded objects unless they are plugging a blood vessel.
2.  **Apply direct pressure:** Place a clean cloth pad over the wound and press firmly. Pressure should feel uncomfortable.
3.  **Maintain pressure:** Hold pressure for a minimum of 15 minutes without lifting the pad. Add more pads if the first one soaks through.
4.  **Secure:** Secure the pad with a bandage or cloth strip tied firmly but not so tight it cuts off circulation. Elevate the limb if it is a limb wound.

**Severe / Life-Threatening Bleeding (Spurting or fast flow)**
1.  **Apply a tourniquet (LAST RESORT for limbs only):**
    *   Use a strip of cloth, belt, or rope at least 4 cm wide.
    *   Place it 5-8 cm above the wound (between the wound and the heart), NEVER on a joint.
    *   Wrap it around the limb and tie a half-knot.
    *   Place a stick on the half-knot and tie a full knot over the stick.
    *   Twist the stick (windlass) until bleeding stops.
    *   Secure the stick so it cannot unwind and mark the time.
    *   A tourniquet should not stay on longer than 2 hours without medical assessment.
2.  **Warning:** Never apply a tourniquet to the neck, chest, or abdomen.

### Wound Care and Infection Prevention
1.  **Irrigate:** Once bleeding is controlled, flush the wound with clean water using pressure (squirt or pour from height) to flush out dirt and debris.
2.  **Debris Removal:** Remove visible debris with clean fingers or tweezers; stabilize deeply embedded objects.
3.  **Antiseptic Application:** Apply a thin layer of raw honey (hygroscopic, kills bacteria) or a dilute alcohol rinse.
4.  **Cover and Change:** Cover with a clean dressing and change dressings daily or when wet/dirty.
5.  **Infection Monitoring:** Watch for increasing redness, swelling, warmth around the wound, pus (yellow, green, or cloudy discharge), red streaks extending toward the heart (lymphangitis—an emergency), fever, or chills.

### Fractures and Splinting
1.  **Assessment:** Look for severe pain, swelling, deformity, crepitus (grinding feeling), or inability to bear weight.
2.  **Splinting:** Splint the limb in the position found. The splint must immobilize the joint ABOVE and the joint BELOW the fracture.
    *   Use rigid objects (sticks, boards) longer than the injured area.
    *   Pad between the splint and the skin with cloth, moss, or clothing.
    *   Secure splints with cloth strips or cordage at multiple points, above and below the fracture. Do NOT tie directly over the break.
3.  **Check Circulation:** After splinting, check if fingers/toes feel warm and pink. Loosen bindings if circulation is compromised.
4.  **Arm Fractures:** Create a sling from a large triangle of cloth to support the arm.

### Burns
1.  **Remove Source:** Get away from heat and remove hot clothing.
2.  **Cooling:** Cool the burn with clean, cool (not ice-cold) running water for at least 10–20 minutes immediately.
    *   **Warning:** NEVER use ice on a burn. NEVER apply butter, oil, toothpaste, or egg whites.
3.  **Severity Assessment:
    *   **1st Degree:** Red, dry, no blisters. Treatment: Cool water, aloe if available.
    *   **2nd Degree:** Red, blistered, wet-looking. Treatment: Cool water, do NOT pop blisters, cover loosely.
    *   **3rd Degree:** White, brown, or charred; leathery. Treatment: Cover loosely, do NOT remove stuck clothing, monitor for shock.
4.  **Covering:** Cover the burn loosely with a clean, non-stick dressing; change daily.

### CPR Basics (Hands-Only)
1.  **Check Responsiveness:** Tap shoulders and shout. Check for breathing (chest movement for 10 seconds).
2.  **Compressions:** If not breathing, begin chest compressions immediately.
    *   Position: Heel of one hand on the center of the chest (between nipples).
    *   Depth: At least 5 cm (2 inches) deep.
    *   Rate: 100–120 compressions per minute.
3.  **Recoil:** Allow the chest to fully recoil between compressions.
4.  **Rescue Breaths (If known):** Cycle: 30 compressions, 2 breaths (1 second each).
5.  **Continue:** Continue until the person starts breathing, you are physically unable to continue, or 30+ minutes have passed without response.

### Shock Management
**Signs of Shock:** Pale, cool, clammy skin; rapid, weak pulse; rapid, shallow breathing; confusion, anxiety, or unresponsiveness; thirst; nausea.

1.  **Position:** Lay the person flat on their back. Elevate the legs 20–30 cm to direct blood flow to vital organs.
2.  **Keep Warm:** Cover the person with blankets or coats; insulate from the ground.
3.  **Oral Intake:** Do NOT give food or water unless the patient is conscious and the underlying cause is treated.
4.  **Treat Cause:** Stop bleeding, splint fractures, calm the patient.
5.  **Monitor:** If the patient vomits or becomes unconscious, roll them onto their side (recovery position).

### When NOT to Move Someone
**Do NOT move the person if:**
*   They fell from a height or were in a vehicle crash.
*   They complain of neck or back pain.
*   They cannot feel or move their arms or legs.
*   They are unconscious from trauma (assume spinal injury).

**Move them ONLY if:**
*   They are in immediate danger (fire, flood, collapsing structure).
*   You cannot access them to provide first aid.
*   They need to be repositioned to open their airway.
*   **Technique:** Support the head, neck, and spine as a single unit using the log-roll technique.

### Common Mistakes
| Mistake | Why It’s Dangerous | What to Do Instead |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | 
| Removing embedded objects | May be plugging an artery; removal causes uncontrolled bleeding | Stabilize the object in place, bandage around it |
| Applying ice to burns | Causes vasoconstriction, worsens tissue damage, risks frostbite | Cool running water for 10–20 minutes |
| Using a narrow tourniquet | Cuts into tissue, damages nerves and blood vessels | Use material at least 4 cm wide |
| Straightening a broken limb | Can sever blood vessels and nerves at the break site | Splint in the position found unless circulation is lost |
| Not irrigating wounds | Debris and bacteria cause infection even in clean-looking wounds | Flush with pressurized clean water every time |
| Removing blood-soaked bandages | Disrupts clotting, restarts bleeding | Add new layers on top of old ones |
| Popping burn blisters | Removes nature’s sterile bandage, invites infection | Leave blisters intact; clean and cover if they break naturally |
| Moving a spinal injury patient | Bone fragments can sever the spinal cord, causing paralysis | Stabilize in place unless immediate danger requires moving |

## Notes
*   **Golden Rule of Wound Care:** A clean wound that is left open heals better than a dirty wound that is closed.
*   **Infection Alert:** Red streaks extending from a wound toward the heart are an emergency signaling systemic infection.
*   **Environmental Injuries:** Treat heat injuries with cool water; treat cold injuries with warm fluids and rewarming steps (skin-to-skin, warm fluids) for hypothermia.
*   **Systemic Infection:** Herbal antiseptics (garlic poultice, honey, yarrow) may help but are not reliable for systemic infection without antibiotics.

## Technology
*   The knowledge required for successful first aid in a post-medical-infrastructure setting.

## Tools
*   Basic first aid supplies (scavenge or improvise).
*   A tourniquet (for severe bleeding).
*   Rigid objects for splinting.
*   Water purification source (implied dependency).

## Process
*   **Core Imperative:** Clean Hands, Clean Water, Clean Materials.
*   **Bleeding Control Process:** Expose $\rightarrow$ Direct Pressure (15 min) $\rightarrow$ Secure $\rightarrow$ Elevate (limbs) $\rightarrow$ Tourniquet (last resort).
*   **Wound Care Process:** Irrigate $\rightarrow$ Remove debris $\rightarrow$ Apply antiseptic $\rightarrow$ Cover $\rightarrow$ Monitor for infection.
*   **Fracture Management Process:** Assess $\rightarrow$ Splint in position $\rightarrow$ Secure $\rightarrow$ Check circulation.
*   **CPR Process:** Check responsiveness $\rightarrow$ Start compressions (5 cm deep, 100-120/min) $\rightarrow$ Give 2 breaths $\rightarrow$ Repeat.
*   **Shock Management Process:** Position flat $\rightarrow$ Elevate legs $\rightarrow$ Keep warm $\rightarrow$ Treat cause $\rightarrow$ Monitor.

## System
*   The overall system prioritizes immediate, clean interventions to bridge the gap until long-term medical care is possible.

## Technology
*   The underlying technology is the application of physical principles (pressure, cooling, elevation, stabilization) to manage physiological trauma.

## Tools
*   The essential tools are improvised resources (cloth, sticks, moss, honey) when basic tools are absent.

## Process
*   **Core Process:** Following the hierarchy of needs: Cleanliness $\rightarrow$ Control Bleeding $\rightarrow$ Manage Specific Injuries (Fracture, Burn, Shock, CPR) $\rightarrow$ Long-term care.

## Notes
*   **Survival Context:** The entire protocol is optimized for a scenario without hospitals, ambulances, or pharmacies.
*   **Key Differentiator:** The difference between a recoverable injury and a fatal one rests on applying these foundational principles correctly.
