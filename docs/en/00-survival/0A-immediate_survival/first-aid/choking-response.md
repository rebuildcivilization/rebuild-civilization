# Choking Response

## Objective
To provide first aid for a blocked airway, recognizing the different types of obstruction, and applying effective techniques to clear the obstruction, especially when emergency services are unavailable.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Without emergency services to call, hands are the only tool available.

## Material
*   Food, liquid, or a foreign object that blocks the trachea (windpipe).
*   The victim's airway.
*   The surrounding environment (for self-administered techniques).

## System
The respiratory system, specifically the trachea (windpipe) and the lungs.

## Technology
*   Back Blows
*   Abdominal Thrusts (Heimlich Maneuver)
*   Chest Thrusts
*   Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR)

## Tools
*   Hands (for back blows and abdominal thrusts).
*   A hard, narrow surface at waist height (for self-administered thrusts).
*   A fist (for abdominal thrusts).
*   A finger (for clearing the airway when conscious).
*   A chair back, table edge, fence rail, or large rock (for self-administered thrusts).

## Process

### Recognizing Choking
Choking occurs when food, liquid, or a foreign object blocks the trachea. Two types of obstruction exist:

**Mild (Partial) Obstruction:**
*   **Signs:** Coughing forcefully, wheezing or noisy breathing, able to speak (with difficulty), distressed but conscious.
*   **Response:** Do NOT interfere. Encourage them to keep coughing. Stay close, stay calm.

**Severe (Complete) Obstruction:**
*   **Signs:** Cannot cough or cough is silent/weak, cannot speak or make sound, clutching at the throat, face turning red then blue (cyanosis), high-pitched squeaking or no sound, wide panicked eyes, rapid loss of consciousness (60-90 seconds without air).
*   **Response:** Act immediately with the sequence: Back Blows $\rightarrow$ Abdominal Thrusts $\rightarrow$ CPR if unconscious.

**Back Blows (First Response):
**
1.  Stand to the side and slightly behind the person. Have them lean forward at the waist.
2.  Support their chest with one hand. Use the heel of the other hand to deliver 5 sharp blows between the shoulder blades.
3.  Check between each blow. If the object is out, stop. If not, continue.
4.  If 5 back blows fail, move to abdominal thrusts.

**Abdominal Thrusts (Heimlich Maneuver):
**
1.  Stand behind the person and wrap arms around the waist.
2.  Make a fist, place the thumb side against the abdomen, midline, above the navel and below the breastbone (xiphoid process).
3.  Grab the fist with the other hand.
4.  Pull sharply inward and upward in a J-shaped motion, directing force under the ribcage.
5.  Deliver up to 5 thrusts. Check after each one.
6.  If 5 thrusts fail, alternate: 5 back blows, then 5 abdominal thrusts. Continue alternating until the object is expelled, the person can breathe, or they lose consciousness.

**If the Person Loses Consciousness:
**
1.  Lower them carefully to the ground, on their back.
2.  Open the mouth and look inside. If visible, use a finger sweep to remove it.
3.  Begin CPR with chest compressions (5 cm deep, 30 compressions).
4.  After 30 compressions, open the airway (head tilt, chin lift) and check the mouth again.
5.  Attempt 2 rescue breaths. If the chest does not rise, reposition the head and try again. Continue the cycle (30 compressions, check mouth, attempt 2 breaths).

**Special Populations:
*   **Infants (Under 1 Year):**
    *   **Warning:** Do NOT use abdominal thrusts.
    *   **Steps:** 1. Lay face-down along forearm, head lower than chest, support jaw.
    *   2. Deliver 5 back blows with firm force.
    *   3. If back blows fail, turn face-up, sandwich between forearms, place two fingers on the breastbone.
    *   4. Deliver 5 chest thrusts (sharp downward pushes about 4 cm deep).
    *   5. Alternate 5 back blows and 5 chest thrusts until resolved or unconscious.
    *   If unconscious, begin infant CPR.
*   **Pregnant Women and Obese Individuals:**
    *   If wrapping arms around the abdomen is difficult, use **chest thrusts** instead of abdominal thrusts. Perform chest thrusts by wrapping arms around the chest under the armpits, placing the fist on the center of the breastbone, and pulling sharply backward (not upward) up to 5 times.

**Choking While Alone:
1.  Try to cough.
2.  Perform self-administered abdominal thrusts (fist above navel, thrust inward and upward).
3.  If self-thrusts are insufficient, use a hard, narrow surface at waist height and drop weight forcefully.
4.  If losing consciousness, get to the ground safely.

**After the Object Is Removed:
*   If conscious, have the person sit upright and breathe slowly.
*   If rescue breaths were given during CPR and the person resumes breathing, place them in the Recovery Position.
*   Monitor for 24-48 hours for signs of airway swelling, difficulty breathing, or abdominal pain from thrusts.
*   A sore throat and hoarse voice for 1-2 days is normal after a severe episode.

**Prevention in a Survival Setting:
| Risk Factor | Prevention | 
| :--- | :--- | 
| Eating too fast after heavy labor | Force yourself to chew thoroughly despite hunger | 
| Unfamiliar wild foods (tough roots, fibrous plants) | Cut into small pieces, cook until soft | 
| Talking or laughing while eating | Swallow before speaking | 
| Children exploring with mouths | Keep small objects (nuts, berries, buttons, stones) out of reach of children under 4 | 
| Dry or crumbly foods | Drink water between bites to soften food in the mouth | 
| Eating while lying down | Always sit upright to eat | 

## Notes
*   **Key Takeaway:** Mild obstruction (effective coughing) requires the response to be 'do NOT interfere, encourage coughing.'
*   **Warning:** Abdominal thrusts can cause internal injuries (bruised organs, cracked ribs, internal bleeding); monitor for abdominal pain, bloody vomit, and signs of internal bleeding for 24-48 hours after using thrusts.
*   **Conscious Management:** In severe choking where the person loses consciousness, CPR compressions serve the double duty of circulating blood and generating airway pressure.
