# Stretcher Building and Patient Transport Guide

## Objective
To safely move an injured person without proper support, distributing weight evenly, keeping the body stable, and preventing worsening of spinal injuries, reopening of wounds, and shock.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Two poles (2–2.5 meters long).
- A fabric panel (blanket, tarp, sleeping bag, two jackets, or large section of canvas).
- Cordage (optional) for reinforcing connections.
- Supports (rocks, logs) for load testing.

## Material
- **Poles:** 2 poles, 2–2.5 meters long, 5–8 cm diameter. Hardwood saplings (oak, ash, maple, hickory) work best.
- **Fabric Panel:** Needs to be at least 60 cm wide and 180 cm long.
- **Crossbars (for Lashed Frame):** 4–5 crossbars (sticks, 70 cm long).
- **Padding:** Clothing, blankets, moss, grass, or pine boughs.

## System
### Stretcher
An improvised stretcher built from poles and fabric, designed to distribute weight.

### Drag Stretcher (Travois)
A system used when insufficient carriers are available, allowing the patient to be dragged.

### Emergency Carry Methods
Various techniques for immediate patient movement, including Fireman’s carry, Two-person seat carry, Fore-and-aft carry, Blanket drag, and Log roll onto board.

## Technology
- **Friction Principle:** The patient’s weight creates friction that locks the fabric around the poles without needing ties.
- **Rigidity Requirement:** A critical principle dictating that suspected spinal injuries demand a rigid surface, as flexible stretchers risk allowing the body to sag or bend.

## Tools
- None explicitly identified for construction, but square lashings (for Method 3) are mentioned.

## Process
### Stretcher Construction
**Method 1 — Blanket Wrap (Fastest, no tools needed):**
1. Lay the blanket flat on the ground.
2. Place the first pole one-third of the way from one edge.
3. Fold the short side of the blanket over the pole.
4. Place the second pole on top of the folded-over fabric, roughly 60 cm from the first pole.
5. Fold the remaining fabric over the second pole.

**Method 2 — Jacket Stretcher (When large fabric is unavailable):**
1. Turn two jackets inside out but do not pull the sleeves through.
2. Zip or button them closed.
3. Thread the poles through the sleeves (each pole through both sleeves).
4. Space the jackets along the poles to create a carrying surface.
5. For a heavier patient, add a third jacket in the middle.

**Method 3 — Lashed Frame (Most durable):**
1. Lay two poles parallel, 60 cm apart.
2. Lash 4–5 crossbars perpendicular to the poles at 40 cm intervals using square lashings.
3. Weave additional branches, vines, or strips of fabric between the crossbars to create a platform.
4. Pad the surface with clothing, moss, or leaves.

### Drag Stretcher (Travois) Construction
1. Use two poles, 2.5–3 meters long.
2. Lash the thin ends together at one point (the front/drag point).
3. Spread the thick ends apart to roughly 60–80 cm (the platform end).
4. Lash crossbars between the two poles starting about 1 meter from the lashed junction.
5. Create a bed surface by weaving fabric, branches, or vines across the crossbars.
6. Pad the bed generously.

### Using a Travois
- One person stands between the poles at the junction end, holding one pole in each hand.
- Lean forward and drag (spreading poles create stability).
- Move slowly over rough ground to minimize jolts.
- On downhill slopes, turn around and go backward to control speed.
- Can also be attached to a pack animal if one is available.

### Carrying Techniques
**Four-Person Carry (Standard):**
- One carrier at each corner of the stretcher.
- All carriers face the direction of travel.
- The person at the patient’s head calls commands: “Ready, lift” and “Ready, lower.”
- Lift on the same count, walk in step.
- Keep the stretcher as level as possible.

**Two-Person Carry:**
- One carrier at each end of the stretcher.
- The carrier at the foot end faces forward; the carrier at the head end faces backward (watching the patient).
- For uphill travel: patient’s head goes uphill (feet first in the direction of travel).
- For downhill travel: patient’s head goes downhill (head first in the direction of travel).
- **Exception:** If the patient has leg injuries, keep legs elevated (reverse the above).

**Obstacle Navigation:**
- **Narrow passages:** Turn the stretcher 45 degrees and shuffle sideways.
- **Fences and low walls:** Set the stretcher on top, two carriers cross to the other side, then slide the stretcher across.
- **Steep slopes:** Use ropes tied to the stretcher poles for additional control (one person ahead pulling, two behind controlling descent).
- **Water crossings:** Keep the patient as dry as possible; carry normally if shallow, consider building a raft or waiting for a better crossing point if deeper.

### Padding and Patient Comfort
- Pad the stretcher surface with clothing, blankets, moss, grass, or pine boughs.
- Place extra padding under the small of the back (lumbar support) and behind the knees.
- Secure the patient to the stretcher with straps or tied cloth at the chest, hips, and legs to prevent sliding or rolling off.
- In cold weather, insulate from below first (ground contact saps body heat faster).
- Cover the patient with a blanket or tarp, especially in rain or wind.
- Talk to the patient throughout transport to reduce anxiety and fear.

## Notes
- **Critical Dimensions:**
    - **Width between poles:** 55–65 cm.
    - **Length:** 190–220 cm.
    - **Pole diameter:** Minimum 5 cm for hardwood; thinner poles risk snapping under load.
    - **Ground clearance for travois:** Crossbar bed should be at least 10 cm off the ground to clear obstacles.
- **Load Testing Protocol:**
    1. Place the stretcher on two supports (rocks, logs) at each end.
    2. Press down firmly in the center with both hands.
    3. Have someone sit on it briefly (if possible).
    4. Check all lashing points for slippage.
    5. Verify poles do not flex more than 5 cm under body weight.
- **Rigidity Warning:** Never place a patient with a suspected spinal injury on a flexible stretcher. A rigid surface (door, wide board, or stretcher with a rigid crossbar frame) is required.
- **Key Takeaways:** The blanket-wrap pole stretcher takes under 5 minutes and requires no cordage. Suspected spinal injuries demand a rigid surface. Secure the patient at chest, hips, and legs to prevent sliding.
