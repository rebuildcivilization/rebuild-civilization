# Snow Mounding

## Objective
To build a cohesive, self-supporting mass (quinzhee) shelter through the process of piling snow into a dome and allowing it to settle.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Ground slope:** Must be flat or nearly flat (less than 10 degrees). A slope causes uneven settling.
*   **Wind protection:** Build in the lee of a hill, tree line, or rock formation to prevent wind erosion during settling and sleep.
*   **Snow depth:** Requires enough snow to build the mound, roughly 8-10 cubic meters of snow for a 2-person quinzhee.
*   **Hazards above:** Must ensure there are no dead branches, steep slopes that could avalanche, or overhanging snow cornices.

## Material
Snow

## System
Quinzhee (a 2-person shelter).

## Technology
**Sintering**
The process by which individual snow crystals bond together through temperature gradients, pressure from weight, and the tendency of ice to minimize surface area, transforming loose crystals into a cohesive mass.

**Factors Accelerating Sintering:**
*   Warmer temperatures (closer to 0 C).
*   Mixing snow types (different crystal shapes interlock better).
*   Compaction (more contact points).
*   Higher humidity (moisture at crystal contacts promotes bonding).

**Factors Slowing Sintering:**
*   Extreme cold (below -20 C).
*   Dry, powdery snow (uniform crystals with fewer contact points).
*   Large air gaps (crystals not touching cannot bond).

## Tools
*   Shovel
*   Guide sticks (minimum 30 cm or 12 inches long)

## Process
### Step 1: Mark the Base
Stamp out a circle in the snow to mark the mound’s footprint. For a 2-person quinzhee, the circle should be 3 meters (10 feet) in diameter.

### Step 2: Gather Snow
Start shoveling or scraping snow from outside the marked circle toward the center, working in a ring pattern from at least 2 meters beyond the base circle.

### Step 3: Build in Stages
Build the mound in three stages:
1.  **Stage 1 (base layer):** Pile snow across the full diameter to about 60 cm (2 feet) high. Pack it down by patting with the flat of the shovel or walking on it.
2.  **Stage 2 (middle layer):** Add snow to bring the center up to about 1 meter (3.3 feet). Begin shaping into a dome, ensuring sides curve, not rise vertically. Pack this layer firmly.
3.  **Stage 3 (top layer):** Build the peak to the target height (1.5-2 meters). The top must be rounded, not pointed.

### Step 4: Shape the Dome
The ideal profile is a flattened hemisphere (turtle shell profile). Avoid shapes that are too tall and narrow (sidewalls too thin), too flat (not enough interior height), or lopsided (uneven settling creates weak spots).

### Step 5: Pack the Surface
Pack the entire surface by slapping it firmly with the flat of the shovel blade, working systematically from bottom to top. This compresses the outermost 5-10 cm.

### Step 6: Insert Guide Sticks
Before walking away, push 12-15 sticks into the mound from outside. Each stick must be exactly 30 cm long. Space them evenly across the dome.

### Step 7: Wait
Allow the mound to settle. Minimum settling times based on temperature:

| Temperature | Minimum Wait | Recommended Wait |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 0 to -10 C | 1 hour | 1.5 hours |
| -10 to -20 C | 1.5 hours | 2 hours |
| Below -20 C | 2 hours | 3 hours |

Use this time to gather floor insulation, collect firewood, eat, hydrate, and rest. Change into dry base layers before entering the shelter.

## Notes
*   **Wall Thickness Safety:** The 30 cm guide sticks represent the minimum safe wall thickness. Walls thinner than 20 cm are structurally unreliable.
*   **Volume Estimation:** The finished mound volume should be roughly 3 times the required interior volume (Target interior volume ~3 cubic meters for a 2-person quinzhee, requiring ~9 cubic meters of snow).
*   **Sweat Management:** Shoveling snow is intense exercise; wear dry base layers before entering to prevent hypothermia caused by sweat-soaked clothing.
*   **Sintering Insight:** Mixing different snow layers during gathering produces faster and stronger sintering.
