# Elevated Platforms and Insect Protection in Tropical Environments

## Objective
To solve the most dangerous threats in tropical environments: flooding, insects, snakes, and constant humidity.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Sufficient sturdy, straight trunks or thick bamboo poles (2-2.5 meters long) for corner posts.
- Availability of appropriate cordage (vine, bark strip, or paracord).
- Availability of palm fronds for roofing.
- Availability of fine-mesh fabric, smudge fires, or natural repellents for mosquito defense.

## Material
- **Structure:** Corner posts (sturdy trunks or thick bamboo, 8-10 cm diameter). Hardwood resists rot better than softwood.
- **Platform Surface:** Straight poles, split bamboo, or sticks.
- **Sleeping Layer:** Broad leaves, woven palm fronds, or bark.
- **Roofing:** Palm fronds (layered 4-6 fronds deep).
- **Binding:** Vine, bark strip, or paracord.

## System
**The Elevated Platform System:** The foundation of the shelter, designed to keep occupants above ground-level threats.
**Roof System:** A steep-pitched roof structure designed to shed torrential rain efficiently.
**Ventilation System:** A system designed to maximize cross-ventilation and create a chimney effect.

## Technology
- **Natural Material Science:** Utilizing naturally rot-resistant materials like bamboo.
- **Geometric Engineering:** Employing steep roof pitch (45-60 degrees) to ensure rapid water runoff.

## Tools
- Heated stick or sharp stone (to bore holes for bamboo lashing).
- Measuring tools (implied by the need for specific dimensions and angles).

## Process
### Elevated Platform Construction
**Step 1 — Select four corner posts:** Find sturdy, straight trunks or thick bamboo poles, each 2-2.5 meters long.
**Step 2 — Set the posts:** Dig holes 30-40 cm deep and set the posts vertically. Pack the holes tight with rocks and tamped earth. Space posts to create a sleeping area of at least 60 cm wide and 2 meters long (for one person) or 1.2 meters wide (for two).
**Step 3 — Attach cross-beams:** Lash horizontal beams between the posts at platform height (60-90 cm up) using strong cordage. Use square lashings at each joint.
**Step 4 — Lay the platform surface:** Place straight poles, split bamboo, or sticks across the frame, perpendicular to the long axis. Space them 1-2 cm apart and lash them to the cross-beams every 30-40 cm.
**Step 5 — Add a sleeping layer:** Place a layer of broad leaves, woven palm fronds, or bark over the poles. Replace this layer every few days as it compresses.

**Bamboo Platform Variation:**
- Use thick bamboo (8-12 cm diameter) for corner posts and frame.
- Split thinner bamboo (5-8 cm) in half lengthwise for platform slats. The flat interior surface faces up.
- Lash bamboo to itself well with vine or cord through drilled holes.
- A full bamboo platform for one person can be built in 2-3 hours.

### Roof Design for Heavy Rain
**Pitch and Coverage:** The roof must have a minimum pitch of 45 degrees, ideally 55-60 degrees. It must overhang the platform by at least 45-60 cm on all sides, with 60-90 cm of overhang preferred in sideways rain.
**Palm Frond Thatching:**
1. Build a ridgepole and rafter frame above the platform. Rafters should angle down from the ridge at 45-60 degrees.
2. Add horizontal purlins (thin poles) across the rafters, spaced 15-20 cm apart.
3. Fold each palm frond over a purlin so it hangs on both sides.
4. Overlap each row by at least one-third the frond length. Use 4-6 layers of overlapping fronds for reliable waterproofing.
5. Cap the ridge with fronds or large leaves/bark.

### Insect Protection
- **Mosquito Defense Priorities:**
    - **Smudge fires:** Build near (not under) the platform using green leaves, damp wood, or green grass on hot coals, maintained from dusk to dawn, positioned upwind.
    - **Mosquito netting:** Drape fine-mesh fabric from the roof frame around the sleeping platform, tucking edges under the sleeping mat.
    - **Natural repellents:** Use lemon eucalyptus leaves, citronella grass, neem leaves, wild basil, or lavender.
- **Ground Insect Barriers:**
    - **Smooth the posts:** Remove bark from corner posts.
    - **Resin barrier:** Apply a band of tree resin or grease around each post at mid-height.
    - **Water moats:** Set corner posts in containers filled with water, refreshed daily.

### Ventilation
- **Wall Design:** Avoid solid walls. Use woven palm screens or half-walls (solid from platform level to 30-40 cm above, open above) to block rain splash and provide windbreaks while allowing air circulation above.
- **Orientation:** Orient the long axis of the shelter perpendicular to the prevailing breeze direction to maximize cross-ventilation. The elevated platform creates a chimney effect where warm air rises off the body and exits through the roof peak.

## Notes
- **Warning:** Test the platform before sleeping on it; reinforce any joint that flexes under load to prevent collapse.
- **Lifespan:** A well-thatched palm roof lasts 2-4 weeks in tropical conditions before needing repair or replacement.
- **Maintenance Schedule:** Expect to rebuild or heavily repair a tropical shelter every 3-6 weeks due to rapid degradation in humidity.
- **Threat Profile Summary:** Tropical environments present simultaneous threats of Rain (high intensity), Humidity (80-100% relative humidity), Insects (malaria, dengue, ground-dwellers), Flooding (30-60 cm rise in minutes), and Snakes (venomous, active at night).
- **Key Takeaways:** The core principle is to elevate the platform (60-90 cm) and ensure constant airflow and waterproofing.
