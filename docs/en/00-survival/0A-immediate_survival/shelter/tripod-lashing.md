# Tripod Lashing

## Objective
To create a stable and versatile structure using poles and rope, leveraging the tripod's ability to find a stable position on any terrain, making it indispensable for shelters, cooking setups, water filtration rigs, hoisting equipment, and signal towers.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Poles:** 3 poles, equal length, 5-10 cm diameter (Green wood is preferred).
*   **Cordage:** 3-5 m, 4-6 mm diameter (Natural fiber, vine, or paracord).
*   **Optional Crossbars:** 3 horizontal poles, used only if building a raised platform or rack.

## Material
| Item | Specification | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Poles | 3 poles, equal length, 5-10 cm diameter | Green wood is better — less prone to snapping |

**Pole Length Guide:**
*   Cooking pot hanger: 100-120 cm (3-5 cm diameter)
*   Water filter stand: 120-150 cm (4-6 cm diameter)
*   Shelter support: 200-300 cm (6-10 cm diameter)
*   Observation tower: 300-500 cm (8-12 cm diameter)
*   Hoisting rig: 250-400 cm (8-12 cm diameter)

## System
**Tripod vs. Four-legged Structure:**
*   A four-legged structure (like a table) will rock on uneven ground because three points define a plane but four do not.
*   A tripod always sits flat, regardless of terrain, making it solid on hillsides, rocky ground, mud, and sand where other structures would wobble.

## Technology
**Stability Mechanism:**
*   Three legs naturally find a stable position, defining a plane.
*   The center pole is laid in the opposite direction and flipped over when raising, creating the natural triangular base.
*   The wrapping turns must be moderately loose to allow the legs to spread; frapping turns provide the necessary grip.

## Tools
*   Clove hitch
*   Square lashing (implied for Method 1)
*   Stakes (for Method 2)
*   Digging tools (for burying feet)
*   Pot hook (for cooking setup)

## Process
### Standard Tripod Lashing (Wrap-and-Frap Method)
**Phase 1: Layout**
1. Lay all three poles side by side on the ground, with tops aligned and even. The center pole's butt end should be at the top, while the outer poles have butt ends at the bottom.
2. Position the poles so the lashing point is approximately 15-20 cm from the top ends.

**Phase 2: Wrapping**
3. Tie a clove hitch around one of the outer poles at the lashing point. Leave a 10 cm tail and tuck it under the first wrap.
4. Wrap the cordage around all three poles together, making 6-8 loose wrapping turns along the length of the poles, covering about 10-15 cm.

**Phase 3: Frapping**
5. Make frapping turns between each pair of poles (e.g., pass cordage between the first and second poles, wrap it around the wrapping turns 2-3 times, pulling tight).
6. Move to the gap between the second and third poles and frap 2-3 times, pulling tight.
7. If enough cordage exists, return to the first gap and add 1-2 more frapping turns.

**Phase 4: Finishing and Raising**
8. Finish with a clove hitch on the opposite outer pole from where the wrapping started.
9. To raise the tripod, flip the center pole over to create the triangular base.
10. Spread the legs until the tripod feels stable. The angle between the legs and the ground should be approximately 60-70 degrees.
11. On soft ground, dig the butt ends of the poles into the soil 5-10 cm to prevent sliding.

### Alternative Method: Figure-Eight Method
1. Tie a clove hitch on an outer pole.
2. Weave the cordage in a figure-eight pattern: over the first pole, under the second, over the third, then back — under the third, over the second, under the first.
3. Repeat for 4-6 complete figure-eight passes.
4. Add frapping turns between each pair of poles (2-3 each gap).
5. Finish with a clove hitch.

### Reinforcement Methods
*   **Horizontal braces:** Lash a horizontal pole between each pair of legs at about one-third of the height, using square lashing.
*   **Stake the legs:** Drive stakes into the ground on the outside of each leg and lash the leg to its stake to prevent outward spread.
*   **Bury the feet:** Dig 15-20 cm holes for each leg, set the legs in the holes, and pack earth tightly around them (strongest method).
*   **Cross-bracing:** Lash diagonal braces between the legs in an X pattern to convert the tripod into a rigid tower.

## Notes
*   **Tension Control:** The crucial difference in the wrap-and-frap method is keeping the wrapping turns loose to allow spreading, relying on frapping turns for the real grip.
*   **Fire Safety:** Use green (live) wood for any tripod near fire. Dry wood can catch fire from radiant heat. Keep legs at least 20 cm from the fire edge. Never leave a cooking tripod unattended.
*   **Stability Metric:** When a tripod feels unstable, the legs are not spread wide enough; the base diameter should be 60-70% of the pole length.
*   **Cordage Maintenance:** If cordage is dry and loosened, re-wet the lashing or relash entirely.
*   **Method Comparison:** The wrap-and-frap method is faster and adequate for most survival applications; the figure-eight method is slower but better for heavy loads.
