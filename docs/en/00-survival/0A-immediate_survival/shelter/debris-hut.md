# Debris Hut | Survival Wiki

## Objective
The debris hut is the gold standard no-tool emergency shelter: a fully enclosed cocoon built from dead leaves, needles, and sticks that uses your body heat alone to stay survivable in freezing conditions. It works by sealing a small air volume around you—just wide enough for your body at the shoulders—so the debris walls trap the warmth your body produces, the same principle as a sleeping bag.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Freezing temperatures for survival.
*   Absence of tools, tarp, or fire.

## System
**Core Principle:** The debris hut succeeds by minimizing air volume. It seals around the occupant on all sides (top, bottom, sides), trapping a small pocket of air that the body heats to a survivable temperature. This is identical to a sleeping bag; the body heats a thin envelope of air directly around it, and the debris walls prevent that air from escaping.

**Performance in real conditions:**
*   Outside temperature: -10C (14F)
*   Inside a well-built debris hut: 5-15C (40-60F)
*   Heat source: body heat only, no fire needed.

## Technology
The system relies on thermal insulation through trapping body heat within a sealed air volume, contrasting with lean-tos or A-frames which have an open end that constantly exchanges warm air for cold.

## Tools
No tools or cordage are required. Everything is gathered and assembled by hand.

## Process
### Phase 1: Framework (30-45 minutes)
1.  **Select and place the ridgepole:** Find a strong, straight pole (1.5 times body length). Prop the high end on a support (stump, forked tree, rock, or bipod) at approximately waist height (80-100 cm). The low end rests on the ground.
    *   *Warning:* The ridgepole must support 50-100+ kg of wet debris. Test it by pressing down with full body weight.
2.  **Check the fit:** Lie down under the ridgepole. Your body should fit comfortably from the high end to within 30 cm of the low end. Shoulders should have about 15 cm of clearance on each side.
3.  **Place the ribs:** Lean sticks against both sides of the ridgepole at 45-60 degree angles. Each rib should touch the ridgepole at the top and rest firmly on the ground at the bottom. Space ribs 10-15 cm apart. Alternate sides to keep weight balanced. Leave an opening just wide enough to crawl through (about 60 cm wide) at the head end.
4.  **Add the lattice:** Weave smaller sticks and leafy branches horizontally through the ribs to prevent loose debris from falling through.

### Phase 2: Debris Application (45-90 minutes)
5.  **Apply bulk debris to the outside:** Start at the ground level and work upward toward the ridge, packing debris into and around the ribs.
    *   *Debris Thickness Guide:* 
        *   Above 10C (50F): 15-20 cm
        *   0-10C (32-50F): 30-40 cm
        *   Below 0C (32F): 45-60 cm
        *   Below -15C (5F): 60-90 cm
6.  **Apply the outer shingle layer:** Add a waterproofing layer on top of the bulk debris, such as evergreen boughs laid tip-down, bark slabs overlapped like shingles, or a thatching layer of large leaves or grass bundles.
7.  **Pin the debris:** Lay long sticks and small branches across the outside of the debris pile to act as a “hairnet” to prevent wind from stripping the insulation.

### Phase 3: Interior Finishing (15-20 minutes)
8.  **Insulate the floor:** Fill the interior with dry debris. Minimum 15 cm, 30 cm in freezing conditions. Use the fluffiest material available and extend ground insulation right to the entrance.
9.  **Build the entrance plug:** Seal the entrance behind the occupant. Options include: a backpack stuffed into the opening, weaving a rough mat of sticks and branches, or gathering a large pile of loose debris near the entrance to be pulled in feet-first.

### Using the Debris Hut
*   **Entry:** Enter feet first, crawling backward so the head is at the high end where there is the most air space.
*   **Seal:** Immediately seal the entrance by pulling debris, a backpack, or a woven plug into the opening.
*   **Fill dead spaces:** Stuff loose debris into any voids between the body and the walls to eliminate pockets of cold air.
*   **Manage moisture:** If the shelter becomes damp, leave the entrance slightly vented in moderate temperatures (above 5C), or rebuild/replace damp inner debris every 2-3 days.
*   **Exit:** To exit, the entrance plug must be disassembled and reassembled upon return.

## Troubleshooting
| Problem | Cause | Solution |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cold despite thick debris | Gaps in walls letting air through, or poor ground insulation | Crawl inside to find daylight. Patch from outside. Add more floor debris. |
| Dripping inside | Rain penetrating thin spots | Add more debris to thin areas. Apply thatching or bark shingle layer on the outside. |
| Shelter collapsed | Weak ridgepole, or ribs too steep/not seated | Rebuild with a stronger ridgepole. Seat rib bases firmly in the ground. Lower ridgepole height. |
| Too cramped to move | Built too tight | If you can roll over easily, the shelter is too big. |
| Condensation buildup | Body moisture with no ventilation | Crack the entrance plug slightly. Air shelter during daytime. |
| Debris falling inside | Insufficient lattice layer | From inside, push debris back through gaps. Add lattice sticks from outside. |

## Improving Over Multiple Days
*   **Daily:** Fluff and replenish floor insulation, as it compresses nightly.
*   **Every 2-3 days:** Add fresh debris to the exterior to replace settled and decomposed walls.
*   **After rain:** Check for saturated areas and rebuild them with dry material, adding more outer shingle layer.
*   **Week 2+:** Consider daubing the lower walls with a mud-and-grass mixture for a permanent wind seal. A raised sleeping platform of logs topped with debris can improve ground insulation.
*   **Transition:** When the situation stabilizes, transition to a Permanent Shelter, but keep the debris hut functional as a backup.

## Key Takeaways
*   The debris hut is the best no-tool, no-fire emergency shelter—fully enclosed and heated by body warmth alone, effective well below freezing.
*   **Build it tight:** The shelter should be just wide enough for your body; smaller means warmer.
*   **Debris Quantity:** Require far more debris than expected; the finished shelter should resemble a giant leaf pile, not a visible structure.
*   **Ground Insulation:** Ground insulation is half the shelter’s effectiveness; pile 15-30 cm of dry debris on the floor.
*   **Entrance Seal:** Seal the entrance completely; an open entrance turns the hut into a cold tunnel.
