# Rapid Shelters

## Objective
To achieve protection quickly when darkness is 30 minutes away and the temperature is dropping, prioritizing speed over perfection.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Sufficient useful daylight (assume 15 minutes after realizing the need for shelter).
- Awareness of the time pressure (body temperature begins dropping within 30 minutes of inactivity without protection).

## Material
- Fallen tree (trunk 60-90 cm off the ground with dense branch coverage).
- Dry leaves, grass, bark, debris (for insulation and rain protection).
- Branches (for roof frames and lean-tos).
- Rocks, logs, or brush (for rock overhangs and brush lean-tos).
- Dug earth (for debris trenches).
- Vehicle parts (seats, floor mats, doors, hoods, trunk lids).

## System
Rapid Shelters encompass quick emergency setups designed to provide protection in 15-45 minutes using immediately available resources.

## Technology
- The core concept is speed-build decision making, prioritizing immediate protection over elegant construction.
- The effectiveness of insulation is tied directly to the quantity of material used (e.g., 5 cm of leaves is better than bare ground).
- The vehicle shelter offers the most immediate shelter capability.

## Tools
- Digging tool (stick, rock, salvaged metal) for Debris Trenches.
- Cordage (helpful for Brush Pile Lean-To).

## Process
### The 15-Minute Rule
If emergency shelter is needed, assume 15 minutes of useful daylight. The priority order is:
1. Get out of the wind (3 minutes)
2. Get off the ground (5 minutes)
3. Get a roof overhead (10-15 minutes)
4. Seal gaps and add insulation (remaining time)

### Shelter Types and Time Estimates
*   **Leaf Pile Shelter (10-15 minutes):** Find a pile (90 cm high, 90 cm wide, 210 cm long), burrow into the center, and cover with 20-30 cm of material.
*   **Fallen Tree Shelter (10-20 minutes):** Find a tree with trunk 60-90 cm off the ground and dense branches. Clear one side, pile branches on top, and insulate the ground.
*   **Body-Sized Debris Trench (15-25 minutes):** Dig a trench (50 cm wide x 190 cm long, 30-45 cm deep). Pile earth walls, lay branches for the roof frame, cover with debris (at least 15 cm thick), and line the bottom for insulation.
*   **Brush Pile Lean-To (20-30 minutes):** Find a support, lean long branches at 45-60 degrees (15-20 cm spaced), weave horizontal branches, and pile debris on the lattice.
*   **Rock Overhang / Cave Entrance (5-10 minutes to improve):** Assess overhang depth (minimum 120 cm / 4 feet), check for dry floors, and improve by blocking wind, insulating the floor, and building a fire at the entrance.
*   **Vehicle Shelter (Immediate):** Intact vehicle requires closing windows and parking rear-facing into the wind. Wrecked vehicles use seats as bedding and detach mats for insulation.

### Speed-Build Decision Matrix
| Situation | Best Rapid Shelter | Time | Tools Needed |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Forest, no tools | Fallen tree or leaf pile | 10-20 min | None |
| Open ground, digging tool | Debris trench | 15-25 min | Stick or stone |
| Rocky terrain | Rock overhang improvement | 5-10 min | None |
| Near vehicle wreckage | Vehicle shelter | Immediate | None |
| Brushy area, some cordage | Brush lean-to | 20-30 min | Cordage helpful |
| Snow on ground, 30+ cm deep | Snow trench (dig, cover) | 20-30 min | Any digging tool |

## Notes
- **Key Takeaways:**
    - Speed beats perfection: An ugly shelter finished before dark is better than a beautiful one half-done.
    - Ground insulation is critical: Always reserve time for ground padding, as 5 cm of leaves prevents significant heat loss.
    - Minimize volume: Build the smallest shelter possible, as body heat is the primary heating source.
    - Orientation Check: The opening must face away from the prevailing wind, requiring a 30-second observation of wind direction.
- **Carbon Monoxide Warning:** Never sleep in a vehicle with the engine running and all windows closed. Limit engine use (15 minutes per hour maximum) or stop running the engine entirely if the exhaust system is damaged.
- **Fallen Tree Danger:** Never shelter under a tree that is actively breaking or has large dead branches overhead; test stability before committing.
