# Retting Process

## Objective
Retting is the controlled decomposition that frees the long bast fibers from the woody core of plant stems. This is identified as the single most important step between raw plant material and functional cordage.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Water and Microbial Action:** Required to dissolve the pectins and lignins that bind the fiber to the core.
*   **Timing Control:** The window between under-retted and over-retted can be less than a day in warm conditions, necessitating daily testing.
*   **Testing:** Daily testing using snap, peel, twist, and strength tests is essential to determine when the batch is ready.
*   **Water Source Management:** The water used for retting must be managed to prevent contamination of drinking water sources.

## Material
*   Raw plant material (plant stalks).
*   Bast fibers (long, flexible fibers running beneath the outer bark).
*   Woody core.
*   Outer bark.

## System
### Retting Methods
There are three primary approaches based on the medium used for decomposition:

1.  **Dew Retting (Field Retting):**
    *   **Procedure:** Spread harvested plant stalks in a thin, even layer on short grass or bare ground, relying on morning dew, rain, and ground moisture, with fungi and bacteria doing the work.
    *   **Best for:** Large batches when time is not critical.

2.  **Water Retting (Pond or Stream Retting):**
    *   **Procedure:** Submerge bundled stalks in standing or slow-moving water. Stalks must stay fully submerged.
    *   **Best for:** Moderate batches when faster processing is needed.

3.  **Warm Water / Accelerated Retting:**
    *   **Procedure:** Cut stalks into manageable lengths and place them in warm water (90-100°F ideal). A bacterial starter culture (like pond mud) can be added. The water must be changed every 24 hours.
    *   **Best for:** Urgent needs and small batches.

### Post-Retting Processing
After retting, fibers must be processed to become functional cordage:

1.  **Rinse and Dry:** Rinse stalks thoroughly in clean water to halt bacterial action. Spread them to dry completely (24-48 hours in warm, breezy conditions) before storage.
2.  **Breaking:** Crush the dried stalks to fragment the woody core. Methods include beating stalks gently with a smooth stick or flat stone, or rolling them under a flat board.
3.  **Scutching:** Remove the broken woody fragments (shives or hurds) from the fibers by holding a bundle and scraping downward with a dull blade or flat stick.
4.  **Hackling (Optional):** For the finest cordage, draw the scutched fibers through a coarse comb (or a row of nails driven through a board) to separate individual fibers and align them.

### Best Fiber Plants for Retting
| Plant | Habitat | Harvest Season | Retting Time (water) | Fiber Quality |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Stinging Nettle | Roadsides, forest edges, moist soil | Late summer/fall | 5-10 days | Excellent — fine, strong |

## Technology
*   **Microbial Action:** Bacteria and fungi are the primary agents used to dissolve binding agents (pectins and lignins).
*   **Thermal Energy (Warm Water):** Used in accelerated retting to increase the rate of decomposition.

## Tools
*   **Testing Tools:** Used for Snap Test, Peel Test, Twist Test, and Strength Test.
*   **Processing Tools:** Smooth stick, flat stone, dull blade, flat stick, edge of a board, coarse comb (or nails driven through a board) for hackling.
*   **Containment Tools:** Watertight vessels, plastic bins, hollowed logs, or lined pits for water retting.

## Process
**Retting Phase:**
1.  **Preparation:** Harvest stalks at the right stage (after flowering but before seeds fully mature for most bast-fiber plants).
2.  **Dew Retting:** Spread stalks in a thin layer, ensuring they are no more than 2-3 inches deep.
3.  **Water Retting:** Bundle stalks (6-8 inches diameter), weigh them down, and ensure they stay fully submerged in standing or slow-moving water.
4.  **Warm Water Retting:** Cut stalks into manageable lengths (18-24 inches) and place them in warm water (90-100°F ideal).
5.  **Testing:** Test daily after the first week (or starting at day 3 in warm weather, day 7 in cool weather) using the four tests.
    *   **Test Criteria:** The Goldilocks Zone is achieved when 80% of test stalks pass all four tests.

**Post-Retting Phase:**
1.  **Rinse and Dry:** Rinse stalks thoroughly and spread them to dry completely for 24-48 hours.
2.  **Breaking:** Crush the dried stalks to shatter the inner woody core.
3.  **Scutching:** Remove the woody fragments (shives or hurds) from the fibers by scraping downward.
4.  **Hackling (Optional):** Draw scutched fibers through a coarse comb to align fibers for spinning or twisting.

## Notes
*   **Critical Risk:** Skipping the drying step leads to mold in storage within 48 hours.
*   **Over-retting Risk:** Over-retting destroys the fiber faster than under-retting; when in doubt, pull the batch out.
*   **Contamination Warning:** Common mistakes include retting in drinking water, which produces toxic byproducts that can contaminate the source for weeks.
*   **Harvesting Note:** Using stalks harvested too early results in immature, short, and weak fibers regardless of retting quality.
*   **Key Takeaway:** Proper post-retting processing is just as important as the ret itself.
