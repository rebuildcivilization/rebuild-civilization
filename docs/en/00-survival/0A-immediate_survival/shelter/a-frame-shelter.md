# A-Frame Shelter

## Objective
The A-frame shelter sits between a lean-to and a debris hut in effort and protection. It is preferred when paired with a fire as it is often warmer and more comfortable than a debris hut, and it can shelter two people side by side.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Fire: Recommended for warmth and optimal use of the shelter.
*   Debris: Must be at least 30 cm thick on both sides and the closed end.
*   Ground Insulation: Mandatory; 15-30 cm of dry debris under the body is required to prevent the ground from draining body heat.

## Material
*   **Ridgepole:** 3-4 meters long, 8-15 cm diameter, strong and straight.
*   **Support Poles or Bipod:** For elevating one or both ends of the ridgepole.
*   **Rib Sticks:** 20-30 sticks, 1.5-2 meters long, wrist-thick, relatively straight.
*   **Lattice Material:** Dozens of finger-thick sticks and small leafy branches.
*   **Debris:** Large quantity of dead leaves, needles, grass, or ferns.
*   **Cordage (Optional):** For lashing.

## System
The A-frame shelter is defined by its structure where the ridgepole is supported at both ends at waist height (90-120 cm), and ribs lean against both sides at 45-60 degrees, with one end closed against the wind and the other open toward a fire.

## Technology
The A-frame functions as a two-sided debris shelter offering better wind protection than a lean-to and faster construction than a full debris hut.

## Tools
No tools are required. Everything can be gathered and assembled by hand.

## Process
### Step 1: Set the Ridgepole
*   **Option A (Between two trees):** Find two trees 3-4 meters apart. Rest or lash the ridgepole in low forks, or tie it to the trunks at 90-120 cm height. (Fastest and most stable).
*   **Option B (Bipod supports at each end):** Build two bipods from sturdy sticks lashed or wedged together in an X-shape. Rest the ridgepole in the V of each bipod.
*   **Option C (One tree, one bipod):** Use a tree fork at one end and a bipod at the other.
*   **Height:** Set the ridgepole at 90-120 cm (roughly waist height). Higher gives more interior room but more air to heat. Lower is warmer but cramped.

### Step 2: Place the Ribs
*   Lean rib sticks against both sides of the ridgepole at 45-60 degree angles.
*   Space ribs 15-20 cm apart along the entire length of the ridgepole.
*   Alternate sides to balance weight.
*   The gap between the ground contact points of opposing ribs should be 120-150 cm.

### Step 3: Add the Lattice
*   Weave smaller sticks and leafy branches horizontally through the ribs on both sides to catch and hold debris.
*   Space horizontal sticks 10-15 cm apart.
*   Weave flexible branches in and out of the ribs where possible. 
*   Add extra lattice material near the ridgepole where gaps tend to be largest.

### Step 4: Close One End
*   Block the windward end of the A-frame by leaning sticks against it to form a triangular wall.
*   Fill this end-wall with lattice and debris, making it as solid as the rest of the shelter.
*   Leave the opposite end open.
*   If using a fire, place it 1-1.5 meters from the open end to allow heat radiation.

### Step 5: Apply Debris
*   Pile debris over the entire structure (both sides and the closed end).
*   **Layering Technique:** Start at the bottom and work upward.
    1. Build to at least 30 cm thickness in moderate cold, 45-60 cm in freezing temperatures.
    2. Pay special attention to the ridge line, packing debris firmly and pinning it to prevent sliding.
    3. Apply a shingle layer of evergreen boughs or bark on the outside for waterproofing.
    4. Pin the outer layer with long sticks laid across the debris to prevent wind from stripping it.

### Step 6: Insulate the Floor
*   Fill the interior floor with 15-30 cm of dry debris (leaves, pine needles, dry grass).
*   **Warning:** The floor insulation compresses under body weight; pile more than necessary and fluff daily.

### Step 7: Build a Windbreak or Fire Reflector (Optional)
*   **Fire reflector wall:** Stack green logs or rocks behind the fire on the far side of the shelter, roughly the same height as the opening, to bounce heat back.
*   **Side windbreaks:** Add short debris walls extending outward from the open end like wings to channel heat inward if cross-winds are present.

## Notes
### A-Frame vs. Debris Hut Comparison
| Feature | A-Frame | Debris Hut |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Interior space | Larger — room for two or gear | Tight — one person only |
| Wind protection | Open on one end | Fully enclosed |
| Fire compatibility | Excellent — open end faces fire | Poor — enclosed space, smoke danger |
| Build time | 1.5-2.5 hours | 2-3 hours |
| Heat retention (no fire) | Good | Excellent |
| Heat retention (with fire) | Excellent | Not applicable |

### Sizing Guidelines
| Occupants | Ridgepole Length | Ground Width | Ridge Height |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 person | 2.5-3 m | 120 cm | 90 cm |
| 2 people | 3.5-4 m | 150 cm | 100-110 cm |
| 1 person + gear | 3-3.5 m | 140 cm | 100 cm |

### Common Mistakes
*   **Both ends open:** Creates a wind tunnel effect, resulting in no heat retention. Fix: Close the windward end completely.
*   **Ribs too steep (near vertical):** Causes debris to slide off and results in poor interior space. Fix: Aim for 45-60 degrees.
*   **Ribs too shallow (near horizontal):** Allows rain to soak through and weakens the structure. Fix: Steeper angles are better for shedding water.
*   **Ridgepole too high:** Creates huge air volume, making it impossible to warm. Fix: Maximum height is 90-120 cm.
*   **Fire too close to open end:** Risks igniting the shelter. Fix: Minimum 1 meter clearance; 1.5 m is better.
*   **No floor insulation:** Allows the ground to drain body heat. Fix: Require 15-30 cm of debris under the body.

### Improving the A-Frame Over Time
*   **Day 2:** Add more debris to thin spots, reinforce the closed end, and thicken floor insulation.
*   **Day 3-4:** Begin daubing lower walls with mud mixed with grass to seal gaps against wind and add thermal mass.
*   **Day 5+:** Add an internal drying rack near the open end for wet clothing. Build a proper fire reflector with stacked rocks (better heat retention than logs). Consider a rain trench around the perimeter.

### Key Takeaways
*   The A-frame is best when paired with a fire: close the windward end, leave the leeward end open toward the fire.
*   Set the ridgepole at waist height (90-120 cm) between two supports, place ribs at 45-60 degrees on both sides.
*   Apply debris at least 30 cm thick on all sides and the closed end.
*   Ground insulation is mandatory (15-30 cm of debris under the body).
*   The A-frame is the best choice when more space than a debris hut is needed or when fire for heating is desired.
