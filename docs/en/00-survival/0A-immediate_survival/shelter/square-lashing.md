# Square Lashing | Survival Wiki

## Objective
Square lashing is the single most important construction technique for binding two poles at a right angle. It is the joint that holds together shelter frames, ladders, tables, bridges, watchtowers, and virtually every pole-built structure in human history.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Square lashing is designed for one specific geometry: two poles crossing at approximately 90 degrees. If the poles cross at a different angle, diagonal lashing should be used. If the poles are parallel, shear or round lashing should be used.

## System
The system relies on a sequence of phases to transform a loose binding into a rigid joint:

**Phase 1: The Starting Clove Hitch**
1. Tie a clove hitch around the vertical pole, positioned just below where the horizontal pole will sit. Leave a 10 cm tail.
2. Tuck the tail under the first wrapping turn to lock it in place.

**Phase 2: Wrapping Turns**
3. Lay the horizontal pole across the vertical pole at a right angle. Hold it firmly.
4. Bring the cordage over the horizontal pole (away from you), then behind the vertical pole (to the right), then over the horizontal pole on the other side (toward you), then behind the vertical pole (to the left). This completes one full wrapping turn.
5. Repeat this wrapping pattern for 3-4 complete turns, ensuring each turn sits neatly beside the previous one.
6. Pull the cordage snug with full hand strength; it should indent into the wood.

**Phase 3: Frapping Turns**
7. After the final wrapping turn, bring the cordage between the two poles. Pass it over the wrapping turns that go around the horizontal pole, then between the poles on the other side, under the wrapping turns on that side.
8. Pull this frapping turn as tight as possible to cinch all wrapping turns together.
9. Repeat for 2-3 frapping turns total, ensuring each is tighter than the last.

**Phase 4: Finishing**
10. Finish with a clove hitch on the horizontal pole (the opposite pole from where the starting hitch was placed).
11. Pull the finishing clove hitch tight and tuck the tail under the lashing.

**Advanced Technique: The Japanese Mark II Lashing**
For critical structural joints:
*   Modification 1: Carve a flat face on each pole at the crossing point to increase contact area.
*   Modification 2: Use 5-6 wrapping turns and 4 frapping turns.
*   Modification 3: After the first set of frapping turns, wrap a second layer of wrapping turns at 45 degrees (diagonal over the crossing), then add 2 more frapping turns.
*   Modification 4: Wet the completed lashing thoroughly (rawhide strips are ideal) to allow shrinkage and maximum tightness.

## Notes
*   **Critical Detail (Clove Hitch Placement):** The clove hitch must be on the vertical pole, below the crossing point. Placing it elsewhere results in a weak joint.
*   **Testing Criteria:** A properly executed square lashing must pass Rotation test (Zero rotation), Lift test (Vertical pole hangs without slipping), Shake test (No movement, creaking, or loosening), and Load test (Holds 20 kg without deformation).
*   **Common Mistakes and Fixes:**
    *   **Wrapping Too Loosely:** Fix by pulling every wrap tight with full hand strength, feeling the cordage bite into the wood.
    *   **Skipping Frapping Turns:** Fix by making at least 2 frapping turns to lock the joint; without them, wrapping turns merely spin around the poles.
    *   **Stacking Wraps:** Fix by ensuring each wrapping turn lays side by side, spreading across 5-8 cm of pole to distribute the load.
    *   **Poles Are Smooth and Slippery:** Fix by carving a shallow notch or score mark (2-3 mm deep) on each pole before lashing.
    *   **Wrong Clove Hitch Placement:** Fix by ensuring the starting hitch is on the vertical pole below the crossing point so the tail is trapped under the first wrapping turn.
*   **Key Takeaways Summary:**
    *   Square lashing is exclusively for 90-degree pole crossings.
    *   The clove hitch starts on the vertical pole, below the crossing point.
    *   Wrapping turns follow a square pattern: over, behind, over, behind, and must be laid side by side.
    *   Frapping turns make the joint rigid and must never be skipped.
    *   Testing is mandatory for reliability.
*   **Safety:** Never trust a lashed ladder for heights above 3 meters without extensive testing. Always have someone hold the base. Inspect every joint before each use because cordage degrades with weather and UV exposure.
*   **Final Tip:** Wet the finished lashing to shrink the cordage for maximum tightness.
