# Charcoal Making | Survival Wiki

## Objective
To make charcoal by heating wood without oxygen (pyrolysis) until water and volatile compounds are driven off, leaving nearly pure carbon that burns hot enough to smelt copper and forge iron.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Wood (preferably dead, dry hardwood)
*   Heat source
*   Absence of oxygen (to achieve pyrolysis)

## Material
*   Wood (Hardwood: Oak, Maple, Hickory, Beech; Softwood: Birch, Pine, Willow)
*   Dirt or clay (for sealing pits/kilns)
*   Leaves or straw (for insulation in mound kilns)
*   Metal containers (for retort method)

## System
**Pyrolysis**
Pyrolysis is the thermal decomposition of wood when heated without oxygen.

**Pyrolysis Stages:**
1.  **100-250°F (38-120°C):** Water evaporates; wood dries.
2.  **250-500°F (120-260°C):** Hemicellulose breaks down; light gases and tar vapors release.
3.  **500-660°F (260-350°C):** Cellulose decomposes; heavy tar and combustible gases pour off (Most active phase).
4.  **660-900°F (350-480°C):** Lignin breaks down; remaining volatiles leave, leaving mostly carbon.

**Resulting Charcoal Properties:**
*   Temperature: 1,100–2,000°F (590–1,090°C) with forced air.
*   Benefits: Burns hotter, cleaner, more predictably than raw wood; produces almost no smoke; filters water; serves as an ingredient in black powder, ink, and soil amendment.

## Technology
**Charcoal Yields:**
*   Pit Charcoal: 15–25% of wood weight.
*   Mound Kiln: 20–30% of wood weight (up to 35% with skilled operators).
*   Retort (Two-Container Method): 30–40% of wood weight.

**Quality Testing Metrics:**
*   Must be jet black throughout (brown spots indicate incomplete conversion).
*   Must ring or clink when pieces are tapped (metallic sound).
*   Must break cleanly with a shiny, glassy fracture surface.
*   Must feel very light relative to the original wood.
*   Must not crumble in the hand.

**Storage Requirement:**
Freshly made charcoal absorbs moisture rapidly. Must be stored in a dry location, covered or bagged. Must be cooled completely before long-term storage to prevent spontaneous ignition.

## Tools
*   Shovel (for digging pits)
*   Fire source (to start and maintain heat)
*   Containers (for retort method)
*   Vent holes/sticks (for mound kiln management)

## Process
### Method 1: Pit Charcoal (Simplest)
1.  **Dig a pit:** 2-3 feet (60-90 cm) deep, 3-4 feet (90-120 cm) wide.
2.  **Start a fire:** Burn to a deep bed of coals.
3.  **Load wood:** Layer wood tightly, ensuring each layer catches the one below it.
4.  **Seal the pit:** Cover with green branches, then a thick layer of dirt (4-6 inches / 10-15 cm) to cut off oxygen.
5.  **Wait:** 12–24 hours. Pack more dirt on spots with flames.
6.  **Open carefully:** Once smoke stops or turns thin and clear. Let it cool completely.

### Method 2: Mound Kiln (Larger Scale)
1.  **Clear area:** Roughly 8-10 feet (2.5-3 m) in diameter.
2.  **Lay base:** Place dry sticks or a wooden platform for airflow underneath.
3.  **Stack wood:** Stack vertically around a central chimney (thin sticks or a hollow core of three poles), building outward in layers. Total height: 4-5 feet (1.2-1.5 m).
4.  **Cover and Vent:** Cover with leaves or straw (2-3 inches / 5-8 cm), then a thick layer of earth (4-6 inches / 10-15 cm). Poke 4-6 vent holes around the base.
5.  **Light the chimney:** Drop embers down the center opening.
6.  **Manage Burn:** Open and close vent holes based on smoke color. Close vents in sections done carbonizing; open vents where carbonization is needed.
7.  **Seal:** Seal completely when all vents produce thin blue smoke. Cool for 24-48 hours.

### Method 3: Retort (Two-Container Method)
1.  **Prepare Inner Container:** Pack the smaller, inner container tightly with wood pieces. Seal the lid but punch a small hole (1/4 inch / 6 mm) to let gases escape.
2.  **Place Inner Container:** Put the inner container inside the larger, outer vessel.
3.  **Maintain Fire:** Build a fire around and beneath the inner container. Gases escaping from the inner container's vent hole will ignite, fueling the process. Pyrolysis is complete when the vent flame goes out on its own.
4.  **Cool:** Let everything cool completely before opening.

## Notes
*   **Wood Selection:** Use dead, dry hardwood for the best yield and quality. Green wood wastes energy driving off moisture.
*   **Pit Charcoal Safety:** Pits can smolder for days; have water or dirt ready to extinguish flare-ups when opening.
*   **Spontaneous Ignition Risk:** Freshly made charcoal in large, tightly packed quantities can self-ignite. Spread new batches to cool completely before piling.
*   **Key Takeaway:** The core principle is the need for enough heat to drive the pyrolysis process while starving the wood of oxygen.
