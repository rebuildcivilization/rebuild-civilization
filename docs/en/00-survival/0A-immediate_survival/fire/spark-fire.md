# Spark-Based Fire Starting## Objective
Spark-based fire starting is faster and more reliable than friction methods once the right materials are available.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Right materials (high-carbon steel, hard sharp stone, proper tinder).
*   Good technique.
*   Significant physical effort (bypassed by spark methods).

## Material
**Striker Materials:**
*   High-carbon steel (produces best sparks).
*   Flint, chert, quartz (hard, sharp stones).
*   Ferrocerium rods (salvage item, 10,000–20,000 strikes).

**Tinder Materials:**
*   Char cloth (spark catcher).
*   Tinder fungus (Fomes fomentarius) (for catching weak sparks).
*   Flash tinders (cattail fluff, dried grass, birch bark dust).
*   Sustaining tinders (fatwood shavings, birch bark strips, wax-infused materials).
*   Other effective tinders: Birch bark, fatwood shavings, dried grass (fine shavings), dryer lint, cotton balls + petroleum jelly.

## System
The system relies on generating high-temperature sparks to ignite tinder, bypassing the need for sustained friction heat.

## Technology
*   **Flint and Steel:** Sparks result from the friction of steel shaving off the high-carbon steel striker against the stone, heating the shavings past the ignition point (roughly 1,500°F / 815°C).
*   **Ferrocerium:** Produces sparks at approximately 3,000°F (1,650°C), twice as hot as flint sparks, capable of igniting fine natural tinder directly.
*   **Quartz-on-Quartz:** Produces visible sparks via piezoelectric discharges, which are cooler than steel sparks and generally useless for fire-starting unless paired with tinder fungus.

## Tools
*   High-carbon steel striker.
*   Flint or sharp stone (flint, chert, quartz).
*   Ferrocerium rod.
*   Char cloth (for catching sparks).
*   A metal tin (for char cloth preparation).
*   Knife spine or hard, sharp edge (for ferro rod technique).

## Process
**Method 1: Flint and Steel (Traditional)**
1.  **Material Preparation:** Ensure the stone has a sharp edge (knap or break it).
2.  **Char Cloth Preparation:** Cut 100% cotton or linen fabric into 2-inch squares. Place in a metal tin with a tight-fitting lid. Punch a small hole (1/8 inch / 3 mm) in the lid. Heat for 10-15 minutes. Remove the tin and immediately plug the hole with a stick or nail.
3.  **Striking Technique:** Rest char cloth directly on the flint, extending past the edge. Strike the steel downward with a glancing blow to shave steel, not smash the stone.

**Method 2: Ferrocerium (Ferro Rod)**
1.  **Technique:** Hold the rod steady near the tinder. Place the spine of a knife against the rod at a 45-degree angle.
2.  **Striking Technique:** Push the scraper firmly along the rod, or hold the scraper steady and pull the rod backward away from the tinder to keep the scraper stationary.
3.  **Tinder Application:** Sparks are hot enough to ignite many natural tinders directly (birch bark shavings, fatwood shavings, dried grass, cattail fluff, dryer lint).

**Method 3: Quartz-on-Quartz (Primitive Spark)**
1.  **Action:** Strike two pieces of quartz together in darkness.
2.  **Success Condition:** Only works if tinder fungus is present and prepared to catch the weak piezoelectric discharges.

**Tinder Preparation Hierarchy (Regardless of Method):**
1.  **Spark catchers** (char cloth, tinder fungus, charred punk wood).
2.  **Flash tinders** (cattail fluff, dried grass, birch bark dust).
3.  **Sustaining tinders** (fatwood shavings, birch bark strips, wax-infused materials).
*   **Layering Rule:** Build tinder bundles in layers: spark catcher in the center, flash tinder around it, and sustaining tinder on the outside.

## Notes
*   The sparks come from the **steel**, not the stone.
*   Char cloth is nearly essential for flint-and-steel fire starting.
*   Ferrocerium rods are a salvage-only resource with a lifespan of 10,000–20,000 strikes.
*   Tinder preparation determines success as much as spark generation.
*   Once high-carbon steel is forged, flint-and-steel fire starting becomes permanently sustainable.
*   Striking two stones together results in cold piezoelectric flashes.
