# Fire Plough

## Objective
Start a fire without matches by using friction between two pieces of wood to produce a hot ember, then transferring it to tinder.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Dry, combustible wood and dry tinder are essential.

Prepare fine tinder and small kindling before beginning, so the ember can be transferred promptly.

Secure the fireboard so it does not slip or wobble during the strokes.

Use the method only where fire is permitted, and keep water or another means of extinguishing the fire nearby.

## Material
* **Fireboard (hearth):** A flat piece of relatively soft, dry wood with a groove running along its surface.
* **Plough (blade):** A straight stick or wooden piece, preferably harder than the fireboard, with a blunt or squared tip that fits the groove.
* **Tinder:** A loose bundle of very fine, dry, easily ignited fibers.
* **Kindling:** Small, dry twigs or similar fuel to grow the flame into a sustainable fire.

## System
The plough is pressed into the fireboard’s groove and moved back and forth. Friction heats and abrades the wood, producing fine, hot dust. The dust gathers near one end of the groove and can form a glowing ember, which is transferred to the tinder.

## Technology
The fire plough is a friction-based ignition method. Unlike a hand drill, it does not spin a spindle; instead, it creates heat by repeatedly driving a wooden blade along a groove. The groove helps guide the blade and collect the hot wood dust.

## Tools
- Knife or other suitable cutting tool for shaping the groove and plough.
- A heavy object, or a knee or foot, to hold the fireboard steady.
- A non-flammable surface for the tinder and ember.

## Process
1. Select dry wood for both pieces. Shape the fireboard with a straight groove, and shape the plough’s end so it sits in the groove without being sharp or pointed.
2. Place the fireboard on a stable surface and secure it against movement. Position the tinder beside the end of the groove where the dust will collect. Angle the board slightly so the far end is lower to collect dust.
3. Set the plough into the groove. Press down firmly and push it  forward along the groovewith heavy downward pressure in rapid, full-length strokes, then return it to the starting position with lighter pressure if needed.
4. Repeat the strokes, maintaining firm contact and a steady, brisk pace. Continue until dark dust gathers at the end of the groove and begins to smoke.
5. Keep working until the dust consolidates into a glowing ember. Avoid disturbing it while it develops.
6. Carefully transfer the ember into the tinder bundle. Close the tinder loosely around it and blow gently until it catches flame.
7. Add small kindling gradually, then larger fuel as the fire strengthens.

## Notes
The fire plough can require substantial sustained effort, and friction methods depend strongly on dry, compatible materials and consistent pressure.
