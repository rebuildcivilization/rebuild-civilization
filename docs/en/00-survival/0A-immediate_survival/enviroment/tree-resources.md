# Tree Resources

## Objective
To recognize trees as year-round sources of construction material, fuel, cordage, food and other useful - resources.
 
## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Reliable tree identification.
- Knowledge of local species.
- Awareness of toxic or protected species.
- Safe cutting practices.

## Material
- Bark.
- Wood.
- Roots.
- Sap and resin.
- Needles.
- Seeds and nuts.
- Leaves and branches.

## System
Different parts of a tree can provide different resources:
- structural wood;
- firewood;
- tinder;
- cordage;
- containers;
- food;
- medicinal materials;
- adhesives;
- shelter material.

## Technology
- Wood processing.
- Bark processing.
- Resin and pitch.
- Natural cordage.
- Shelter construction.
- Fire preparation.

## Tools
- Knife.
- Saw.
- Axe.
- Digging stick.
- Containers for collecting resin or seeds.

## Process
1. Identify the tree before harvesting.
2. Prefer naturally fallen material where possible.
3. Select material according to the intended use.
4. Separate structural wood from tinder and firewood.
5. Process bark, roots, resin or seeds only when positively identified.
6. Avoid unnecessary damage to living trees.
7. Preserve useful material for later tasks.

## Notes
- Trees are particularly valuable because useful material can remain available outside the short growing - season of many herbaceous plants.
- Resource use should favor deadfall where possible to reduce environmental impact.
- Tree identification should be regional rather than based solely on a generic species list.
