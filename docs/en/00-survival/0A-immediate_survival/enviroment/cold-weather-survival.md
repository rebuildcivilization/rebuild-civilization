# Cold Weather Survival

## Objective

To maintain body temperature, prevent cold-related injury, and preserve energy and mobility in cold environments.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

Cold-weather survival depends on controlling:

* Heat loss.
* Moisture.
* Wind exposure.
* Clothing condition.
* Shelter.
* Fire.
* Hydration.
* Energy expenditure.

Cold, wet, and windy conditions can become dangerous even when temperatures are not extremely low.

The following factors can contribute to hypothermia:
- wind;
- wet clothing;
- exhaustion;
- inadequate clothing;
- cold-water immersion;
- dehydration;
- insufficient caloric intake.

## Material

Useful equipment includes:

* Insulating clothing.
* insulated boots
* Waterproof outer layer.
* Extra and thick socks.
* Sleeping insulation.
* Fire-starting equipment.
* Shelter material.
* Water container.
* Insulated sleeping surface.
* wool/synthetic base layers;
* gloves;
* balaclava/neck protection;
* eye protection;
* spare clothing.

It’s better to wear synthetic or woollen clothing than cotton in cold weather.

## System

### Cold-Weather Risk Management

Cold-weather survival requires managing heat loss while avoiding unnecessary exhaustion and moisture accumulation.

Priorities include:

- Protecting the head, hands, feet, and other exposed areas.
- Keeping clothing and insulation as dry as possible.
- Reducing exposure to wind and precipitation.
- Insulating the body from cold ground.
- Adjusting activity and clothing to avoid excessive sweating.
- Recognizing early signs of cold-related illness.
- Establishing a safe shelter before exhaustion prevents further work.

Wet clothing, wind, fatigue, and inadequate insulation can combine to increase heat loss rapidly.

Do not rely on a fire as the only means of protection. A suitable shelter, dry insulation, and appropriate clothing remain essential.

### Heat Management
Protect the body's core first.

Reduce heat loss by:
* Staying dry.
* Blocking wind.
* Insulating from the ground.
* Using shelter.
* Maintaining an appropriate fire when safe.
* Avoiding unnecessary exertion.

### Clothing

Clothing must provide insulation while limiting exposure to wind and precipitation.

Wet clothing loses insulating effectiveness.

Keep spare socks and essential clothing protected from moisture.

It is advisable to wear three layers:
- base layer;
- insulating mid-layer;
- wind/water-resistant outer layer.


### Feet
Feet are particularly vulnerable during prolonged cold and wet exposure.

Keep them:
* Dry.
* Warm.
* Clean.
* Regularly inspected.

### Hydration
Cold weather does not eliminate the need for water.

Heavy clothing and physical activity can still result in significant water loss.

## Technology

### Insulation

Insulation works by reducing the movement of heat away from the body.

The ground can remove substantial body heat, so an insulating layer beneath the body is important.

### Fire

Fire provides heat but also consumes fuel and requires continuous management.

In snow-covered terrain, a prepared base can prevent the fire from sinking into the snow.

## Tools

* Insulated clothing.
* Waterproof storage.
* Fire starter.
* Knife.
* Shelter material.
* Sleeping insulation.
* Extra socks.

## Process

1. Stop unnecessary exposure to wind and precipitation.
2. Establish shelter.
3. Insulate yourself from the ground.
4. Replace or dry wet clothing when possible.
5. Protect spare clothing and socks from moisture. Keep the gearbox dry.
6. Establish a reliable fire when appropriate.
7. Drink regularly.
8. Eating to maintain body heat;
9. Inspect hands, feet, face, and ears for loss of sensation or abnormal color.
10. Reduce unnecessary exertion and sweat.
11. Maintain enough fuel for the expected duration of the cold period.
12. Avoid prolonged immobility.


## Notes

* Loss of sensation in hands or feet is a warning sign.
* Prolonged wet exposure near freezing can damage the feet even when freezing injury does not occur.
* Extra socks should be kept protected from water.
* Wet socks can be dried using body heat when necessary, but care must be taken to avoid burns or excessive moisture.
* Cold-weather clothing can hide dehydration because sweat and moisture may be absorbed by clothing.
* Specific medical treatment of hypothermia and frostbite should be verified against current medical guidance.
* It does not have to be extremely cold for it to become dangerous.
