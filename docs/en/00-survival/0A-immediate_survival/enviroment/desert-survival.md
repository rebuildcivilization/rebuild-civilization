# Desert Survival

## Objective

To preserve water, reduce heat exposure, and maintain mobility in arid environments where water and shade may be scarce.

## Dependencies and prerequisites

Desert survival depends primarily on:

* Water management.
* Heat management.
* Shelter from the sun.
* Navigation.
* Energy conservation.
* Protection of feet and skin.

## Material

Useful equipment includes:

* Water containers.
* Sun protection.
* Shelter material.
* Head covering.
* Appropriate clothing.
* Navigation tools.
* Fire-starting equipment.

## System

### Water

Water is the primary resource.

Plan movement around known or suspected water sources rather than treating water as something to search for only after becoming thirsty.

Possible indicators include:

* Human tracks.
* Animal trails.
* Vegetation.
* Insects.
* Natural depressions.
* Wells or other established water points.

Animal behavior as an indicator of water, but different species have different reliability.

### Heat

Reduce exposure to direct sun.

Use shade whenever possible and avoid unnecessary exertion during the hottest period.

### Movement

Movement consumes water.

Choose routes that balance distance, terrain, shade, and the likelihood of finding water.

## Technology

### Passive Cooling

Shade, ventilation, loose clothing, and reduced physical activity decrease heat gain and water loss.

### Water from Plants

Several regional plants that can contain water, including certain roots, vines, and palms.

Plant identification must be positive before consuming plant fluids because not all water-bearing-looking plants are safe.

## Tools

* Water container.
* Shelter material.
* Knife.
* Navigation equipment.
* Head covering.
* Appropriate clothing.

## Process

1. Establish shade or shelter.
2. Inventory available water.
3. Estimate the distance to known or suspected water.
4. Reduce unnecessary movement.
5. Travel during cooler periods when practical.
6. Observe animal trails and vegetation for possible water indicators.
7. Protect water supplies from evaporation and contamination.
8. Treat newly obtained water using the established water-purification procedure.
9. Monitor for signs of heat stress and dehydration.

## Notes

* Water conservation is generally more important than food procurement during immediate desert survival.
* Carnivorous animals are poor indicators of nearby water because they can obtain moisture from prey.
* Grain-eating birds and some insects can provide useful indications, but observations must be interpreted carefully.
* This document intentionally does not duplicate the repository's dedicated water-procurement and shelter documents.
