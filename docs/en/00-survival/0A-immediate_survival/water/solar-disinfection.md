# Solar Disinfection (SODIS)

## Objective
To kill pathogens in water using the synergy of UV-A radiation and heat.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Water Clarity:** SODIS only works with relatively clear water. Cloudy water must be pre-filtered.
*   **Sunlight/Weather:** Requires 6 hours of full sun or 2 full days of overcast conditions.
*   **Bottle Type:** Use only clear PET bottles.
*   **Water Temperature:** Ideal conditions involve water reaching $50^{\circ}$C or above for accelerated disinfection.

## Material
*   **Clear PET plastic bottles:** The standard disposable water/soda bottle.
*   **Reflective surface (Optional but recommended):** Corrugated metal roofing, aluminum foil, or a white-painted surface.

## System
Solar Disinfection (SODIS) is a household water treatment method endorsed by the World Health Organization (WHO).

## Technology
SODIS works by combining two mechanisms to damage microbial DNA and kill pathogens:

1.  **UV-A Radiation:** Specifically UV-A radiation in the $320-400$ nm range damages the DNA of bacteria, viruses, and protozoa, making them unable to reproduce.
2.  **Thermal Heating:** Solar infrared radiation heats the water. When temperature exceeds $50^{\circ}$C, the disinfection rate accelerates dramatically.
3.  **Synergistic Effect:** The combination of UV exposure and elevated temperature creates a synergistic effect where organisms weakened by UV are finished off by heat, and vice versa.
4.  **Dissolved Oxygen:** Oxygenating the water before capping allows dissolved oxygen to react with UV to form reactive oxygen species (hydrogen peroxide, superoxide), which are toxic to microorganisms.

## Tools
*   **Clear PET bottles:** The primary container.
*   **Pre-filter material:** Cloth, cotton t-shirt, or improvised sand filter (if water is cloudy).
*   **Reflective surface:** Used to maximize UV exposure by reflecting light into the bottle.

## Process
**Step 1 — Assess water clarity:** Hold the bottle against a newspaper or printed text to check clarity. If insufficient, pre-filter the water.

**Step 2 — Pre-filter if necessary:** Pour cloudy water through a cloth or filter to ensure UV penetration.

**Step 3 — Fill the bottle three-quarters full:** Leave the top quarter empty to allow for oxygenation.

**Step 4 — Oxygenate the water:** Cap the bottle and shake it vigorously for $20-30$ seconds. Fill the bottle completely and re-cap.

**Step 5 — Place bottles in direct sunlight:** Lay bottles horizontally on a reflective surface. A corrugated metal roof is ideal.

**Step 6 — Expose for the correct duration:**
*   Full sun, clear sky: $6$ hours.
*   Partly cloudy ($<50\%$ cloud cover): $6$ hours.
*   Overcast ($>50\%$ cloud cover): $2$ full days ($48$ hours).
*   Heavy rain or continuous dense cloud: Do not use SODIS; switch to boiling or chemical treatment.

**Step 7 — Check water temperature (if possible):** If the water feels hot enough to exceed $50^{\circ}$C, disinfection is dramatically improved. Dark surfaces under the bottles increase thermal heating.

**Step 8 — Store treated water:** Store the treated water in the same bottle with the cap on. Consume within $24$ hours, as there is no residual disinfectant.

## Notes
*   **Bottle Size Limit:** Maximum bottle size is $2$ liters; larger containers block too much UV penetration.
*   **Limitations:** SODIS has limited effectiveness against *Cryptosporidium* oocysts, which are the most UV-resistant pathogen. Chemical pollutants must be filtered before SODIS.
*   **Optimization:** Painting the bottom half of the bottle black or using dark surfaces boosts temperature above $50^{\circ}$C, reducing required exposure time to approximately $1$ hour in full sun.
*   **Batch Processing:** For a group of $10$ people, $40$ bottles rotating daily is suggested.
*   **Comparison Summary:** SODIS is preferred when fuel is unavailable, when moving, and when chemical supplies are exhausted, excelling in these scenarios where boiling requires fuel and chemical treatment uses finite supplies.
*   **Key Takeaway:** Dissolved oxygen is critical for pathogen killing.
