# Filtration Methods

## Objective
To achieve water purification by the physical removal of suspended particles, sediment, and many pathogens from water by passing it through porous media. Filtration serves as the first line of defense in any multi-barrier water treatment approach.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Time available (determines the appropriate filtration strategy).
*   Materials at hand (for filter media and containers).

## Material
*   Porous media (sand, gravel, charcoal).
*   Containers (bottles, barrels).
*   Filter media (e.g., sand grains of 0.15-0.35 mm for slow filters).
*   Cloth (for straining).

## System
Filtration systems range from simple cloth straining to engineered multi-layer systems.

**Filtration Spectrum Summary:**
| Method | Removes Sediment | Removes Protozoa | Removes Bacteria | Removes Viruses | Removes Chemicals |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cloth straining | Partial | No | No | No | No |
| Improvised sand filter | Yes | Partial | Partial | No | No |
| Slow sand filter (mature) | Yes | Yes | Yes (95-99%) | Partial | Partial |
| Charcoal filter | Yes | No | No | No | Partial |
| Ceramic filter | Yes | Yes | Yes (most) | No | No |
| Combined sand + charcoal | Yes | Partial | Partial | No | Partial |

## Technology
Filtration relies on four distinct mechanisms to remove contaminants:

1.  **Mechanical Straining:** Particles larger than the gaps between filter grains are physically trapped. Sand grains of 0.15-0.35 mm effectively strain out protozoan cysts (8-15 micrometers).
2.  **Sedimentation Within the Filter:** Particles settle onto grain surfaces by gravity as water slows down through the filter bed. Filter depth increases the opportunity for settling.
3.  **Adsorption:** Certain filter media attract dissolved contaminants to their surfaces via chemical and electrostatic forces. Charcoal is the classic adsorptive medium for binding organic chemicals, chlorine, and some heavy metals. Activated charcoal offers superior adsorptive capacity due to its enormous internal surface area.
4.  **Biological Activity:** In continuous systems (like slow sand filters), a living ecosystem (bacteria, algae, protozoa, invertebrates) develops on the filter surface, which actively consumes waterborne pathogens.

## Tools
*   Container (drill, punch, or poke drainage holes).
*   Support layers (coarse gravel).
*   Main filter material (coarse sand or fine sand).
*   Top gravel layer (for distribution).

## Process
**Basic Gravity Filter Procedure:**

**Step 1:** Select a container and drill/punch drainage holes in the bottom. For a barrel, use a perforated pipe or gravel underdrain.
**Step 2:** Install a support layer of coarse gravel (10-20 mm) at the bottom, 5-10 cm deep, to prevent sand from washing through the drain.
**Step 3:** Add the main filter layer. Use coarse sand (0.5-1.0 mm) for rapid filters or fine sand (0.15-0.35 mm) for slow filters. Depth should be at least 30 cm for rapid and 60 cm for slow.
**Step 4:** Add a top gravel layer (5 cm) to distribute incoming water evenly and prevent the sand bed from being disturbed.
**Step 5:** Flush the filter by running several batches of water through and discarding the output. The first runs will be cloudy.
**Step 6:** Begin filtering. Pour water onto the top gravel layer gently, never blasting it directly onto the sand. Collect output from the drain.

**Flow Rate Diagnostic:**
*   **Slow flow** (0.1-0.3 meters per hour for slow sand filters) indicates thorough contact with the filter medium and good filtration.
*   **Fast flow** indicates the water is finding channels through the sand rather than percolating uniformly, signifying poor removal.

## Common Mistakes
*   **Using beach sand without washing:** Contaminates the output with salt and organic residue. Prevention: Rinse sand thoroughly with clean water before use.
*   **Letting the filter run dry:** Causes cracks in the sand bed and kills the biofilm. Prevention: Keep water level above the sand surface.
*   **Pouring water too aggressively:** Disturbs the sand bed, creating channels. Prevention: Pour onto a diffuser plate or the gravel layer.
*   **Trusting filtration alone:** Bacteria and viruses can pass through most filters. Prevention: Always disinfect after filtering unless using a fully mature slow sand filter.
*   **Never cleaning the filter:** Causes flow rate to drop to zero and biofilm to become anaerobic. Prevention: Scrape the top 1-2 cm when flow rate drops below usable levels.

## Notes
*   Filtration is not a single technique; combining mechanisms (straining, sedimentation, adsorption, and biological activity) yields the cleanest water.
*   The most powerful low-tech treatment available is a fully mature slow sand filter, which approaches safe drinking water quality by itself.
*   The key takeaway for survival is that filtration must always be paired with disinfection unless a slow sand filter is confirmed to be operating correctly.
