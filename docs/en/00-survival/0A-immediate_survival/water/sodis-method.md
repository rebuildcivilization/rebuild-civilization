# SODIS Method: Bottle UV Exposure

## Objective
To turn sunlight into a daily water-purification pipeline that requires no fire and no chemicals through solar disinfection.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Clear source water that is clear enough to read text through a filled bottle (Newspaper Test).
*   Clear, minimally scratched PET bottles (recycling symbol 1, 0.5–2 L capacity).
*   A reflective surface for positioning bottles.
*   A location with sufficient sunlight.

## Material
*   Clear PET bottles (0.5L to 2L capacity).
*   Source water.
*   Reflective surfaces (e.g., corrugated metal, aluminum foil).
*   Pre-filter materials (cloth, sand filter, or layered grass bundle).
*   Labeling system (scratch number, marker, or rubber bands).

## System
For a permanent or semi-permanent camp, a dedicated SODIS station can be built, including:
1.  **Exposure rack:** A south-facing (Northern Hemisphere) or north-facing (Southern Hemisphere) angled surface at roughly 30 degrees from horizontal, covered with reflective material (corrugated metal is ideal).
2.  **Pre-filter station:** A sand/charcoal filter gravity-feeding into a collection vessel.
3.  **Labeling system:** To track treatment dates.
4.  **Buffer stock:** 2 days’ worth of treated water stored in shade.

## Technology
*   **Solar Disinfection (SODIS):** Utilizing UV radiation from sunlight to disinfect water.
*   **Oxygenation:** Shaking water to introduce dissolved oxygen, which reacts with UV to create reactive oxygen species that enhance pathogen killing by 3-5 times.
*   **Thermal Effects:** Utilizing reflective surfaces to heat bottles and boost water temperature.

## Tools
*   Newspaper for the Newspaper Test.
*   A system for marking bottles (scratch numbers, markers, or rubber bands).

## Process
### Preparation
1.  **Find Bottles:** Source clear, minimally scratched PET bottles with the recycling symbol #1.
2.  **Inspect:** Check material (PET, #1), color (completely clear), scratch level (minimal), shape integrity (round), cap condition (tight/no cracks), and smell (none after rinsing).
3.  **Prepare:** Remove all labels, rinse thoroughly 2-3 times, inspect for scratches under sunlight, and mark each bottle with a scratch number or symbol.

### Morning Setup (Perform Daily by 9:00 AM)
1.  **Collect Source Water:** Use the cleanest available source. Pre-filter turbidity through cloth, a sand filter, or a layered grass bundle. The water must be clear enough to read text through the filled bottle.
2.  **Fill Bottles:** Fill each bottle three-quarters full, leaving approximately 25% air space at the top.
3.  **Oxygenate:** Cap tightly and shake vigorously for 20–30 seconds until small bubbles form.
4.  **Top Off and Cap:** Remove the cap, fill the bottle completely to the brim, and re-cap tightly.
5.  **Position Bottles:** Lay bottles horizontally on the exposure surface, spacing them at least 5 cm apart to prevent shading.

### Positioning for Maximum Effectiveness
*   **Surface Selection (Ranked by Effectiveness):** Corrugated metal roof (Best), Aluminum foil on a flat surface (Excellent), Car hood/roof (dark color) (Very good), White-painted board (Good), Dark rocks or asphalt (Good), Bare ground (Adequate), Grass (Poor).
*   **Angle:** Place bottles horizontally to expose the maximum surface area to direct overhead sun. Do NOT stand bottles upright.
*   **Orientation:** Align the bottle axis roughly east-west so the broadest face catches the sun as it arcs from east to west through the day.

### Timing Decision Matrix
*   **Clear sky, strong sun, warm day:** Standard SODIS (6 hours).
*   **Partly cloudy, sun breaking through:** Standard SODIS (6 hours).
*   **Overcast but bright (can see shadows):** Extended SODIS (2 consecutive days).
*   **Dark overcast, no shadows visible:** Abort SODIS (Use boiling or chemical treatment).
*   **Rain:** Abort SODIS (Use boiling or chemical treatment).
*   **Shadow Test:** If your shadow is visible on the ground, UV levels are sufficient for SODIS.

### Retrieval and Storage
1.  **Retrieve:** Take bottles out after the exposure period. If placed at 9 AM, bottles are ready at 3 PM on a full-sun day.
2.  **Label/Group:** Mark the treatment date on the bottle.
3.  **Store:** Move treated bottles out of direct sun to prevent algae growth. Store upright with caps tightly sealed.
4.  **Consume:** Consume treated water within 24 hours, as SODIS provides no residual disinfectant.

### Scaling SODIS for Groups
| Group Size | Bottles Needed Daily (1L) | Total Bottles in Rotation | 
| :--- | :--- | :--- | 
| 1 person | 3-4 | 8-10 | 
| Family of 4 | 12-16 | 30-40 | 
| Group of 10 | 30-40 | 80-100 | 
| Community of 50 | 150-200 | 400-500 |

### Troubleshooting
| Problem | Cause | Fix |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Water still cloudy after treatment | Insufficient pre-filtration | Filter again; never SODIS cloudy water |
| Bottles fog up inside | Temperature differential (normal condensation) | Not a problem — disinfection still works |
| Green tint developing in stored bottles | Algae growth from sunlight during storage | Store treated bottles in darkness; consume within 24h |
| People getting sick despite SODIS | Insufficient exposure, scratched bottles, or cloudy water | Audit every step; replace old bottles; enforce clarity test |
| Not enough bottles for the group | Supply shortage | Scavenge more; consider a mix of SODIS and boiling |
| Caps degrading | UV breaks down plastic caps faster than bottles | Replace caps from discarded bottles; stockpile extras |

### Combining SODIS with Other Methods
*   **Pre-filter + SODIS:** Standard protocol for common scenarios.
*   **Pre-filter + SODIS + lime/lemon juice:** Add 30ml of citrus juice per liter before sun exposure to accelerate pathogen killing.
*   **SODIS + chlorine:** Treat with a half-dose of chlorine (1 drop/liter) before sun exposure to provide residual protection.

## Notes
*   **Bottle Degradation:** Heavily scratched bottles transmit up to 50% less UV-A than new ones. Replace bottles every 3-6 months.
*   **Consistency is Key:** Small inefficiencies (partial shade, forgetting to oxygenate) compound, leading to water that looks clean but harbors pathogens.
*   **Residual Disinfectant:** SODIS provides no residual protection; consumption within 24 hours is mandatory.
*   **Buffer Stock:** For groups, maintaining a 2-day buffer stock is crucial for cloudy-day coverage.
