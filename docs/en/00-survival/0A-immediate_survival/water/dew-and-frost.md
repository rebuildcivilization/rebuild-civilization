# Dew and Frost

## Objective
Predicting moisture, fair weather, and weather changes from ground-level condensation signs.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Three conditions are required for dew formation:
1. Clear skies
2. Calm or light wind
3. Sufficient moisture in the air

## Material
Dew forms most heavily on objects with low thermal mass that cool quickly: grass blades, leaves, car roofs, and thin metal surfaces. It forms least on thick stone, packed earth, and objects sheltered under trees or overhangs.

## System
Weather Forecasting

## Technology
Dew forms when objects cool below the dew point temperature through radiative cooling, causing water vapor in the air to condense on them. Frost forms when the surface temperature drops below 0C (32F) AND below the dew point simultaneously (deposition).

## Tools
Absorbent cloth (t-shirt, bandana) and containers.

## Process
**How Dew Forms:** Objects cool below the dew point, causing water vapor in the air to condense on them.
**Dew as a Weather Indicator:**
*   **Heavy dew in the morning** indicates fair weather ahead (skies were clear overnight, winds were calm, atmosphere was stable).
*   **No dew when expected** indicates a weather change is coming (cloud cover moving in, wind picking up, or dry air mass arrival).
**How Frost Differs from Dew:** Frost occurs when the surface drops below 0C and below the dew point. White frost appears when the dew point is at or above freezing but the surface drops below freezing. Black frost occurs when temperatures drop below freezing but the air is too dry for visible ice to form.

**Frost vs Dew Decision Points:**
| Condition | Result |
| :--- | :--- | 
| Surface below dew point, above 0C | Dew |
| Surface below dew point, below 0C, moist air | White frost |
| Surface below 0C, air very dry | Black frost (no visible ice) |
| Surface above dew point | Nothing forms |

**Practical Applications:**
*   **Morning planning:** Check grass and metal surfaces at dawn. Heavy dew means plan outdoor work confidently.
*   **Campsite selection:** Pitch camp on slight slopes or elevated ground. Low spots collect both cold air and heavy dew, increasing frost risk.
*   **Water collection:** Drag absorbent cloth through dewy grass at dawn and wring into a container. A productive meadow can yield 0.5-1 liter per hour of collection. The water is clean.
*   **Crop timing:** Plant frost-sensitive crops after the last heavy dew/frost pattern of spring. Wait for consistent dew-only mornings before transplanting tender seedlings.

## Notes
- Dew is explicitly described as not being ‘falling’ moisture.
- Black frost is described as more dangerous to crops because there is no visual warning.
- The conditions leading to dew (clear skies, calm wind, sufficient moisture) typically persist into the following day.
