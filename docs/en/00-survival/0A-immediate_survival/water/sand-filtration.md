# Sand Filtration: A Comparative Analysis

## Objective
To remove waterborne contaminants using layers of granular sand as a physical and biological barrier against waterborne contaminants. It is the oldest engineered water treatment method and remains one of the most effective without modern technology.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   **Washing Sand Before Use:** Non-negotiable. Unwashed sand contains silt, clay, and organic matter that will seal the filter bed and produce foul-tasting water.
*   **Pre-treatment:** Required for rapid sand filters (coagulation/settling).
*   **Biological Maturation Time:** 2-4 weeks for a slow sand filter before the output is considered safe.
*   **Maintenance:** Need to keep the slow sand filter wet to prevent biofilm death.

## Material
*   **Sand Grain Sizes:**
    *   Fine Sand (0.15-0.35 mm): Correct for slow sand filters.
    *   Medium Sand (0.5-1.0 mm): Correct for rapid sand filters.
*   **Gravel:**
    *   Coarse gravel (10-20 mm) for underdrain support layers.
    *   Fine gravel (3-6 mm) for transition layers.
*   **Sand Quality:** River sand (inland) is best due to well-rounded grains and minimal organic matter.

## System
Sand filtration involves two fundamentally different systems:

1.  **Rapid Sand Filter:** A coarser, faster version of the gravity filter, chosen when high volume is needed quickly and disinfection is planned.
2.  **Slow Sand Filter:** The more powerful system, designed for detailed construction and achieving high pathogen removal through biological action.

### Comparison of Sand Filtration Types

| Property | Slow Sand Filter | Rapid Sand Filter |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Flow rate | 0.1-0.3 m/hour | 5-15 m/hour |
| Sand grain size | 0.15-0.35 mm (fine) | 0.5-1.2 mm (coarse) |
| Bed depth | 60-120 cm | 60-75 cm |
| Primary removal mechanism | Biological (schmutzdecke) | Mechanical straining |
| Requires pre-treatment | No (handles moderate turbidity) | Yes (coagulation/settling) |
| Pathogen removal | 90-99% bacteria, 99%+ protozoa | Minimal without chemicals |
| Maintenance | Scrape top layer every 1-6 months | Backwash every 1-3 days |
| Startup time | 2-4 weeks (biofilm maturation) | Immediate |

## Technology
Sand grains in a filter bed remove contaminants through several simultaneous mechanisms:

*   **Physical straining:** Traps particles larger than the pore spaces between grains.
*   **Sedimentation:** Particles settle onto grain surfaces as water velocity drops in narrow passages.
*   **Adsorption:** Binds dissolved substances to grain surfaces via electrostatic attraction and Van der Waals forces (more significant with biological coatings).
*   **Biological predation:** The dominant mechanism in slow sand filters, where the schmutzdecke actively consumes bacteria, viruses, and organic matter.

## Tools
*   Containers (20L buckets, 200L drums, Concrete boxes).
*   Sieves (implied for grain size measurement).
*   Sticks or hands (for agitation during backwashing).

## Process
### Washing Sand Before Use (Non-Negotiable)
1.  Place sand in a container (bucket, barrel, or pit lined with cloth).
2.  Fill with water and stir vigorously for 30 seconds (water turns brown or grey).
3.  Carefully pour off the dirty water without losing sand (tip slowly or use a siphon).
4.  Repeat until the rinse water runs clear (typically 5-8 washes).
5.  Test a handful by rubbing it between palms; clean sand feels purely gritty.

### Building a Rapid Sand Filter
1.  Select a container at least 60 cm tall (200L drum ideal).
2.  Add 10 cm of coarse gravel (10-20 mm) as an underdrain support layer.
3.  Add 5 cm of fine gravel (3-6 mm) as a transition layer.
4.  Add 60 cm of medium sand (0.5-1.0 mm), pre-washed.
5.  Add 5 cm of coarse gravel as a diffusion layer on top.
6.  Fill the container with water and open the drain valve. Target a flow rate that empties a full container in 2-4 hours.
7.  Discard the first three full volumes of output, as the water must be disinfected.

### Backwashing a Rapid Filter
1.  Close the drain valve.
2.  Fill the filter to the top with clean water.
3.  Agitate the top 15-20 cm of sand vigorously with a stick or by hand to break up sediment.
4.  Open the drain valve and let the dirty water flush out (a dark plume will appear initially).
5.  Repeat 2-3 times until the flush water runs relatively clear.
6.  Resume normal operation.

### Building a Slow Sand Filter
*   Use finer sand (0.15-0.35 mm).
*   Build a deeper bed (80-120 cm).
*   Design for continuous operation (sand must never dry out).
*   Allow 2-4 weeks for the biological layer to mature before relying on the output.
*   **Never backwash:** Instead, scrape and replace the top 1-2 cm when flow slows.

## Notes
*   **Critical Distinction:** A rapid sand filter is a pre-treatment step that must be followed by disinfection. A mature slow sand filter can produce drinking-quality water on its own.
*   **Critical Mistake:** Mixing slow and rapid sand in one filter destroys either approach due to incorrect grain size.
*   **Critical Mistake:** Letting a slow sand filter dry out kills the biofilm, removing the main treatment mechanism.
*   **Critical Mistake:** Backwashing a slow sand filter destroys the biofilm that took weeks to grow; only scraping is appropriate.
*   **Backwash Optimization:** If source water is very turbid, a pre-settling step (letting water sit for 2-4 hours before pouring into the filter) can dramatically reduce the need for backwashing a rapid filter.
