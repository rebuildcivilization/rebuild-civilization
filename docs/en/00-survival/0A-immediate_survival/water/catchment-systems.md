# Catchment Systems: Collection Infrastructure

## Objective
To intercept, channel, and deliver water from where it falls to where it is stored, ensuring the system works together without leaks or clogs.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   A collection surface, a conveyance channel, and a screened inlet must work together.
*   Catchment area maximization is the single most impactful variable.
*   Gutters must be sized for peak storm flow, not average flow.
*   System components must be sealed and screened to prevent contamination.

## Material
### Collection Surfaces
*   Metal (corrugated iron/steel):
    *   Efficiency: 90-95%
    *   Contamination Risk: Low
*   Tile (clay or concrete):
    *   Efficiency: 85-90%
    *   Contamination Risk: Low
*   Slate:
    *   Efficiency: 90-95%
    *   Contamination Risk: Very low
*   Plastic sheeting:
    *   Efficiency: 90-95%
    *   Contamination Risk: Very low (Ideal improvised surface)
*   Thatch (grass/palm):
    *   Efficiency: 40-60%
    *   Contamination Risk: Moderate
*   Wood shingle:
    *   Efficiency: 60-75%
    *   Contamination Risk: Moderate
*   Tarpaper/asphalt:
    *   Efficiency: 85-90%
    *   Contamination Risk: HIGH (Petroleum leaching)

### Ground Catchment Surface Treatments
*   Compacted clay (Effort: Low-medium; Durability: 3-5 years):
    *   Efficiency: 70-80%
*   Plastic sheeting over ground (Effort: Low; Durability: 1-3 years due to UV degradation):
    *   Efficiency: 85-95%
*   Poured lime-concrete slab (Effort: High; Durability: 20+ years):
    *   Efficiency: 90-95%
*   Flat stone pavement (Effort: Medium-high; Durability: Indefinite):
    *   Efficiency: 80-90%
*   Rammed earth (stabilized) (Effort: Medium; Durability: 5-10 years):
    *   Efficiency: 65-80%

### Conveyance Materials
*   Impermeable material (used to line ground channels) must match the catchment surface material.
*   Pitch, clay, or mortar (used for sealing cracks).

## System
The catchment system consists of three essential parts:
1.  **Collection Surface:** Where water lands.
2.  **Conveyance:** How water travels to storage (gutters, channels, pipes).
3.  **Inlet:** Where water enters the storage vessel.

## Technology
### The Three Rules of Catchment
1.  **Maximize collection area:** A 1% increase in catchment area produces a 1% increase in yield.
2.  **Minimize loss in transit:** Achieved through tight joints, smooth flow paths, and adequate slope to prevent leaks, splashes, and evaporation.
3.  **Exclude contamination:** Achieved through screens, covers, sealed joints, and first-flush diversion.

### Sizing and Efficiency
*   **Roof Catchment:** Metal and plastic roofs offer 90-95% efficiency.
*   **Gutter Sizing:** Must handle peak flow rate. Oversize gutters by at least 25% of the calculated need to prevent overflow.
*   **Ground Catchment Sizing Example:** Required area = Target liters / (Expected rain mm x Efficiency).

### Terrain Catchment
*   **Rock Outcrop Catchment:** Requires sealing cracks with clay or mortar and building small dams to direct flow.
*   **Hillside Catchment:** Involves digging contour trenches to intercept sheet runoff, lining the trench, sloping it toward a collection point, and building a settling basin to filter silt.

## Tools
*   Screens (wire mesh, woven branches, cloth stretched over a frame) for inlets and gutter guards.
*   Drip edges (metal or wood strips) for roof edges.
*   Clay, mortar, or pitch for sealing joints and cracks.
*   Containers or stone-and-clay basins for collection at the lowest drainage point.
*   Measuring tools for calculating required area and flow rates.

## Process
### Catchment Design Principles
1.  **Identify the three parts:** Collection surface, conveyance, and inlet.
2.  **Rule 1 (Area):** Maximize collection area.
3.  **Rule 2 (Transit):** Minimize loss in transit.
4.  **Rule 3 (Contamination):** Exclude contamination.

### Ground Catchment Process
1.  **Step 1 — Select a sloped area:** Create artificial slope if the ground is flat.
2.  **Step 2 — Prepare the surface:** Seal the ground using an appropriate treatment (e.g., compacted clay, plastic sheeting).
3.  **Step 3 — Build perimeter berms:** Surround the area with low earth berms (15-20 cm high) to contain water and direct it downhill.
4.  **Step 4 — Install a collection channel:** Dig a shallow (V-shaped or U-shaped) channel at the low point and line it.
5.  **Step 5 — Screen the inlet:** Place a coarse screen at the channel entrance to catch debris and sediment.

### Conveyance Process
1.  **Determine Peak Flow:** Calculate the flow rate based on roof area and expected rainfall intensity.
2.  **Size Gutters:** Ensure the gutter cross-section capacity exceeds the peak flow rate.
3.  **Prevent Clogs:** Install gutter guards, downspout screens, and establish a regular cleaning schedule.

### Maintenance Schedule
| Task | Frequency | Consequence of Neglect |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Clean gutters and screens | Monthly (and after storms) | Clogs, overflow, wasted water |
| Check gutter slope and supports | Every 3 months | Pooling, sagging, eventual collapse |
| Inspect and patch catchment surface | Every 6 months | Leaks, reduced efficiency |
| Clean settling basins | Monthly during rainy season | Sediment overflow into storage |
| Re-seal joints (pitch, clay, mortar) | Annually | Leaks, contamination entry |
| Re-compact ground catchments | Annually (before rainy season) | Surface becomes permeable, water soaks in |
| Inspect and replace mesh screens | Annually | Holes allow debris and insects through |

## Notes
*   Catchment engineering is defined as a collection surface, a conveyance channel, and a screened inlet working together without leaks or clogs.
*   Thatch roofs require filtering and treatment due to the absorption of the first 5-10 mm of rain and the release of organic matter.
*   The greatest gain in yield comes from increasing catchment area (Rule 1), making it the priority variable.
*   The difference between collecting 20 liters and 2,000 liters is attributed to catchment engineering.
