# Contamination Prevention: Keeping Water Clean

## Objective
To prevent the recontamination of purified water by implementing a discipline of hygiene and systematic control over the pathway between water purification and consumption.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- Clean water supply.
- Basic tools (containers, hands, soap production materials).
- Behavioral discipline from all users.

## Material
- Water containers
- Soap production materials (Wood ash, rendered animal fat).
- Abrasive cleaning agents (Fine sand, crushed charcoal).
- Hand hygiene tools (Hands, cups, utensils).
- Insect prevention materials (Fine mesh covers, cordage).
- Spigot components (Hollow tube materials like bamboo, elderberry pith, plastic pen barrels, rubber hose).

## System
### Water Safety Zone
Water storage locations relative to camp functions:
- Inner Zone (0-5m): Water containers, hand-wash station.
- Clean Zone (5-15m): Food preparation, cooking, eating areas.
- Buffer Zone (15-30m): Sleeping areas, general camp activities.
- Contamination Zone (30m+): Latrines, animal pens, garbage, compost, butchering.

### The Pathway Principle
The path between latrines and water supply must include the hand-wash station as a mandatory waypoint to make correct behavior automatic.

### Group Water Discipline
- Designated water handler to minimize hand contact.
- Each person must have their own drinking vessel (no communal cups).
- Sick people must receive separate water.
- Children require adult supervision at the water supply.

### Contamination Response Protocol
1. Stop dispensing suspect water.
2. Assess the event (e.g., dirty hands, open container, visible contamination).
3. Decide: re-purify or discard.
4. Clean the container before refilling.
5. Identify and fix the system failure that caused the contamination (e.g., fix the lid, move the wash station).

## Technology
- **Physical Modification:** Building a simple spigot by drilling/punching a hole near the bottom of a container and sealing it with pitch, beeswax, or cloth.
- **Long-Term Infrastructure:**
    - Covered well with a windlass or pump to prevent surface contamination.
    - Piped distribution (bamboo or hollowed-log pipes) for transport.
    - Dedicated water storage building (roofed, walled structure).
- **Force Multiplier:** Soap production (Wood ash lye combined with rendered animal fat).

## Tools
- Spigot/Tap (for pouring/dispensing).
- Dedicated ladle (must be stored hygienically).
- Measuring instruments (implied for process control).

## Process
### Hand Hygiene Protocol
**When to Wash:** Before filling containers, before dispensing, after using the latrine, after handling raw meat/hides/soil, after caring for a sick person, after handling waste.

**How to Wash Without Soap:**
1. Wet hands thoroughly.
2. Apply abrasive cleaning agent (Wood ash, fine sand, crushed charcoal, plant leaves, or clean water alone).
3. Scrub for a minimum of 20 seconds, focusing on between fingers, under nails, and backs of hands.
4. Rinse thoroughly with clean water.
5. Air-dry or shake dry; do not wipe on dirty clothing.

### Water Contamination Vectors
| Vector | How It Works | Frequency |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Hands | Fecal-oral transmission via unwashed hands touching water, container rims, or ladles | Most common |
| Insects | Flies and mosquitoes carry pathogens on their bodies and deposit them on water surfaces | Very common |
| Airborne particles | Dust, ash, and windblown debris carry bacteria and parasites into uncovered water | Common |
| Container surfaces | Biofilm on unwashed container walls, dirty lids, contaminated spigots | Common |
| Cross-contamination | Using the same utensil for raw food and drinking water, or storing water near waste | Occasional |

### Dispensing Methods
| Method | Contamination Risk | Notes |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Spigot/tap at bottom | Very Low | Best option — nothing enters the container |
| Pouring from a narrow mouth | Low | Tip and pour; do not touch the interior |
| Dedicated ladle | Moderate | Must be stored hygienically; handle only |
| Scooping with a shared cup | High | The cup carries whatever was on the last person’s hands |
| Reaching in with hands | Very High | Guaranteed contamination |

### Insect Prevention Measures
1. Fine mesh covers: Stretch fine-weave cloth, netting, or woven grass over openings, tied down securely (mesh must be fine enough to stop mosquitoes, approx. 1 mm gaps or smaller).
2. Eliminate standing water near storage: Drain puddles and empty unused containers.
3. Smoke deterrent: A small smoky fire (damp wood or green leaves on coals) near storage.
4. Sealed containers: The most effective prevention is a sealed container with a spigot.
5. Move away from latrines: Place water storage at least 30 meters from latrines, compost, garbage, or animal pens (Upwind is better than downwind).

## Notes
- Recontamination via unwashed hands is identified as the number one cause of waterborne illness in crisis situations.
- The cost of re-boiling water in response to contamination is trivial compared to the cost of an outbreak.
- Container hygiene rules include: One purpose per container, Clean before every refill (scrub with sand/ash, rinse, sanitize), Lids must stay on, No fingers past the rim (sterile zone), and Store above the ground.
- Behavioral design is emphasized, making the right action the easy action (e.g., the hand-wash station placement).
- The five contamination vectors (Hands, Insects, Airborne particles, Container surfaces, Cross-contamination) are the primary pathways for recontamination.
