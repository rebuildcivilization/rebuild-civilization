# Water Finding

## Objective
To locate safe water sources and ensure survival, recognizing that finding water is about reading the landscape, understanding where water collects and flows, and knowing which signs point toward hidden sources.

**Key Survival Principle:** Water outranks every other survival priority except immediate physical safety. Dehydration degrades judgment, coordination, and endurance, making finding water a high priority before building shelter or foraging food.

**Daily Water Requirements:**

| Condition | Minimum (liters/day) | Recommended (liters/day) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Sedentary, cool climate | 2 | 3 |
| Light activity, moderate climate | 3 | 4 |
| Heavy activity, hot climate | 4-6 | 6-8 |
| Extreme heat or exertion | 6-10+ | As much as available |

## Dependencies and prerequisites

Before finding water, one must read the landscape, listen for running water, and understand the rules of gravity and geology.

**Prerequisites for Safe Water:**

*   **Water Purification:** Even spring water must be purified before drinking because bacteria, viruses, or parasites are invisible.
*   **Observation Skills:** The ability to read topographic indicators and observe vegetation patterns is necessary.

## Material

*   Clear plastic sheet (1-2 meters square) for solar stills.
*   A container (cup, bottle, can) for collection.
*   Absorbent cloths (cotton, any fabric) for dew collection.
*   Digging tool.

## System

### Landscape Analysis System
Water obeys gravity, flowing downhill and collecting in low points. The system relies on identifying topographic indicators, listening, and recognizing specific geological and biological markers.

### Water Source Hierarchy
1.  **Springs (Gold Standard):** Water filtered through rock and soil, often emerging cleaner than surface water. Require purification.
2.  **Rivers and Streams:** Flowing water, requiring caution in collection points.
3.  **Groundwater:** Water found when no surface water is visible, requiring digging or extraction methods.
4.  **Standing Water (Lakes and Ponds):** Least desirable, but often the most available, requiring collection from the deepest point.

### Water Source Avoidance System
Sources to avoid include those near mining operations (heavy metals), agricultural fields (pesticides/nitrates), cities/industrial zones (chemical contamination), salt water/brackish water, and areas with dead animals.

## Technology

### Solar Still Technology
*   **Mechanism:** Extracts moisture from soil and vegetation using evaporation and condensation.
*   **Construction:** Involves digging a hole (60-90 cm deep, 90 cm wide), placing a container in the center, filling the space with moist material, stretching a plastic sheet over it, sealing the edges with soil, and placing a weight on the sheet to create a cone for condensation.

### Dew Collection Technology
*   **Mechanism:** Traps moisture that forms heavily before dawn.
*   **Process:** Tying absorbent cloths around the legs, walking through tall grass before sunrise, and wringing the cloths into a container.

## Tools

*   Digging tool.
*   Containers.
*   Absorbent cloths.
*   A small rock or weight (for solar still).

## Process

### 1. Reading the Landscape
*   **Topographic Indicators:** Identify valleys and draws (lowest terrain), green vegetation in dry areas (indicators of near-surface water like cottonwood/willow), rock formations where permeable meets impermeable layers (look for springs), and converging animal trails.
*   **Listening:** Stop, stay still, and listen to determine the audible distance of running water (Small brook: 50-100m; Moderate stream: 100-300m; River: 300-1000+m).

### 2. Surface Water Collection
*   **Rivers and Streams:** Collect from fast-moving sections over rocky beds, upstream of settlements/stagnant sections, and where the water is deepest in the channel.
*   **Springs:** Look for rock type changes, lush vegetation, wet ground, or pools with no obvious stream feeding them.
*   **Lakes and Ponds:** Collect from the deepest accessible point; seek areas where moving water mixes with standing water.

### 3. Groundwater Extraction
*   **Digging for Water (Dry Riverbeds):** Identify the lowest point on the outside bend of a riverbed, dig a hole (30-60 cm diameter, 30-90 cm deep), and wait for seepage.
*   **Solar Still Application:** Execute the construction steps to yield 200-500 ml per day per still in good conditions.

### 4. Emergency Sources
*   **Rain Collection:** Spread waterproof material to catch rain.
*   **Plant Transpiration Bags:** Tie a clear plastic bag around a leafy tree branch in full sun to trap transpired moisture (Yield: 50-200 ml per bag per day).
*   **Morning Frost/Ice:** Scrape frost into a container and melt it.
*   **Vines:** Cut vines high first, then low, to catch water dripping from the upper cut.

## Notes

*   **Purification Imperative:** The most critical takeaway is that *all* water sources must be purified (boiled, filtered, or treated) because bacterial, viral, and parasitic contamination is invisible and can occur even in seemingly safe sources like springs.
*   **Solar Still Supplement:** The solar still yield (200-500 ml/day) is considered a supplement, not a primary water source.
*   **Contamination Risk:** Springs near agricultural or industrial sites, and lakes with no visible inlet/outlet, pose higher contamination risks.
