# Escape Routes

## Objective
An escape route is a pre-planned alternative path that leads to a specific safe location — not just away from danger — and must be designed, briefed, and rehearsed before a crisis, not during one.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   An escape route must be designed, briefed, and rehearsed before a crisis.
*   Group members must know the primary escape route for each leg of travel.
*   Communication protocols (hand signals or code words) must be established.

## Material
**Types of Escape Routes**
*   **Lateral Escapes:** Move perpendicular to the primary route to bypass an obstacle or threat, then resume the original direction of travel.
*   **Retreat Routes:** Reverse direction to return to the last safe position.
*   **Alternate Destination Routes:** Abandon the original destination entirely and head for a different safe location.

**Fixed Location Escape Planning (Camp)**
*   Two exits minimum are required for any camp or shelter.
*   Pre-positioned supplies (water, food, fire-starting kit) should be cached along the escape route, 500-1000 meters from camp.
*   Night escape rehearsal is necessary.

## System
**Core Concept**
Escape routes handle cases where the primary route becomes impassable, dangerous, or compromised, buying time and options between a controlled withdrawal and a panicked flight.

**Principles of Planning**
1.  **Plan Before Need:** Every route plan must include at least one escape route for each major leg of the journey.
2.  **Lead to Safety:** The route must lead to a specific safe location (defensible position, shelter, water source, rally point), not just away from danger.
3.  **Divert Early:** Escape routes should branch off early, at clear decision points, before reaching the hazard zone.
4.  **Navigable Under Stress:** Routes must follow obvious features (roads, rivers, ridgelines, fences) so they can be followed when running, injured, or terrified.

**Rally Point Protocol**
*   Rally points must be set at every major waypoint, the start/end of hazard zones, and obvious features.
*   Rules: Group members must know all points. If separated, go to the nearest rally point and wait for an agreed time (e.g., 2 hours).
*   If no one arrives within the agreed time, proceed to the next rally point.
*   Signaling: Leave a sign indicating presence and direction of travel.

**Trigger Protocol**
*   **Hard Triggers (Immediate Action):** Gunfire/explosions, flash flood warning signs, wildfire smoke thickening rapidly, direct confrontation with hostile groups.
*   **Soft Triggers (Assessment Required):** Route taking significantly longer than planned, weather deteriorating, group member’s condition worsening, signs of unexpected recent human activity.

**Communication Protocol**
*   Must include: Primary escape routes, all rally points and wait-time rules, hard triggers, and hand signals or code words for "execute escape route."

## Technology
*   **Navigation Aids:** Use handrail features whenever possible for escape routes.

## Tools
*   Sketch map or mental model for planning.

## Process
**Planning Escape Routes: Step by Step**

**Step 1: Identify Hazard Zones**
Mark areas where problems are most likely:
*   Choke points (bridges, mountain passes, narrow valleys, tunnels)
*   Exposure zones (open ground with no cover, elevated ridgelines visible from far away)
*   Crossing points (rivers, roads, borders between territories)
*   Known threat areas (settlements controlled by hostile groups, areas with recent conflict)

**Step 2: For Each Hazard Zone, Identify an Alternative**
Determine the escape option for each hazard:
*   Bridge out: Ford the river upstream at a wide, shallow section (scout in advance if possible).
*   Road blocked or ambushed: Parallel the road at 200-500m offset through adjacent terrain.
*   Mountain pass snowed in: Retreat to the valley and take the longer route around the range.
*   Settlement hostile: Bypass through forest/fields at night, rejoining the route 2-3 km beyond.
*   Valley flooding: Climb immediately to the nearest ridgeline; follow it in your direction of travel.
*   Fire approaching: Move perpendicular to the wind direction; seek water, rock, or bare ground.

**Step 3: Define Rally Points**
Set rally points at:
*   Every major waypoint on the primary route.
*   The start and end of each hazard zone.
*   Obvious, easily found features (road junctions, bridges, hilltops, large distinctive trees).

**Step 4: Define Triggers**
Decide in advance the conditions that trigger an escape route.

**Camp Escape Planning**
1.  **Two exits minimum.** Identify an off-trail escape direction if only one trail exists.
2.  **Pre-position supplies.** Cache supplies 500-1000 meters from camp.
3.  **Night escape rehearsal.** Practice following the route in the dark.
4.  **Sentries and early warning:** The sentry's purpose is to buy 2-5 minutes to execute the plan.
*   **The 30-Second Grab Rule:** Keep critical gear (water, knife, fire kit, first aid) in one bag to grab in 30 seconds.

## Notes
*   **Crucial Distinction:** An escape route must lead to a specific safe location, not just away from danger.
*   **Followed Rule:** Do not retreat along a route where you were followed; instead, use a lateral escape to move off the trail at a 90-degree angle, then change direction later.
*   **Rehearsal is Key:** The difference between a controlled withdrawal and a panicked flight is planning and rehearsing.
*   **Communication Mandate:** A plan that only one person knows is not a plan.
