# Objective

Putting together a survival kit suited to the environment and the way it is carried.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
- environment;
- expected duration;
- mobility;
- season;
- possibility of rescue.

## Material
Break down into:
- fire;
- water;
- food procurement;
- shelter;
- signalling;
- first aid;
- navigation;
- repairs;
- cutting tools;
- lighting.

## System
Include the principle:

the kit must be organised in layers and critical items must remain accessible even if the rucksack is lost.
 
### Layered Kit Design
A survival kit should be adapted to the expected hazards, climate, duration, mobility requirements, and likelihood of rescue.

Organize equipment in layers:

- **On-person essentials:** Items that remain accessible if the main pack is lost.
- **Immediate-access equipment:** Items needed quickly for shelter, water, communication, or first aid.
- **Main pack:** Equipment for sustained needs and less immediate tasks.

Prioritize reliable equipment and redundancy for critical functions.

A compact kit can provide a useful starting point, but no fixed list of items is suitable for every environment or emergency.

## Technology
- multi-purpose equipment;
- redundancy;
- waterproofing;
- modular packing.

## Tools

Give examples, not a mandatory universal list:
- knife;
- metal container;
- lighter;
- backup fire starter;
- cordage;
- shelter material;
- compass;
- signalling device;
- first-aid kit;
- torch/headlamp.

## Process
- Analyse the environment.
- Identify the main threats.
- Select multi-purpose items.
- Create redundancy for fire/water/signalling.
- Protect critical items from water.
- Keep essential items on your person.
- Test the kit in real-life conditions.

## Notes 
A minimalist configuration comprise four items: a space blanket, headlamp, lighter and metal cup. I would suggest this as an example of the bare minimum, not as a universal kit.
