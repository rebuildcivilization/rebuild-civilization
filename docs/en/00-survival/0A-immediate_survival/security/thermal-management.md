# Thermal Management

## Objective
Part of Emergency Shelter. The primary objective is staying warm in a shelter by understanding how the body loses heat and implementing strategies to stop it.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
*   Windproof walls
*   Insulation layers (ground barriers, wall insulation, ceiling retention)
*   Dry materials (to prevent moisture)
*   Pre-sleep activity (eating or brief exercise)

## Material
*   Insulation materials (dry pine/spruce boughs, dry grass, hay, dry leaves, bark slabs, logs)
*   Clothing layers
*   Reflective materials (for the ceiling)
*   Heat sources (fire, hot rocks)
*   Plastic sheet (for the vapor barrier trick)

## System
### The Dead Air Principle
Every effective insulation system works by trapping small pockets of air that cannot circulate. Still air is one of the best insulators available.

### Three Concentric Thermal Zones
1.  **Layer 1 — Ground Barrier (Most Critical):** Minimum 10 cm (4 inches) of compressed insulation beneath the body.
2.  **Layer 2 — Air Space Management:** Shelter walls must block wind completely on the windward side, with a small ventilation gap near the top on the leeward side to prevent condensation.
3.  **Layer 3 — Heat Retention Ceiling:** A low ceiling traps warm air near the body. Reflective material on the ceiling doubles effectiveness, and the ceiling should be angled to reflect fire heat downward.

### Temperature Zones and Requirements
| Outside Temp | Insulation Needed Below | Shelter Priority |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 10°C / 50°F | 5-8 cm (2-3 in) | Windbreak + ground pad |
| 0°C / 32°F | 10-15 cm (4-6 in) | Enclosed shelter + sealed gaps |
| -10°C / 14°F | 15-25 cm (6-10 in) | Small enclosed shelter + fire reflector |
| -20°C / -4°F | 25+ cm (10+ in) | Snow shelter or heavy debris hut + fire |

### Heat Loss Mechanisms
| Mechanism | How It Works | % of Heat Loss (Typical) | Countermeasure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Conduction** | Direct contact with cold surfaces | 40-50% when lying on ground | Insulation layer beneath you |
| **Convection** | Moving air carries heat away | 20-30% in wind | Windproof walls, small shelter volume |
| **Radiation** | Body emits infrared energy outward | 15-25% | Reflective surfaces, layered clothing |
| **Evaporation** | Sweat and moisture wick heat | 10-20% | Stay dry, ventilate to prevent condensation |

## Technology
*   **Insulation Effectiveness:** Achieved by trapping dead air pockets (fluffy grass outperforms packed grass; multiple thin clothing layers beat one thick layer).
*   **Moisture Management:** Managing moisture is key; damp insulation loses 90% of its effectiveness.
*   **Heat Generation:** Digestion (fats and proteins) generates heat; brief exercise raises core temperature.

## Tools
*   Fire
*   Hot rocks (fist-sized, wrapped in cloth/grass)
*   Space blanket or reflective material (for the ceiling)
*   Vapor barrier trick (plastic sheet)

## Process
### Thermal Layering Sequence
1.  **Layer 1 (Ground Barrier):** Place minimum 10 cm of compressed insulation; use dry pine/spruce boughs or dry grass for best results.
2.  **Layer 2 (Air Space Management):** Ensure walls are windproof; maintain a small ventilation gap on the leeward side.
3.  **Layer 3 (Heat Retention Ceiling):** Use a low ceiling and reflective material to trap warm air.

### Moisture Management Protocol
1.  **Test Materials:** Squeeze a handful; if water drips, the material is too wet.
2.  **Rotation:** Flip or replace ground insulation every 2-3 days.
3.  **Dry Near Fire:** Spread tomorrow’s bedding near (not on) the fire during the day.
4.  **Vapor Barrier:** Place a plastic sheet between the ground and the insulation layer to stop upward wicking of moisture.

### Body Heat Optimization
1.  Eat before sleeping (fats and proteins preferred).
2.  Exercise briefly before bed (5 minutes of vigorous movement, stopping before sweating).
3.  Apply heat to the core (chest, abdomen) using hot rocks, avoiding extremities.
4.  If sharing, lie facing the same direction for combined heat output (roughly 150 watts for two people).

### Quick Diagnostic Checklist
1.  **Wind:** Check for air movement; seal gaps with debris, moss, or mud.
2.  **Ground:** Feel the cold through the insulation; add more material if cold.
3.  **Volume:** Fill corners with debris or pull walls closer.
4.  **Moisture:** Check for dampness; replace or add a dry layer.
5.  **Ventilation:** Confirm one small opening for air exchange to prevent condensation.

## Notes
*   Ground contact kills first; prioritizing insulation beneath the body is paramount.
*   The ground steals heat 25 times faster than air at the same temperature.
*   The main takeaway is that all insulation works by trapping dead air.
*   Body moisture migration downward is a critical factor that requires proactive management.
*   Avoid sweating at all costs, as wet clothing dramatically accelerates hypothermia.
*   A solo shelter interior of roughly 0.6 m wide × 2 m long × 0.6 m high (2 × 6.5 × 2 feet) is considered ideal for volume.
