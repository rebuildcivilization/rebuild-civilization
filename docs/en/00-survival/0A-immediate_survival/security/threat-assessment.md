# Threat Assessment

## Objective
The objective of threat assessment is the systematic process of cataloging dangers, estimating their probability and severity, and allocating limited resources to address the most critical ones first. Survival depends on accurately identifying what can kill you and how likely it is to happen.

## Dependencies and prerequisites
Survival depends on accurately identifying threats and correctly prioritizing responses. Effective assessment requires the ability to observe, analyze, and act on the identified risks.

## Material
Threats are categorized into three main areas:

### Wildlife Threats

*   **Predators to assess by region:**
    *   Large carnivores: bears, wolves, mountain lions, feral dog packs (feral dogs are the most underestimated threat).
    *   Venomous animals: snakes, spiders, scorpions.
    *   Disease vectors: rats, mosquitoes, ticks (historically kill more people than all predators combined).
    *   Livestock gone feral: bulls, boars (feral pigs), and feral horses.

*   **Assessment questions:**
    1. What large predators exist within a 20 km radius of your location?
    2. Are there bodies of standing water that will breed mosquitoes?
    3. Have you seen signs of feral dog packs — tracks, scat, howling at night?
    4. What venomous species are native to your region and where do they shelter?

### Environmental and Weather Hazards

*   **Flash flooding:** Requires knowing elevation relative to waterways. Fatal if in a valley or floodplain during heavy rain.
*   **Extreme cold:** Hypothermia kills in hours; requires assessing shelter insulation and fuel supply.
*   **Extreme heat:** Results in dehydration and heat stroke; water requirements double or triple in hot conditions.
*   **Wildfire:** Requires assessing prevailing wind directions and vegetation density, as grass fires can cover kilometers quickly.
*   **Structural collapse:** Requires avoiding sheltering in damaged buildings, bridges, and infrastructure.

### Human Threats

*   **Phases of human threat escalation:**
    *   Days 1-7: People are in shock, cooperative, waiting for help.
    *   Days 7-21: Desperation sets in as food runs out; theft and opportunistic violence increase.
    *   Weeks 3-8: Organized groups form (some cooperative, some predatory).
    *   Months 2+: Territorial patterns establish; groups are either well-organized communities or dangerous raiding parties.

*   **Indicators of hostile intent:** Groups that approach without calling out, armed individuals who flank rather than stay grouped, scouts observing the location over multiple days, and signs of recent looting (broken doors, scattered goods, abandoned vehicles with emptied trunks).


## System
### Building a Risk Matrix
The risk matrix plots probability against impact to create a priority ranking, determining where time and energy should be spent.

**Probability Scale:**
| Rating | Description | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 — Rare | Could happen but unlikely | Bear attack in urban area |
| 2 — Unlikely | Possible under certain conditions | Flash flood in dry season |
| 3 — Possible | Has a reasonable chance | Encounter with desperate strangers |
| 4 — Likely | Expected to happen eventually | Food spoilage without refrigeration |
| 5 — Almost certain | Will happen without action | Waterborne illness from untreated sources |

**Impact Scale:**
| Rating | Description | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 1 — Negligible | Minor inconvenience | Mosquito bites (no malaria zone) |
| 2 — Minor | Manageable setback | Small food cache discovered by animals |
| 3 — Moderate | Significant but recoverable | Injury requiring rest and basic first aid |
| 4 — Major | Life-threatening or major loss | Loss of primary shelter or food supply |
| 5 — Catastrophic | Fatal or total loss | Armed confrontation, structural collapse |

**Applying the Matrix:**
Multiply probability by impact. Threats scoring 15+ require immediate action. Threats scoring 8-14 need a plan within the first week. Threats below 8 are addressed as resources allow. 

**Sample Assessment:**
| Threat | Probability | Impact | Score | Action |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Contaminated water | 5 | 5 | 25 | Immediate: establish water purification |
| Food exhaustion | 4 | 5 | 20 | Immediate: inventory and ration |
| Desperate strangers | 3 | 4 | 12 | This week: establish perimeter, plan escape routes |
| Feral dog pack | 3 | 3 | 9 | This week: secure shelter, noise deterrents |
| House fire (no fire dept) | 2 | 5 | 10 | This week: fire prevention, water buckets |
| Snake bite | 2 | 4 | 8 | Ongoing: watch footing, learn species |

### Terrain and Location Analysis

Defensibility factors include elevation (provides visibility and drainage), approach routes (fewer approaches are better), cover and concealment (hides threats but exposes you if open), water access (critical but waterways are travel corridors), and distance from population centers (more competition for resources in the short term, more potential for long-term community building).

**360-degree Survey Conduct:**
1. Walk the perimeter at 50m, 200m, and 500m distances.
2. Note every approach path (roads, trails, game paths, dry creek beds).
3. Identify dead ground (areas unobservable from shelter).
4. Mark water sources, food sources, and natural barriers.
5. Record findings on a hand-drawn map and update regularly.

### Resource Vulnerability Assessment

Resources that advertise supply worth taking include visible resources (smoke, lights, gardens, livestock, vehicles), a noise signature (generators, tools, conversation carries further), a scent signature (cooking food smell travels hundreds of meters downwind), and waste (trash/human waste indicates population density and diet).

**Signature Reduction Strategies:**
*   Cook during daylight when smoke is less visible.
*   Use concealment techniques to hide presence.
*   Dispose of waste away from living areas and bury it.

### Reassessment Protocol

*   **Daily:** Walk the immediate perimeter, check water source for contamination/upstream changes, note new animal behavior.
*   **Weekly:** Extend patrol radius (1-2 km in each direction), update map, reassess risk matrix based on new information.
*   **Seasonally:** Account for wildlife shifts (hibernation, migration), weather profile changes, and human behavior changes (competition increases in winter).

### Common Assessment Mistakes

*   Anchoring on one threat (e.g., focusing only on raiders while ignoring water quality).
*   Normalcy bias (assuming things will return to normal).
*   Underestimating timeline (thinking there are weeks when there might only be days).
*   Ignoring the mundane (twisted ankle, infected cut, spoiled food).
*   Assessment paralysis (spending too long analyzing threats without acting; a good-enough plan executed now is better than a perfect plan next week).

### Working with Limited Information

*   **Assume capability, not intent:** Observe what someone is capable of, not what they intend. A group with weapons is capable of violence regardless of intent.
*   **Update continuously:** Every new piece of information must feed back into the risk matrix by crossing out old entries, adding new ones, and adjusting scores.
*   **Share assessments:** Threat assessment should be shared; the person noticing an anomaly may not be the assessor, but they need to know what to watch for.
*   **Trust your gut, then verify:** Instinct processes information faster than conscious thought, but all instincts must be followed with investigation.

## Process
1. Catalog dangers (Threat Categories).
2. Determine probability and impact (Risk Matrix).
3. Calculate score (Probability x Impact).
4. Determine action based on score (Immediate, Within Week, As Resources Allow).
5. Conduct 360-degree terrain survey.
6. Assess resource vulnerability.
7. Execute Reassessment Protocol (Daily, Weekly, Seasonally).


## Notes
- Threat assessment is defined as the systematic process of cataloging dangers, estimating probability and severity, and allocating resources.
- The core principle is to focus on the right threats; focusing on the wrong ones leads to death (e.g., ignoring contaminated water while defending against raiders).
- The assessment process emphasizes continuous iteration rather than a single perfect plan.
- Wildlife threats are a major concern once human infrastructure collapses, with feral dogs being a highly underestimated predator.
- Resource management is critical; reducing the noise, scent, and visible signature is essential for survival.
